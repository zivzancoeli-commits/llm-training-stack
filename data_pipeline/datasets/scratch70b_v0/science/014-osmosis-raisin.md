title: Osmosis with a raisin in water vs salt water
approx_words: 280
---

Osmosis is water moving across a membrane toward the side with more dissolved stuff (lower water "availability"), if the membrane lets water through more easily than the solute.

Raisin in plain water: the raisin's interior is sugary. Water tends to enter. The raisin plumps.

Raisin in very salty water: the outside may be "more concentrated" than the inside. Water tends to leave. The raisin shrivels further.

The membrane and the concentration difference set the direction. There is no magical attraction of water to salt in a cartoon sense; it is about probabilities and chemical potential. For this level, "water follows the higher solute concentration across a semipermeable membrane" is the working rule.

Check: if you punch holes in the raisin so sugar can freely mix, you no longer have the same osmotic setup; you have diffusion of sugar plus water, a stew.

People mix up osmosis and diffusion. Diffusion is spreading of a substance down its own gradient. Osmosis is specifically the water (solvent) flow tied to solute that cannot cross easily.

This is also why drinking seawater is a bad plan for humans: kidneys cannot cheaply dump salt enough to keep cells from losing water.
