# Photosynthesis as a Mass-Balance Story

Where does the mass of a tree come from? Most people say "the soil." Weigh the soil in a pot before and after growing a plant for years, though, and the soil loses only a small amount of mass while the plant gains kilograms. The bulk of a tree's dry mass comes from the air — specifically, from carbon dioxide — combined with water. Photosynthesis is the accounting process that makes this happen, and treating it as a mass-balance problem keeps the story honest.

## The ledger

The overall (simplified) reaction is:

6 CO₂ + 6 H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6 O₂

Read this as a ledger. On the input side: six molecules of carbon dioxide and six of water. On the output side: one molecule of glucose and six of oxygen. Sunlight provides the energy to drive the reaction uphill, but light carries no atoms — every atom in the glucose must be traceable to CO₂ or H₂O.

Count the atoms to verify the books balance:

- **Carbon:** 6 on the left (one per CO₂), 6 on the right (all in glucose). ✔
- **Hydrogen:** 12 on the left (two per H₂O), 12 on the right (all in glucose). ✔
- **Oxygen:** left side has 12 from CO₂ plus 6 from H₂O = 18; right side has 6 in glucose plus 12 in O₂ = 18. ✔

No atom appears or vanishes. That's conservation of mass doing its job.

A subtlety worth knowing: isotope-labeling experiments showed that the O₂ released comes from the *water* molecules, not from the CO₂. The water is split; the CO₂'s oxygen mostly ends up in the sugar and in newly formed water inside the cell (the fuller equation uses 12 H₂O in and 6 H₂O back out). The simplified equation is still mass-balanced, but the atom-tracing detail matters if you ask "which oxygen goes where."

## From sugar to wood

Glucose is the plant's raw currency. Some is burned back (respiration) for energy — running the reverse reaction — and some is polymerized into cellulose and other structural molecules. Cellulose is essentially chains of glucose units, so a log is, to a decent approximation, captured atmospheric carbon plus hydrogen and oxygen from water, organized by sunlight.

## Check: rough numbers with units

Do the units and magnitudes make sense? Molar masses: CO₂ ≈ 44 g/mol, H₂O ≈ 18 g/mol, glucose ≈ 180 g/mol, O₂ ≈ 32 g/mol.

Inputs per "batch": 6 × 44 + 6 × 18 = 264 + 108 = 372 g.
Outputs: 180 + 6 × 32 = 180 + 192 = 372 g. ✔ Mass in equals mass out, in grams, as it must.

Now a plausibility check on the "trees come from air" claim. To build 1 kg of glucose-based material, a plant needs 264/180 ≈ 1.47 kg of CO₂. A tree adding 10 kg of dry mass per year therefore needs roughly 15 kg of CO₂ annually drawn from the atmosphere. Air is about 0.04% CO₂ by volume, which sounds tiny, but the atmosphere is vast and leaves are efficient collection surfaces with enormous total area. The numbers are demanding but not absurd — consistent with the observation that plants grow slower when CO₂, water, or light is scarce.

The mass-balance framing also explains a converse fact: when wood burns, the reaction runs backward, releasing the stored CO₂ and water vapor. The mass of the log doesn't disappear into "nothing" — it leaves as gas, which is exactly where it came from.
