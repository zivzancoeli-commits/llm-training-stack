title: Greenhouse effect vs the ozone hole, different mechanisms
approx_words: 330
---

These two get mashed together in casual speech. They are different.

Greenhouse effect: some gases (CO2, water vapor, methane) let visible sunlight in but absorb infrared light that Earth tries to radiate to space. Energy stays in the climate system longer; surface and lower air warm. This is not a glass greenhouse's main trick (those also stop convection), but the name stuck.

Ozone hole: ozone (O3) in the stratosphere absorbs a lot of UV. Certain chlorine compounds, in polar winter chemistry, destroy ozone, letting more UV through to the surface. That is a shielding issue, not the same as trapping infrared.

You can have both problems at once. Fixing ozone (the Montreal Protocol, phasing down CFCs) does not automatically solve CO2-driven warming. Some ozone-safe replacements are still greenhouse gases.

A crude check: more CO2 -> more trapped IR -> warming story. Less stratospheric ozone -> more UV at ground -> sunburn and ecosystem story. Different wavelengths, different altitudes, different policy levers.

Common mash-up: "the greenhouse hole in the atmosphere." There is no hole in the greenhouse effect. There was a seasonal thinning of ozone over Antarctica, which is not a puncture in a CO2 blanket.
