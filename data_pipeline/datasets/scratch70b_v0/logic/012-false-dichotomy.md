title: False dichotomy, a third option that dissolves an argument
approx_words: 260
---

"Either we ban all cars or we do nothing about pollution." Those are not the only options. Filters, transit, different fuels, fewer trips — a menu, not a coin flip.

A false dichotomy treats a many-way choice as two. It is persuasive because two boxes are easy to punch.

Test: list a third option that is live, not sarcastic. If you can, the original "either/or" was incomplete. If you cannot, maybe it really was exhaustive (a number is either even or odd, in integers).

"Either you are with us or you are against us" hides "I am with you on this bill and against you on that one."

Check on a clean exhaustive case so you do not become allergic to all dichotomies: a light is on or off if we ignore burned-out bulbs and dimmers. Add a dimmer and the dichotomy was the model's fault.

When you train a model on argumentative internet text, this pattern is everywhere. Prefer "at least these options exist" over "only two."
