### User
Four of us ate. Three people got the regular plates. One person, Cam, added an appetizer, a second drink, and a dessert that was clearly not for the table. The server dropped one check. Someone said "just split four ways" like that was kindness. I can do the math. I just don't want to be the person who ruins the night over a dessert. I also don't want to pay for Cam's extra as a personality tax. How do I say this kindly and specifically so we leave still friends?

### Assistant
You almost have it. Kind is the tone. Specific is the numbers. "Just split four ways" is kind only if the orders were close. They were not.

Do not announce a moral. Announce a split.

A sentence that works at the table: "Let's split the three similar plates equally, and Cam covers the extra apps, drink, and dessert. I can check the line items with you." Then pause. You are not accusing Cam of stealing. You are matching money to food.

If you have the paper check, point at the lines with your finger, not with a speech. "This cluster is the three entrees and shared tax on those. This cluster is the extras." If the extras are listed as separate, even better. If everything is lumped, you can still group by memory while it is fresh: who ordered what. Do it now, not in the parking lot where it becomes gossip.

Kind specifics beat vague fairness. Vague fairness is how you seethe on the bus.

I will not invent the dollar amounts. You can see them on the ticket. I also will not invent a rule that friends must always even-split. Friends can itemize without a breakup.

If Cam says "it's only a little," you can stay kind and still not fold: "It adds up on my end this month. I'm good covering my plate plus a fair slice of tax and tip. Not the dessert." Short. No spreadsheet personality.

### User
Cam is already reaching for the card and saying they'll "get it this time" which I think means put the whole thing on their card and we venmo later. Two people already said "even is easier." I don't want to interrogate Cam in front of the server who wants the table.

### Assistant
Cam putting down a card can be helpful if it is a float, not a blur. If the whole check goes on one card, the later Venmo still needs the same split or you will recreate the fight in six smaller notifications.

Kind and specific, to the group, before the card is run: "If Cam floats it, I'll send my share for my meal plus tax and tip, not a fourth of the extras. Anyone who wants even-split can do that with Cam separately." You are not running a tribunal. You are stating your number.

To Cam, quieter if you can: "Thanks for floating. I'll send X when I see the total, minus the extra stuff that wasn't mine. Text me the receipt." X is whatever your lines plus your share of tax and tip. You calculate it from the ticket, not from a vibe.

The two people who want even can even. You are not their parent. You are not required to join a method that overpays. Friendship is not a group coupon.

Server is hovering: that is a reason to be brief, not a reason to surrender. One check, one card, one sentence, then you can stand up. Do this while everyone still has their coats, not after you are in three different cars.

If you truly cannot see the lines, take a photo of the check. Photo is specific. Memory after garlic bread is not.

Tip: split tip in the same ratio as the food you are actually paying for, or pick a simple percent on your portion. Do not under-tip the server because you are mad at dessert. The server did not order the extra. I will not quote a required percent; people and places differ. Be decent.

You already knew even-split was the lazy peace. You wanted language that does not sound like a cross-examination. You have it: your plate, your extras-not-yours, receipt, send the number. Stay warm in the face. Stay exact in the math. Leave with people you can still text tomorrow.
