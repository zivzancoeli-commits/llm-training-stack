### User
There's a new camera on the neighbor's gutter. The lens points at my back steps and the kitchen window over the sink. I noticed it at 6:45 a.m. while putting coffee grounds in the bin. I wear a T-shirt I wouldn't put on the internet. I don't know if it records audio. I don't want a fight over a gadget. I also don't want to wash dishes on a livestream. They just moved in in April.

### Assistant
Coffee first, then a look from your own yard, not from their driveway. Confirm the angle. If it's clearly your steps and window, that's a conversation. If it's their side yard and your window is a slice, that's a narrower ask: tilt it.

I'm not a lawyer. I will not invent recording laws, audio consent, or what a camera "can" point at where you live. Those rules vary. I can tell you that asking a new neighbor to not stare at your sink is a reasonable human request.

Don't bang on the door at 6:45 in the T-shirt. Evening or a note is enough.

Have you met them enough to knock, or is this still a wave-from-the-bin relationship?

### User
I helped them with a package in April. We know first names. I haven't been in their house. The camera has a little solar panel. It looks cheap. My partner wants to put up a sheet on the window tonight. I think a sheet looks like we have a secret.

### Assistant
A sheet is a curtain with worse PR. A cheap blind or the existing curtain pulled at dish time is ordinary. You don't have to live in a dark kitchen. You also don't have to perform innocence for a gadget.

For the next ten minutes this evening: write a short note or a text if you have their number. "Hi, it's [name] next door. The new camera on your gutter points at our back steps and sink window. Could you angle it toward your own yard? Happy to help you look from our side." That's specific. Not "I'm watching you watching me."

If you knock, knock at a human hour, not during their dinner. One of you talks, not a two-person tribunal on their stoop.

If they say it's for packages, you can say packages don't live in your kitchen. They can point at their own door. I won't tell you they must. You can still ask.

Don't cover their camera. Don't shine a laser. Don't start a gadget war. That's how cheap solar panels become a personality.

### User
If they say no, do we just live with it? My partner already named the camera. They called it The Eyeball. I'm going to hear that every night.

### Assistant
If they say no, you still have curtains, a different dish time, and whatever complaint path your lease or local office actually offers. I won't pretend that path is quick or that it exists in a useful form. Some towns have something. Some don't. You can look that up in daylight without quoting internet myths as law.

Living with it is an option. So is repeating the ask once with a photo from your steps showing the lens. One photo, not a dossier. If they still refuse, you stop expecting a friendly tilt and you treat the window as public-facing: shirt, curtain, don't have private calls at the sink.

Rename The Eyeball in your house if the joke is making you smaller. Jokes that run every night become the camera winning rent-free.

April neighbors can still be people you help with packages. This is a separate ask. Send it tonight after work, not at dawn with coffee grounds. Angle, not a fight. If they tilt it, thank them and drop it. If they don't, curtain and a factual follow-up, not a nickname war. You deserve to wash a mug without a lens as a guest. Ask for that in plain language. Then see what April does.
