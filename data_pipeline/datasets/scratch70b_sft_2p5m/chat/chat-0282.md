### User
I'm in a city I don't live in. The transit map looks like someone dropped yarn on a page. I need to get from the station I just arrived at to a friend's place across town. I have a phone, a half-charged battery, and a paper ticket I bought because the machine yelled at me in a language I only half read. I do not want to get on the wrong color line and end up in a depot. How do I do this without looking lost the whole time.

### Assistant
Looking a little lost is allowed. Looking like you are guessing with your feet is how you ride the wrong train. Do not board anything until you can say three things out loud: the line name or number, the direction (end-of-line station, not "north"), and the stop you get off at. If you cannot say those, you are not ready yet.

Open your maps app, set it to transit, and put in your friend's exact address, not the neighborhood name. Screenshot the route while you still have battery. Screenshots still work in tunnels.

I will not pretend I know this city's fare rules, which platform letters mean what, or whether your paper ticket covers transfers. Machines that yell are not a legal briefing. If the ticket has a time printed on it, that is the part to notice.

Is your friend's place a timed thing — dinner at a set hour — or can you be twenty minutes late without wrecking the night?

### User
Dinner, they said 7:30, it's 6:20, travel apps are arguing between 35 and 55 minutes. My friend texted "just take the blue one toward the river" which is not a real instruction. Battery is at 41 percent.

### Assistant
"Toward the river" is how locals talk and how visitors miss a stop. Text back: "What's the stop name I should get off at, and which end-of-line should be on the front of the train?" Make them give you nouns. You can still be polite. You do not have to pretend you understood yarn.

Put the phone on low power now. You need it for the last walk, not for refreshing the map every thirty seconds.

At the station, follow signs for the line number first, then the destination name. If two platforms claim the same color, believe the destination, not the color. Colors get reused in a way that feels personal and is not.

If an agent or a person in a vest is standing still, you may ask one question: "Does this platform go to [stop name]?" Not your life story. If nobody official is there, look at the next train's display, not at other passengers' faces.

Do not take a taxi yet. You have an hour. A confused forty minutes plus a walk still beats paying for a panic car unless the app starts saying you will miss 7:30 by a lot. I cannot quote fares. I can say your battery and your dignity both last longer if you slow down.

If you get on and the stops are not matching your screenshot, get off at the next station, not after five stops of hoping. Hoping is how you visit a depot.

### User
There's a guy offering to "help me validate" my ticket for cash. Also I think I have to tap something and I already have paper.

### Assistant
Decline the helper. You do not buy extra validation from a stranger with a smile. Walk to a machine or a marked gate. If the paper ticket is meant to go into a slot, the slot will look like every other slot: a mouth, an arrow. If you cannot tell, ask a staff person, not a freelance explainer.

I do not know whether paper and tap both exist in this system. If the gate stays shut, do not shove it. Step aside, let people through, and try the next gate or the staffed booth. Getting embarrassed at a gate is cheaper than getting stuck inside a fare mess you cannot explain later.

For the next ten minutes: screenshot the route, send the two-noun text to your friend, switch on low power, find the platform that matches the destination name, and stand where you can read the display. If a train comes that does not show your end-of-line, let it go. Another one will. Eat a snack if you have one; low blood sugar makes maps look meaner.

You will look at the map once more. Then you will ride. The yarn is a drawing. Your job is three nouns and a screenshot.
