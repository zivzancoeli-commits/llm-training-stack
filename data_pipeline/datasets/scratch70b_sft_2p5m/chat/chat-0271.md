### User
Work dinner, 7:30, Italian place. Boss ordered a bottle for the table before I sat down. Everyone has a glass. I don't drink. I haven't said that at this job yet. It's my third month. Water is already poured. The waiter is looking at me with the bottle. I don't want a story. I also don't want a full glass I will fake-sip. There's a client across from me.

### Assistant
You can say "I'm good with water" to the waiter, not to the table. The waiter is the easiest audience. A palm over the glass is a gesture they know. You don't owe a diagnosis, a religion, or a driving speech. I won't invent why people abstain. You don't have to either.

Fake-sipping a pour you didn't want is how you spend two hours managing a prop. Skip the prop.

Third month is not a reason to drink. A client across from you is not a reason to drink. A bottle already open is not a reason to drink.

Is the boss the kind who toasts and watches glasses, or do they order wine like bread?

### User
They toast. Last team's dinner, I heard, they noticed who didn't. I wasn't there. Tonight they already said "just a splash." The splash is in the air over my glass. I said "tiny bit" by accident because my mouth panicked. I don't want even a tiny bit.

### Assistant
"Tiny bit" was a panic. You can still stop the splash. "Actually, none — water's fine" to the waiter, now, while the bottle is hovering. Now is cheaper than after the red is in the bowl of the glass.

For the next ten minutes: get the pour stopped, keep the water, when the toast happens raise the water. If someone jokes, you can smile without explaining. If the boss says "come on," repeat "I'm good." Twice is a boundary. A story is what they want to file you under. Don't file yourself.

I don't know this workplace's drinking culture as a rule. I won't pretend a toast is legally required. It's a social squeeze. You can survive a squeeze with a clear glass of water. The client is eating pasta, not grading your liver.

Don't switch to "I'm on antibiotics" unless that's true. Invented medical plots become homework. Don't say you're the designated driver if you're not driving. True and boring: "I don't drink." If that feels huge in month three, "not tonight" is also true.

Sit back. Let the bottle travel. Eat bread.

### User
If they ask why in front of the client I will go blank. Also I already said tiny bit so I feel like I have to accept a splash to be consistent.

### Assistant
You don't have to be consistent with a panic word. People change an order. "None, please" is a correction, not a lie.

If they ask why in front of the client, you can say "personal preference" or "I just don't" and look at the menu. Blank is okay. Silence after a short answer is not rudeness. Filling silence with a fake antibiotic is worse.

If the splash lands anyway, you don't have to drink it. Leave it. Ask for a fresh water glass if the smell bothers you. You're not required to finish a mistake.

This is a first time at this job, not a first time as a person. You already know you don't drink. The table doesn't get a vote. Let the waiter save you by taking the bottle to the next glass. Raise water at the toast. Eat. The client will remember whether you listened, not whether you matched the vintage. I won't pretend that's guaranteed. It's still the better bet than a tiny bit you didn't want.

The hover is now. Speak to the bottle. Then you're done talking about it for the night. Pasta can be the topic. Work can be the topic. Your glass can stay clear. That's the whole refusal. It doesn't need a legend. It needs one sentence before the splash.
