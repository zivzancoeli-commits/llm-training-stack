### User
I got off to use the platform bathroom during a stop. The doors closed. The train left. My backpack is in the overhead two cars forward of where I was sitting, with my laptop and my wallet in the front pocket because I am an idiot. I have my phone and the jacket I was wearing. The next train is in 18 minutes according to the board. I do not know if I should tell staff or just get on the next one and chase it.

### Assistant
Tell staff now, before you chase. A backpack on a seat is easier to freeze in place if someone in a vest knows the train number, the car, and a description. Chasing without a report is how you and the bag play tag across stations.

Do not sprint across tracks. Do not jump down. The 18 minutes is for talking to a person and getting a plan, not for cardio.

I cannot tell you this railroad's lost-and-found rules, whether they will hold a train, or what they charge for anything. I can tell you what to say: train time, direction, last station, car if you know it, backpack color, and that a wallet and laptop are inside. Ask them to radio ahead. Ask where unclaimed bags go if nobody radios.

Stay on the platform where staff actually stand, not in the bathroom hallway.

Do you remember the train's final destination name, and is your ID only in the wallet or do you have a photo of it on your phone?

### User
Final destination is on the screenshot in my photos, I think. ID is in the wallet. Phone has a transit card in the wallet app. The board now says 14 minutes. There's a person in a uniform by the stairs, on a phone.

### Assistant
Go to the uniform, even if they are on a phone. Stand where they can see you. When they glance, "Sorry, I got off and my bag is still on the train that just left." Then the facts. If they wave you off, repeat once: "Can you radio the conductor?" If they truly cannot, ask who can. Do not accept a shrug as the end of the world; ask for the station number or the lost-and-found location in writing if they have a card.

Do not buy a new ticket you do not understand. Use the app if that is how this system works, or ask the same staff whether your existing fare still covers the next train. I will not guess.

Photograph the departure board showing the train that left and the one coming. Times help later if you have to tell a story to a desk.

If they tell you to take the next train and get off at the next major stop, that can be a real plan. If they tell you the bag will be pulled at the terminus, going to the terminus may be smarter than hopping every local. Follow the instruction you can repeat back. Repeat it: "So I go to X and ask at Y." If you cannot repeat it, you do not have a plan yet.

Your laptop may be gone. It also may be sitting there because people are not all thieves. Do not freeze on that. Change passwords from your phone if the laptop is unlocked by habit: email, work, bank. Do it from cellular, not a mystery station wifi. If the laptop is locked, still assume someone can try.

### User
I feel like if I change passwords I'm admitting it's stolen and I'll jinx it. That's dumb. Also I might cry in front of the uniform.

### Assistant
Changing passwords is not a jinx. It is a door lock. Do the ones that matter: email first, then work, then anything that can move money. If you cannot remember them all, start email. Crying in front of a uniform is allowed. They have seen worse than a backpack. A wet face does not cancel a radio call.

For the next ten minutes: talk to the uniform, write down the plan they give you, screenshot the board, start the email password change, and only then get on a train if that is the plan. If they tell you to wait here, wait here. Do not freelance a chase that staff did not describe.

You left a bag because you used a bathroom. That is human. The next move is a person with a radio, then your email, then a train with a destination you can name. Two cars ahead is still on the same system. Put it in the system before you try to outrun it.
