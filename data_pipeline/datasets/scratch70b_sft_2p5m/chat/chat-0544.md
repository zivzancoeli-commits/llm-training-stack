### User
Grocery line. Register screen faces the cashier. I can't see the total. I have a card. I always just tap and leave because asking feels like I'm accusing them of math. Today the bag of stuff looks smaller than the number I guessed in my head. First time I want the total said out loud before I tap. There's a person behind me with ice cream. Help me do this without a speech about rights.

### Assistant
You can ask for a number. That's shopping, not a lawsuit.

"Could you tell me the total before I pay?" One sentence. Look at them, not at the ice cream person. The ice cream person can wait ten seconds. That's a line, not a moral.

You are not accusing anyone of math. You are asking what you will be charged. Hidden screens are common. Repeating a total is also common. Cashiers do it all day when someone is buying alcohol or splitting cash. You are allowed to want the same number.

I will not invent a price or a scanning error. If the spoken total surprises you, then you ask to see the item list. That's the second sentence, only if you need it: "Can I see the lines? That seems high." Calm. Not a speech about rights.

Card stays in your hand until you hear the number. Don't tap out of embarrassment.

Is this a regular store where they know your face, or a big place where you're a blur?

### User
Big place. Self-checkout was down. This cashier looks tired. I already put the divider up like that would help. I feel like I'm about to be the difficult customer in their night.

### Assistant
Tired cashiers still say totals. Difficult would be filming them or lecturing. A total is their job.

If they rattle it and you miss it, "Once more, please." Missing a number is allowed. Pretending you heard it is how you tap blind.

Divider doesn't matter. Eye contact and the sentence matter. You can smile without making it a joke about being bad at money. Jokes invite them to rush you.

If they turn the screen, great. If they don't, the spoken number is enough to decide. If you need the list, they can scroll. You can also step aside with the cart if the ice cream is melting and you need a minute — but only after you've heard the total or asked for the list. Wandering off mid-pay is how lines get worse.

You are a blur in a big store. Blurs still get to know the charge.

### User
What if they already hit card and the machine is waiting and I freeze.

### Assistant
If the terminal is waiting, you can still say "what's the total?" before you tap. Waiting is not a contract. If you already tapped by reflex, look at the receipt before you leave the belt. Receipt is the delayed screen. If a line is wrong, you deal with it at customer service with the paper, not with a scene at the empty terminal.

Tonight, try the ask before the tap. First time will feel louder in your head than in the room. Ice cream can survive ten seconds. You don't need a rights speech. You need a number, then a tap or a question about the lines. That's a complete grocery trip.

### User
I asked. They said the total. It was higher than my guess because of a bag of something I didn't put up. I said so. They took it off. The ice cream person sighed. I tapped the new number. I feel both proud and like I delayed the city.

### Assistant
You delayed the line by the length of a correction. That's grocery, not a civic failure. A bag you didn't buy is supposed to come off. The ice cream person will freeze or they will pay. Not your ledger.

Next time you can ask for the total even when the guess matches. Practice when it's boring so the first time isn't only when you smell a miss. Hidden screens stay hidden. Spoken numbers stay cheap.

You didn't give a rights speech. You asked, they fixed a line, you paid. That's the whole skill. Proud is allowed. Don't apologize to the belt on the way out. Take the receipt anyway until you're outside. If a second ghost item shows later, you have paper. Tonight you already caught the live one. Go home.
