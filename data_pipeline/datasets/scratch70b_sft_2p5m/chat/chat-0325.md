### User
I've had eight sessions. I still freeze on the same kind of problem. The tutor is kind, on time, and spends a lot of the hour talking through methods I already watched on video. I am paying, or my parent is. Next session is tomorrow. I keep saying "yeah that makes sense" because I do not want to hurt them. It does not make sense when I am alone.

### Assistant
Kind and useful are different. You can keep the kindness in the room and still change the hour. "Yeah that makes sense" is how you bought eight sessions of a lecture.

Tomorrow should not be a surprise firing unless you already know you want out. The more useful move is a redirect: "I need to spend this hour on problems I get wrong, with me talking first, not a recap of the video." If they cannot do that, then you end it. If they can, you get a test week.

I will not diagnose a learning issue, and I will not say you are "bad at" the subject. Freezing on one type of problem is a specific clue. Bring two examples tomorrow, already written.

Who pays, and do you want to keep this person with a different format or actually stop?

### User
Parent pays. I think I want a different format first. If that fails I want to stop. My parent likes this tutor because they send a summary email after. The summaries sound like I am progressing. I am not.

### Assistant
The summary email is a product your parent can see. Your homework is a product they cannot. You need both of you to see the same picture. That might mean you tell the parent, before or after tomorrow: "The emails sound good. I still can't do X alone. I'm going to ask for a practice-heavy hour." You are not snitching on the tutor. You are correcting a brochure.

In the session, interrupt the method recap at minute five, not minute fifty. "I already saw this. Can I try number 4 while you watch, and only step in when I'm stuck?" If they take the pen back immediately, say, "Please let me stay stuck for a minute." Kind tutors often rescue. Rescue feels like teaching. It is not practice.

If they get hurt, that is uncomfortable. You can say, "You're easy to work with. The format isn't getting me to independent work." That is a review of a service.

If you do stop, a short message is enough: "I'm going to pause sessions. Thank you for the time." You do not need a three-page critique. If a parent has to send it, they can use your sentence.

I am not sure this person can switch styles. Eight lectures is a habit. One direct hour is the experiment.

### User
If I freeze tomorrow I will let them take the pen. That is also my exam problem.

### Assistant
Then put the pen on your side of the table at the start, physically. Start with a problem you freeze on, not a warm-up they choose. Tell them your goal in one line: "Success today is me doing one problem with you quiet." If they talk, you can hold up a hand. It feels rude. It is the point.

For the next ten minutes: pick two failed problems, write the one-line goal, text your parent the brochure-versus-reality sentence if that relationship can take it, and put a note on your laptop for minute five. Tomorrow you try the format. If it is another video recap, you pause the sessions that week, not after eight more yeahs.

You are allowed to need a tutor who watches you struggle. Kindness without that is a summary email. Ask for the struggle. Pay for that, or stop paying. Both are respectful. "Yeah" is not. Write the failed problem numbers on a sticky now so tomorrow you do not blank and hand the hour back.
