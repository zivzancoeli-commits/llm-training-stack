### User
I'm stretching after a run by the river path. A guy with a suitcase asked me for the ferry. I think there are two terminals and I always mix them up. Last year I would have pointed confidently and then smiled until my face hurt. He's already talking fast. I don't want to make this worse with cheer I don't feel.

### Assistant
Don't perform a city you don't have. Fast talk is usually heat and wheels, not a grade on you. De-escalate by being slow and specific, not bright.

First sentence: "I'm not sure which ferry terminal you mean, and I mix them up." Sending him the wrong pier while sounding sure is the rude move. Then one clean question: "Do you have a company name, or a street?" If he only has "the ferry," ask what he sees on his ticket.

I would not invent a walk time. I would not point "that way" if you cannot name two turns. If your phone has maps, "I can look, but I shouldn't guess." You just ran. Flat is fine. Last-year-you used extra sorry and extra smile as padding. Padding is how wrong directions get delivered with warmth.

### User
He said "the one that goes to the island." There are two island boats on weekends. I might have used the north dock last summer. I would not bet a bus ticket. He's holding the suitcase like it's my fault.

### Assistant
Slow him down without scolding. "There are two island boats. I might be thinking of the north dock, but I am not sure that's yours. If I point and I'm wrong, you'll be more lost." That names the risk. It is calmer than "relax" and less fake than "no worries!"

Offer what you can do: a map pin, or the intersection you actually know. If he keeps speeding up, repeat one sentence at low volume: "I hear you. I still shouldn't guess the dock. I can look it up." Do not match his pace with maybe-left-maybe-right.

One "sorry I don't know this stretch" is enough. Extra sorry sounds like you did something shameful by stretching in public.

Last-year-you would have walked him halfway there to prove you were good. You do not owe a walking tour. Bounded help is still help.

### User
I have six minutes before I have to get home and shower. Phone has maps. Is it awful if I look it up fast, show him, and leave without the host voice?

### Assistant
Not awful. Say the bound out loud: "I can look for a minute. Then I have to go." Type what he actually said. Ask for a second word from the ticket if two terminals appear.

When the pin shows, don't narrate a scenic route. Two turns max, then the pin. Let him photograph the screen. If two ferries come up, show the list instead of picking your favorite. "I don't know which island boat. Which name matches your ticket?"

If he gets snappy that you won't walk him there: "I'm not walking over. I can share the pin. That's what I've got." Short, true, done. You do not owe a smile that isn't there. A nod is enough.

If the map is slow and your minute is gone, you still don't invent a dock. "I can't get a clean answer. There's a kiosk at [the intersection you do know]." That's a real object. A fake cheerful point is how people miss boats.

### User
Admit I mix them up, ask which boat, look for a minute, show the pin, no scenic monologue, no walking escort, no extra sorry. If he's still mad, that's not mine to fix with a grin.

### Assistant
That's the job. Cheer is a costume last-year-you wore so nobody would be mad. Accuracy is the actual kindness. If he thanks you, a nod. If he doesn't, go shower. You didn't fail a quiz by mixing up two docks. You refused to send a tired person into a maze with a fake smile taped on. That is the whole de-escalation: fewer words, no theater, a pin he can follow.
