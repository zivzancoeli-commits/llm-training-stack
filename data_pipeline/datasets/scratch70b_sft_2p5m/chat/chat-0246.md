### User
Potluck at 6:30. It's 4:50. I said I'd bring dessert. The oven clicked twice and went dark. No heat, no error code I understand. There's a mixing bowl of batter on the counter and a pan I already greased. The host is my coworker Maya. Twelve people. I don't want to show up with a story instead of food, and I don't want to pretend this is fine.

### Assistant
The batter can wait in the fridge. Don't keep hoping the oven will have a personality change in the next hour. Click-dark with no code is a dead appliance for tonight's purposes. I won't diagnose the oven. I'm not an appliance tech.

You still have ninety-plus minutes. Dessert does not have to be a baked square. Fruit, ice cream, cookies from a store, a cold thing in a tub — all of those count as dessert at a potluck. Showing up with a true sentence and a store box is better than showing up late with raw batter and a speech.

Have you already told Maya the oven died, or is she still expecting a pan at 6:30?

### User
I haven't told her. I was going to bake the bars, let them cool, and cut them in the pan in the car like a person with a plan. There's a grocery on the way that closes at 6. My car keys are in the bowl with the mail. I feel dramatic about an oven.

### Assistant
Tell her now, in one text: "Oven died. I'll grab dessert on the way instead of bars. Still coming at 6:30." That's not drama. That's a menu change. If you wait until you're in her doorway, she spends 6:20 wondering where the sweet thing is.

For the next ten minutes: batter in the fridge, pan in the sink, keys out of the mail bowl, shoes on. At the grocery, pick something that doesn't need a knife demonstration. Cookies, a pint of ice cream if she has a freezer, grapes and chocolate. I won't invent prices. Buy what you can carry and what will survive a car.

Don't try to bake in a toaster oven you don't own. Don't light the stove and experiment with a skillet cake unless you already know how. Tonight is not a test kitchen.

If Maya replies "just bring yourself," you can still bring the store dessert. Empty-handed after you claimed dessert makes the table feel like it has a hole. A box fills the hole.

### User
What if people ask why I didn't bake? There's a person at work who comments on homemade versus store. Also the batter feels like a waste.

### Assistant
"Oven died at 5" is a complete answer. You don't need a homemade defense. The commentator can eat a cookie without a lecture from you. If they comment anyway, "store cookies because the oven quit" plus a shrug ends it. Don't apologize to the idea of from-scratch.

Batter in the fridge can become muffins another day if the oven lives, or it can go in the trash if it sits too long. I won't give you food-safety theater. If you don't know how long that batter keeps, throw it out tomorrow rather than serve a science project. Tonight, it is not the potluck.

Leave by 6:10 if the store is on the way and closes at 6. That's tight. Go now if you need the 6 o'clock door. Maya has the text. The greased pan can soak. You said dessert, not a specific bar recipe in a legal document. Bring a box, arrive at 6:30, put it on the table, take a plate of someone else's salad. If you cannot beat closing, tell Maya you'll swing by a corner store instead and still show up with something wrapped. The oven can be a weekend problem. The potluck is a Tuesday. Ninety minutes is enough to replace a pan. It is not enough to resurrect a dead coil. Move.
