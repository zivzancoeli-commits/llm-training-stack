### User
I knocked a iced coffee onto my laptop keyboard in the quiet floor of the library. It went between the keys. The screen flickered and stayed on. There are no paper towels in sight, just a trash can with napkins that look used. My charger is in my bag. People are staring. I need to not destroy this computer and also not make a bigger scene.

### Assistant
Power off now. Do not keep typing to "see if it still works." Every extra keypress can push liquid further. Hold the power button until it dies. Unplug the charger if it is connected. Do not attach the charger to "keep it alive."

Tip the laptop so the liquid can run toward the keys and out, not toward the screen hinge. Keyboard down, like you are pouring out a pan, over the least bad surface you have — a trash can, a tiled floor, even a notebook you are willing to sacrifice. Do not shake it.

Skip the used napkins. Used wet fiber on an open keyboard is a second mess. Your sleeve, a clean notebook page, or asking out loud for a paper towel is better.

I cannot tell you whether this machine will survive. Iced coffee is water plus sugar. Sugar stays behind after water dries. Survival is a later question.

Are you on a table with an outlet in the wall, and is the laptop still warm or already going dark?

### User
It's going dark now, I held the button. There's a puddle on the table. A librarian is walking over. The nearest bathroom is down a flight of stairs. I have a hoodie.

### Assistant
Good. Dark is what you wanted. Use the hoodie as a blotter, not a soak rag: press, lift, do not wipe in circles. Get the table puddle before it hits someone else's books.

When the librarian arrives, one sentence: "I spilled a drink on my laptop, I powered it off, do you have paper towels or a plastic bag?" You are not presenting a crime. Quiet floor or not, they would rather hand you towels than mop sugar out of carpet later.

Do not open the laptop wider to "air it." Do not pop keys off in a library unless you already know how that model comes apart; a key in a vent is a new problem. Do not put it on a radiator or under a hand dryer. Heat and wet boards are a bad mix.

If they have a bag, bag it keyboard-side down with the lid slightly open so it is not a sealed swamp, and get to the bathroom. In the bathroom, keep it inverted, blot again with real towels, and do not rinse it under the tap. Rinsing a laptop is not a kitchen trick that transfers. Wipe the table. Throw the cup away.

### User
Should I try to turn it on in an hour to see if files are okay? I have a paper due tomorrow that I hadn't saved to the cloud in two hours. I feel like throwing up.

### Assistant
Do not turn it on in an hour. An hour is not dry. Coffee under keys can sit for a day or more. Powering on a wet board is how you turn a maybe into a no. I am not a repair shop, and I will not promise a timeline. A common home move is: stay off, keep it inverted in a dry room, and get it to whoever you already trust with hardware — a campus repair desk, the maker's support, a shop — before you gamble on a boot.

The paper is the other problem. Two unsaved hours hurts. From your phone, email the professor or the portal a short note: "Laptop took a liquid spill, I'm getting to another computer, I'll send what I can." That is not an excuse essay. Then go to a library desktop or a friend's machine and reconstruct from memory, email drafts, and whatever auto-save the software actually had. Check the web version of the doc if you were in a browser. Do not wait on a miracle boot.

For the next ten minutes: keep it powered off and inverted, take the towels or bag from the librarian, blot, photograph the spill area for your own records if this is a work or school machine, wipe the table, and walk to a desktop to start recovering the paper. Message one person who can sit with you if you are shaky.

You knocked a drink. That is a Tuesday. The scene is already over once the towels appear. The machine might be fine, might not. The paper still has a path that does not require you to press power out of panic.
