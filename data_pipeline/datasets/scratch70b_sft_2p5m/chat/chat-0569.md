### User
Scholarship portal needs a form a counselor was supposed to upload. Counselor is in meetings until after the deadline. Deadline is in three hours. I have a PDF of the same form signed, sitting in my email. Portal only accepts counselor-side upload. Help desk is a ticket. I am trying not to cry in a library. I need a sequence. No fake promises that they'll extend.

### Assistant
Sequence, then feelings. Three hours is short and not zero.

Call the scholarship contact on the award page while you walk to a hallway. Email is slower. Say: counselor cannot upload until after the deadline, you have the signed PDF, what alternate intake exists. I will not promise an extension. You ask. If they have a late-file email, you use it in the first hour, not the last ten minutes.

At the same time, email the counselor, the counseling office generic address, and any assistant listed, PDF attached, deadline stamped in the subject. "Please upload or reply with who can." People come out of meetings. Some check phones.

Portal help ticket: submit anyway with screenshots of the upload wall and the clock. Ticket is a timestamp.

Do not create a fake counselor account. Do not guess a colleague's login. That's a different disaster.

Library crying: bathroom, water, then back. You can be wrecked and still send the packet.

Is the counselor the only human with the portal role, or is there a department uploader?

### User
There's a department secretary who has done uploads when counselors are out. I have their email. I don't have their phone. Counselor said this morning "I'll get to it" and then the calendar filled. I already look like the student who nags.

### Assistant
Nag. Three hours is nag o'clock.

Email the secretary now, same PDF, same subject line with the deadline hour. CC the counselor. "I know this is last minute. Portal requires staff upload. Form is attached. Deadline today [time]." Nagging is a story. A missed scholarship is a number.

If the school has a front desk, you walk there with the PDF on your phone and a polite panic. Secretaries sometimes upload from a counter faster than from a buried inbox. I will not guarantee they're there. Walking is still a step.

"I'll get to it" was a morning sentence. Afternoon is a new sentence from you. You are not cruel. You are the clock.

If someone tells you to relax, you can say "I will once it's uploaded." Then you stay on the task.

### User
If nobody uploads, do I send the PDF to the funder and hope.

### Assistant
If the funder told you a late email is acceptable, yes, send it before the minute dies, with the portal screenshot and the staff chain. Hope is not a plan. A complete packet in the right inbox is a plan. If they said portal-only and nothing else, you still send, labeled as backup, because a human sometimes opens it. I will not promise they must. You already knew that.

Three-hour stack: funder call, secretary email plus CC, ticket, walk to the desk, counselor ping again at the one-hour mark. No fake login. No self-blame novel in the library. You did your side this morning. Their calendar slipped. You are allowed to chase a form like it matters, because it does.

### User
Secretary uploaded with forty minutes left. Portal flipped to received. I am shaking in the stacks like it was a near miss, which it was. Counselor texted "you could have waited."

### Assistant
You couldn't. The deadline was the deadline. "I'll get to it" was not an upload.

You can reply to the counselor later, short: "Portal was due today. Secretary could upload. Thank you for the form itself." Or you can reply tomorrow. You don't need a fight in the last forty minutes of a clock that already flipped.

Shaking is leftover. Water, bathroom, then a screenshot of received in a folder with the emails. Near miss is data: next time you loop the secretary the morning of, not the last three hours. Nag o'clock can start earlier. Today you chased a form because it mattered. Library can stop being a panic room. Received is the word. Sit down until your hands remember it.
