### User
I was at a show. A person I don't know took a photo of the crowd that includes my face, showed me the screen, and asked to put it on their story. I froze and said "uh." They are waiting with a smile. I do not want my face on a stranger's account. I also don't want to make a scene in a dark room. The photo is already taken. I need a no that works in noise.

### Assistant
You can still say no after a photo exists. A shutter is not a consent form.

Lean in toward their ear so you don't have to shout a speech: "Please don't post my face." If you need two more words: "I'd rather not." Point at yourself on the screen if that is faster than language. Then you turn back to the stage. You don't have to smile to make it okay.

If they say "I'll blur you," you can accept blur only if you mean it. If you don't trust blur in a dark club, the answer stays no. "Please delete it or crop me out. Don't post it." You cannot force their phone. You can be clear. Clear is not a scene. A scene is a lecture about the internet over a guitar.

Don't invent a job that "can't be photographed" unless that's true. You don't need a professional excuse. Preference is enough.

I will not tell you the legal rule for crowd photos in your city. I don't know it. I will not promise a platform will take it down. I will tell you to say the no now, while they still have the smile and the draft.

Are they holding the phone in your space, or did they already walk back to their friends?

### User
Still in my space, waiting. A friend of theirs laughed and said "it's just a story, it vanishes." I know stories get screenshotted. My "uh" is hanging in the air like I might still say yes.

### Assistant
Close the "uh." "No — please don't post me." The dash is you interrupting your own freeze. You don't debate vanishing. You can say, if you want one clause, "Stories get saved. I don't want that." Then you stop. Their friend is not a court.

If they look hurt, let them look hurt. Your face is not a prop to keep a stranger's night cute.

If they post anyway, you don't snatch the phone. You don't start a fight in a pit. You note the account if you can see it, and you leave the moment. Later you can ask them to take it down, or use whatever reporting the app actually offers. I won't promise results. Tonight's tool is the no while the draft still exists.

If you have a friend with you, you can look at them so they help you extract: a tug toward the bar is allowed. You don't owe the photographer a pose.

"Just a story" is sales. Treat it as sales. You already know about screenshots. Trust that knowledge more than their laugh.

### User
What if they post it while I'm standing there and show me the upload bar?

### Assistant
You repeat, louder if you must: "Take it down. I said no." If they don't, you walk. You don't watch the bar like it's a fuse you can blow out with manners. Later, if the story is up, you message the no again if you have the account. You don't spend the rest of the show litigating.

Right now they are still waiting. Use the two seconds. No, don't post my face. Turn back to the music. You can like a show and still refuse to be content. That's not a scene. That's a sentence in a noisy room. If your "uh" is still hanging, cut it with the no before the smile turns into an upload. Two seconds is enough. You don't need a better personality. You need the sentence. Say it. Then watch the band, not their screen.
