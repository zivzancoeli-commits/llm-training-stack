### User
My kid is twelve. They asked me last night not to do the spring team. Same coach as last year. The coach already emailed a parent meeting. My kid is not injured. They are tired of weeknights and of being yelled at from the sideline. I agree with the rest. I am scared of the conversation. I don't want to diagnose my kid. I don't want a speech about grit. I need to tell the coach we are sitting out, and I have never been the parent who quits a season before it starts.

### Assistant
Sitting out is a family decision. You can deliver it without putting your child on trial.

Email or a short call before the parent meeting is cleaner than a public no in a gym. "We're not doing the spring season. Thanks for last year." If you want one reason, pick a true, small one: "They need a lighter spring." You do not owe a catalogue of sideline yelling. You also do not have to pretend it was all joy.

I will not invent league refund rules or whether a roster spot can transfer. If money already moved, you ask the league or the coach about their process. I will not guess the answer.

Do not send your kid to tell the coach alone. Twelve is old enough to have a preference. It is not their job to manage an adult's disappointment.

Has the kid asked you to keep the yelling part private, or are they fine with you naming it?

### User
They said "don't make it a thing." They still have friends on the team. They don't want a speech at school. The coach texts parents a lot. I think if I am vague the coach will try to recruit them back in the thread.

### Assistant
Vague invites a campaign. Clear and short reduces replies.

Use: "We're sitting this season out. Please don't loop them into reminders. I'll tell the parent group they aren't on the roster." Then you mute or leave the thread if you can. If the coach replies with grit, you do not debate psychology. "We've decided. Thanks." One repeat is enough.

At school, your kid can say "I'm not doing it this spring" without a press conference. You can agree on that sentence so they are not ambushed by a teammate. Friends can still sit together at lunch. A roster is not a friendship license.

If you want to mention the sideline later, that is a separate note after the no is complete. Mixing them now turns a schedule decision into a complaint file, which your kid asked you not to make a thing. Honor that unless you believe safety is involved. Yelling from a sideline can be miserable without being a report. I will not sort that legally. You know your kid.

Do not over-explain to other parents in the parking lot. Other parents recruit too.

### User
What if the coach says this will hurt their development and I fold because I also worry they will regret it in two years?

### Assistant
Two-year regret is a guess. Twelve-year exhaustion is a report from the person who has to do the weeknights. You can revisit next season. That is what seasons are for.

If the coach says development, you can say, "We hear you. We're still sitting this one out." You do not have to prove a training plan. Rest is a plan. Another activity is a plan. Nothing organized is also a plan.

If you fold in the moment, you can still send the email after: "I thought about it. We're out for spring." Folding in a phone call is not a contract.

Tell your kid what you sent so they are not surprised by a coach greeting. Then let the topic leave the dinner table for a week. You are not the first parent to decline a season. You are just the first time at this kitchen table. Send the short note. Skip the meeting. Let the kid have their spring.
