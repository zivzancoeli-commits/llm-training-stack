### User
I'm at Priya's place. She made a "texture experiment" with lentils, coconut, and something she boiled until it looked like wet cardboard. She's standing there with two spoons. She said "be honest" which in this kitchen means "love it." I have a thing with surprise textures. Last time I faked a smile through a foam dessert and I thought about it for a week. I don't want to insult her. I also don't want that spoon. This is the first time I'm trying to actually decline instead of swallowing. What do I say while she's holding the spoons?

### Assistant
You decline the bite, not the person. Do it before the spoon is in the air toward your face.

"I'm going to skip tasting this one. Texture stuff is hard for me and I don't want to fake it." That's the whole speech. You can add "It smells like you worked" if that's true and you can say it without a grimace. You don't have to invent an allergy you don't have. You don't have to invent a stomach bug. Last year's you used the bug. This year's you can have a boundary that isn't a medical drama.

"Be honest" is not a contract to perform disgust or rapture. Honesty can be "I'm not the tester for this." If she pushes — "just a tiny bit" — you repeat once: "No thanks. I'm not tasting it." Tiny-bit is how foam dessert happens.

I won't diagnose you. Texture aversion is common enough to name; it isn't a condition I get to label. I also won't tell you the food is unsafe. I can't see it. If you have an actual allergy, say the allergen. If you don't, don't borrow one.

Is she a close friend who cooks at you often, or is this a one-off dinner?

### User
Close friend. She cooks when she's anxious. This is the third "experiment" this month. I ate the first two. She already looks like she might cry if I say the word no. There's other food — rice and a cucumber salad I can eat. I just feel like the experiment is the point of the evening.

### Assistant
Then the evening can still happen. Eat the rice and the cucumbers. The experiment is her project. It does not have to enter your mouth to count as a visit.

If she looks close to tears, you still don't swallow to manage her face. You can be kind and specific: "I like being here. I'm not doing surprise textures. I'll eat the rice. You can tell me what you were going for without me being the guinea pig." If she cries, that's uncomfortable. Uncomfortable is cheaper than another week of thinking about a spoon.

Don't rate the wet cardboard. "It's interesting" is a lie with a hat on. Don't trash it either. "I'm not tasting it" leaves the dish in her hands, which is where it belongs.

If she says you don't support her, translate that. Support is not a digestive stunt. You can say, "I support you cooking. I'm not a texture tester." If she wants feedback, she can ask a person who likes that game. You are allowed to be the friend who sits at the table and talks about her week instead.

The third experiment this month is useful information. You can set the pattern out loud: "I should have said this after the first one. I'm going to start declining the experimental bites. Regular food is fine." That's a future rule, not a trial of her character.

### User
She's saying I used to be more fun. I can feel the old grin starting. There's a second spoon for a sauce she hasn't described. How do I not fold in the next thirty seconds?

### Assistant
Look at the rice. Not at the spoons. The grin is a muscle. You don't have to fire it.

"I can be here and not eat that. Those aren't opposites." Then you put something you will eat on your plate. Movement helps more than a second paragraph. If the second spoon comes at you, hand doesn't have to rise. "That one too. No thanks."

If she keeps going, you don't prosecute her anxiety. You also don't become the audience. "I'm going to eat this salad and we can talk. I'm done with the tasting." Then you ask a real question about her day so the night has a track that isn't a spoon.

You used to disappear. That's not a compliment to earn back. Fun can be staying without a texture hangover.

Next thirty seconds: no, rice, sit, question. Leave the experiment in the pot. You already noticed the spoon before it became a shower story.
