### User
My aunt gave me a sweater that does not fit and also is not my taste. The tags are still on. I lost the gift receipt, or she never put one in. I want to exchange it for something I will wear, or store credit, I don't even know. I feel ungrateful and also I know this thing will sit in a drawer. I'm in the parking lot of the store whose bag it came in.

### Assistant
Ungrateful is a feeling. A drawer sweater is a fact. You can return a gift without sending your aunt a report.

Before you go in, look at the sweater: brand, size, any SKU sticker on the tag, and whether it looks worn. Phone photos of the tags help if the line gets picky. Check your email and payment apps in case someone forwarded a receipt. Check the original bag for a tiny slip.

I will not tell you this chain's policy, whether they need ID, or how many days you have. Those are store rules, not a universal law. The person at the desk knows their script. Your job is to be calm and specific, not to recite a customer bill of rights you saw online.

Is your aunt likely to ask whether you wore it, or is this a "I never want to hear about it" aunt? That changes whether you exchange quietly or also need a cover story.

### User
She will ask at the next dinner. She notices clothes. I was going to say I exchanged for a different size. That's close to true if they let me. There's a line of people with receipts in their hands and I have a bag and a feeling.

### Assistant
A different size is a decent cover if you actually walk out with something from this store. If you only get store credit, pick a boring truth for later: "I swapped it for something that fits." You do not need to name the item. You do not need to announce that you rejected her taste.

In line, put your phone away and have the sweater folded with tags visible. When it is your turn: "This was a gift, I don't have the receipt, can I exchange it or get store credit?" Then stop. Extra stories about drawers and aunts make you sound like you are selling them a reason. They have a button to press or they do not.

If they ask who paid, say you do not know. If they offer to look up by card, you probably cannot, because it was not your card. That is normal. If they offer a lower merchandise credit than the tag, you can ask them to explain how they arrived at the number. Asking how is not a fight. I will not guess prices.

If the answer is no, you have choices: keep it, donate it, or try the brand's own site later with the SKU. Do not argue about "but it's tagged" for ten minutes. The next person in line is not your audience.

If the answer is yes, do the exchange in one visit. Wandering the store for an hour hunting a personality transplant is how you spend the credit on another mistake. Pick a fitting thing in a similar season.

### User
What if the clerk is rude. I get small when people are rude, and then I just say never mind.

### Assistant
Rude is their problem until you abandon your own errand. You can repeat the ask once, slower: "I don't have a receipt. What options do you have for a tagged gift?" If they say none, "okay, thanks" and you leave. That is not you losing. That is a closed door.

If they are rude and also offering a yes, take the yes. You do not need them to like you.

For the next ten minutes: photograph the tags, search your email for the store name, walk in with the sweater out of the bag, say the one sentence, and accept the first fair option they can actually do. If you get credit, spend it on something you would have bought anyway, not a consolation prize.

If dinner comes and she asks, "I got it in a size that works" is enough. You are not required to wear a sweater to prove love. You showed up in the parking lot. Go inside and keep it boring.
