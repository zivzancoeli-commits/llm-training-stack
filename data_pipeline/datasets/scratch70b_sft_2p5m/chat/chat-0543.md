### User
Friend broke up last month. Now they want to drop a box at my place: the ex's hoodie, a game console, some cables, a winter coat. "Just until they pick it up." Ex lives across town. Friend doesn't want to see them. I have a studio. I didn't date either of them. I don't want to be a storage unit or a diplomat. They'll be here in an hour unless I say no.

### Assistant
Say no before the hour. Boxes get heavier once they're on your floor.

"I can't store their stuff. You'll have to find another way." Not cruel. Not a maybe. Studio is a complete reason. You don't need a better one.

You are not the drop point, the scheduler, or the referee. If they want to avoid the ex, they can use a lobby, a mutual friend who offered, or a ship-to. Those are their logistics. Your apartment is not public infrastructure.

I will not invent a law about returning property. People do need to get their things back. That still doesn't appoint you.

If you've already said "maybe" in a weak moment, correct it now: "I thought about it. I can't take the box."

Have they already told the ex that your address is the pickup?

### User
I don't think so. They said they'd "figure out pickup after it's at yours." Which means I would be texting a stranger about a console. That's the part that made my stomach drop. I like this friend. I don't want to punish them for a breakup.

### Assistant
You're not punishing them. You're declining a job.

The after-it's-at-yours plan is how you inherit the ex. Don't.

If you want to be kind without storage: "I can sit with you while you text them a public pickup, or help you pack the box at your place. I can't host it." Sitting-with is friendship. Hosting a console is a third roommate.

Public pickup ideas they can use without you: building lobby with a camera, a workplace front desk if the ex agrees, a shipping drop-off. I will not pick a service or a price. They can choose. Your help is moral support and a no to the key.

If they cry, you can still not take the box. Crying and a hoodie on your chair is how studios become storage.

### User
They just texted "on my way, it's one box." I haven't answered.

### Assistant
Answer now. "Don't come. I can't take the box. Turn around." If they're already in a car, the earlier you send it the less they treat arrival as consent.

If they show up anyway, you don't have to buzz them in with a box. Talk in the hallway if you talk at all. Hallway is not a loading dock. Repeat the no. Then you can offer the sit-with-a-text plan for later this week, not tonight with a box at your shoes.

One box is still the ex's winter coat in your life. Like the friend. Don't take the inventory. Text before the buzzer.

### User
I texted don't come. They're in the lobby anyway with the box. Buzzer just rang. I can see the hoodie through the glass. If I don't buzz, I look cruel. If I do, the box is in my life.

### Assistant
Don't buzz them in with the box. You can go down to the lobby if you want to be a body in the conversation. Lobby is not your studio. "I said I can't take it. I didn't change my mind." If they try to leave it at your feet, you don't pick it up. Picking it up is a yes.

If you don't want the lobby scene, don't go down. Unanswered buzzer after a clear text is not cruelty. It's you keeping a console out of a studio. They can take the box back to a car, a locker, a shipping place. Those still exist at whatever hour this is.

Like the friend. Don't take the inventory. If they cry in the lobby, you can stay for five minutes of human and still not take the coat. Five minutes is friendship. A winter coat on your chair until the ex texts is a second breakup you didn't sign. Leave the box with the person who owns the breakup.
