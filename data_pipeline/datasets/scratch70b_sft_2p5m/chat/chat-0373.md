### User
I have a numbered space. The neighbor in 2G parks a wide pickup so the wheel is over my line. Tonight half my space is truck. I had to park on the street, which I don't have a permit for. I don't know if I should knock, leave a note, or call whoever runs the lot. I've never spoken to 2G except a nod. I need my spot tomorrow morning. It's 8:50 p.m.

### Assistant
Note on the windshield plus a knock if lights are on in 2G. Call the lot only if you know they actually tow or ticket overnight. A call into a void wastes the hour.

Note: "You're over the line into [your number]. Please straighten so I can use my space. — [your unit]." Time, date. No essay about the street. No threat you cannot keep.

Knock: same content, shorter. If nobody answers, the note is the message.

I will not invent towing rules or permit fines. Street parking without a permit is a risk you already know. Don't add a fake legal speech to the note.

If you can still squeeze in without hitting the truck, that is a last resort and a bad angle. Do not. A scrape is worse than a night on the street if the street is actually survivable. If the street is a tow zone, you try harder on 2G and the lot number now.

Do you have the lot's after-hours number on the gate, or only an office that opens at 9?

### User
Gate has a number. I called once; it rang. Pickup has a bumper magnet for a softball team. Lights are on in 2G. I am sitting in my car on the street, flashers off, feeling like a spy.

### Assistant
Go up and knock. You are not a spy. You are a person whose space has a truck in it.

If they answer, be boring: "That's my numbered space. I can't fit. Can you pull in straight?" Wait while they do it if they offer. People mean "later" and then sleep.

If they don't answer, note on the glass, then try the gate number again. Leave a voicemail with space numbers and your callback. Then decide the street: if you stay, set an alarm to move at a legal hour. If you know it is a tow-risk, you may need to sit in a legal place even if it is farther. I will not name your curb rules.

Do not block them in as revenge. Do not let air out of tires. Fantasy, not a plan.

Tomorrow morning, if the truck is still eating the line, photos of the wheels over the paint, then the office. Pattern plus pictures is what lot managers understand. Tonight is a human and a note.

### User
They opened the door. A kid answered. Adult yelled from inside "I'll move it in a bit." The kid looked scared that I was there.

### Assistant
Do not use the kid as the messenger beyond what already happened.

"Please tell them I need it tonight — I'll wait five minutes in the lot." Then wait in view of the truck, not in their doorway. If it moves, you park, you nod, you go upstairs. If five minutes become twenty, knock once more on the adult, not through the child: "I still can't park." If they won't come, note plus gate voicemail, and you stop recruiting a kid.

"In a bit" is how numbered spaces disappear. You can be polite to the child — "thanks, I'll wait by the truck" — and still need the adult's keys.

You did the right first move. Finish it by the paint, not by a long talk in their hall. Get your number back. Then tomorrow, if this is a habit, the office gets the photos. Tonight: five minutes, then the next step you already have. The softball magnet can stay. The truck needs to live in one rectangle. Yours is the other rectangle. That is the whole lot.
