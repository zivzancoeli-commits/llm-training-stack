### User
My friend and their new partner booked a beach week and then invited me as "the fun extra." They already have the house. They want me in a small third bedroom that is basically a closet. I like my friend. I have met the partner twice. Both times I felt like a podcast they were performing couplehood for. I don't want a vacation where I'm furniture. They keep saying it will be cheaper split three ways. I haven't said yes. They need a headcount tomorrow.

### Assistant
A third bedroom is still a trip with a couple. If you already feel like an audience, a week of that will not magically become your holiday. Cheaper is not the same as good.

You can decline the trip without diagnosing their relationship. "I'm going to skip this one. You two should take the house. We can hang when you're back." That is a complete RSVP. You don't need to say "third wheel" out loud unless you want to. If you do, keep it dry: "I think I'd feel like a tagalong, and I don't want that for a vacation."

Don't negotiate yourself into "just the long weekend" unless you actually want a slice. Slices of a couple-trip are still couple-trips.

I will not guess what the house costs or whether splitting three ways is fair. I don't know the booking. If money is the only reason you're tempted, name that to yourself. A cheaper miserable week is still a week.

Have they already put your name on a reservation, or is this still an invite?

### User
Still an invite. There's a group chat with just the three of us and a lot of sunset emojis. The partner sent a packing list that includes "couple sunrise" and then "optional hang for [me]." So they already wrote me in as optional. That should make saying no easier, and somehow it made me mad.

### Assistant
Mad is useful data. They sketched a vacation where you are a maybe in your own slot. You don't have to punish them for the packing list. You also don't have to occupy the closet to prove you're easy.

Reply in the three-person chat so both of them hear the same no. "I'm out for the beach week. Have a good time. Don't hold the small room." Then mute the packing thread. Sunset emojis will keep happening. You don't need to perform enthusiasm for a trip you refused.

If your friend DMs "did I do something," you can be specific without a trial: "I don't want to third-wheel a couple vacation. That's about the format, not a veto of your partner." If it is partly the partner, you still don't owe a character essay. Format is enough and it is true.

Optional hang on a packing list is them being honest by accident. Believe the list. Optional is not a compliment. It is a role.

If they pivot to "come for two days," use the same no unless two days is a trip you independently want. Don't accept a consolation prize to soothe your anger. Anger wants a clean exit, not a shorter closet.

### User
I keep thinking I'll lose the friendship if I don't go and be easy.

### Assistant
Easy is how you got the closet invite. Friendships that only work when you are furniture are already thin. You can still text them about ordinary life this month so the no isn't a disappearance. Showing up as a person at home is a better test than swallowing a week.

Tomorrow they need a headcount. Give them the real number: you are not coming. Let them have the sunrise. You keep your week and your self-respect, which travel worse in a third bedroom than a suitcase does. Send it in the three-person chat so the partner hears it too. Then mute the packing list. Easy can wait for a dinner you actually want.
