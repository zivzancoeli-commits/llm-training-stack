### User
Deck is due at 9 a.m. It's 10:20 p.m. I left a clean 12-slide version in the shared folder at 6. Teammate Devon uploaded "final_final_v7" at 9:50 with clip art, a new thesis on slide 1 that contradicts our data, and my name still on the footer. We present in a 15-minute slot. I have not slept. If I revert, Devon will say I can't take feedback. If I don't, we look like we can't read our own chart.

### Assistant
Revert the thesis. Feedback that contradicts the chart is not feedback. It's a new paper at 9:50 p.m. Your name on the footer means you get to refuse to present nonsense.

Don't start a voice note war. Put the 6 p.m. file back as "presenting_copy" and message the group: "We're using the 6 p.m. deck. v7 changed the claim in a way the data doesn't support. Devon, we can talk after, not at 10:20." Then you sleep enough to speak at 9.

I'm not your professor. I won't invent the grading rubric. I will say a 15-minute slot cannot hold two theses.

Did anyone else in the group like v7, or is this Devon solo?

### User
The third person, Sam, reacted with a thumbs-up emoji at 10:01 and went offline. I think the thumbs-up was for "thanks for making it pretty." Our chart is on slide 6. Devon's new slide 1 says the opposite of slide 6 in bigger type. I can fix slide 1 in fifteen minutes. I cannot rebuild Devon's feelings.

### Assistant
Fix slide 1. Leave the clip art if you must. The contradiction is the fire. Pretty is optional. Feelings are for after the slot.

For the next ten minutes: open presenting_copy, make slide 1 match slide 6 in one sentence a sleepy room can hear. Put your name, Devon's, and Sam's on the title if that's the deal. Export a PDF so v7 cannot silently overwrite at 8 a.m. Upload PDF plus the ppt. In the chat: "PDF is what we present. Slide 1 now matches the chart." Then close the laptop.

If Devon is online, they can comment. You don't wait. 10:20 is already late. A thumbs-up from Sam is not a vote to invert data. You can say that without calling Sam a coward.

Don't delete v7 from the folder. Leave it. Paper trail. If the professor asks who changed the claim, the timestamp exists. You're not running a trial. You're preventing a 9 a.m. self-own.

Sleep. Set an alarm that is not 8:58.

### User
Devon just typed "wow okay." That's it. I have to sit next to them at 9. I don't want a silent war at the table.

### Assistant
"Wow okay" is a sulk in two words. You don't have to answer it tonight. If you answer, keep it dull: "We needed the claim to match the chart. See you at 9." Then mute.

At the table, you can be civil and still present the PDF. Civility is passing the clicker, not adopting a false thesis. If Devon tries to open v7 on the classroom computer, you say, out loud, to the room if needed: "We're on the PDF in the folder." That's logistics. It will feel sharp. It is still correct.

After the slot, if they want to talk pretty slides, they can. If they want to talk credit, the 6 p.m. file and the 9:50 file are both there. I can't script the grade.

Silent war is uncomfortable. Presenting a lie with your name on it is worse. You already did the adult thing: matched the data, exported a format that doesn't drift, told the group. Eat a thing, drink water, go to bed. 9 a.m. is a short room. You only need 15 minutes of a deck that isn't at war with itself. Let "wow okay" sit. You don't have to make it feel good at 10:30. You have to be able to point at slide 6 without sliding into a hole. That's the job. Do the PDF. Sleep.
