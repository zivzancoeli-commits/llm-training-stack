### User
I joined a Zoom at 2. Three minutes in, a banner popped up: "this meeting is being recorded." Linh said "oh yeah, for people who couldn't come." They never put it on the invite. I don't want to be on a file I didn't agree to. I also don't want to make a scene in front of a vendor. I'm on camera. The vendor is talking. Chat is open. What do I do in the next sixty seconds that isn't a career tantrum?

### Assistant
You can leave the recording without tanking the vendor meeting.

If your company uses a button to stop your video and mute, do that while you decide. Chat, private to Linh if you can: "I didn't know this was recorded. Please pause the recording or I'll drop and you can catch me up unrecorded." If private chat isn't possible, a short verbal: "I need a second — I didn't consent to recording. Can we pause it?" Then you stop talking content until the banner is gone or you're gone.

I will not invent your state's recording law or your company's policy. I won't tell you this is illegal. You can still not want to be in the file. That's enough.

If Linh says it's "just internal," you can still say no. Internal files travel.

Don't keep presenting while you privately seethe. That's how you end up in the file anyway.

Is this a vendor who might care, or only your team plus one guest?

### User
Vendor plus us. Linh is sharing a deck I helped with. If I drop, it looks like I sabotaged. If I stay, I'm in the MP4. I turned my camera off. Mic is still live because I might have to answer a number.

### Assistant
Mute until you have to speak. If they ask you a number, you can say, "I'll send that in email after — I need the recording off to stay on this call." That's a little awkward and it's clean. If you must answer live, you can say the number and then, "Please pause recording; I didn't agree." You're not a tantrum. You're a person with a banner.

If Linh refuses, you drop. Email the vendor the number. That's not sabotage. That's you not being in a file. Catch Linh after: "Invite has to say recorded. I won't join surprise recordings." If they need the MP4 for absentees, they can record a version without you, or they can take notes. Absentees are not a reason to trap a person who showed up.

Don't stay "just this once" as a personality. That's how banners multiply.

### User
They paused it. Vendor looked confused. I feel like I made it weird. Do I apologize to the vendor?

### Assistant
A short, true line is enough: "Sorry for the interruption — I needed to check the recording setting." Then you go back to the deck. You don't tell the vendor your feelings about Linh. You don't over-apologize. Weird is a paused banner, not a crime.

After the call: the invite rule, in writing to Linh. Next sixty seconds of the meeting: numbers, agenda, done. You already did the hard part. Camera can stay off if you want. Mic when needed. You don't owe the MP4 your face. Get through the vendor, then the Slack. That's a completed surprise recording, with a pause instead of a tantrum.

If Linh jokes later that you're "sensitive about recordings," you can stay dry: "I need it on the invite. I'll join when it's labeled." That's a process, not a tantrum. If they start the next one with a banner and no warning, you leave again. One pause taught the room more than a paragraph in Slack. You can still send the number by email. The vendor can live. The file can exist without your face in it. That's a complete professional no.
