### User
The smoke alarm in the unit next door has been going for twenty minutes. I can hear it through the wall. I am the new person in this building — intern energy, first month — and I do not know if I knock, call the super, or pretend I am worldly enough to know what "the protocol" is. It might be burnt toast. It might not. I do not want to be the neighbor who panics and I do not want to be the neighbor who documents a disaster from the couch. Help, but do not treat me like I already know the building.

### Assistant
Before I tell you to knock or call, I need one thing that changes the advice.

Do you smell smoke in your own unit, see haze in the hallway, or is it only the sound through the wall with otherwise normal air?

### User
Only the sound. Hallway looks normal when I cracked the door. No smoke smell in my place. The beeping is the loud, single-note kind, not a chirp every minute. It started around 9. I have not seen the neighbor come or go. I feel stupid standing here in socks.

### Assistant
Sound without smell is often a battery, steam, or a toast situation you cannot see. It can also be something you cannot diagnose through drywall. I will not pretend I know which. Interns get in trouble when they pick a story and commit.

First move: knock. Not a kick. Knock, wait, knock again. If someone answers, "Your alarm is going — are you okay?" is the whole script. Then you go back in unless they ask you to help. You are not the fire department.

If nobody answers, you do not pick the lock in your head. You call the super, the after-hours number on the lease, or the emergency line posted in the lobby — whichever this building actually gave you. I do not know your posting. Look at the paper on the fridge or the email from when you signed. Say: "Alarm next door has been going about twenty minutes, I knocked, no answer, I don't smell smoke in the hall." Give the unit number. Then you follow what they tell you.

If at any point you smell smoke, see haze, or hear people yelling, you leave your unit with keys and phone and you treat it as get-out-and-call, not a puzzle. I am not going to list medical symptoms or invent a fire code. I am saying: sound only, knock then building contact. Smoke you can detect, you leave.

Standing in socks is fine. Put on shoes before you go into the hall for real, because if you do need to leave the building you will want them.

### User
There is no super answering. The after-hours voicemail is a mailbox that is full. The alarm is still going. I feel like I should call 911 and I also feel like I will be the intern who called 911 on a smoke detector with a dying battery. What would you do if you were new and you did not want to bluff?

### Assistant
I would not bluff a "protocol." I would call the non-emergency number for the fire department or the city's 911 and say exactly what I know, including what I do not know.

"I'm in [address], unit [yours]. The smoke alarm in the unit next door has been sounding for about half an hour. I knocked. No answer. I do not smell smoke in the hallway. Building after-hours is not answering. I don't know if anyone is home." Then you let them decide whether to send someone. That is their job. Yours is the honest report.

If you are embarrassed, you can live with embarrassed. A dispatcher would rather sort a detector than miss a person who cannot get to the door. I cannot promise what they will send. I will not invent a response time.

While you wait, keep your door closed, keep shoes on, keep the phone with you. Do not go hunt the alarm through the neighbor's window. Do not disable anything that is not yours.

If the beeping stops and nothing else happens, you can still leave a note on their door in the morning: "Alarm was going last night, I knocked, wanted to make sure you were okay." That is neighbor, not hero.

You asked like a new intern. The clarifying question was smell versus sound. You had sound only. Knock, building contact, then an honest call that includes "I don't know." That is not panic and it is not a couch documentary. It is the line you can stand on without pretending you know the building better than you do.
