### User
My bedroom is above a platform. Fine most nights. Tonight the PA is stuck in a loop: "Stand clear of the closing doors" every forty seconds, then a chime, then the same voice. It's 12:40. I have a 6:50 alarm. I don't know if I should go downstairs in socks and yell at a speaker. I just want the loop off, or a way to sleep through it without inventing a transit bylaw.

### Assistant
Do not yell at a speaker in socks. Speakers do not take feedback. People in a booth might.

I will not invent the agency's night rules. I will not invent a quiet-hour statute. You can still treat a stuck loop as a malfunction, because a normal announcement does not fire every forty seconds until dawn.

Tonight's stack: earplugs or a fan you already own, phone on do-not-disturb except the alarm, then one call or text to the posted station number if you can find it on the platform sign from your window or on the agency site. "PA loop at [station], repeating stand-clear, since [time], bedroom facing platform." Short. You are reporting a stuck tape, not starting a campaign.

If there is an agent booth still lit, you can go down in real shoes and say the same sentence through the window. Then you go back up. Do not tour the tracks.

Is the booth light on, or is it just the speaker and the empty platform?

### User
Booth looks dark. There's a help point intercom on the platform. I can see the blue light from bed. I don't love going down there alone at 12:40. The loop is still going. I already put a pillow over my head like a child.

### Assistant
Pillow is a start. Keep it. You do not have to go to the help point alone at 12:40 if that feels wrong. A dark booth plus an empty platform is a reason to stay upstairs and use the phone. The agency app or the number on a ticket, a screenshot, a previous commute email — any of those beats a solo intercom at midnight. If you cannot find a number, use the app's "report a problem" if it exists. If it does not, write the note now and send at 6 when a human might read it. The loop may still run. You still have a timestamp.

If you do go down, shoes, phone, stay on the platform side of the yellow line, press the help point, say station, loop, time, then leave. No extra wandering. No arguing with the recording.

Sleep math: you will not get a perfect night. You can get a worse night by standing in the cold waiting for a voice that might be remote. Set the 6:50 extra loud in case the earplugs stay in. Water. Accept a grouchy morning. That is cheaper than a 1 a.m. debate with a speaker.

### User
If I complain tomorrow they'll say "trains are trains." I don't want to sound like I moved next to a station and just noticed.

### Assistant
You moved next to trains. You did not move next to a broken forty-second loop. Those are different sentences. Tomorrow: "The PA was stuck on a loop after midnight, every forty seconds. That is not a passing train. Please check the system at [station]." Keep the distinction. If they still say trains are trains, you have asked once. I will not promise a technician. I will promise that looping stand-clear is a repair request, not a personality.

Tonight: stay up unless you already feel safe at the help point. Fan, plugs, pillow, alarm louder, note with times. Do not yell at the box. The voice will keep saying stand clear. You can lie down anyway. Morning you send the malfunction, not a memoir about urban living. That is the whole play. Loop off if they can. Sleep through if they cannot. Either way you do not owe the speaker an argument.
