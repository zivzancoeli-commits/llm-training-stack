### User
I moved out last month. The deposit accounting arrived with a charge for a floor scratch in the bedroom. I have a move-in video dated the first day, walking that exact board, with the scratch already there. I sent the video. They replied that the video is "unclear" and the charge stands. I am trying to write a response that is calm and not a novel. I don't want you to invent the law of deposits. I want the next email and whether I should pick up the phone.

### Assistant
You have dated video of the same board. That is a fact packet, not a vibe.

Email is better than a first phone call because it keeps the clip and the timestamps in one place. Phone can follow if they ignore.

Keep the email short. Order: the charge line, the video filename or link, the timestamp in the video, a still screenshot if you can export one, the sentence "This scratch is visible at move-in in the attached video at 0:47. Please remove this charge." Ask them to confirm in writing. Do not narrate your character. Do not insult their inspector.

If the video is truly dark, export a still, brighten it on your side only enough to see the board, and say it is a still from the same file. Do not edit the original.

I will not tell you they must return money by a certain day. Deadlines vary. I will tell you to put a date on your request for a reply, as a courtesy calendar: "Please confirm by next Friday."

Do you have a move-in checklist they signed, or only the video?

### User
Only the video and the original listing photos, which are too wide to prove a scratch. No signed checklist. They like phone calls. Last time I called about a leak they talked in circles. I can send a still. I sound angry when I type.

### Assistant
Then type when you are not angry, or write it in notes first and paste. Angry adjectives are optional. The timestamp is not.

Skip the listing photos if they do not show the board. Weak evidence invites "see, nothing." Lead with the video and the still.

If they want a call, you can take it after the email is in, so you both have the attachment. On the call: "I'm following up on the email with the move-in video at 0:47. I need that line removed." If they circle, repeat the timestamp. Do not follow them into a tour of the rest of the unit. One charge, one clip.

If they say the video is unclear, ask what would make it clear. If they cannot say, note that in a follow-up email: "On the phone you said the video was unclear. Here is a still at 0:47. Please identify what is missing." You are building a file, not winning an Oscar.

If they never move, your next step is whatever local process people use for deposits where you live. I will not name a court or a form. I will say: keep the original file, the emails, and the accounting sheet together.

### User
What if they add more charges once I push on this one? And should I accept a "compromise" that still takes some of the scratch money?

### Assistant
I will not predict retaliation, and I will not invent whether extra charges are legal. If new lines appear, you answer those with the same method: evidence or a request to explain. You do not have to accept a grab bag.

A compromise is a choice, not a moral requirement. If the amount is small to you and you want the thread dead, you can accept. If the principle and the video are the point, you can decline and keep asking for a full removal of that line. I will not pick a dollar. Look at the number on the sheet and decide if it is worth more of your month.

Send the short email with the still tonight. Do not add a paragraph about how you were a good tenant. Good tenant is not the exhibit. The board at 0:47 is the exhibit. Attach. Timestamp. Ask for the line to come off. Then wait for their reply date. That is the next move.
