### User
My aunt and uncle just pulled into the driveway. They live four hours away. Nobody told me they were coming. My parents are at a work thing until tonight and I am twenty-six, in the house I grew up in for the weekend, in sweatpants, with a sink full of dishes and about a third of a loaf of bread. They are already waving. I do not know if this is a two-hour stop or an overnight. I do not know if I am supposed to feed them. I was about to tell them "yeah of course stay, we have plenty" because that is the family script, and I do not actually know if we have plenty of anything. Help me not fake a host I am not.

### Assistant
Do not say you have plenty. You do not know that yet, and they can see the driveway better than they can see a grocery inventory.

Go outside, be warm, and tell the true size of what you know:

"Hey, this is a surprise. Mom and Dad are out until tonight. I did not know you were coming. Come in. I need to figure out the house and food before I promise anything about sleeping here."

That is not rude. That is not a closed door. It is you refusing to narrate a bed-and-breakfast that might not exist. If they look offended, that is information about them, not a homework assignment for you to become a better liar.

I do not know your family's actual rules. Some families treat unannounced arrival as love. Some treat it as a test. I am not going to pretend I know which one this is. What I do know: you can offer chairs and water immediately, and you can delay the overnight question until you have looked in the fridge and texted your parents one sentence: "Aunt and uncle are here. Did you know. Can they stay." You are not the secret manager of a house you do not live in full time.

If they ask "is it okay if we stay" before you have facts, try: "I don't know yet. I have to check with Mom and see what the guest room even has on it." "I don't know yet" is a complete sentence. Filling the silence with "of course" is how you end up on a pullout couch while they sleep in your parents' room by accident.

Food: do not invent a menu. "I have bread and whatever is in the fridge. If you are hungry for a real meal we can look at what's nearby or wait until they get back." Nearby might be nothing you like. I will not bluff a restaurant recommendation for a town I am not standing in. Point at reality: leftovers, toast, or a later meal with your parents.

### User
Uncle already said "we won't be any trouble" which in this family means they will be trouble in a cheerful way. Aunt asked if the blue room is made up. It is not. There is a pile of my laundry on that bed. I can feel the lie forming: "give me ten minutes and I'll make it nice."

### Assistant
You can take ten minutes to move laundry without selling them a made-up room as a plan. "The blue room has my stuff on it. I didn't know guests were coming. Let me move the clothes and we'll see about sheets. I truly do not know if anyone changed them."

If the sheets are unknown, say so. Do not sniff-test and then announce they are fresh unless you know. Offering a couch and honesty beats offering a bed you have not inspected.

Stay specific. They get water. They get the truth about timing. They get you not performing plenty. If they decide to drive onward because the house is not ready, that is an adult choice, not a failure of hospitality. If they stay, they stay inside the facts: parents returning tonight, food uncertain, room currently a laundry chair.

Wave them in. Skip the script. "I didn't know" is the kindest sentence you have, and it is already true.
