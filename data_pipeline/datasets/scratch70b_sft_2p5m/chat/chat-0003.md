title: Talk through a neighbor's smoke alarm that will not stop

### User
It's 11 p.m. and the smoke alarm in the unit next door has been going for twenty minutes. Not a chirp. Full scream. I knocked once, nobody answered. I can smell a little something but I'm not sure if it's toast or actual smoke. I don't want to be the dramatic hallway person, but I also don't want to ignore a fire. Help me think.

### Assistant
Before we pick a plan: is the smell getting stronger while you stand there, or is it the same faint whatever it was when this started?

### User
Same faint smell. Not eye-stinging. I opened my own door a crack and it's not filling my place. The alarm is definitely theirs, not the building hallway detector. Lights are on under their door.

### Assistant
Lights on and a flat smell is a different problem than smoke climbing. You are close to the right move already: you noticed duration, you knocked, you checked whether it was entering your unit.

Knock again, louder, and say who you are through the door. "It's Jordan from 4B, your alarm's been going." Names lower the drama. If someone answers, you can stop being the fire department. If they say it's burnt popcorn, you can go back to bed with the facts.

If nobody answers after a second knock, do not pick the lock or play hero with a wet towel in their unit. Call the non-emergency number for your building or the fire department's non-emergency line and describe exactly what you know: alarm twenty-plus minutes, lights on, knock unanswered, smell faint and not increasing. Let them decide whether engines roll. That is their job. Guessing "it's probably fine" for another hour is how people talk themselves out of a real call.

Stay in the hallway only long enough to knock. Then wait in your doorway or back inside with your door closed. If the smell jumps from faint to sharp, or you see haze, that is the moment you leave the building and call emergency services, not a group chat.

You already did the hard part of not pretending you didn't hear it. One more knock, a clear sentence, then a phone call if the door stays shut. That sequence is calm, not dramatic.

### User
There's no front desk after ten. I think we have a super's after-hours voicemail. I feel weird calling 911 for what might be a battery or steam.

### Assistant
You are allowed to feel weird and still use the right number for the facts in front of you. A full scream for twenty minutes with nobody answering is not "a chirp I can sleep through." Non-emergency fire or police dispatch exists for exactly this: alarm, occupancy unknown, smell present but not growing. If you only have 911 in your head, say you are reporting a smoke alarm with an unanswered door, not that you see flames. Dispatchers route that.

Skip the super's voicemail as your first line. Voicemail cannot walk into the unit. It can be a second call after you have spoken to a live dispatcher, so the building has a record.

If a neighbor opens while you are still in the hall, great. Ask them to silence it or to crack a window if it is steam. You do not need to go in. If they get irritated that you knocked, that irritation is cheaper than a missed fire.

You almost had the whole plan: duration, smell check, knock. The missing piece is not a lecture about batteries. It is not waiting for the scream to become your personality. Second knock, identify yourself, then a live person on the phone if the door stays closed. Then you go back inside, pack your keys and phone by the door in case the smell changes, and you let professionals own the maybe.

You do not need to diagnose toast versus wiring from the hallway. You need to not be the only one who heard it.
