### User
We had an IEP meeting at 3:15. It's 4:50. We sat in the front office. The special ed teacher left at 4:10 because they "had a bus duty they couldn't miss." The general ed teacher never came. The coordinator said "we'll reschedule" and started packing a tote. My kid is in aftercare until 5:30. I took unpaid time. I have a printed list of questions about reading support. I don't want to cry at a secretary. I also don't want this to vanish into a calendar hole. What do I do before I leave the building?

### Assistant
You leave with a date, a named person, and your questions on the record. You don't leave with a vibe.

Before the tote zips: "I need the reschedule on the calendar before I go, with the people who were supposed to be here. I took time off. What days and times exist this week or next?" If they say they'll email, you still ask for two actual slots while you're standing there. Write them down. If they can't give a slot, ask who owns the invite and when you'll have it. Get a name.

Hand them a copy of your question list or email it to the coordinator from the lobby: "These are the items for the missed 3:15 IEP on [date]. Please include in the next meeting." Timestamp. You're not being extra. The meeting didn't happen. Paper happens.

I will not invent IDEA timelines, how many days they "have" to meet, or what an IEP "must" contain. Those are real rules and they depend on your situation. I won't fake them. If you already have an advocate or a parent group, that's who you ask about clocks. Today, in this office, you can still refuse to be packed into a tote.

Don't unload on the secretary. They're not the teacher. "Who do I talk to about a meeting that didn't happen?" is the question if the coordinator is already in the parking lot.

Is your kid supposed to be in that meeting, or is this adults-only today?

### User
Adults-only. Kid doesn't know we were here. Coordinator said the next opening is "maybe three weeks" and that bus duty was "non-negotiable." I can feel the unpaid hours like a bruise. They offered a phone call tomorrow with only the coordinator, not the teachers. That feels like a consolation prize.

### Assistant
A coordinator-only call can be a scheduling call. Don't let it replace the IEP.

You can take tomorrow's call if you say out loud what it is: "I'll do a short call tomorrow to lock a meeting with the teachers. I don't consider that the IEP." If they try to "get through some items" without the people who teach reading, you can say, "I'll wait for the team. Here's the list so nobody starts from zero." You don't have to refuse the call. You have to label it.

Three weeks: ask what happens for your kid in the meantime. That's a practical question: current supports stay, or is something lapsing? I won't guess. They should answer. If they don't know, "Please find out and email me by Friday" is a close.

Unpaid time: mention it once, dry. "I used unpaid leave for 3:15. I need a time that holds." You're not asking them to feel it. You're explaining why a maybe-three-weeks shrug is expensive. If they have before-school slots, ask. If they only have 3:15s that collapse, ask for a first-period meeting. I don't know the contract. You can still ask.

Don't cry at the secretary. If you need to cry, car. Then aftercare pickup like a normal Tuesday so your kid doesn't inherit the bruise.

### User
I'll take the labeling call. Should I cc the principal tonight or is that a grenade?

### Assistant
A factual email to the coordinator, cc the principal if that's how your school actually gets meetings to exist. I don't know your building politics. If the principal is how calendars move, cc. If it will only make the coordinator petty, send to coordinator and bcc yourself, then escalate in a week if there's still no invite.

Tonight's email: date of no-show, who was missing, you waited until 4:50, you need a full team date, your list attached, you'll join tomorrow's call only to schedule. No novel about bus duty. Don't call anyone a liar.

Pickup at 5:30. Tell your kid nothing they don't need. The delayed IEP is an adult mess. You already did the hard part, which was staying after they packed the tote. Get the slots, get the list on the record, get your kid. That's a completed bad afternoon, even if the meeting still isn't.
