### User
I've been in this waiting room for almost two hours. My slot was 9:40. The TV is on a cooking show with no sound. People keep getting called who arrived after me. I took unpaid time and I have a shift later. I do not know if I should ask the desk, leave, or sit here like a houseplant. I have not done this alone before. Walk me through it like I am new, because I am.

### Assistant
I need one thing before I advise: is this a clinic visit you cannot easily reschedule this week, or is it something you could move without wrecking care or a deadline? That changes whether you wait, ask for a real time, or walk.

### User
It is a follow-up. Not an emergency. If I leave I might wait weeks. I still have to be at work at one. It is 11:10 now. I already told my supervisor I would be back. I do not want to be rude to the desk person. They look slammed.

### Assistant
Thanks. That is enough to plan with. Follow-up plus a hard stop at one means you need a clock, not a vibe.

Go to the desk when they are not mid-call. Stand where they can see you. One question, specific: "Hi, I was checked in for 9:40. Can you tell me how much longer, and whether I will be seen before I have to leave at twelve-thirty?" You are asking for information, not a scolding. "Twelve-thirty" is your buffer so you can still make the shift. Do not say "I have been waiting forever." They already know the room is full.

If they give you a range — "maybe twenty minutes" — write the time down. If twenty minutes would land you past your buffer, say so immediately: "If it is later than that I will need to reschedule. Is there a same-day opening after my shift, or should I book another day?" You are not diagnosing anything. You are matching a calendar to a queue.

If they cannot give a time, that is also an answer. Then you choose: wait until twelve-thirty and check again, or reschedule now. Sitting with no number until one is how you miss work and still miss the visit.

### User
What if they say "the provider is running behind" and nothing else. And the people arriving after me really are going in. I will look like I am accusing them of skipping me.

### Assistant
"Running behind" is a weather report. You can nod and still ask the useful part: "Understood. Can you check that I'm still in line for the 9:40 slot, and what's a realistic window?" You are verifying your place, not writing an exposé.

Later arrivals can be different visit types, add-ons, or a second hallway. I will not pretend I know this office's rules. If it bothers you, keep it observational: "I just want to make sure my check-in didn't get lost." That is a systems question. It is allowed. You are not calling anyone a villain.

Tone: intern energy is fine here. Short sentences. Thank them when they look it up. If they look slammed, your brevity is the courtesy, not disappearing.

If they find you were lost in the software, stay calm. "Thanks for catching that. What's the next step?" If they find you are correctly in line, go sit. Set an alarm for twelve-fifteen. At twelve-fifteen, return once: "I have to leave at twelve-thirty for work. Should I stay or reschedule?" Then follow the answer. Do not hover at the desk between those two visits. Hovering makes everyone brittle.

### User
Okay. If I reschedule, do I apologize for taking the slot.

### Assistant
You do not need a confession. "I can't wait past twelve-thirty. What's the next available follow-up?" is complete. If they offer a cancellation list, take it. If they do not, do not invent a policy.

You took unpaid time. That already cost you. Being new in a waiting room does not mean you forfeit a clock. Ask once for a real window, protect the shift, and leave a clean reschedule if the window is fiction. That is the whole job of the next twenty minutes.
