### User
Group of four. One person hasn't answered Slack, email, or the doc comments in nine days. Presentation is in six. We have their section title and nothing under it. The other two want me to "be the bigger person" and just write it. I think that's how I become the person who always writes it. Don't sell me teamwork posters. What are my actual options?

### Assistant
Two options, then I'll tell you which I'd pick. I'm not going to pretend the vanished person is having a secret emergency I can verify. They might be. They might have quit the class in their head. You don't know.

Option A: you three freeze their section as a clearly labeled hole, tell the instructor in writing today, and present what's yours. You keep a paper trail: last ping date, who pinged, no reply. You do not ghost-write a full extra chapter the night before.

Option B: you three split their section *once*, in a short written agreement between the remaining people, and you email the vanished person a deadline: "If we don't hear by Thursday noon, we submit without your pages and we'll note that to the instructor." Then you actually stop waiting.

I would take B if the missing section is required for a grade you can't afford to tank, and A if the instructor already said individual contribution matters and empty slides are allowed with an explanation. Most of the time, B is the less naïve move. A is cleaner ethically and riskier on points. You asked like a skeptic, so I'm not going to bless "just be nice."

Have you already emailed the instructor, or only the group thread?

### User
Only the group thread. Instructor hasn't been looped. The vanished person posted a meme in a different class chat yesterday, so I'm not buying the hospital story some of us were floating. The other two still want me to write it because I "care more."

### Assistant
A meme yesterday is not a medical chart, and it's also not proof of evil. It is enough to stop treating silence as a mystery.

Loop the instructor today. Short: names, due date, nine days no reply on the project channels, meme is none of the instructor's business unless you need to say you know they're active elsewhere. Don't write a prosecution. Write a status.

Then tell the other two, in the thread, that you are not writing the whole missing section alone. Offer the split: each of the three takes a subsection, timeboxed, or you present with a labeled gap. If they still point at you because you care more, that's them voting you the sucker. You can decline a vote.

I recommend B with the Thursday noon cutoff, plus the instructor email today so nobody can claim they "didn't know." If the person reappears Wednesday with a draft, you can still use it. If they reappear Friday with excuses, you already moved.

You're allowed to care about the grade without becoming the group's spare brain.

### User

### Assistant
That's option B with you still carrying the vanished person's weight and hoping a grade fairy notices later. Credit-later is how the person who disappeared still gets a full share on a slide with four names. If you split the work, put initials on subsections in the doc. If you present a hole, say the hole out loud for ten seconds and move on. Don't pad it with filler so it looks even.

Yes. Put the cutoff in writing in the group channel so it isn't a vibe you said in the kitchen. Then do your own original pages, not theirs in a different font. If they vanish from *this* conversation too, that's a second data point, and you still have option A. Don't wait nine more days to notice. I recommended B because six days is short and a labeled split is cleaner than a secret martyr draft. If you hate B, take A tonight and email the instructor with the empty outline attached. Those are still the two doors. There isn't a third door called "be the bigger person" that doesn't dump the labor on you.
