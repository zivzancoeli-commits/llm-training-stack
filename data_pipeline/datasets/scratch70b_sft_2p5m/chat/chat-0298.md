### User
I booked a hotel for one night because of an early thing. My room is directly across from the ice machine. It dumps ice like a hailstorm every few minutes. My pillow faces the door. Earplugs helped until someone laughed in the hallway. Front desk already said "we're full." I have to be a person at 7 a.m. It is 10:40.

### Assistant
Full means they will not magic a silent wing. It does not mean you are out of moves. You can still ask for a room away from ice, even if they said full; sometimes full means "I do not want to look." You can also rearrange the room you have.

Do not pick a fight with the machine. Do not prop the door open. Do not leave a passive-aggressive note on the ice bin.

Ask once more, specifically: "Is there any room not on an ice or elevator wall, even a different floor? I'll take a worse view." If they still say no, ask for a fan, extra blankets, or a quieter corner of the building they use for staff overflow. I do not know this chain's playbook. Specific asks beat "I can't sleep."

Have you already paid through a third-party site, and did you tell them you would take a different floor?

### User
I booked through the hotel site. I said any floor. The clerk shrugged and offered earplugs I already own. There's a desk I could drag. The bed is heavy.

### Assistant
You booked direct, which sometimes makes a second ask easier, not because of a law I am going to invent, but because they have your reservation on their screen. Go down once, in person, not by phone from the pillow. People solve more when you are standing there looking like a guest who has to work at 7, not a review waiting to happen. Still keep the tone flat. "I can't sleep across from the ice. Please look again for anything else tonight."

If the answer stays no, change the geometry of the room. Drag the desk if it is safe to drag. Turn the bed if the frame allows and you will not wreck your back; many hotel beds are two twins shoved together or a platform you should not haul. More realistic: sleep with your head toward the window wall, even if that means the wrong way on the mattress. A 90-degree rotate of your body is cheaper than rotating the whole bed. Hang the extra blanket over the door to dull the hallway. Run the bath fan or the AC on a steady noise so the ice becomes one layer in a stack, not a surprise.

Put the "do not disturb" and a spare towel at the door bottom if there is a gap. People with ice buckets still exist. You are reducing peaks.

Set two alarms. Sleep fragmentation plus one alarm is how people miss 7 a.m.

I cannot promise a refund for noise. If you want to ask in the morning, ask in the morning with the times it dumped, not with insults. Tonight is for sleep architecture.

### User
I feel petty. It's just ice. Other people can sleep through it.

### Assistant
Other people are not the ones who have to speak at 7. Pettiness would be unplugging a shared machine. Asking for a different room is ordinary. Ice against a pillow is a design problem, not a character test.

For the next ten minutes: go to the desk with the specific ask, take whatever they actually give you, come back, flip your head to the window wall, start the AC, hang the blanket, set two alarms, and put the phone face down. If a better room appears, take it even if you already unpacked. Unpacking is five minutes. Ice is all night.

You are allowed to want quiet in a hotel. That is what the room was for. Try the desk, then try geometry. Then stop narrating petty and lie down the other way.
