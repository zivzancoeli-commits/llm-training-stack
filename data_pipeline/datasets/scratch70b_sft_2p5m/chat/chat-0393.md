### User
Someone is walking around with a leaving card for a manager on another floor. I have spoken to that manager twice, both times about a printer. The card already has paragraphs. My coworker stopped at my desk and waited. I wrote "best wishes" and now I feel like I participated in a fiction. There's a second card coming for a gift. I don't want to add money to a person I don't know. I also don't want to be the only person who didn't sign the next round.

### Assistant
A card is not a deposition. "Best wishes" is an honest small sentence. You did not commit fraud.

You can skip the gift. Skipping money is not the same as skipping humanity. When the envelope comes, "I'm going to sit this one out" is enough. You do not need a speech about printers.

If they hover, you can keep your hands on the keyboard: "I barely work with them. I'll pass on the gift." Then you let them walk to the next desk. Hovering is their technique. Your pause is not a vote.

I will not invent a workplace gift rule or a suggested amount. If your office has a culture of collecting, you can still opt out of a collection for a stranger.

Is the second card a group gift you were told is mandatory, or just an envelope with a smile?

### User
Envelope with a smile and "we're all chipping in." Nobody named a number. I don't want to be cheap. I also don't want to Venmo a mystery. The manager once CC'd me by accident. That's my relationship.

### Assistant
CC-by-accident is not a bond. "We're all chipping in" is social pressure, not a payroll deduction.

Pass the envelope. If you need a line, use: "I'm going to skip. I don't really know them." Smile if that is your face. You do not have to fill the silence with a joke about being broke. Jokes invite bargaining.

If they say even a little helps, you can repeat skip. You are not required to name a figure to prove you understood. I will not pick a figure.

The first card already has your "best wishes." That is the participation you were willing to give. Two artifacts are not required.

If you worry about being the only skip, remember you will not see the list. Envelopes feel public. They are usually a messy pile. Even if someone notices, they will move on when the next card appears next month.

### User
What if this coworker later says I wasn't a team player, in a way that reaches my actual manager?

### Assistant
Then you have a different problem: a gossip story. Your actual manager can hear "I didn't chip in for someone I don't work with" and survive. Team player is a stretch goal for a printer acquaintance.

If it comes up, stay boring: "I signed the card. I skipped the gift because I don't know them." No counterattack on the envelope person.

You already wrote the honest small thing. Let the second card travel without you. Go back to your work. The printer will still jam next week. That is the relationship. You do not have to fund a goodbye.

### User
The envelope is on the next desk. They'll be here in five minutes. I already signed the card. I can feel myself reaching for my wallet to make this smaller.

### Assistant
Leave the wallet where it is. Five minutes is enough to pick the skip sentence and put your hands back on the keyboard. When the envelope arrives, "I'm going to sit this one out. I don't really know them." If they wait, let them wait. You already gave best wishes on paper. That was the human part. The collection is optional. Do not buy a smaller awkwardness with cash you do not owe a stranger. Look at the screen. Pass the envelope. Return to the printer problem, which is the actual relationship. Five minutes. Then it is gone.
