### User
The fire alarm has been doing the long beep every few minutes for a half hour. There is no email, no sign in the lobby, no one with a clipboard. My dog is losing it. I grabbed my keys and then I felt stupid because it might be a test. I also feel stupid if I stay and it is not. I live on the fourth floor. I can see the street. No trucks. People are still walking their groceries in.

### Assistant
No trucks plus groceries is a clue, not a guarantee. Alarms can be tests, faults, or real. I cannot know from here. I will not tell you to ignore a fire alarm as a lifestyle. I will tell you how to get information without standing in a stairwell for an hour on a guess.

Look for a posted notice you missed — elevator, laundry, inside the front door. Check the building email and portal, not social media rumors. If there is a manager number, call it. If a neighbor is in the hall looking calm with a dog, you can ask, "Did they tell you this was a test?" One question.

If you smell smoke, see smoke, or the alarm pattern changes to something your building told you means get out, you leave. Groceries in the lobby do not cancel smoke.

Is there a smell, heat, or a sprinkler, or is it only the beep, and is your dog crateable for ten minutes while you check the lobby?

### User
Only the beep. No smell. Dog can crate. I checked the lobby camera if that counts — a guy in a polo is at the panel with a laptop. I didn't see a sign. I feel like I should still go down.

### Assistant
A polo at the panel with a laptop is the closest thing you will get to a clipboard. Going down is reasonable. You can take the dog if the crate makes you feel trapped, or crate for a short lobby trip if that is safer for the dog in a stairwell of beeps. I am not a fire marshal. Stairs with a panicked dog is its own mess. If you go, leash, keys, phone, close the door.

Ask the polo one thing: "Is this a scheduled test, and how long?" If they are actually testing, ask why there was no notice. You can be annoyed and still not block their work. If they do not know, or they are a vendor who only knows the panel, ask who in the building authorized it. Then you decide whether to wait it out with headphones and a crated dog or to leave the building until it stops. Leaving is allowed even for a test. Tests are loud on purpose.

Do not pull a pull station to "see." Do not post in a city-wide app that the building is on fire if you see a laptop and groceries. Do not start a stairwell evacuation of strangers based on your adrenaline. Share facts with a neighbor if they ask: "There's a person at the panel, I'm checking."

If you cannot get a straight answer and you are not okay, leave. A false trip to the sidewalk is cheap. Take the dog.

### User
If I go to a coffee shop for two hours I'll feel dramatic. The dog will still be upset. I have a call at 2.

### Assistant
A call at 2 is a constraint. If the polo says "until 1:30," you stay and use headphones, dog in the quietest room, door closed. If they say "could be all afternoon," you take the call from somewhere else or you tell the other person you may have alarm noise. You are not dramatic for wanting to think.

For the next ten minutes: crate or leash, go to the lobby, get a time from the polo, come back, text a building chat if you have one with that time, and set a personal cutoff. If there is still no human at the panel when you go down, treat it as unknown: grab the dog and bag and stand outside long enough to see if trucks arrive. Unknown plus fourth floor is why people use stairs.

I am not certain it is a test. A laptop at a panel is a strong hint. Verify, then pick stay-with-a-time or leave. The dog is information too: you can comfort a test. You cannot explain a real fire to a pet after you waited because you were afraid to look silly. Look once. Then act.
