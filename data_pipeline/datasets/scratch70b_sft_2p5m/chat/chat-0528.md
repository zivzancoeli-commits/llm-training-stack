### User
A coworker asked me to block two hours on my calendar as a "working session" so it looks like we're in a room, while they take a video interview at a cafe. They want me to ping them if our manager walks the floor. They said everyone does it and that they'll cover me next time. I don't want to run a decoy calendar. I also don't want to be the office snitch. The invite is already sitting in my inbox as a tentative.

### Assistant
I won't help you fake a working session or run lookout for a secret interview. I won't coach you on calendar camouflage or floor-ping protocols.

Decline the invite. A one-line reply to them, not the manager: "I can't dummy a meeting on my calendar. Good luck with your search, but I won't cover." You don't need to copy HR. You don't need to announce their job hunt. Declining a fake block is not snitching. Snitching would be volunteering their cafe to the boss. You weren't asked to do that. Don't.

"Everyone does it" is how people recruit. You can let everyone, if that's even true, do it without you.

The reciprocal cover is not a voucher you have to honor later. Decline that future too.

If they already put the block on a team calendar you both share, remove yourself. If you can't, message them to cancel it. A tentative sitting there is a trap with a clock.

Have they already told the manager you're booked together, or is it only the invite so far?

### User
Only the invite. They named the event "sync — don't move." Our manager sometimes clicks into titles. I don't want a conversation about a sync that never existed. They texted "please, this job would change my rent."

### Assistant
Rent is real. A fake sync is still a fake sync. You can hope their rent improves without lending your calendar as a stage set.

Decline. If the title is already visible, declining is what makes it not your event. You don't need to walk to the manager and confess a meeting that didn't happen. You need your calendar to tell the truth about you.

If they plead in person, same sentence, shorter: "I won't fake a hold. I won't ping you about the boss." Then you go back to work. You don't have to debate job-search ethics as a sport. You don't have to promise confidentiality like a lawyer. You can simply not talk about their search, which is the ordinary thing, and also not participate in it.

Don't offer a true coffee at the same hour as a compromise. That's how you become the alibi with better lighting.

If the manager later asks why a sync vanished, "It wasn't a real meeting" is a problem they created. "I declined because I wasn't meeting" is accurate if asked. You don't have to pre-explain.

I will not invent whether job hunting on work time violates your handbook. Read it if you care. I won't guess a firing. I will say you shouldn't add your name to a story you don't want to retell.

### User
I feel like I'm blocking their future.

### Assistant
You are blocking a decoy, not a future. They can interview on a lunch they actually take, a PTO block they own, or a time that doesn't require your calendar to lie. Those are their tools.

Decline tonight so "don't move" doesn't become a room you have to occupy. Be a coworker who keeps their mouth shut about a search and their calendar honest. That's not snitching. That's not a working session. That's you, not a lookout. Let them change their rent without using your hours as a prop. Remove yourself from the tentative. Don't ping the floor. Don't block two hours that aren't work. Their interview can exist. Your calendar can tell the truth. Both things can be true in the same afternoon.
