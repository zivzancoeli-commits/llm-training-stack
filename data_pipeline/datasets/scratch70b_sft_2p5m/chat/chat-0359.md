### User
The train died. They put us on buses. The first bus filled. I'm on the second, standing in the aisle, holding a pole, backpack in my stomach. The driver said "next stop might take forty." It has already been twenty. Somebody's bag is on the last empty-ish seat with no person attached. I have a bad hip on long stands. I don't want to be the person who starts a bus argument. I also don't know if I should get off and wait for a third bus that might have seats. It's raining. What is the least-stupid move for the next forty minutes?

### Assistant
Stay on this bus unless you can see a third bus that is emptier and actually leaving. Rain plus a maybe is how people miss the replacement entirely.

Ask about the bag in one sentence, not a speech. "Is this seat taken?" to the people nearest it. If nobody claims it, you can move the bag to the floor or the owner's lap if the owner is obvious. If the owner is not obvious, hold the bag up and say "whose is this?" Then sit if it is abandoned. If someone snaps, you tried a normal question.

If the bag's person is there and using the seat as storage, you can say, "I need to sit — hip. Could I have the seat?" Specific need, short. Most people move. Some do not. If they do not, you are not required to hold a trial in the aisle. You go back to the pole.

I will not invent your transit system's rights. Drivers sometimes help; sometimes they cannot leave the seat. You can tell the driver at a stop, not while they are merging: "There's an empty seat with a bag and I can't stand the whole way." Then accept the answer.

Is the "seat" a real seat or a wheel-well ledge people are pretending is a seat?

### User
Real seat, window, bag and a coat. The person might be the one standing at the back on a phone. I can't tell. My hip is already loud. Next official stop is a park-and-ride.

### Assistant
At the park-and-ride, if the bus opens and people shuffle, that is your window. Ask again, louder, to the whole area: "Who owns the bag on the window seat? I need to sit." Phone-person in the back can claim it. If they do, ask them to take the bag. If they don't, sit and put the bag at your feet or on the coat.

Do not dump the bag in the aisle where it becomes a trip. Do not open it. You are relocating an obstacle, not searching.

If standing is actually unsafe for you — dizziness, pain that scares you — get off at the park-and-ride and ask staff or the driver when the next vehicle is, and whether it has seats. A later bus you can sit on may beat forty more minutes of damage. That is not weakness. That is a hip.

Hydrate if you have water. Loosen the backpack so it is not a stone on your gut. Face a pole you can lock an elbow around. Those are the unglamorous forty-minute skills.

### User
Phone-person claimed the bag and didn't move it. They said "I'll be sitting in a minute." They are still standing, scrolling.

### Assistant
"I'll be sitting in a minute" is a hold. You can say, "I need it now." Then wait five seconds. If they sit, fine. If they keep scrolling, you can sit on the remaining slice or take the seat and hand them the bag. You are not stealing. They chose the phone over the chair.

If that feels like a fight you cannot do, get off at this stop if you can see a seated option. Ask out loud. Rain is real. So is your hip.

You already asked once. A second line is enough. After that, either sit or change buses. Do not spend the rest of the ride rehearsing a speech. Pole, bag, seat, or exit. Pick one at this stop. The replacement bus is not a personality test. It is a ride. Take the version you can stand — or better, sit.
