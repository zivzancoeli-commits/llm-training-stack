### User
Coworker who "owns" the client call asked me to join muted and only unmute if they look at me to agree. They said it looks cleaner. I am on the hook for the timeline we will discuss. Last time I stayed mute, they promised a date I cannot hit. I have to be on this call in twenty minutes. I don't want a power struggle on camera. I also don't want to nod along to a fantasy calendar.

### Assistant
You unmute when the timeline is wrong. Cleaner is not more important than a date you have to live with.

Before the call, in chat, one line to them: "I'll stay mostly quiet. If the date isn't feasible I'll speak. I won't agree on mute to a timeline I can't do." Now they can't pretend they didn't know.

On the call, you can still let them lead. Leading isn't a gag. When the fantasy date lands, you say, "I can do [real window], not that date." Short. No speech about being silenced. The client hears a constraint, not a feud.

I will not invent your company's meeting norms. If a manager should be there, that's your org. Twenty minutes is enough to ping them if this is a pattern that already burned you.

Are you supposed to be a visible expert on this call, or did they only add you as backup?

### User
Visible enough that the client asked for me by name. That's why the mute instruction made me feel like a prop. They said "too many cooks." I am one cook. The date is the food.

### Assistant
Too many cooks is a fear of interruption. You can not interrupt and still not be a prop.

Join with camera if that's the culture, mute until needed, unmute for the date. If they talk over you, say the date again once. Then put it in the chat box so it exists in writing: "Confirming we cannot hit Friday; next possible is ..." Chat is hard to gaslight later.

If they look at you for a silent agree, you can shake your head and unmute. Silent yes is how last time happened. Don't smile-nod to keep the video pretty.

Client asked for you by name. That's permission to talk about your work. You don't need a monologue. You need the calendar to be true.

### User
If I contradict them, they'll do the laugh and say I'm being conservative.

### Assistant
Let them say conservative. You can answer the client, not the insult: "It's not conservative. It's the work remaining." Then stop. Laugh-defense is their extra. You don't debate your personality on a client call.

After the call, a recap email with the date you stated. You send it or you ask them to send it with that date in it. Recap is the real meeting.

Twenty minutes: chat warning, true window in your pocket, unmute once, chat backup, recap later. Mute for filler. Unmute for the calendar. You can look clean and still not agree to a week that doesn't exist.

### User
I unmuted for the date. They did the laugh. I put the real window in chat. Client said "got it." Coworker pinged me after, "thanks for the conservative note," like a sticker on my face.

### Assistant
Client said got it. That's the meeting. The sticker is leftover.

You don't have to defend conservative in the after-ping. "It's the remaining work" once, or no reply. Recap email with the window, sent to the same people who were on the call. The laugh doesn't live in the recap unless you quote it, so don't.

Next time they pre-mute you, you still send the pre-chat line. Pattern is now documented in DMs plus a recap. You were asked for by name because of the timeline. You used the name. Mute for filler, unmute for the calendar. Clean video is not a fantasy date. You already knew. You said it out loud. That's the job done.
