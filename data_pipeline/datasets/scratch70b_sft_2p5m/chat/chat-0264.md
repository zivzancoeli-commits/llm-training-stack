### User
The van was supposed to be here at 2. It's 4:10. The tracker says "arriving." The old lease ends at midnight. New place keys are in my pocket. I have a mattress on its side in the hallway and a fridge that still has mustard. Landlord texted "unit must be vacant and swept." I have two friends who can stay until 6. After that I'm alone with a mustard fridge and a maybe van.

### Assistant
Keys in the pocket means you have a destination. Midnight is a clock, not a personality test. "Arriving" on a tracker is a mood. Call the van company. Talk to a human. Ask for a window, not a vibe. If they can't give a time, ask whether the truck is actually assigned.

I'm not going to invent what happens if you're not swept at 12:01. Leases and walkthroughs vary. I won't guess fees. I can tell you a photo timestamp of an empty room is useful, and a mustard fridge is not empty.

Friends until 6 is a resource. Use them on the heavy things now, not on waiting for a truck that might be a ghost.

Is the new place close enough to start shuttling boxes in a car tonight, or is the van the only vehicle that fits the mattress?

### User
New place is 12 minutes. I have a hatchback. Mattress won't fit. Fridge might if we unplug and wait, but it's a rental fridge that stays. I forgot that. Mustard is mine. Van is for the couch, desk, and mattress. Tracker jumped backward. Friends are hungry.

### Assistant
Rental fridge stays. Mustard goes in a grocery bag now. That's a five-minute win. Hungry friends don't lift well. Feed them something cheap or send one for food while the other helps you stage the couch by the door.

For the next ten minutes: call the van, mustard out, sweep the rooms that are already empty, take photos as you go. If the van is truly late, ask the driver whether they can still do a same-night load. If they say morning, you need a mattress plan: floor of the new place, or a friend, or you sleep at the new apartment without it. Uncomfortable. Possible. Better than a hallway mattress as an invitation for whoever walks the old building.

Text the old landlord: "Van delayed. Aiming to be out tonight. Fridge is the rental, staying. I'll send photos when swept." Don't promise midnight if you cannot. Don't write a novel. If they insist on a walkthrough at 8 p.m., ask for a time. If they don't reply, you still sweep.

Hatchback can move boxes, lamps, the desk drawers. Start a shuttle. 12 minutes is a gift. Don't sit on the floor staring at "arriving."

### User
If I'm not out at midnight I feel like I'll be squatting in my own mess. The couch is blocking the door.

### Assistant
Move the couch off the door first. That's safety, not symbolism. You need an exit that isn't a cushion maze.

You're not squatting because a truck lied. You're in a delayed move. If midnight comes and the van is still a myth, you take what the hatchback can take, sweep, photo, lock, and go to the new keys. Leave a labeled note on the rental fridge if you must. The rest is a morning van or a dispute with the movers. I won't script the landlord's penalty. You document. You communicate. You don't sleep in a blocked doorway to prove a point.

Friends leave at 6. Before then, get the door clear, the mustard out, one full hatchback run, a human on the phone. Tracker backward is a tell. Believe the call, not the map. Tonight you need a swept empty as close as you can get, a mattress plan, and keys used. Midnight is a line. You can cross it with photos and a message instead of with a couch in the hall. Pull the couch. Call. Shuttle. Eat. That's the order. The van can still show. You don't wait like furniture.
