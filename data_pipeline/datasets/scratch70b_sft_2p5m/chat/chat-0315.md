### User
They told me, in the hallway, that the birthday would wrap at ten. It is 11:40. The bass is in my floor. I have work at 7. I already sent a polite text at 10:50 that they hearted and did not change. I don't want cops. I don't want to be the neighbor from a story. I also want sleep.

### Assistant
A heart is not a volume knob. You asked once. 11:40 with a 7 a.m. is past the hallway promise.

You can knock. A knock is not a raid. Short, dressed, not in a rage costume: "Hey, you said ten. I've got 7 a.m. Can you kill the bass or move it to the other side?" Then wait. If they lower it, you go home and do not give a speech.

I will not tell you when your city considers this a violation, or that you should or should not call anyone official. That is your judgment and your building's process. If you have a manager after-hours number, that is a middle path some people use. If you do not, you have earplugs, a second ask, and whatever you already know about your own tolerance for official noise.

Did they give you a number, and is this a first offense or a pattern?

### User
I have a number. First loud party, they have been fine before. There is a building emergency line that I think is for fires not birthdays. I can hear people on the balcony now.

### Assistant
First party plus a broken ten o'clock is still allowed to be corrected. Use the number you have, not the fire line, unless you actually see a fire. A balcony at 11:40 is extra volume. You can mention it: "The balcony is carrying. Could people come in?" You are not their parent. You are the wall.

If you do not want to knock because you live alone and the hallway feels off, stay behind your door and call or text: "Bass is still in my floor. I need it down now, please." If they ignore a second text, you decide whether sleep tools get you to 7 or whether you use the building's non-emergency path in the morning as a written note. I will not script a police plot.

Earplugs, fan, couch if the bedroom is the bass wall. That is not you losing. That is you getting four hours.

Do not bang a broom. Do not threaten. Do not go onto their balcony.

If they invite you in as a joke, say no. You asked for quiet, not a beer.

### User
If I knock I will be shaking. I hate conflict. I already feel like the promise at ten makes me extra mad.

### Assistant
The promise is why you are mad. They set a clock and then treated it as decoration. Shaking is allowed. You can knock anyway, or you can skip knock and do the second text if your body will not do the door. A sent sentence is better than a fantasy confrontation you postpone until 1 a.m. when you are worse.

For the next ten minutes: send the bass-and-balcony text or knock once, start the fan, put in earplugs, set two alarms, and lie down even if you are mad. If it drops, good. If it does not, you sleep in the less bad room and you write the manager in the morning with times: promised ten, texted 10:50, still going 11:40. Facts, not adjectives.

You are not a storybook villain for wanting the end time they offered. Ask twice, then protect the 7 a.m. The birthday can exist without using your floor as a subwoofer. That was the deal. Remind them. Then go to bed angry if you have to. Angry and horizontal still beats angry and pacing. If you cannot sleep even after it drops, do not start a second conversation at 1 a.m.; morning is for the manager note. Tonight is earplugs and a clock.
