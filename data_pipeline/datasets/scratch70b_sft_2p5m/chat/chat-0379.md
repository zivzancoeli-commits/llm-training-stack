### User
I'm at the high school field. Game was 7:00. Lightning delay, then they said 8:15, then they posted "still evaluating." It's 9:02. The lot I parked in is a church lot with a sign that says it closes at 9. The gate is already across the exit. My car is one of about twenty still inside. My sister is in the stands with her kid. I don't know if the game is happening and I don't know how we leave. I don't want to make a scene at the gate.

### Assistant
Treat the car as the clock, not the scoreboard. A closed gate is not a sports problem. It is a logistics problem with a human on the other side of it.

Do not wait for a PA announcement to solve the lot. Walk to the gate now, before more cars idle and the mood turns. Look for a person, not a sign. Churches usually have a sexton, a volunteer, or a number on the gatebox. If there is a keypad and no person, photograph the sign and the closed arm, then call the number if there is one. Do not lift the bar yourself.

Is the gate a chain, a bar, or a rolling fence, and can you see anyone in the church building with lights on?

### User
It's a metal bar. There's a small light on in the office side of the church. I can see a silhouette. My sister says the umpires are still here and parents are starting to argue with the coach about refunds for the snack bar, which is not my problem. Her kid is melting down. She wants me to "just talk to the church guy" like I have a script.

### Assistant
You do have a script, and it is short. Walk to the lit office. Knock. If the door is locked, knock again and wait. When someone opens, you are not there to debate the delay. You say: "Hi — I parked for the game next door. The lot bar is down and our cars are still inside. Can you raise it, or is there a number we should call?" Then stop. Let them answer.

If they say they already closed, do not argue theology. Repeat the fact: cars are still in the lot. Ask whether they can open it once for the remaining vehicles. If they want names or a list, offer to walk the row and count. Counting is useful. Speeches are not.

If they refuse or vanish, go back to your sister. Do not leave her kid in the stands while you hunt a second entrance. Text one parent in the lot, if there is a thread: "Gate is down, I'm at the church office, stay with your cars." One message.

Refunds and snack-bar anger are noise. You are not the coach. You are the person who can get twenty cars moving if the bar goes up. Take the kid to the car once you know the bar can open. A bleacher is a bad place to wait out a gate.

### User
What if the silhouette is just a cleaner who doesn't have the key, and we get stuck until morning? Also I feel stupid because I saw the 9 p.m. sign when I parked and told myself the game would be done.

### Assistant
The sign was information. The delay was not your invention. Feeling stupid does not open a bar. If the person inside is a cleaner without a key, you still want a name, a number, and a next step. Ask: "Who usually closes this lot, and how do we reach them tonight?" Write it down. If they only have a church office voicemail, leave one message with your name, the number of cars, and that the bar is down with vehicles inside.

I will not promise a morning outcome or a towing window. I do not know this church's vendor. What you can control: nobody should sleep in a locked lot by accident. If you cannot raise the bar, call a non-emergency line for the town only if you actually cannot reach anyone with a key and people are stranded. That is a last step, not a first one. First is the lit office. Second is a number. Third is staying with your sister and the kid until there is a plan.

For the next ten minutes: walk to the office, knock, ask the three questions, text the lot once, then return to your sister with whatever answer you got. If the bar goes up, leave. Do not linger for a first pitch that may not exist. The game can be delayed. Your exit should not be.
