### User
The bus is late. It is raining. It is a weeknight. I am at the stop with a paper bag that is starting to collapse. I already checked the app twice. It said seven minutes, then twelve, then "delayed." I know I should have left a jacket. I almost have a plan — wait, walk to the next stop, or rideshare — and then I second-guess all three. Talk to me like a person, not like a delay announcement.

### Assistant
You are allowed to be wet and annoyed. That is not a character flaw.

Stay if the app still shows a real vehicle moving toward you, not a ghost number that jumps. If the map shows the bus two stops away, wait. If the map has gone vague and you have already been there long enough that your bag is failing, you need a different move than hope.

Walk to the next stop only if it is a short block, the sidewalk is lit, and you are okay carrying the bag that far. A next stop that is "maybe better" three long blocks in rain is how people get wetter and miss the bus that finally comes on the original corner. I do not know your street. If the next stop is across an unlit lot, skip it.

Rideshare is a money decision I will not price for you. If you take one, step under the awning of a store that is still open, not into the street. Pin the corner, not the middle of the block, so the driver can see you.

Kind and specific, not pep: put the bag inside your shirt or under your arm so the bottom does not blow out. If there is a trash bag in a can nearby, I would not steal liner from a public can. Use your own tote if you have one in a pocket. If you do not, hold the paper from the bottom with both hands and accept you may need to transfer stuff at home.

### User
The app now says the bus is "arriving" but I do not see headlights. There is a shelter with one bench and a guy already on it. I do not want to be rude and I do not want to stand in the gutter. I live about a twenty-five minute walk from here if I committed. I almost think I should walk. Then I think about the bag.

### Assistant
"Arriving" with no headlights is a maybe. Give it two minutes of actual watching the street, not the screen. If nothing comes, treat the app as wrong for tonight.

The bench: you can stand at the edge of the shelter without asking the guy to move. You do not need a speech. If you want the bench, "Mind if I sit on the end?" is enough. If he says nothing, you can still stand under the roof. Standing in the gutter is how tires find you. Stay on the sidewalk side of the shelter.

Walking twenty-five minutes in rain with a dying paper bag is a yes only if you can re-pack. Is there a pharmacy or grocery still open on the way? One cheap reusable bag, then walk. If nothing is open, walking means you may lose the bottom of the bag at minute twelve. Then you are gathering cans in a puddle. That is worse than another ten minutes at a shelter.

If you walk, choose the route with storefronts, not the cut-through. Text someone the route if that makes you feel less invisible. I am not trying to scare you. Rain plus late plus a quiet street is when people get jumpy, and a lit block is a practical preference.

### User
Okay. Two minutes of watching, then either the bus or I duck into the corner store for a bag and walk the lit street. What do I do with the app rage? I keep refreshing like that will shame the bus into existing.

### Assistant
Close the app after you decide. Refreshing is a slot machine. It will not dry you off.

If the bus appears in those two minutes, get on, even if you already emotionally committed to walking. A bus that exists beats a principle.

If it does not, store, bag, lit street, walk. At home, put the food away, put the wet shoes somewhere they can dry, and do not reconstruct the evening as a failure of character. The bus was late. The bag was paper. You picked a next action that matched the rain in front of you. That is the whole night. You were already close to the right plan. You only needed to stop treating the app like a person you could persuade.
