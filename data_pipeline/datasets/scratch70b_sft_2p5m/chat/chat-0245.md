### User
I dragged two bags six blocks. First time at this laundromat. Every washer has an "out of order" sign taped on, some handwritten, some printed. One dryer is rumbling with someone else's sheets. The attendant is on a phone behind plexiglass. It's 7:10 p.m. I work at 6:30 a.m. I have no clean scrubs. The app I used for the address did not mention a graveyard of machines.

### Assistant
Put the bags down so you're not negotiating with your shoulders. Look at the signs closely. If every washer is tagged, this place is not going to invent a working drum because you walked far.

Ask the attendant one question when they get off the phone: "Is any washer actually on?" If they say no, ask "nearest place still open?" You want a name and a direction, not a speech about the repair guy.

I'm not going to invent hours of other laundromats or what a load costs. I also won't pretend your scrubs will wash themselves in a rumbling dryer. Dryers don't wash.

Did you already put money on a card or in a machine, or is your wallet still closed?

### User
Wallet closed. I almost fed a washer that had a sign on the back, not the front. There's a handwritten list on the wall with two other shops. No hours. My phone map shows one 11 minutes away that might be a dry cleaner. The attendant shrugged and pointed at the list. Scrubs are the navy pair in the right-hand bag.

### Assistant
Don't feed a machine with a sign on the back. That was a good almost.

For the next ten minutes: photograph the wall list, pick the closest name you can confirm is a wash-and-fold or a laundromat, not a dry cleaner that will eat a day. Call if a number is on the list. If nobody picks up, you walk or bus with the bags only if your map shows machines, not a counter that closes at 7.

If you cannot confirm a second place is open, the backup is a sink. Hotel-style wash for the navy scrubs: bathroom sink, soap, rinse, roll in a towel, hang over a door. It won't be pretty. It will be wearable at 6:30. I am not a fabric scientist. I won't promise the navy won't fade. I will say a damp-but-clean top beats a dirty one you dragged six blocks for nothing.

Leave this shop without tipping a plexiglass shrug unless you want to. You bought nothing.

Text whoever might have a washer — a friend, a coworker, a building laundry room you forgot — with a time: "Need a machine tonight for scrubs, I can be there by 8." One ask. If they say no, sink.

### User
I feel like I failed a basic adult task. Also the dryer with the sheets smells like bleach and I briefly thought about stuffing my scrubs in with a stranger's load. I didn't.

### Assistant
Good that you didn't. Stranger sheets are not a communal washer. Bleach smell is not a welcome.

Failing a basic adult task would be standing here until 10 hoping the signs were a joke. You gathered information: dead washers, a wall list, a shrug, scrubs in the right-hand bag. That's the adult part. The app was wrong. Apps are wrong about tape on machines.

Go. Call the next place on the sidewalk, not under the fluorescent hum. If it's open, take both bags. If it isn't, go home and do the sink for the navy pair and sleep. Set them near a fan if you have one. In the morning they may still be slightly damp. That's allowed. Put a dry undershirt under if you need to.

You can come back to this laundromat another week with one sock as a scout, not two full bags. First time can be a bust. Your shift cannot. Move the bags. Ask the air for a working drum, and if the air says no, use the sink like a person who still has to be somewhere at 6:30.
