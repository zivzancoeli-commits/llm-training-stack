### User
Friend had a bad meal at a place I like. Slow service, wrong side, they felt ignored. Now they want me to agree it's "dead to us." We have a group dinner planning thread. They keep reacting with a skull to the restaurant name. I wasn't there. I had a fine time last month. I don't want to sign a boycott and I don't want to call my friend dramatic. Script.

### Assistant
You can believe their night and still not adopt a skull as policy.

DM, not the group thread: "That sounds like a bad night. I don't want to kill the place for the group. Let's pick something else if you don't want to go there." Validation plus a fork in the road. Boycott is optional for them. It isn't automatically yours.

In the thread, don't skull-react. Don't write a defense essay either. "I'm open to other spots" is enough if you're the one who suggested it. If someone else suggested it, you can stay out until you're asked.

I will not invent health-code drama. One bad night is one bad night unless you have more data. You don't.

Were they asking you to hate it as loyalty, or do they actually want a different reservation?

### User
Loyalty. They said "after what they did to me I need you on my side." What they did was forget a side and take a while. I get that it felt lousy. The skull in a group chat is the part that's doing too much.

### Assistant
On your side can mean "I believe you were treated sloppy." It does not mean "I will perform hatred in a thread."

"I'm on your side that the night sucked. I'm not going to skull the restaurant in the chat. If you don't want to eat there, we pick another place." Loyalty with a boundary. If they call that betrayal, they're asking for a chorus, not a friend.

Don't minimize the forgotten side. Don't maximize it into a saga. Match their facts, not their campaign.

If the group picks that restaurant anyway, you can go or not based on your own last month. You don't need permission. If you go without them, you don't live-post the food. That's just salt.

### User
They want me to post in the thread that I also had a bad experience so it isn't "just them." I didn't.

### Assistant
Don't invent a bad experience. That's a fake review with extra steps.

"I won't post a story I don't have. We can change restaurants." If they drop the skull, great. If they don't, you still don't pile on.

Group dinner can survive one person skipping a venue. It can also move venues. What it shouldn't get is a ghostwritten grievance.

You're allowed to like a place. They're allowed to avoid it. Skulls are optional. Skip the boycott signature. Keep the friend. That's the script: believe the night, refuse the campaign, pick food like adults.

### User
I DMed the script. They skull-reacted my DM. The group picked another restaurant anyway. I still like the original place. I feel like I have to swear off it for six months so the loyalty accounts balance.

### Assistant
You don't. Group picked another spot. That's the dinner. Your last-month fine time can stay true in your own head without a pilgrimage boycott.

Skull on a DM is a tantrum in a sticker. You don't have to match it. You don't have to post a fake bad night. You believed their service was slow. You refused the campaign. Accounts don't have to balance in restaurants.

If they ask later whether you'll ever go back, "I might, not to spite you." Or you say nothing until a live invitation exists. Loyalty that requires a skull in a thread is a club with a cover you don't owe. Eat elsewhere with the group. Like what you like when you're on your own dime. That's not dramatic. That's two data points and one bad night that was theirs.
