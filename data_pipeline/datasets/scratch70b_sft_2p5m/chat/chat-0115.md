### User
Weekend was a disaster. Dishes in the sink, trash full, bathroom looking like a sports locker. My roommate sent a paragraph with words like "bandwidth," "labor equity," "mental load," and "I need you to hold yourself accountable to our shared ecosystem." I am sixteen and I do my own laundry. I can tell they are mad. I cannot tell what they want me to do by Tuesday. Translate that into normal speech and help me answer without sounding like a jerk or a corporate Slack bot.

### Assistant
Plain speech first. They are saying: the house is gross, they think they did more of the cleanup thinking than you did, they are tired, and they want you to do specific jobs without being asked every time. "Bandwidth" means they are out of energy. "Labor equity" means they want the work split more evenly. "Mental load" means remembering the trash day and the soap, not just washing a plate when someone points. "Shared ecosystem" is just "our apartment." "Hold yourself accountable" means "do it without a reminder, please."

They are mad about the weekend pile, and also about feeling like the manager. Those are two different chores: the mess now, and who notices next time.

Do not answer with their vocabulary. Answer with a list and a time.

### User
Okay so what do I actually text back. If I say "sorry you feel that way" they will explode. If I say "you didn't assign me anything" I sound like a lawyer. There were three of us here Friday. I left Sunday morning for a game.

### Assistant
Text like a person who heard the point.

Something in this shape: "You're right the place is a mess. I left Sunday and I didn't handle dishes or trash before I went. I can do kitchen and take the trash out tonight when I'm back. Bathroom I can do tomorrow after school. If you want a standing split, tell me which two jobs are mine on weekdays so I'm not guessing."

That admits the weekend without a fake therapy paragraph. It puts two concrete jobs on a clock. It asks them to name the repeating jobs instead of sending another essay.

Do not litigate Friday's three-person math in the first text. You can say later, in person: "When three of us make a mess, I need us to divide it before people leave, not after." That is fair. Leading with it sounds like you are dodging the sink.

"Sorry you feel that way" is a shrug. Skip it. "I dropped the ball on Sunday" is the real apology if you did.

### User
They also said I have "task initiation issues" and maybe I should "time-box a reset ritual." I do not want a diagnosis from my roommate. But the kitchen does sit there until it smells.

### Assistant
More translation. "Task initiation issues" is a fancy way to say you do not start until it is bad. They are not your clinician. You do not have to accept the label. You can still take the useful bit: the kitchen waits because starting is the hard part.

"Time-box a reset ritual" means "set a short timer and do a quick clean on a schedule." In human: "Fifteen minutes after dinner, dishes and counters, then stop." You can agree to the timer without agreeing you have a disorder.

Reply to that part with: "I'm not going to diagnose myself over text. I will do a fifteen-minute kitchen reset on nights I'm home. Trash is mine on pickup day. If something else is sitting, text me the thing, not the essay."

If they want a chart on the fridge, that is fine. A chart is clearer than "ecosystem." Two columns. Your name, their name. Jobs that exist every week, not vibes.

You are not a jerk for wanting plain instructions. They are not a villain for being tired of nagging. The weekend still needs the sink emptied tonight. Talk in jobs and times. Leave the jargon on the floor with the towels.
