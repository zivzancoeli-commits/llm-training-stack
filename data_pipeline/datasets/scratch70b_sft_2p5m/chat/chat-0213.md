### User
I have been in this waiting room for fifty minutes past my appointment time. The TV is on a cooking show with no sound. I already asked the desk once and they said "he's running behind." I am close to knowing what to do — stay, reschedule, or leave a note — but I keep almost deciding and then sitting back down. Talk me through it. I do not want a rant about the health system. I just want to not miss the rest of my day.

### Assistant
Before I pile on a plan: is this a visit you cannot easily redo this month — something already hard to book — or is it a routine follow-up you could move without much pain?

### User
Routine follow-up. Blood pressure check and "how are the meds." I could theoretically come back in two weeks. I took a half day off work to be here. Parking is already paid. I do not know if they will charge me if I walk out. I also do not know how behind "behind" is. The waiting room still has four people who arrived after me.

### Assistant
Routine plus a half day is a real cost, even if the visit itself is ordinary. I will not invent whether they charge a no-show if you leave. That is a desk question, not a guess. You are allowed to walk back up and ask two things only: about how much longer, and if I need to leave, do I owe anything for today. Then you decide with numbers, not with the cooking show.

You almost have it. Stay if they give a time you can live with — say twenty more minutes — and you still want the check done today so work is not twice interrupted. Leave if they cannot give a window, or the window eats the rest of the half day. Reschedule at the desk before you walk, so you are not playing phone tag from the car.

The four people after you does not mean you are skipping. It means the delay is real. Do not use them as a reason to stew. Use them as a reason to ask the time question once, clearly.

If you stay, set a timer on your phone for the window they named. When it rings, you ask again. Sitting without a timer is how fifty becomes ninety. You already asked once. The second ask is not nagging if the first answer had no clock in it.

Parking being paid is a sunk thing. Do not stay to "get your money's worth" from a stall. The stall does not care.

### User
If they say they do not know how long, I freeze. I do not want to be the difficult patient. My boss thinks I will be back by 2:30. It is 1:50 now. Travel is twenty minutes if traffic is kind.

### Assistant
If they do not know, that is an answer. Translate it: they cannot protect your 2:30. You say I have to leave by 2:10 to get back to work. Can I be seen in that window, or should we reschedule. You are not slamming a clipboard. You are giving them a clock they can use. Difficult is yelling. A deadline is information.

If they say they will squeeze you in, believe the squeeze only if someone writes you into the next slot out loud. If they say have a seat and we will see, that is the same fog you already sat in. Fog plus a 2:30 boss is a reschedule.

I still will not invent a fee. Ask. If they look annoyed, stay polite and repeat the two questions. You can even say I took a half day; I am not trying to be a problem. Then stop. Extra apology is how you sit another half hour to prove you are nice.

2:10 leave time is the math if travel is twenty and you want a sliver of margin. If traffic is not kind, you already know that. I will not pretend I know your highway. Build the buffer you actually need. If 2:10 is too tight, name 2:00 at the desk instead.

You were close: stay with a named window, or reschedule and go. The missing piece was asking how long before choosing. You have that now. Walk to the desk, clock in your sentence, then sit or leave on purpose. The cooking show can keep going without you.
