### User
My friend asked me to go with them to a funeral on Saturday. It is their former coworker. I never met the person. My friend does not want to walk in alone. I have never been a plus-one to a funeral. I don't want to be a tourist in someone else's grief. I also don't want to leave my friend in the parking lot. They texted "please" twice. I need to know what the job actually is if I say yes, and how to say no if I can't do it.

### Assistant
The job, if you go, is logistics and quiet. Drive or ride, sit, stand when others stand, keep your phone away, let your friend do the greeting line or skip it, get them water, get them out. You are not there to eulogize a stranger. You are there so your friend is not a solitary body in a row.

You can say yes to the service and no to a reception. You can say yes to the parking lot and the door, then wait in the car if churches wreck you. Name the shape.

If you cannot go, say it soon: "I can't do the funeral. I can sit with you Friday night or after, or I can drive you and not go in." A clear no with an offer is better than a maybe that becomes a no on Saturday morning.

I will not invent dress codes or cemetery rules. Ask your friend what they were told. Black is common. Comfortable shoes matter more than a costume.

Do you freeze in ceremonies, or is the issue only that you didn't know the person?

### User
I can sit still. I just feel fake. My friend cries easily and I am the one who hands tissues in movies. If I go I think I'll become staff. If I don't go I think I'll replay the please texts. I work until 3 on Saturday. The service is at 4:30 across town.

### Assistant
3 to 4:30 across town is tight. Only say yes if the travel is actually possible with a buffer. Funerals do not wait for a bus. If you would arrive in a sweat at the last hymn, that is a gift to nobody. Then the honest no is also a kindness.

If you go, you are allowed to be staff-adjacent. Tissues, a bottle of water in a bag, a charger, a pen if they have to sign a book. That is not fake. That is useful. Fake would be claiming you knew the coworker. You can say, if asked, "I'm here with Jordan." Then you stop talking.

Sit where they sit. Do not network. Do not take photos. If there is a slideshow, look at it. If you feel blank, blank is fine. Grief is not a performance you have to match.

Afterward, ask one question: "Do you want food, or do you want to go home?" Then do that. Do not debrief their character in the car unless they start.

### User
I'll go if I can leave work at 3 on the dot. What do I wear that isn't a costume, and what do I say to their family if they thank me?

### Assistant
Wear the darkest ordinary clothes you already own that are clean: trousers, a sweater, shoes you can stand in. You do not need to buy a funeral outfit this week. I will not name a store or a price.

If family thanks you, "I wanted to be here with them" is enough. Do not add a speech about the deceased. Do not say "they're in a better place" unless that is your actual mouth and their actual faith. Simple beats poetry.

Text your friend tonight: yes, you'll meet them where, you'll leave at 3, you'll stay through the service. If the reception is extra, say so now. Confirm the address. Set two alarms. Go be a quiet body in a chair. That is a real plus-one.
