### User
My cousin is pregnant. She texted a list of twelve names and said "you're good with words, pick." I have not met this baby because the baby is not born. I don't know if this is a genuine ask or a trap where every name I like is secretly the one her partner hates. I also don't want to name a human I will see at holidays. Help me participate without becoming the naming committee.

### Assistant
You can play lightly and refuse the throne.

"I can tell you which ones are easy to say. I shouldn't pick the name." That's participation. Committee is "this is the one."

If you want to be useful: sort her list by sound, nicknames, and whether two names on the list sound like the same person in a noisy room. That's craft. That's not destiny.

I will not invent a cultural or legal naming rule. If they have family customs, they already know. You don't import one.

Trap risk: don't campaign. Don't say "if it were mine." It isn't. If she pushes "just choose," you choose a maybe and label it a maybe. "Of these, I like Mara as a sound. You should ignore me if you don't."

Has she already told other people you're "helping name the baby," like it's a job title?

### User
She told our group chat "the writer cousin is on naming duty." So if I duck, I look precious. If I pick, I'm on the hook when the uncle hates it. Partner hasn't said a word in the thread. That's the quiet that scares me.

### Assistant
Correct the job title without a tantrum. In the thread or in a DM: "I can react to the list. I'm not on duty. You two pick." Public is better if the title is public. One sentence. Precious is a story people tell when you won't be entertainment. Let them.

Partner silence: don't fill it. Ask her, privately, "Does [partner] want crowd input?" If the answer is no, your only job is to stop the group chat from becoming a vote. If she doesn't know, that's a couple problem. Not yours to solve with a favorite.

If uncle hates the eventual name, that's uncle. You can pre-empt your own future: "Whatever you pick is yours. I'll practice saying it." That's loyalty to the child, not to your taste.

Writer cousin can still like words. You can say two names sound good and one is a mouthful. Mouthful is allowed as feedback. Mandate is not.

### User
She sent a second list and "okay but seriously your top three ranked." Ranked is worse.

### Assistant
Don't rank. Ranking is a vote tally she'll screenshot.

"No ranking. I like the sound of A and B. C is harder to shout across a park. You and [partner] decide." If she insists, repeat no ranking. Twice is enough. A third is you muting the thread for the evening.

You have not met the baby. You don't need to. Names are theirs. You can be good with words and still not chair the committee. Group-chat duty is a costume. Take it off in one message, then react like a cousin, not like a consultant.

### User
I said no ranking in the thread. She DMed "you're no fun, just pick so I can tell him it was you if he hates it." There it is. The trap had a lid.

### Assistant
There it is. Don't pick so she can hand your name to a partner as a shield.

"I'm not going to be the reason he hates a name. You two choose. I can still say what sounds good without a vote." If she tells the group you're ducking duty, you already corrected duty. Fun is not a committee chair.

Writer cousin can like words and still refuse a scapegoat ranking. Baby you have not met does not need your top three. They need two adults who will say the name in a kitchen at 3 a.m. without blaming a cousin. Lid off. No ranking. Sounds only. That's participation without the throne.
