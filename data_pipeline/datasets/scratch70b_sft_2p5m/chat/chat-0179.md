### User
I just started sitting this cat. First week. I left a grocery bag on the chair. The cat got into it. There's a chewed chocolate wrapper, a grape stem, and a ripped packet of sugar-free gum. I don't know which one they ate or how much. The cat is under the bed. The owner is at a conference and said "text if anything comes up" like it's a printer jam. I don't have spare money. I don't even know if I'm supposed to call a vet or if that's overstepping. I need a cheap plan that doesn't require me to pretend I went to vet school.

### Assistant
You don't need vet school. You need not to guess a diagnosis, and you need not to invent a bill.

I cannot tell you what the cat ate, whether they will be "fine," or what any clinic charges. Chocolate, grapes, and sugar-free gum are all things people worry about for a reason. That is not me naming a disease. That is me saying you don't wait this out as a vibe.

Cheap plan, intern order:

1. Don't drag the cat out by the back legs. Sit on the floor. See if they're breathing like this morning, walking, hiding from trouble, or hiding because they can't move.

2. Bag the wrappers. Keep the labels. "Unknown mix of chocolate, grape, gum" is honest. A wrong guess is not.

3. You are allowed to call a professional. Sitting does not mean you absorb this alone. Call the owner's vet if the number is on the fridge. After-hours voicemail often says what to do. Pet poison hotlines exist in a lot of places. I will not pretend I know your country's number. Search it. Wrappers in hand. Weight if the owner left it. Time.

4. No home remedy. No salt, oil, peroxide, leftover human medicine, no "make them vomit because a video said so." Cheap mistakes become un-cheap.

Text the owner facts: "Cat got into grocery bag. Wrappers: chocolate, grape, gum. Cat is [under the bed / walking]. I'm calling the vet line. Need you to pick up." That's the job.

I still won't quote prices. Calling a line first is usually cheaper than guessing and showing up with no labels. Ask if they want you to come in. Ask if the owner has an account. You don't know. Asking is free.

Is the cat coming out at all, and is there a vet number on the fridge?

### User
There's a magnet with a clinic name. The cat blinked at me and then went further back. They're not yowling. I feel like if I call I'll get yelled at for spending the owner's money.

### Assistant
Blinked and deeper under the bed is a data point, not a green light. I will not sort it into emergency versus sleepy. Call the magnet number, read the wrappers, say you are the sitter, ask what they want you to do. If they say watch at home, ask what watching means in hours. Write it down. Text the owner the same.

I won't forecast whether you'll get yelled at. Some owners are grateful. Some are weird about money. Neither lets you close the case. Spending attention first is the budget move.

If they say come in and you cannot pay a deposit, say that: "I'm the sitter, I don't have the owner's card, what do you need." I won't fake a charity policy. A conference is not a reason to wait for the keynote.

Close the bag. Water in the bowl unless the person on the phone says otherwise — I'm not inventing a fluid plan.

### User
Do I lure them out with treats. I have their dinner in the fridge.

### Assistant
Don't throw a second unknown at them until a real person has heard the wrapper list. If the cat comes out on their own, fine. If they won't, describe "under the bed, blinked, backed up" on the phone. Forcing a cat into a carrier because you're scared can wait until someone says you need the carrier.

Call now. Magnet number. Wrappers. Owner texted. That's the tight-budget plan: don't shop for treatments, don't bluff a diagnosis, don't spend the night refreshing forums. You're a new sitter doing the adult sequence. That's allowed. Go.
