### User
My friends want me to go on a weekend trip. I don't want to. I also don't want to invent a dead grandma. How do I just... not go. People keep saying "set a boundary" and I don't know what that actually sounds like out loud.

### Assistant
A boundary here is just a true sentence about your limit. Not a speech. Not a medical note. "I'm not going this weekend" is the whole thing. The rest is you not taking it back.

You don't owe a fake reason, and you don't have to dump your inner life. "I don't want to" is allowed. If that feels too blunt, add one true extra: money, rest, work, or not wanting to travel. One extra. Three extras sound like a cover story.

Who asked, and how close are they? That changes wording, not honesty.

### User
It's my friend Maya. Group chat. Four people, cabin two hours away. She already sent a split for groceries. I haven't paid. I also haven't said yes. I just reacted with a thumbs-up to the cabin photos like an idiot.

### Assistant
A thumbs-up on photos is not a yes. People treat it like one because it is easy. You can still say no. Do it soon, before someone books more stuff in your name.

Plain version: you are not "ghosting." Ghosting is disappearing after they reasonably thought you were in. You never confirmed. You are late, not vanished. Late still needs a message today.

Don't open with "I'm the worst." That makes them comfort you on top of the no. Try:

"Hey — I'm not coming this weekend. I should have said that instead of just liking the photos. I'm out on the groceries split too, so don't count me."

If you want one true extra: "I need a quiet weekend at home" or "I can't spend the money right now." Pick the one you will repeat if they push. Repeating is not rude. Changing the reason three times is what smells like a lie.

### User
She's going to say "come on, you never come." That part is kind of true. I skip a lot. I don't want to promise the next one either.

### Assistant
Then don't promise the next one. "I'll come next time" is a future lie if you already know you might skip that too.

If she says you never come, agree with the fact without signing up. "Yeah, I've skipped a lot lately. I'm still not coming this weekend." That is not a debate about whether you are a good friend. It is one question: cabin, this weekend, you. Answer is no.

"You never come" works if you start defending your personality. Skip the defense. You can like Maya and still not go.

If she asks why, give the same true extra once. If she asks again: "I already told you. I'm not changing it." That feels mean in your head. Out loud it usually sounds like an adult who finished a thought.

Do this in the group chat, not a private side thread, unless the split was only between you and her. Everyone planning food and beds needs the same information.

### User
What if the others pile on with "just for Friday night" or "you can leave Saturday morning"? I fold when people shrink the ask.

### Assistant
That's called moving the goalposts. They shrink the trip so your no looks dramatic. Treat the new ask as a new trip, and still say no if you still don't want it.

Script: "Friday night still isn't a yes for me. I'm staying home the whole weekend." If they shrink it again: "Same answer." You are not negotiating a discount.

Folding happens when you argue details. Don't discuss drive time, sleeping bags, or whether you'll "regret it." Those are their talking points. Yours is one decision, already made.

Send it before you over-rehearse. If your hands shake, write it in notes, check that every sentence is true, then paste it. True and short beats polished and fake.

After you send it, mute the thread for an hour if you need to. Check back later for logistics only: the split, a key, that kind of thing. Not for a score on whether they still like you.

Liking you and wanting you on the trip are not the same. You can stay friends and still spend Saturday on your couch. That's the whole trick, without the workshop words.
