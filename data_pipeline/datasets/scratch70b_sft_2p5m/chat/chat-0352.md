### User
I'm at the gym. The person before me left the bench shiny with sweat. The spray bottle is empty. The paper towels are across the room. They are still standing there, scrolling, earbuds in. I have used this bench after them before and just wiped it myself. Today I don't want to. I also don't want a confrontation in tank tops. Staff is at the front, not on the floor. What do I say, or do I just get the towels?

### Assistant
You can do both: get a towel if you need the bench now, and still use a sentence if they are right there.

They are standing there. That makes it easier, not harder. A tap on the air, not on their body, and a point at the bench: "Hey, can you wipe that down?" If the earbuds are in, a wave, then the same line. You do not need a policy lecture. The gym already printed the policy on the wall.

If they do not hear you, you still can wipe it. Your workout does not have to wait for a moral victory. Wiping it yourself is not a failure. It is hygiene. The sentence is for the pattern.

Do not spray them. Do not photograph them. Do not perform it for the room.

Empty bottle is a staff problem. After this set, tell the front: "Floor spray is empty by the benches." That is a useful sentence. It does not require a villain.

Are they between you and the bench, or can you work a different one for five minutes?

### User
They're between me and this bench. The other benches are taken. I have twelve minutes before I have to leave for work. They just sat on a different machine still looking at this bench like they might come back.

### Assistant
Twelve minutes: you need the bench or you need a different exercise that uses a free thing. Do not stand in a queue of resentment.

If they parked their towel on your bench as a claim, you can say, "Are you still using this? I need it if not." Claims with no body on the equipment are the gym's favorite gray zone. A question pops it.

If they say they are coming back, you can wait one minute, not twelve. Then take it, wipe it, do your set. Coming back from a phone is not a reservation.

If you still want the wipe sentence: "It's wet — please wipe when you're done." Then you wipe if they don't, because work is in twelve minutes. Pride that makes you late is a bad trade.

Front desk after, if you pass it: empty spray. If this person is a regular who never wipes, staff can be told "the bench by the mirrors keeps getting left wet" without a name if you do not want a name. Names are for repeats you can identify. Today might be a one-off.

### User
I said "can you wipe that" and they pulled one earbud out and said "that's the gym, man." They're still not wiping.

### Assistant
"That's the gym" is a shrug, not a rule. You do not have to debate culture.

Get the paper towels, wipe the bench, do the set, leave on time. If you have a second in the doorway, "Spray's empty" to staff. You already asked. They declined. Your remaining job is the bench and the clock.

If you see them next session, you can ask once more or you can pick a different circuit. You are not required to become the wipe enforcer of 6 a.m. You are required not to lie down in someone else's sweat if you can avoid it. You asked. That was the hard part. Towel, set, work. Let "that's the gym" stay their slogan. It does not have to be yours. Twelve minutes is enough for the set if you stop negotiating. Start the clock.
