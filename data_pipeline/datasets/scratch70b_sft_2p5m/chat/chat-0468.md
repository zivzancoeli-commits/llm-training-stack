### User
I found my student loan envelope on the kitchen table, opened, sitting on top of the takeout menus. The flap was tucked back in like that makes it polite. Sam said they "thought it was theirs, same window envelope, oops." My name is on it. They already know the number-ish because they "didn't want to just leave it." I feel sick. I don't want a roommate trial. I want the mail to be mine. They're making pasta. I have the envelope in my hand.

### Assistant
The oops is the story. The opened envelope is the fact.

"This has my name on it. Don't open my mail. If it's not yours, leave it in the pile or put it on my door closed." That's the rule. If they start the same-window speech, you don't need a visual analysis of envelopes. "Name is enough. Don't open it."

I will not invent mail law. I won't tell you this is a federal case. You can still not want them to read a loan letter. You don't have to quote a statute to have a kitchen.

Take the letter to your room. Don't leave it as a prop on the table while you argue. The pasta can wait thirty seconds.

If they "know the number-ish," you don't have to interrogate how much they saw. "Please don't repeat anything you saw. It's not house business." Then you stop. A trial is how they learn more.

Do you have a slot or a box that's only yours, or is all mail a shared pile?

### User
Shared pile on the table. They offered to "help me budget" now that they "sort of saw it." That's worse than the flap. I haven't said the rule yet. Water is boiling.

### Assistant
Turn the burner down if you're going to talk, or talk while they stir. Don't let boiling be a reason to delay.

"I'm not doing a budget with you from my mail. Don't open my stuff. I'll get a hanging file / I'll pick up my pile every day." If you can put a cheap file on your door, do that this week. Shared pile plus curiosity is how oops happens.

Help with budget can be a kind offer in another universe. Here it's a prize they took from a letter. "No thanks" is complete. You don't have to explain the loan. Don't confirm the number. Don't correct their "sort of." Extra numbers are how it becomes house lore.

If they get defensive — "I was trying to be helpful" — "Help is leaving it closed." Then you walk with the envelope.

### User
They said they won't do it again and asked if I'm "mad or just private." I hate that quiz. Pasta is almost done. Do I eat with them tonight?

### Assistant
You can eat. You don't have to take the quiz. "I'm not okay with opened mail. I'm private about money. Let's eat." If eating feels like a reward, you can take the bowl to your room without a speech. That's allowed. It's not a sitcom if you need a door.

Next week: your mail off the table, file on the door, repeat once if a flap appears again. Then it's a landlord/mailbox conversation if you have a real box. Tonight: sentence, letter in your room, pasta or not. You don't need a trial. You need a closed envelope. Get it off the menus. That's the whole accidental, which wasn't that accidental once they offered to budget. Keep the number in the paper, not in the kitchen.

If they bring it up at dinner as a joke, you don't have to laugh. "Still not house business." Then you pass the salt. The envelope can live in a drawer. The pasta can be dinner. Those can share a night without a budget workshop. Tomorrow you pick up the pile before they get home if that helps your stomach. That's a habit, not a war.
