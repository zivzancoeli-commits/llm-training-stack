### User
We were four people. The bill came and Maya dumped it in the middle like, "just split even." I ordered the cheapest bowl and water. She got an appetizer, a cocktail, and dessert that nobody else touched. I paid my quarter anyway because I froze. Now I'm walking home mad. How do I not do this again without sounding like a cheap teenager?

### Assistant
You're not cheap. You paid for food you didn't eat. That's a different problem than being stingy.

Next time, the kind version is specific, not a speech. When menus hit the table, one sentence: "I'm doing even split only if we're ordering in the same range. If someone adds extras, those extras stay on that person." Then stop. You don't need a thesis.

If you missed that window — which you did tonight — you still have a second one when the check lands. You look at it. You name the extras out loud, calmly. "Maya, the app, drink, and dessert are yours. The rest we can split four ways." You are describing the bill, not accusing her character.

Keep the check as the object, not her. Naming the extra plates is softer than "you always do this." Kind and specific can live in the same sentence.

Want a line you can actually say next time, or a text for tonight so this doesn't rot?

### User
Both. I don't want to text something that starts a fight. And I want the table line to sound like a person, not a spreadsheet.

### Assistant
Tonight's text, if you send one at all: "Hey — I should've said this at the table. I was on the cheap bowl and water. Splitting the extras even didn't feel fair. Next time I'll call it out when the check comes so nobody's guessing." That's it. No emoji pile. You already paid; you're not collecting. You're closing the loop so you don't stew.

If she replies "it was only a few dollars," you don't litigate the dollars. You say, "Yeah, and I'd rather split the shared stuff and keep extras on whoever ordered them. Easier for me." That's kind because it's about your preference, not her math skills.

Table line, spoken, not performed: "I'm going to split the shared food even. Drinks and extra plates stay with whoever got them, cool?"

If the group is noisy, say it to the person next to you first, then to the table. Repeating it once is fine. Repeating it four times is a lecture.

If someone says "just make it easy," answer, "Even split of the pizza is easy. The cocktail on my bowl isn't." That's specific enough that a sharp fourteen-year-old can follow it. An adult can too.

Ask for separate checks when you sit down if the place does that. It's a setting, like extra napkins. If they can't, use two piles: shared, personal. Don't silently Venmo a smaller amount later with a paragraph. That feels like a trap. Say it at the table or send the short text. Pick one.

### User
What if Maya gets quiet and everyone stares at me?

### Assistant
Then you already did the hard part. Silence is not a verdict that you were cruel.

Keep normal volume. Don't fill the gap with "sorry, I'm being weird, forget it." That trains people to steamroll you.

A kind fill, if you need one: "I'm not mad. I just don't want to guess." Then look at the check again so everyone has a place to put their eyes. People stare when they don't know the next move. You give them numbers on paper.

If Maya says it wasn't a big deal, you can agree with the size and still keep the rule. "It's not huge. Still want extras on the person who ordered them." Small preferences are how bills stay boring.

If someone offers to cover her dessert, say thanks — and do not also pay a quarter of that dessert. Let the volunteer actually volunteer.

If staring still feels awful, put a card down for only your share and say, "I'll take mine on this one." Some places will split that way if you ask the server. That's their job, not a scene.

After you leave, skip the group-chat recap. Let it be a two-minute moment. You're allowed to like your friends and still not subsidize a cocktail.

### User

### Assistant
That's the whole system. One spoken rule, one backup text, no novel. Practice the line once on the walk home so it isn't the first time your mouth hears it. Kind, specific, then done.
