### User
I came home and my bed was made with the wrong corners. Riley used my sheets because theirs were in the dryer they forgot to run. They said "it's just cotton" and that they would wash mine. I don't want anyone in my bed when I'm not there. I feel dramatic. How do I say that's not okay without a linen TED talk?

### Assistant
Your bed is not a spare linen closet. Cotton is not the point. Consent is the point. They slept in your stack, or at least remade it. You get to say no. You do not need a philosophy of fabric. You need your pillow to have only been your pillow.

Say: "Don't use my sheets or my bed. If yours are wet, use a spare, the couch, or wait." If they already used them, "Wash them, put them back, and don't do that again." I will not invent a laundry fee. Wash means the way you actually wash sheets, not a spritz and a folded apology.

You are not dramatic. People who like a private bed are ordinary. The wrong corners are the tell. Believe the corners.

Do they have a spare set that they just will not buy, or truly only one set?

### User
One set, they admit it. They thought sharing was "house energy." We are not a couple. We are two names on a lease. I already stripped the bed and it's in my hamper like evidence.

### Assistant
One set is their planning problem. House energy is a slogan for using your mattress. Text or kitchen, tonight: "We are not sharing beds. Buy a second set this week. Mine stay in my room. If the dryer is full, ask before you touch my stuff — and the answer on the bed is no." Ask-before is for a towel maybe. It is not a back door into the mattress. If they look confused, you can point at the stripped bed. The hamper is already the visual aid.

If they offer to "just sleep in it once more until Friday," no. Once more is how this becomes a policy. Couch, floor pad, a friend. Not your fitted sheet. They can run the dryer tonight. Dryers exist after sunset.

Stripping the bed was correct. Wash them yourself if you do not trust their "I'll wash yours," and tell them that: "I'll wash my own. Don't put them in your pile." Trust is low tonight. That is allowed. You can still be polite in the hallway.

Buy nothing for them. Their second set is their errand.

### User
They left a sticky on the washer: "sorry king, house energy lol." I want to peel it and also I don't want a war of notes.

### Assistant
Peel it. Notes are how they keep the bit alive. You already need a spoken or texted no, not a comedy fridge. If you reply on a sticky, you are in their genre. Genre is how "lol" eats a boundary.

Send the text. Buy-your-own-set, not my bed, I washed mine. If they sulk, they can sulk on the couch with a blanket they own. If they start the dryer now, that is the useful sorry. The sticky was not. If they use your bed again, that is not a joke, and you say so once more, then you look at whether a lock on your door is in your lease. I will not invent that it is allowed. Read the lease before you drill. A wedge alarm is a smaller step some people use. I will not prescribe hardware. I will prescribe the sentence.

Tonight: peel, text, your sheets in your hamper, their dryer actually run. House energy can mean taking out the trash. It does not mean your pillow. Wrong corners were enough evidence. You already saw it. Keep the bed yours. Let cotton be cotton in their own dryer.
