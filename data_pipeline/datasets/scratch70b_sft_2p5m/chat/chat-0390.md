### User
My friend Nia told me something about my partner and then said "please don't tell them I told you." It isn't a crime. It's a plan my partner hasn't mentioned to me: looking at jobs in another city. Nia thought I already knew. I didn't. Now I am holding a secret that is about my life. I have to see my partner tonight. I don't want to blow up Nia. I also don't want to pretend I don't know.

### Assistant
You can refuse a secret that is about your own household. A friend does not get to park information in you and then lock the door.

You do not have to open with Nia's name. You can open with the content, if you choose to talk tonight: "I heard you might be looking at jobs in another city. Is that true? I want to hear it from you." That puts the conversation where it belongs.

If you do not want to lie about the source, you can say you heard it from a friend and you are not going to play detective. You do not need to dump Nia in the first sentence unless you are asked and you decide to answer.

I will not tell you what the job search "means" for the relationship. I do not know. I will tell you that sitting on it for weeks usually makes the next talk worse.

Are you more angry at Nia for telling, or at the idea that a move was in motion without you?

### User
Both. More the move. We rent together. I asked Nia not to tell me more. She is spiraling that I'll "ruin the friendship." My partner will be home in an hour. I keep rehearsing a courtroom.

### Assistant
Drop the courtroom. You are not cross-examining. You are asking whether a rumor is a plan.

In the next hour, pick one opening question. Not five. Eat something if you have not. If you need a walk around the block first, take it. Do not start the talk in the doorway with shoes still on unless that is the only time you get.

If they say it is true, you can ask what stage it is: looking, applying, already interviewing. You can say you needed to hear it from them. You can say you are scared. You do not have to solve the city tonight.

If they say it is false, you can say you are relieved and that you still want a habit of hearing big logistics from each other, not from friends. Then you stop prosecuting.

Nia: a short text after your talk, or tomorrow. "I'm going to talk to them about the job thing. I won't make you the villain on purpose. I can't keep household secrets." She may still spiral. That is hers. You did not ruin a friendship by refusing to be a vault for your own lease.

### User
What if they ask who told me and I freeze? I don't want to lie. I also don't want Nia getting a midnight call.

### Assistant
If they ask, you can say Nia's name without a character review. "Nia mentioned it. She thought I knew." Then you steer back: "I care about the plan, not the messenger." If they reach for the phone, you can say, "Please don't call her tonight. Talk to me." You cannot control their thumbs. You can state the request.

Do not invent a fake coworker as the source. Fake sources collapse. Silence is better than a story.

Tonight: one question, listen, say you want to be in the planning. Tomorrow: Nia gets a calm text, not a play-by-play. You are allowed to love both people and still refuse the triangle. Go home. Ask. Listen. That is the whole first night.

### User
They're parking. I can hear the engine. I still want the one question, not a list.

### Assistant
One question. "I heard you might be looking at jobs in another city. Is that true?" Then you listen. If you need a glass of water first, take thirty seconds. Do not start with Nia's name. Do not start with the lease. Start with the rumor and let them fill it. You can love them and still need the plan in the room. Go inside. Ask. Stop stacking courtroom exhibits on the welcome mat.
