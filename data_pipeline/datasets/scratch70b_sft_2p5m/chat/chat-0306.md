### User
The shelf tag says one price. The scanner at self-checkout says higher. There's a big poster by the door with the shelf number. I took a photo of the tag. A guy behind me sighed. I don't want to make a scene and I also don't want to pay the higher number if the store already printed the lower one. I've never called anyone over for this.

### Assistant
This is a normal, boring problem. You are not a Karen for a mismatched tag. You are also not in a courtroom.

Finish the rest of the items or pause the order if the machine lets you. Hit the help button or walk to a staffed register with the item and the photo. One sentence: "The shelf tag says this, the scanner says that. Can you honor the tag?" Then let them look.

I will not tell you they must. Stores have policies, and I do not have this store's. Some match, some recount, some say the system wins. Asking is still the move. The poster is supporting evidence, not a gavel.

Do not lecture the line. Do not film the clerk's face.

Is this one item or a whole cart of tagged things, and is the difference big enough that you would put the item back?

### User
One item. The difference is enough that I'd put it back, not enough that I want a fight. The self-checkout is yelling "help is on the way" in a voice. I feel hot in the face.

### Assistant
Put-back is your floor, which is good. You already know you will not die on this. Help-is-on-the-way is theater. Breathe. You can smile at the person behind you with a "sorry, tag issue" if you need to, then stop talking to the line.

When staff arrives, show the photo first, then the item, then the scanner number. Order matters: evidence, object, conflict. If they honor it, say thanks and finish. If they need a manager, wait if the wait is short. If they need a manager and the line is melting down, you can say, "I can take it to customer service after I pay the rest," or you can pay for everything else, void this item, and walk to the desk. I do not know which buttons they have. They do.

If they refuse, put it back or pay the scanner price with your eyes open. Do not steal. Do not argue about "false advertising" as a legal term you are not going to litigate in aisle 4. You can email the store later with photos if you still care. Tonight you have rice to get home.

If they honor it but make a joke at your expense, take the price and leave the joke. You came for the number.

### User
What if the tag was old and they say I should have known. I did not know.

### Assistant
You cannot know a hidden old tag. They can still choose to honor or not. "I went by what was under the item" is enough. If they still refuse, your floor is put-back. You do not need to win a philosophy debate about knowing.

For the next ten minutes: stay with the machine or go to a human, show the photo, ask once, accept honor or refuse, and either finish the checkout or set the item on a reshop shelf. Then leave. Do not stand in the lot rewriting the conversation.

A shelf tag and a scanner disagreed. That happens. You asked like a person with a photo. That is the whole skill. The sigh behind you is not a review of your character. If your face is still hot after, walk the long way to the car and do not replay the clerk's tone. Pay or walk. Eat dinner. You did not make a scene; you asked a price question once, with evidence, and then you left.
