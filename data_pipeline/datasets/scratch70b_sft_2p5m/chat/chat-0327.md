### User
The dishwasher died in the middle of holiday cooking. It beeps, sits with a puddle, and will not drain. There are already two sheet pans, a roasting pan, and every mixing bowl I own. People arrive in four hours. Someone said "just run it anyway." I am not running it anyway. I feel like the day is now about dishes.

### Assistant
Do not run it anyway. A machine that will not drain will give you a lake. Holiday cooking can survive a sink. It cannot survive a flood under the kick plate.

Stop loading it. Bail what you can with a cup into a bucket, not onto the floor. If you know how to check a cheap knockout — a loose chunk of food at the bottom filter — you can look once, with the power off, if you already know this model. If you do not know, skip surgery. You have four hours and guests, not a repair class.

Set up a two-basin logic even if you have one sink: wash, rinse, park on towels. Assign a person. You should not be the only pair of hands if other adults are in the house.

I will not diagnose the pump. Beeps plus puddle plus no drain is "stop using it." I will not quote a service fee.

Are there other adults in the house now, and is the puddle still growing?

### User
Puddle is not growing. My sibling is here. They are more useful at chopping. Parents are in transit. I already pulled the bottom rack and stared at sludge. I don't know what I'm looking at.

### Assistant
Push the rack back. You looked. If nothing obvious like a glass shard is sitting on a filter you know how to remove, you are done. Wash the sludge feeling off your hands and go back to food.

Sibling chops; they can also dry. Drying is a job with dignity. Say "you're on towels and stacking" as a fact, not a plea. If they only want to chop, they can chop over a cutting board you will wash in the same water.

Prioritize tools you will use again in the next four hours: knives, the one bowl you mix in, the roasting pan. Ugly serving dishes can wait. Paper plates are allowed if that is how you actually get through a holiday. I will not lecture you about the environment during a dead dishwasher. Use what you have.

If the floor is wet, towels and a fan, not a hair dryer in a puddle. Keep kids and socks off that strip.

A note on the machine so a helpful parent does not "just start it": "Broken, do not run." Tape. Parents in transit love to press buttons.

If you have a dishwasher-safe item you were counting on to be clean, hand-wash it now, not at minute of arrival.

### User
I wanted the kitchen to look like I had it together. A sink full of pans will be the first thing people see.

### Assistant
They will smell food first if food happens. A sink of pans is a holiday. You can park dirty pans in the oven you are not using, or in a plastic tub in a bedroom if you need the sink visual gone. That is not failure. That is staging.

For the next ten minutes: bail the standing water, tape the note, recruit sibling to dry, wash the next two tools you need, and start the thing that actually has a clock — the roast, the potatoes, whatever. Do not search videos of drain pumps until after guests leave.

The dishwasher picked a dramatic day. You still have a sink, a sibling, and four hours. Cook. Stack. Let the beep be background. Together is a feeling people get from being fed, not from a quiet appliance. You can call a repair person next week. Today is pans and a towel.
