### User
I need flowers in the next ten minutes. Grocery store. I don't know rose from not-rose except when it's obvious. Skeptical-uncle energy please — no "follow your heart" and no fake Latin. It's for someone who had a rough week, not a funeral, not a proposal. Walk me through what you would actually do starting now.

### Assistant
I'd walk in and go to the bucket display, not the internet for a quiz.

Next ten minutes, in order.

Minute one: look at the person at the floral wrap station, if there is one. If not, you're on your own with plastic sleeves. That's fine.

Minute two: ignore names. Pick with your eyes and nose. You want color that isn't hospital-white unless they like white, and you want stems that aren't slime-soft. Bend a head gently. If it droops like it's already tired, skip it.

Minute three: decide size. One mixed bunch from the bucket is enough for "rough week." Don't build a showpiece. Uncle version: you're not buying a personality.

I won't invent prices. Look at the sticker. If two bunches blow your number, take one.

Who is this for — partner, friend, parent — and do they have a cat that eats plants? I ask because some common flowers are a problem for cats, and I won't pretend I can see their apartment.

### User
Friend. No cat. They like yellow. There's a bunch labeled something I can't pronounce and a cheaper mixed one that's mostly green with three sad yellow bits.

### Assistant
Yellow is your filter. The cheap mixed with three sad bits is a decoration for the store's leftover problem, not a gift. Skip it.

The unpronounceable bunch: you don't need the name to buy it. You need: yellow you actually like, heads that aren't brown at the edges, stems in water, no rotten smell.

Minute four: take that bunch. If the yellow is only in the unpronounceable one and the heads look alive, that's the one.

Minute five: wrap. If there's a person, say "for a friend, rough week, can you wrap it so it survives a bus." If there's no person, sleeve it yourself, don't crush the heads under your armpit.

Minute six: card. A grocery store card that says "thinking of you" is better than a speech. Write one sentence in your handwriting: "Heard this week was a lot. These are yellow." That's it. Don't write a poem you wouldn't say at their door.

Minute seven: water. If you have a long ride, ask for a wet paper wrap or keep the rubber band and a bit of the bucket water in a bag if they let you. I don't know this store's policy. If they say no, go anyway; flowers die of delay more than of missing Latin.

Minute eight: check for thorns if it's actually roses. Roses are the ones with the obvious flower-shape a kid would draw, often with thorns. If you're grabbing those, don't hug them to your coat.

Minute nine: pay. Don't add a balloon. Balloon says birthday or hospital. You said rough week.

Minute ten: text "coming by with flowers, you home?" if dropping in. If they might not want company, leave them at the door only if that's a building where a bunch won't get stolen in five minutes. I won't guess your city. If the door is risky, wait for the yes.

### User
What if they ask what kind they are and I don't know.

### Assistant
You say, "I don't know the name. I picked yellow ones that didn't look dead." That's honest. Teaching you a pronunciation in the parking lot so you can perform expertise is the opposite of uncle advice.

If you want a name for yourself, photograph the store label before you leave. That's for you, later, not a test at their sink.

Don't claim they mean friendship or healing. Flower-meaning lists are mostly marketing. Yellow is yellow.

Vase: if you know they don't have one, a jar from their kitchen is fine. Don't buy a vase unless you already wanted to spend that extra. I won't quote what a vase "should" cost.

If the only yellow left is wilted, switch color rather than gift a corpse. "I wanted yellow, these were the alive ones" is an acceptable story.

Go. Bucket, alive yellow, one bunch, short card, no balloon, text before you appear. That's ten minutes. You don't need the name to be a decent friend.
