### User
Len from my shift asked me to "just put them down for the full eight" on Friday even though they left at 3:15 for a thing. We use a shared sheet the supervisor barely reads. Len said everyone does it, and that they covered me once when I was late. I was late, once, by nine minutes, and they waved me in. This is not the same. I don't want to be the cop. I also don't want my name next to a fake eight. What do I say, tonight, without a speech about integrity?

### Assistant
You say no to the fake eight. You can be brief.

"I can't put you down for time you weren't here. I can put 3:15, or you can talk to the supervisor." That is the whole speech. "Integrity" does not need to appear. The sheet does.

"Everyone does it" is not a reason to add your initials. Covering a nine-minute late is not a token you redeem for five hours.

If the sheet is shared and your name is on the edit, treat it like your name. If Len can edit their own row, they can do their own fiction without you. Do not volunteer as the typist.

I will not lecture you about labor law. I will say that dummy hours can land on more than Len. You do not need that.

Did they ask you to type it because you are the person who submits the sheet, or because they want a witness?

### User
I submit it. I have the spreadsheet tab open. They are waiting by the printer. If I put 3:15 they'll see it before I send. If I delay sending, the supervisor texts me.

### Assistant
Put 3:15. Send on time. Let them see it.

Waiting by the printer is pressure. You can say the sentence while you type the true time. You do not need to wait until they leave. Delay is how you get talked into an eight.

If they get loud, repeat: "I'm sending actual hours." Then send. If they go to the supervisor and say you are difficult, you have a sheet that matches the door. That is a better story than a matching lie.

You can acknowledge the Friday thing without paying in hours: "Hope the thing went fine." Kindness is allowed. Fake time is not kindness. It is a second job as a forger.

If the supervisor barely reads it, that is not permission. Barely is how these things live until the one week someone reads.

### User
Len said "so you won't even do this after I saved you." They're not whispering.

### Assistant
You were not saved. You were nine minutes late and they didn't make a scene. That is ordinary shift courtesy. It is not a timesheet credit.

"I'm not changing it." If they keep talking, you can walk the paper to the office or hit send and go clock out. You do not owe a public debate by a printer.

If this becomes a campaign — they tell the crew you snitched — you tell the supervisor the hours you submitted were actual, once, if asked. You do not pre-write a manifesto.

Tonight: true time, send, leave. You can still be a decent coworker tomorrow on the floor. Decent is showing up and not asking people to type fiction. Len can be angry at a 3:15. You can live with that anger. You cannot live as easily with your name on an eight that did not happen. Close the tab. Go home.

### User
If the supervisor asks tomorrow why Len's row is short, do I explain the printer conversation?

### Assistant
You explain the hours, not the theater. "Len left at 3:15. I entered 3:15." If they ask whether Len wanted eight, you can say yes, and that you entered actual time. You do not need a dramatic reading of "so you won't even do this." Facts first.

If they never ask, you never volunteer a saga. The sheet already speaks. Tomorrow you work the floor like a person who did not fake a row. That is the quieter kind of decent. It travels farther than bagels and full eights. Send the sheet. Walk past the printer. Let Len's Friday thing be Len's. Your name stays next to what happened. That is the whole integrity speech, and you never had to use the word.
