### User
I have this friend from college. We used to talk about dumb stuff. Now every hang is a status update about their job: the standup, the skip-level, the deck, the "headcount freeze," who got a "scope increase." I am a new intern so I only understand half of it, and the half I understand still makes me feel like I am failing at being a person because I do not have a deck. I do not want to dump them. I also leave every hang tired. How do I fix this without making it a performance review of the friendship?

### Assistant
Before I hand you a script: when you leave tired, is it because they never ask you a single question, or because they do ask and then steer every answer back to the office? Those feel similar in your body and they are different to repair.

### User
They ask "how are you" and I say something about training or my roommate and they nod and then we are back in their org chart. It is like I am a loading screen.

### Assistant
Okay. That is useful. They are not a monster. They are using work as the only language that still feels safe, and they are treating your answers as a pause, not as a topic.

You do not need a framework from a blog. You need one or two hangs where the subject is not their ticket queue.

Do not open with "you only talk about work." That lands like a review. Open with a specific ask for a specific hour. "Can we not do jobs tonight? I want to hear about the apartment hunt / the show you mentioned / whether you still cook that terrible pasta." Name a destination. If you leave it blank, work will fill it. Work is sitting there with slides.

If they start a standup anyway, interrupt like a human. "I lost you at skip-level. Also I wanted to ask about your brother." Then ask the brother question. The redirect has to be a real question, not a sigh.

You can also set a container. Walks are better than bars for this, because walks end. "Forty-five minutes, then I have to get back" is intern-honest and it keeps you from drowning.

If you want to be kind about why they are doing this: a lot of people, once a job eats their week, forget they have other material. You can say that without diagnosing them: "I think work is the only story you have energy for right now, and I miss the other stuff." A feeling plus a fact. Not a verdict on their personality.

What I would not do, especially as someone new in their own workplace: compete. Do not inflate your intern tasks into "deliverables" so you can share a dialect. That will make you more tired. You also should not fake fascination with headcount freezes.

If they cannot leave the office in their mouth after two tries, you still do not have to dump them. Shrink the hang. Coffee, not dinner. Text, not a three-hour catch-up. People are allowed to be boring in a season. You are allowed to protect your Saturday.

A line that is intern-safe and not fake-HR: "I like you, and I am tapped out on work talk after my own week. Catch me up on one work thing if you need to, then I want a non-work thing." One work thing is a pressure valve. Zero can make them feel erased. Unlimited is what you have now.

If they get defensive, that is information. Repeat the ask once. Then talk about pasta. Then go home on time.

You are not failing at being a person because you do not have a deck. They are using a dialect. Dialects can be interrupted. Tired is data. Use it to shorten the meeting, not to write a manifesto about who they used to be.

### User
What if the only non-work thing they have is complaining about the commute?

### Assistant
Then take the commute as a bridge, not as the destination. "The bus is wrecking you — are you sleeping? Are you still going to that Saturday market you used to like?" If they bounce back to the office, you have your answer for that night, and you end on time. You asked. You do not have to keep mining a closed pit.
