### User
Dana from Platform asked to "grab coffee this week." I said sure because I thought it was a normal coffee. Then they sent a calendar invite titled "Quick chat — team fit" with a bland agenda. A friend on their team pinged me: "Dana's trying to poach you, heads up." I like my current team. I also like not being poor at office politics. If I cancel, I look scared. If I go, I might be walking into a pitch I have to refuse in a cafe. What do I do with the invite, and what do I tell my manager?

### Assistant
You can go and still not be poached. Coffee is not a transfer.

Reply to Dana, not the friend, with a clarifying line: "Happy to coffee. I'm not looking to change teams right now — if it's still useful as a catch-up, I'll be there." That lets them cancel if the only point was a pitch. It lets you attend if they still want to talk shop. You have named the boundary before the pastry.

If they say "just keep an open mind," you can still go, listen, and say no at the table. Open mind is a slogan. Your no can be calm: "I'm staying put. I appreciate the interest."

Your manager: you do not have to pre-report a coffee. If Dana's team is known for raids, a short heads-up can be smart: "Dana asked to coffee about team fit. I told them I'm not moving. Flagging so it's not a rumor first." That is grown-up. It is not scared. Scared is hiding and then getting described by someone else.

I will not invent your company's internal mobility rules. If there is a posted process, that process matters more than a cafe.

Do you actually want information about their team, even as a maybe-later, or do you want this coffee gone?

### User
Maybe-later in a year, not now. I don't want my manager to think I'm halfway out the door. The friend wants a play-by-play. Dana already booked a room, not a cafe, even though they said coffee.

### Assistant
A room is a pitch. Treat it like one.

You can move it back to a public coffee or a 20-minute video. "Can we keep this to a coffee place / 20 minutes? I'm not doing a transfer conversation this week." Rooms make people perform. Pastry is harder to turn into a closing meeting.

Tell the friend you do not want a play-by-play. "I'll handle it. Please don't narrate it on our team." Friends with heads-ups sometimes become the rumor. Close that valve.

Manager: the one-line flag is still useful because a room plus "team fit" will leak. You saying "I'm not moving" first is how you don't look halfway out. Silence plus a closed-door with Platform is how stories start.

In the meeting, let them talk. Take the org chart if you want it for a year from now. Do not trash your team to be polite. Do not promise "I'll think about it" if you won't. "Not now; maybe I'll look if a role opens later" is honest enough if true. If you want a cleaner no, use the cleaner no.

### User
Dana replied "the room is just quieter." I still have the invite. My manager is in meetings until 4.

### Assistant
Keep the invite or shrink it. Quieter is a reason. It is also still a room. You can go, sit near the door, and run your pre-sent sentence: not looking, catch-up okay.

Message your manager before 4 if you can drop it in chat: the one flag. Then you are not waiting for a hallway collision.

Do not let the friend sit in a virtual waiting room of gossip. You already asked them not to narrate.

Coffee, room, whatever: you are allowed to like your team and still be polite to Platform. Polite is showing up on time, hearing the pitch, declining, and going back to work. Politics is mostly not lying. You already told Dana you are not looking. Repeat it in the quieter room. Then eat an actual coffee after if you still want one. The poach only works if you say yes. You can say no without being bad at this. You are already doing the part most people skip: naming the thing before the calendar wins.
