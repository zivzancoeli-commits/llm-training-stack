### User
I changed my streaming passwords last month because my bill showed extra devices I didn't recognize. I told the group chat, jokingly, "password refresh, ask me if you still need it." Two people asked. I said no, I'm keeping it to my household now. Today I got a login prompt on my TV: someone in another city is trying to sign in. The city is where Devon lives. Devon did not ask. Devon used to have the old password on a sticky note, I think. I feel spied on in my own living room, which is dramatic, but the prompt is sitting there. Do I text Devon, ignore it, or change everything again?

### Assistant
Change the password again. Then text Devon. In that order.

The prompt is useful. It tells you the old secret is still out in the world, on a note or in a saved browser. A new password kills the leftover. Do that before the conversation so you are not negotiating while they still have a door.

Text, not the group chat: "Hey — I'm not sharing that login anymore. Please delete the old password if you still have it. I got a sign-in attempt from your city." You can skip "your city" if you want less accusation. The first two sentences are the point. The third is why you're texting today.

You already told the group you were not handing it out. Devon skipped the ask. That is the actual rudeness. You do not have to be cruel. You do have to be done sharing.

I will not invent the service's household rules or what they charge for an extra user. If you want to pay for a proper extra slot later, that is a separate, sober decision. Tonight is lock the account.

Did you already deny the prompt on the TV, or is it still waiting?

### User
It's still waiting. I didn't want to tap the wrong thing. Devon and I have a trip in three weeks. I don't want this to be the whole trip. They've used my logins for years. It used to feel like generosity. Now it feels like I need permission to watch my own show.

### Assistant
Tap deny. Then change the password on your phone, not on the TV hunt-and-peck. Sign out extra devices in the account page if the service shows a device list. That is the household part.

Generosity that requires you to flinch at your own TV is not generosity anymore. It is an unpaid subscription with social overhead. You can still be generous in ways you choose — a meal, a ride, a password you actually intend to share. This one is closed.

For the trip: do not pre-fight for three weeks. Send the text, answer one round of "wait really?", then stop. If Devon sulks, they can sulk. You can still go on the trip. People have gone on trips after smaller grudges than a streaming login.

If they say they "didn't know," you can say, "Now you do. Please delete it." If they say everyone shares, you can say, "I'm not doing that on this account." You do not need a TED talk on intellectual property. You need them out of the device list.

Do not post a callout in the group. That turns a boundary into theater. The two people who asked and heard no already behaved. Devon is the leftover sticky note. Handle Devon as one person.

### User
What if they log in with a code from an old email because I used to forward the family plan emails to them?

### Assistant
Then treat email as part of the lock. Change the streaming password. If that inbox still forwards, stop the forward. If they have an old invite in their mail, the new password still matters more than the PDF from 2022. On the account, look for recovery email and phone; those should be yours. If Devon is listed as a household extra, remove the extra the way the service allows. I will not click the menus for you. The idea is: secrets, devices, recovery, forwards.

Deny the TV prompt. Lock it. Text once. Save the trip for the trip. You are allowed to watch a show in your living room without a committee. That is not dramatic. That is the product you pay for.
