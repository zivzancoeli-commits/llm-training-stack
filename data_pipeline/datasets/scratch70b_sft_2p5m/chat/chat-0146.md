### User
Flight's delayed. Board says "delayed" with no new time. Phone is at 8 percent. There's one free wall outlet at this whole gate and a stranger already plugged in, but they have a splitter and they kind of nodded at me. I'm on my break from trying to be a calm adult. Two options, please, and tell me which one you'd actually pick. I also don't know if I should tell work I'll miss the meeting.

### Assistant
Two options for the charger, then work.

Option A: you sit down, say "mind if I use the other side," plug in, and keep your bag on your own knees. You charge enough to be a person with a map again. You don't become friends. You don't leave the phone unattended while you walk to the bathroom.

Option B: you don't share. You put the phone in low power, screenshot the delay board, and save the battery for one call or one rideshare later. You stay near the gate screens.

I'd pick A if they already nodded and the splitter is public and visible. Eight percent is a dying brick, not a personality test. Sharing a wall for twenty minutes is normal airport behavior. I'd pick B if the vibe is off — they cover the outlet with a bag, they want you to hold their cable, they want your name. Then keep the brick and stay mean about battery.

Work: I won't invent your company's policy. Don't promise a time you don't have. "Delayed, no new departure yet, I'll update when the board does" is enough. Don't write a novel in Slack while your phone is dying.

Do you have a charging cable that fits that splitter, or only a brick in your bag?

### User
I have a cable. Their splitter looks cheap. I'm afraid of frying something or of them unplugging me when I look away. Also the meeting is in ninety minutes and I was supposed to present.

### Assistant
Cheap splitter: possible it's fine, possible it's junk. I won't pretend I can see the voltage from here. Use your own cable. Plug your phone, not your laptop, if you only get one slot — phone is how you rebook and tell people. If the phone gets hot in a weird way or the plug sparks, unplug. That's not dramatic. That's stop.

Unplugging: keep the phone in your hand or on your bag where you can see the cord. Don't wander for food while it's on a stranger's brick. If they need the splitter back, they can say so. You say "thanks" and leave with whatever percent you got.

Presenting: two tracks, not a speech.

Track one, if you might still make it: tell them the delay has no new time, send the slides now from your phone if you can, and name a backup person if you have one. "I may miss the start" is truer than "I'll definitely be there."

Track two, if the delay already killed the window: say you'll miss it, send the slides, and ask them to run without you. Don't keep the meeting hostage to your optimism.

I won't guess whether ninety minutes is enough. Look at the board, not at hope. If there's still no departure time, treat the meeting as at risk and send the slides. Tired-coworker move: protect the team, then sit down.

Charge first at 8 percent. Eat later. A sandwich does not call a rideshare.

### User
They just said "take it, I'm at 90." I feel like I should offer them money or something. And I still don't know the new flight time.

### Assistant
Don't offer money for a wall outlet. That's making a simple share weird. "Thanks" is the whole transaction. Don't volunteer to be airport security.

Still no time: stay at this gate until the board or the app moves. Delays love to change rooms. Screenshot every change. When a time appears, that's when you do math about the meeting.

Recommendation: take the share (A), phone only, your cable, stay put, send slides now, tell work "delayed, no time yet, slides attached, I may miss the start." If a real departure time clearly blows the meeting, upgrade that to "I will miss it." Don't drip-feed hope every ten minutes.

Bathroom: unplug and take the phone. An unattended phone on a stranger's splitter is worse than a low battery in a stall.

Tired is not a reason to skip the slides. Plug in, send the file, watch the board. That's the whole gate.
