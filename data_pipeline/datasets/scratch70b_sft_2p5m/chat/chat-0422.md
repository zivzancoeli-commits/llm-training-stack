### User
I walked into a store to touch a jacket I saw in the window. A salesperson greeted me in thirty seconds. I said "just looking" in the automatic way. They kept asking sizes. I gave a size. Now they are holding a dressing room. I do not want a room. I do not want a pitch. I also do not want to be rude because they are doing their job. I have never successfully left after "just looking." I always buy a sock to escape. I am in the middle of the store.

### Assistant
You can leave without a sock. "Just looking" can be true even after you said a size. A size is not a contract.

Walk to them, or speak across the few feet: "I'm only browsing. I don't want a dressing room today. Thanks." Then you either keep browsing in silence or you walk out. Both are allowed. You do not have to try the jacket to justify the window.

If they keep holding the room, you can say, "Please give that room to someone else." You are freeing them, not insulting them.

I will not invent a commission structure. They may still want a sale. Your job is not to manage their quota with a sock.

Are you actually interested in the jacket later, or was the window just curiosity?

### User
Curiosity. I cannot afford a thing in here this month and I knew that. I wanted the fabric. They are being nice. I feel like I used them. There is a sock wall by the door. The old escape.

### Assistant
Touching a sleeve is not using a person. Nice is their professional face. You can be nice back with a complete sentence and an exit.

Do not buy a sock as a tip. That trains you. It also spends money you said you do not have this month. I will not name a price. You already know it is a tax on discomfort.

If you still want thirty seconds with the fabric, take them after the sentence, then leave. Set a timer in your head: two minutes, then sidewalk. If they follow with "when are you looking to buy," you can say, "Not today." Not today is a full answer. You do not owe a later date they can put in a book.

Used is a story. You walked in a store that is open to walk-ins. Walking out is the design.

### User
If I say not today they might ask for an email for when it goes on sale. I freeze at clipboards. What is the body language of leaving so I don't get walked to the sock wall?

### Assistant
Hands off the racks. Angle toward the door. "No email, thanks. Have a good one." Keep walking. Clipboards need a pause. Do not pause. You can smile while your feet move. Smiling is not a yes to a list.

If they step into your path, you sidestep the same way you would a display. "Excuse me" plus motion. You do not have to narrate your budget.

The sock wall is a magnet for the old pattern. Look at the glass, not the socks. You are going to the sidewalk. The jacket can exist without you. You can exist without a consolation sock. Say the browsing sentence, decline the room, decline the email, leave. That is a successful first time even if your voice shakes. Shaking on the sidewalk is still a leave. Go.

### User
They're walking me toward the room. I already said a size. My feet want the sock wall.

### Assistant
Stop walking. "I'm only browsing. I don't want a room today. Please give it to someone else." Turn your shoulders toward the door. If they keep shepherding, sidestep and keep the sentence. Do not look at the socks. Do not invent a later date for their book. Curiosity already happened at the window. The rest is an exit. You did not use them. You entered an open store and you are leaving. Nice can be "thanks, have a good one" while you move. The jacket can stay on the hanger. Your budget can stay this month's budget. Feet to glass, not to socks. Say it. Leave. That is meaning it.
