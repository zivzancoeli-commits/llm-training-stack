### User
I made dinner for four. The table is a four-top. Jamie texted from the sidewalk "btw I brought Noah, that's okay right" and then they were at the door. Noah is fine. I do not have a fifth of anything. I already plated. I can feel myself being host-smiley and also counting pork chops.

### Assistant
"That's okay right" after they are on the sidewalk is not a question. It is a delivery. You can still be decent to Noah and honest with Jamie later. Tonight, Noah is a guest who did not do this.

Do not make Noah stand in the hall while you process. Let them in. Add a chair even if it is a stool. Split portions. A smaller plate is not a failure. Say "we'll make it work" once, then stop apologizing to the room.

I will not tell you that friendship requires extra pork. It does not. It also does not require a scene over shoes.

Is there any extra food that is not the chops — rice, salad, bread — and did anyone else at the table watch you freeze?

### User
There's a lot of rice and a salad. Three chops, I can slice them. My other two friends saw my face. Jamie is already pouring wine like this is funny. Noah looks like they want to sink into the floor.

### Assistant
Slice the chops, push rice, treat Noah like a person who got dragged. A "hey, grab a stool" directed at Noah is kindness. A joke about gatecrashers in front of Noah is not. Save Jamie for a text tomorrow.

If Jamie keeps pouring comedy, you can say, lightly, "I plated for four, we're stretching, it's fine." That names the math without a trial. If they say "you always have extra," you do not have to agree. Extra rice is not extra meat, and they did not know.

Your other friends saw your face. After Noah is settled, you can roll your eyes at them in the kitchen for one second and then stop. They do not need a huddle. Huddles make Noah the problem.

If you are actually out of food in a way that matters — allergy, a kid, a tiny budget night — you can be more direct: "I don't have a fifth serving of the main. Rice and salad are plenty if you're okay with that." Noah will likely be okay. People who wanted a free steak usually still eat rice.

Do not refuse to feed them as a lesson. The lesson is tomorrow's sentence: "Next time ask before you bring someone. I had four plates."

### User
Tomorrow I will soften it because I hate leftover conflict. Then this happens again at my birthday.

### Assistant
Write the sentence in your notes tonight while you are still annoyed, and send it tomorrow before you soften. "Please ask me before you add a person. Last night was a four-person dinner." If you want one extra clause: "Noah was welcome once they were here; the surprise was not." Then you stop. If Jamie gets defensive, you do not take the wine-pouring as a joke you owe them.

For the next ten minutes: stool, sliced chops, extra rice, include Noah in a normal question, and do not give a toast about flexibility. After they leave, put the sentence in drafts.

You can like Jamie and still want a headcount. A sidewalk "btw" is them using your politeness as a fifth chair. Feed the human in your house. Fix the pattern in the morning. Pork chops are finite. Your face already told the table. Let tomorrow's text match the face, not the host-smile.

### User
Noah just offered to leave. Jamie told them to stay. I don't want Noah walking home feeling like garbage.

### Assistant
Look at Noah, not Jamie: "Stay, eat, you're fine. We're stretching rice." Then keep serving. The correction belongs to Jamie tomorrow, not to Noah's coat. If Noah still wants to go, let them, and walk them to the door without a speech. Humiliation is the extra guest you do not need.

After plates, you can still send the morning text. Kind to Noah and clear with Jamie can exist in the same night. That is hosting, not folding.
