### User
We are maybe two hours in. Not a crazy peak, just a ridge trail I have done before. My friend sat down on a rock and said they want to turn around. They are not crying. They are just done. I wanted the view. Last year I would have guilted them into "one more mile." I do not want to be that person. Also I do not want to be stupid about weather or ankles. Tell me what you would actually do in the next ten minutes, like you are talking to last-year me.

### Assistant
Next ten minutes, not the summit speech.

Minute one: sit down too. Do not stay standing over them like a coach. Water out. If you have food, offer it without a lecture. Ask one question: "Done for the day, or do you need a breather and then we'll see?" That split matters. Done is a turnaround. Breather is five minutes on the rock.

Minute two: look at them, not at the ridge. Are they talking in full sentences. Are they pale, wobbly, or holding a knee. I am not going to name a condition. If they look worse than "tired and over it," turning back is the plan and you stop bargaining. If they look like a person who is simply over the hike, you still do not guilt. You just need a clean decision.

Minutes three to five: check the boring stuff out loud. Time of day. How long back to the car at the pace you already walked. Water left for two people. Phone battery. Sky. If any of those are ugly, you do not debate the view. You say, "We're heading down." Last-year you skipped this inventory because it felt unromantic. It is the whole job.

### User
They said "I don't want to ruin it for you." That is the sentence that used to make me push. The sky is fine. Water is fine. They just hate the last steep bit coming up.

### Assistant
Minutes five to seven: answer the sentence they actually said. "You're not ruining it. We can turn around." If you still want a little more trail, offer a small fork that is not the steep bit: "We can go back now, or we can walk five minutes on the flat and turn around there. Your call." Two options, both include them. No third option that is secretly the summit.

If they pick now, you stand, you pack, you go. Do not sigh. Do not say "I guess." Last-year you sighed and they heard it for the whole descent.

If they pick five minutes of flat, you set a phone timer. When it rings, you turn. You do not "see how we feel." That is how one more mile happens.

Minutes seven to ten: start moving the direction you agreed. Talk about something else — food after, a dumb story, the dog at the trailhead. Do not recap their courage. Do not recap your disappointment. Walking is the repair.

### User
What if I am honestly sad about the view and I get quiet. They will think I am mad.

### Assistant
Then say one true line while you walk: "I'm a little sad about the lookout. I'm not mad at you." That is enough. Last-year me would have gone silent and let them guess, which is meaner than the steep bit.

You wanted the view. You can come back another day without them, or with them on an easier trail. Today you had a friend on a rock who was done. Ten minutes is for deciding and starting down, not for a TED talk about grit. If anything about their body looks off — they say they feel wrong, they cannot put weight on a foot, they seem confused — you skip the flat option and you go to the car. I will not pretend I can diagnose from here. You turn around and you do not make them earn the right.

That is it. Sit, ask done-versus-pause, inventory, two clean options, start walking, one honest sentence if your face goes sour. The ridge will still be there. Last-year you is the one who would have turned a tired friend into a project. Do not.
