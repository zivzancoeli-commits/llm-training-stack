### User
Graduation is Saturday. Each graduate got a ticket allotment. I applied for an extra for my aunt. The portal still says pending. She is already in the air, landing tonight. My parents have the two confirmed seats. I have a plus-one that is my roommate, which I cannot uninvite without wrecking that. I need a plan for an aunt who may not get a chair, and a way to talk to the school that isn't me crying at a desk. I don't want fake promises about how commencements work.

### Assistant
You have a pending extra, a plane in the air, and a finite number of chairs. Treat the pending as a no until it is a yes.

Call or visit the registrar or commencement desk today, not only the portal. Ask: is there a waitlist, a will-call, standing room, a simulcast room, or a hard cap. Write the answers. I will not invent whether your school allows extras at the door. Schools differ. You want their sentence.

Tell your aunt the truth before she lands if you can get a message through someone on the ground: the extra is not confirmed. She should not plan on a seat. She can still come to town for dinner, photos, and whatever overflow option the school names.

Do not uninvite the roommate to solve an aunt. That trades one hurt for another unless the roommate offers. Ask the roommate only if you are truly willing to accept either answer.

Who can meet her at the airport besides you?

### User
My dad can. He still thinks "they'll work it out" because he once got into a basketball game. My roommate would be sad and would say yes anyway. I don't want a martyr. The school email says no empty-seat swapping. I don't know if that's real.

### Assistant
Assume the email is the policy you will be told at the desk. You can still ask about overflow. You cannot plan on a secret empty seat. Dad's basketball story is not a ticket.

Tell dad he is on truth duty at the airport: pending means not a seat. If he undercuts you with "it'll be fine," you will have an aunt in a gown line with nowhere to stand. Send him the exact portal screenshot.

Leave the roommate on the plus-one. A sad yes is not a free chair. You asked them first.

Tonight: desk or phone, overflow options, a meeting spot on campus that is not the gated entrance. Photos happen outside many ceremonies. Dinner happens either way. The stage is one hour. The visit is the rest.

If a ticket frees up Friday, you assign it then. Do not make the aunt hover as a maybe in the family's group chat all week. Maybes are stressful in airports.

### User
What do I say to her face tonight if the school says no extras, and she flew anyway? I feel like I invited her with a hope.

### Assistant
You invited her to a graduation weekend. A seat was a hope. Say that without a speech: "I couldn't get the extra ticket. I'm sorry. I want you here for dinner and photos. The ceremony itself is capped." Then you tell her where to stand if there is a livestream room, or where you'll find her after.

If she is angry at the school, let her be angry at the school. If she is angry at you, you can take a fair portion: you let pending sit too long. You can still not produce a chair.

Do not offer to sneak her in. That is how ushers and families have a worse morning.

Tonight: dad gets the screenshot, you get the school's real options, roommate stays on the list, aunt gets a plan that does not depend on a portal miracle. Hope was a feeling. Saturday needs a map. Make the map before the plane lands.
