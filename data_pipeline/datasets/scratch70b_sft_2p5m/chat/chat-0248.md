### User
Lena texted at 8:02 p.m. Can I take her Saturday 7 a.m. bakery shift. I already told my brother I'd help him move a couch at 9. She knows that because I mentioned it Sunday. She's adding "I'd owe you" and a photo of her sick-looking tea. I don't know if she's sick or if she wants the morning off. I like her. I also like my brother not moving a couch alone. I haven't replied. The typing bubble keeps appearing.

### Assistant
You already have a Saturday. Covering 7 a.m. and a 9 a.m. couch in two places is a fantasy commute unless the bakery is in his driveway. It isn't, I'm guessing.

You don't need to diagnose the tea. I'm not a clinician. Sick or not, her ask is still an ask. You can believe she feels bad and still say no. Those stack.

Don't wait out the typing bubble. Waiting makes you draft a novel. One message: "Can't cover Saturday. Helping my brother at 9. Hope you get someone." That's the whole letter.

Is there a group thread for shift swaps, or is this only in your DMs?

### User
There's a work group chat. She DMed me first because I said yes last time. Last time I canceled brunch. The group chat is where people dump shifts nobody wants. I don't want to be the private yes. My brother rented the van from 8:30 to noon. If I flake he pays for a van and no second pair of hands.

### Assistant
Private yes is how you got here. Last time you canceled brunch. This time the cost is a van window. That's enough data.

For the next ten minutes: reply in the DM with the no. Then, if you want to be decent without being the solution, you can post in the group chat only if she asks you to — or you can leave the hunting to her. You are not required to advertise her shift. You are required not to lie that you're free.

If she sends a second photo of tea, you don't need a medical cross-examination. "Still can't. Ask the group." Repeat once. Then mute if the bubble continues. Mute is not a betrayal. It's how you keep 8 p.m. from becoming 11 p.m. of guilt.

Tell your brother tonight that you're still on for 9, so he doesn't hire a stranger in a panic. One line. The van is 8:30. You can show up at 8:45 if the bakery guilt makes you slow. Don't show up at the bakery.

I won't invent the store's call-out policy. If she's actually sick, she uses that policy. If she wants a morning, she still needs a yes from someone who doesn't have a van clock.

### User
She just sent "please, you're the only one I trust with open." That got me. Open is the hard shift. I do know the bread rack.

### Assistant
Trust with open is flattery with a 7 a.m. attached. Knowing the bread rack does not make you the only human in the building. It makes you convenient.

"Please" plus "only one" is designed to isolate you from the group chat that exists for this. You can be trusted and unavailable at the same time. Reply: "I know open is hard. I still can't. Text the group." If you want one extra kindness, add "I can look at a weekday if you need a swap later." Only add that if it's true. Don't sell a weekday you haven't checked.

Then put the phone down and text your brother confirmation. Lay out shoes for a 9 a.m. couch. The typing bubble can live without you. Lena can call the manager. That's the path for a sick morning. You already spent a brunch on this friendship. You don't have to spend a van deposit too.

8:02 was the ask. 8:15 can be the no. You like her. You like your brother. Saturday at 7 belongs to the person who is actually on the schedule or to someone who answers the group. You already answered, in your calendar, on Sunday.
