### User
There's a board but the lighting is bad and I left my glasses in the car. I need to look up the menu on my phone. The barista already said "what can I get you." There are three people behind me. I have never asked someone to wait while I scroll. I usually panic-order a drip I don't want. Help me do the first time without a TED talk about accessibility.

### Assistant
You can ask for thirty seconds. That is not a TED talk. That is a queue with a human in it.

The sentence is: "I need a minute on my phone for the menu. You can take the next person if you want." Offering the next person is how you do not freeze the line. If they would rather wait, they will wait. If they wave the next person up, you step aside with your phone. Step aside is the whole courtesy.

I will not invent the drink list. I will not invent prices. If you already know you want caffeine and milk, you can filter the scroll to that. Do not browse seasonal poetry at the register. Browse at the side.

Glasses in the car: if the car is two minutes, you can also say "I'll be right back" and actually be right back. Only if the lot is close. Do not start a quest.

Did they already start a cup because they assumed drip, or are they still waiting on your mouth?

### User
Still waiting. I smiled like I knew. There's a laptop guy behind me who sighs as a hobby. The board is white on yellow. My phone brightness is up. I can feel the panic drip forming in my throat.

### Assistant
Kill the drip in your throat. Say the minute line now, then physically rotate your body forty-five degrees so there is a gap. Gap invites the sighing laptop to order. You are not cutting. You are unblocking.

If the barista looks annoyed, you still get the minute. Annoyed is their face. Your eyes are yours. White on yellow is a bad board. Phone is the accommodation you have in your pocket. You do not have to name a disability. "I can't read the board, one minute" is optional and true. Use it if you want. Skip it if you want. The ask stands either way.

Pick two candidates, not twelve. Ask one clarifying question when you return: iced or hot, size you actually drink. Then stop. First time fails when the scroll becomes a tour.

If they already typed drip from your smile, you can say, "Cancel that — I need a second." Cancel is allowed before they pour. After they pour, you either take it or you pay for a second drink. I will not invent which they will do. Ask before the pour if you can still see the cup empty.

### User
What if I step aside and they forget me? I have done that in other shops and just left.

### Assistant
Then you rejoin with a hand and "I was looking at the menu, I'm ready." You are not a new stranger. You were the glasses person. If they forgot, one reminder is enough. Do not leave as a personality test. Leaving is how you drink nothing and still feel rude.

If the line grew, you still go to the side of the register, not the back of a new line, unless they tell you to. One sentence: "I stepped aside to read. Ready when you are." Then order the thing you already picked on the phone.

Right now: "Need a minute, take the next person." Sideways body. Two drinks in mind. Back. Order. No drip as a panic button. The laptop can sigh. The board can stay ugly. You still get a beverage you meant. That is the first time. Scroll on the side, speak when you know, leave with the right cup.
