### User
I need to print a two-page form at the library. Last time I tried, the librarian started a tour of the computer policy, the guest log, and a story about people who print too much. I nodded for ten minutes and missed my bus. I have twenty minutes this afternoon. I am not a regular. I feel dumb about printers. I want help, not a class. How do I ask so they hear the job and not my incompetence?

### Assistant
Lead with the job, the time, and the pages. People lecture when the request is vague.

Walk to the desk with the file already on a USB or in your email. "Hi — I have a two-page PDF to print. I have about twenty minutes. Can you point me to the guest print and the first step?" Then you stop talking. Extra autobiography is how tours start.

If they begin the policy saga, you can nod once and steer: "Got it. What's the first button for me right now?" Repeat the time if you must: "I have to catch a bus in twenty." That is a clock, not rudeness.

I will not invent their print prices. If a cost appears on the screen, you pay what they show. Ask "how do I pay" as a single question, not a debate.

Are you printing in black and white, and is the file already openable without a password?

### User
Black and white. No password. It's on my phone. I don't have a USB. Last time they sighed about phones. I can email it to myself on their PC if that's faster. I get flustered when they watch me type.

### Assistant
Phones are normal. If they sigh, you do not have to perform shame. "The file is on my phone. What's the method you prefer — email to the guest account, or a cable?" Let them pick the path they already know. You are not there to win a technology argument.

If they watch you type, you can say, "I'll call you when I hit the print screen." Some people cannot leave. Then you type slower than you think you need. Fluster is allowed. Two pages will still come out.

If a lecture starts about guest logs, listen for the one action: sign here, click there. Write it on your hand if your brain dumps. You do not need the philosophy of public computing.

Twenty minutes: desk first, not a wandering hunt for a machine. Machines without a login will waste the bus. Start at a human.

### User
What if they still launch the tour and I feel trapped because they're being "helpful"? I don't want to be the impatient patron.

### Assistant
Helpful tours are still optional. You can smile and interrupt once: "This is really useful, and I have to print in the next few minutes. First step for my two pages?" If they look offended, let them. You came to print, not to enroll.

If you still get stuck, ask for a different staff member if more than one is visible: "Is there someone who can start the guest print with me?" That is allowed.

Go with the file ready, the sentence ready, and the bus time in your pocket. You are not dumb for needing a printer. Libraries print things. Ask for the button. Catch the bus. The lecture can wait for a day you have an hour. Today you have twenty minutes and two pages. Use them.

### User
I'm in the parking lot with twenty-two minutes. The PDF is open on my phone. I can feel the lecture starting in my head before I walk in.

### Assistant
Walk to the desk, not to a machine. "Two-page PDF, twenty minutes, guest print, first step?" If your voice is small, the paper time is still on the clock. You do not need to narrate last time's tour. If they start one, steer to the button. Email the file to yourself on their PC only if they say that is the path. Pay what the screen shows. Leave with two pages. The bus does not need you to become a regular. It needs you to print and go. Start at the human. Hold the phone with the file ready. Twenty-two minutes is enough if you do not enroll in a class. Go inside.
