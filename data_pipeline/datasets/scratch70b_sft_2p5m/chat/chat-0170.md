### User
I've been in this waiting room since 6:40. My brother went back for "tests" and nobody has said a useful sentence in two hours. My group chat is exploding. My aunt wants a play-by-play. His roommate keeps texting "is he dying." I have twelve minutes left on my break-brain before I have to sound like a person at work tomorrow. I don't even know if I want advice or if I just want someone to sit here in the sentence with me. Help me figure out what to send people without lying or turning this into a medical TED talk.

### Assistant
I'm not going to diagnose your brother or guess what "tests" means. I also won't pretend I know hospital rules in your city. I can help you send one honest update so the group chat stops filling silence with disasters.

Before I pile on a script: do you still want help with the messages, or do you want me to shut up after this and just let you dump? Either is fine. I'd rather ask than keep coaching a person who already has enough voices.

If you still want the practical bit: three facts and one boundary. Where you are, no new status from staff, when you'll text again. You are not a news channel. "No new info from staff. I'm in the waiting room. I'll text at 10 or when I know something real. Please don't ask me if he's dying. I don't know, and I'm not going to guess." That's the whole post.

The roommate question is fear wearing a joke. You don't have to answer it on their terms.

### User
Yeah. I still want the help. I'm just fried. His roommate is going to keep going. My aunt already called twice and I declined. I feel rude. Also a nurse said "we're waiting on labs" to the person next to me, not to me, and now I'm not sure if I even heard that right.

### Assistant
Tired is the correct setting. Call your aunt back when you have a chair and a sentence, not because guilt set a timer.

Do not report the labs line as fact. You overheard something that might not be his. If you tell the group "they're waiting on labs," you invented a status. Leave it out. Silence plus a next-check time is cleaner than a maybe.

Roommate: one text, then mute. "He's in the hospital. I don't have results. I'm not going to answer 'is he dying.' I'll send one update when I have something from staff." If they keep going, "I'm putting my phone down" is enough.

Aunt: same three facts. Lead with "I don't have news." If she wants to drive over, that's her choice. You can say the waiting room is full without pretending you know visitor policy. I don't know it either.

Work tomorrow is separate. One sentence tonight to whoever covers you: "Family hospital thing, I may be late or out. I'll confirm in the morning." You don't have to name the tests.

### User
What if they come out and it's actually bad and I already told everyone "no news" like it's nothing.

### Assistant
"No news" is not "it's nothing." It's "I refuse to narrate a blank." If the next update is bad, you send a new message. People who care can handle a change.

When someone in scrubs talks to you, write their words down. Repeat it back: "So you're saying X, next step is Y, we'll know more at Z?" If they use a term you don't know, ask in plain language. Don't translate it into a disease name from a search. Don't send the group a half-heard noun.

You still wanted help a minute ago. If that's flipped, say so and I'll stop. If it hasn't: bathroom, water, one group text with the three facts and a time, mute the roommate, sit. You are not rude for being a person in a chair instead of a spokesperson. You showed up at 6:40. The chat can wait for a real sentence.
