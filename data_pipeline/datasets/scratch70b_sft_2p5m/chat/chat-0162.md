### User
I used to draw every night. Then work ate it. That was years. Last month I bought a cheap sketchbook and I have been doing ugly still lifes after dinner. I like it. A friend from the old days found out and asked me to join their weekend figure group, plus they want me in a group chat that posts daily prompts. I can hear last-year-me inventing a schedule conflict so I do not have to say I just want my kitchen table. How do I say no without a fake excuse.

### Assistant
You say the true sentence. You do not owe them a calendar injury.

Last-year-you used excuses because "no" felt like you were quitting again. You were not quitting. You were protecting a small thing that had already died once under other people's plans.

The figure group is a different hobby than twenty minutes of pears after dishes. Both can be drawing. Only one is the thing you actually restarted. If you join the group to prove you are "back," you will miss the kitchen on purpose and then resent the easels.

Try: "I'm drawing again at home. I'm not joining the weekend group or the prompt chat. I want it small for now." That is a complete answer. It is not a story about a dentist, a shift, or a mysterious bus.

If they push, you repeat it once. You do not add a nicer reason. Nicer reasons are invitations to problem-solve your no.

What nights are actually free, if any, or is "none" the honest number?

### User
Sundays are theoretically free. That is why the excuse would have been easy. I just do not want an audience yet. My drawings are bad in a way I can live with alone and not in a room. The friend said "you'll get better faster with us." They mean well.

### Assistant
They might be right about speed. Speed is not your metric. You did not come back for a training camp. You came back because the sketchbook made your evenings feel like yours.

"You'll get better faster" is a sales pitch wearing friendship. You can thank them for the invite and still decline. "I believe you. I'm still not coming. I need it to be private while I remember how to sit down." If that sounds stiff, shorten it: "Not this year. I'll keep drawing at home."

Do not bargain with Sunday. Theoretically free is how last-year-you ended up with a commitment that ate the only quiet block. If you later want a class, you can ask. You are not signing a treaty by being polite today.

The group chat is the sneaky one. Mute still counts as membership in your head. Daily prompts will turn a hobby into homework. Say no to the chat as its own sentence so they do not add you "just in case." "Please don't add me to the prompt thread." Direct. Not rude. Rude is adding someone who said they wanted the kitchen.

If you feel the itch to soften it with "maybe in a few months," ask whether that is a real maybe or a cushion. If it is a cushion, cut it. A fake future yes is an excuse with a calendar.

### User
They already sent the address and "we save you a seat." I have not replied. I feel like I waited too long and now a no is dramatic.

### Assistant
A delayed no is still better than a resentful yes. Drama is showing up and being miserable. Silence plus a seat is them hoping. You did not sign.

Reply today. Do not write a paragraph about your artistic journey.

"Thanks for holding a seat. I'm not coming to the figure group. I'm drawing at home and I want to keep it that way. Don't save me a spot."

If they answer with hurt, you can be kind without folding: "I know you were excited I started again. I'm protecting the part that is actually working." Then you go draw the ugly still life. That is the point of the no.

Last-year-you would have manufactured a Sunday obligation. This-year-you can say no because the kitchen is the hobby. The seat can go to someone who wants the room. You want the table. That is allowed.
