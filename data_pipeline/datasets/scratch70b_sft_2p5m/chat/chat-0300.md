### User
I came back to the car and there is a metal boot on the wheel. There is a sticker with a phone number and a lot of capital letters. I was in a lot I thought was for the store I was in. I have a meeting in 50 minutes across town. I feel like if I yank anything I will break the car. I also feel like this is a scam because the number is not a city number.

### Assistant
Do not yank. Boots are designed to make yanking expensive. Do not drive. Do not pry with a jack you saw in a video.

Read the sticker slowly. Photograph it, the boot, the wheel, the signs you parked under, and the lot entrance. Photos are for you, not for an argument in the street.

Call the number on the sticker, or the number on the posted signs if they differ. A boot can be a city contractor, a private lot, or something uglier. I will not tell you which one this is, and I will not invent the fine. The person who answers will quote you a process. Write it down.

If the lot has a booth or an office, walk there while you call. Humans in booths sometimes move faster than a phone tree.

Is the car blocking anyone, and do you have the payment method the sticker names, or only a card they may not take?

### User
Not blocking. Sticker wants a card by phone or a website. The signs at the entrance were small and I honestly missed them. Meeting is with a client. I already look flaky.

### Assistant
Missed signs still count as signs, which is annoying and common. Text the client now, before you solve the boot: "I'm delayed by a parking issue, I'll update in 20 minutes, happy to push or call in." That is better than silence plus a sprint.

Pay through the channel they name if the signs, the sticker, and the lot office agree on the same company. If the website looks like a random form asking for extra identity stuff that has nothing to do with a boot, pause and use the office or a listed city number from the city's own site, not from a search ad. I cannot authenticate the URL from here. Matching names across sticker, sign, and a posted permit company is the practical check.

Do not post your plate in a group chat asking if anyone has a "boot key." That is how you add a crime to a parking problem.

Once they say they are on the way, stay with the car if you can. Boots get removed from empty cars more slowly in some lots, and I will not pretend I know this lot's habit. Waiting is still the safer default.

If they cannot come before your meeting, you go to the meeting by rideshare or bus if that is possible, and you deal with the car after. A client meeting usually beats a wheel. I will not quote what a ride costs.

### User
If I leave the car, someone could do something to it. And I'm embarrassed. There are people watching.

### Assistant
People watching is free entertainment for them and zero information. You do not give them a show. Phone, photos, office, client text.

Leaving the car is a trade. If the remover requires you present, stay. If they give a window that misses the meeting, the client is the job; the car is a parked object with a boot, which is already the bad thing. Lock it. Take valuables.

For the next ten minutes: photograph everything, text the client, call the number, walk to the office if there is one, and only then decide stay versus go to the meeting. If you are shaking, sit in the car with the doors locked while you call.

A boot is a expensive inconvenience, not a verdict on you as a driver. Do not yank, do not DIY, do not disappear on the client. Read, call, update, wait or go. That is the order.
