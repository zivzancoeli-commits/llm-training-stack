### User
I ordered a cappuccino. She started the shot. I remembered I don't want caffeine this late. The machine is already doing the thing. There is a line. I feel like if I speak I'm wasting milk and making her hate me. If I don't speak I'll be wired until 2 a.m. I am still at the pickup end, not gone. Do I ask, and what's the least annoying sentence?

### Assistant
Ask now. A shot in progress is cheaper than your 2 a.m.

Step back to where she can see you, not a shout from the door: "Sorry — could that be decaf? I caught it late." Then you accept the answer. Sometimes they dump the shot and start a decaf. Sometimes they finish this one and you pay for a second. Sometimes they hand you the caffeinated one and you walk away from it. You will not know until you ask.

Do not narrate your sleep. Do not blame the menu. One sentence, a sorry that is about timing, not about her skill.

I will not guess whether they will charge twice. Ask if they mention a new drink: "Do you need me to pay for a second?" Then pay if they say yes. Line people survive ten extra seconds.

Are you actually sensitive to caffeine at night, or is this a preference you can live without this once?

### User
I'm sensitive. If I drink this I will be up. She already steamed milk. The person behind me sighed. I haven't said it yet.

### Assistant
Say it. The sigh is not your boss.

"Decaf, please — I spoke too late." Point at your cup if there is a cup. Then stand out of the espresso zone so she can move. If she says it's too far along, ask "Can I get a decaf instead and leave this?" You are offering to not take the caffeinated one. That helps them more than vanishing.

If she finishes it and puts it on the bar with your name, you can leave it and wait for the decaf. You do not have to drink it out of politeness. Polite insomnia is still insomnia.

If the line is ugly, you can also take the caffeinated cup, walk to the trash or the sharing table if they have one, and order decaf as a new drink. That costs more. It is still your call. I will not pick the price. I will pick you speaking once.

Steamed milk is not a moral debt. Milk gets wasted in cafes. Baristas would rather hear "decaf" than watch you suffer and still not say it.

### User
She heard me and said "I'll do a decaf, this one's a mistake on us." I feel like I got away with something.

### Assistant
You didn't steal. You corrected an order in time for a human to change a shot. If they comp it, say thanks, take the decaf, tip if you tip, move. Do not write a paragraph about your circadian rhythm.

Next time, say decaf in the first sentence at the register. Put it in the app note if you order ahead. This time you spoke at the machine. That is allowed. The sigh behind you will order their drink. You will sleep. That was the point of opening your mouth. Take the cup. Leave the guilt on the bar. It isn't a menu item.

### User
They handed me the decaf and the original cappuccino "in case you change your mind." I don't want the extra cup.

### Assistant
Leave the caffeinated one on the bar or ask "can this go to someone else / the waste." Do not take it home as a trophy of politeness. Two cups in your hands is how you sip the wrong one on autopilot at the door.

Say thanks for the decaf. Walk. If they already poured both, that is their call, not a debt. You asked in time. They chose to be generous. Generous can still sit on the bar. You chose sleep. Carry the decaf only. That is the whole errand. The line has already forgotten you. You are allowed to forget the extra foam. Go. The 2 a.m. version of you does not need a backup cappuccino in a tray. One cup. The right one. Out the door.
