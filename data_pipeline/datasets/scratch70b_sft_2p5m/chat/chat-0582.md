### User
Priya asked me to "just dummy a timeline" for a deck. The dates would show we started discovery in March. We started last week. She said leadership likes a story that looks like we were ahead. I know it's fake. She wants it in two hours. I don't want to be the person who fakes a calendar, and I don't want to do a TED talk in Slack. Help me refuse without lighting the team on fire.

### Assistant
Do not dummy the timeline. A fake start date is not a formatting favor. It is a story with your name in the footer if you build the slide.

Say no in the same channel she asked, if she asked in writing. If she asked in person, put the no in writing after: "I can't put March on a timeline we started last week. I can make a true timeline from last week, or a plan from today forward." That gives her a real artifact. It does not give her a backdated novel.

Do not add "maybe we could shade it" or "what if we say preliminary." Softening is how fake March happens with extra adjectives. Leadership liking a story is their taste. It is not a reason to invent weeks you did not work.

I will not invent your company's policy. I will not invent a law. I will tell you the practical split: true dates versus theater. You can do the first. Decline the second.

Did she already put March on a draft, or is she waiting for you to build it?

### User
She sent a screenshot with March, April, May already typed and asked me to "make it look official" with the real template. My name is on the template footer. The meeting is at 4. It's noon.

### Assistant
Then the job is even cleaner. She built the fiction. You would be the person who dressed it. Do not open the official template and pour March into it.

Reply on the thread: "I won't put this in the official template. The dates aren't true. Happy to format a timeline that starts last week. Need that by 3:30 if you want it for 4." Timestamp, offer, deadline. Then you actually build the true one if you have time, so you are not only a blocker.

If she says everyone does this, you can say, "I'm not doing it." You do not need a manifesto about integrity. You need a closed door. If she takes it to your manager, your written no is the record. A hallway maybe is how you get blamed later for a slide you "helped polish."

Do not secretly fix two dates and leave March. Partial fiction is still fiction with your footer.

If 4 o'clock happens without your template, that is her deck. You can attend and answer questions about the real work. If someone asks why the slide looks unofficial, "I can speak to last week's start" is a true sentence. You do not have to accuse Priya in the room. You also do not have to nod at March.

### User
She's now saying it's just a planning visual, not a report, so it doesn't count. Also she cc'd me on the invite. I feel like if I don't make the pretty version I'm tanking the meeting.

### Assistant
"Doesn't count" is how fake calendars travel. A planning visual in a leadership meeting still gets screenshotted. Your footer still sits there. The invite cc is pressure, not a verdict that you must draw a lie.

You already offered a true timeline by 3:30. Make that. Send it on the thread with the sentence: "True dates, official template. I won't send the March version." Then mute the guilt for an hour and do the real boxes: last week, this week, next. Ugly and dated beats pretty and invented.

If the meeting tanks because the story was the product, that is not you tanking it. You tanked a forgery. You can still talk about the actual discovery you started last week, which is the only work in the room that exists.

Do not dummy it. Send the true slide. Sit at 4 with facts. Let March stay in her screenshot, not in your template.
