### User
It's my aunt's 60th. I own a camera that isn't a phone. That's my mistake. They want me to shoot the welcome lunch, the park walk, the dinner, and "candids of the cousins." That's Saturday 11 through Sunday 4. I was going to be a guest. I have a job on Monday. They said "it would mean so much" and that a real photographer is "out of the budget." I don't want to be the ungrateful niece with a bag of lenses. I also don't want a 12-hour unpaid shift with a smile. They're waiting in the kitchen for a yes so they can tell the group chat.

### Assistant
You can take some pictures. You don't have to be the weekend's vendor.

"I can shoot the cake and a group photo at dinner. I'm not covering the whole weekend. I want to be a guest the rest of the time. If you need wall-to-wall, you'll need someone else or phones." That's a scoped yes. If they need a no, "I'm not the photographer. I'll bring the camera for the cake if you want, otherwise I'll just be there." Don't let "out of the budget" become your problem. I will not invent what a photographer costs. Their budget isn't your invoice.

Don't apologize for owning equipment. That's not a contract.

If they want you to "just wear it around your neck," that's still a shift. A neck is a job. Leave the camera in the trunk except for the window you named.

Are you staying overnight in their house, or can you leave Saturday night?

### User
Staying overnight. If I leave the camera in the trunk they'll say I'm making a point. I already know. Cousin wants "just the park, it's golden hour." That's two extra hours. Group chat isn't posted yet. Aunt is holding a wooden spoon like a gavel.

### Assistant
Golden hour is how weekends vanish. "Cake and dinner group. Not the park. Phones for the walk." If that makes a point, the point is you have a Monday.

Overnight: you can still have a boundary in a guest room. You don't have to be on call because the bag is in the house. Put the camera in the closet until dinner if you need it out of sight. "Making a point" is their story. You can live with a story better than a second-day shoot.

Group chat: if they post "Nia is shooting everything," you correct once: "I'll do cake and a group shot. Please don't advertise me as weekend coverage." Then you stop arguing in the thread. You already told the spoon.

If they guilt you with "family," you can say, "I'm family as a guest. Photography is extra, and I'm offering two pieces." If they decline the two pieces and want all or nothing, you can choose nothing. That's allowed. A guest with no camera is still a niece.

### User
I'll do cake and dinner. They asked me to "edit a few" by Tuesday. That's the second job. Monday is work. Do I say no to edits too?

### Assistant
Yes, or you define a tiny box. "I can drop the JPEGs in a folder Sunday night, unedited. I can't retouch by Tuesday." If they need edits, they can use the phone shots or a tool themselves. I won't name a price for your Monday. Your Monday is already priced as your job.

Kitchen, now: scoped yes, no park, no weeknight edit. Then you take a soda as a guest. The wooden spoon can tap. You're not ungrateful. You're not a vendor. You're a person with a camera and a workday. Get the two shots, get your weekend back. That's the whole unpaid photographer, without a fake invoice or a fake smile for 12 hours.

If they post the group chat anyway as "Nia has the camera all weekend," you correct once and you stop. You don't have to live in the thread. Sunday you drop the folder and you put the camera away. Monday you go to work without a retouch queue. That's the actual gift: showing up as a niece, not as unpaid crew. The 60th can still have photos. Phones exist. Your neck can be a neck, not a strap.
