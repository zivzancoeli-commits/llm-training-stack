### User
I got a wedding RSVP. I love them. I also cannot afford the weekend the way it's set up — hotel, outfit I don't have, maybe a gift. Last year I would have said yes and then panicked in the parking lot. I think I want help writing the no, or the yes-but. Do I even still want help, or do I want you to talk me into going.

### Assistant
I need that question answered by you, not by me. Do you still want help deciding the RSVP, or do you want me to sell you on going? Those are different jobs. I can do the first. I shouldn't do the second if money is already tight and you already know the panic.

If you want help, say so and we'll make a true reply. If you want permission to stretch, I won't pretend a stretched yes is free. Last-year-you already knows that hangover.

Which one is it tonight?

### User
Help. Actual help. I keep opening the form and closing it. There's a plus-one I don't have. There's a hotel block. There's a "we just want you there." I believe them and I still have a bank account.

### Assistant
"We just want you there" can be true and still not pay the hotel. You can believe them and still RSVP in a way that matches your money.

Two honest shapes:

One: you go to the parts you can actually reach. Ceremony and a few hours, no hotel, no extra events, no plus-one. That only works if travel is cheap enough that the day itself doesn't wreck you. If getting there requires a room you can't pay for, that shape is a trap with nicer lighting.

Two: you decline the event and still show up as a friend. A note, a call, a cheaper celebration later, a gift you can actually afford — or no gift now, and honesty about that. You can love them without buying a weekend.

I'm not going to invent hotel prices or a "normal" gift. Those numbers are yours to look up, and "normal" is often other people's anxiety. If the form has meal choices and a plus-one, leaving those blank while you stall is how they get a maybe. Maybes are expensive for them too. They need a count.

If you go, RSVP yes for one person and no hotel if you're not booking it. If you don't go, RSVP no before the deadline, in a sentence that isn't a novel.

### User
I feel like a no will mean I don't care. That's the story in my head. Also the group chat already has outfits.

### Assistant
The group chat is a highlight reel, not a bill you signed. Outfits are optional theater. You don't owe a costume to prove loyalty.

Caring is contact. It's "I can't do the weekend and I am happy for you." It's putting the no in on time so they aren't paying for an empty plate. The story that a no equals not caring is the same story that made last-year-you say yes and then sit in the car.

You can tell one person, not the whole chat, if you're close enough: "I'm RSVPing no because of money. I'm not mad, I'm not distant, I just can't do the travel." If they offer to cover things, you still get to say no to a gift that would make you feel owned. You also get to say yes if a couch actually solves it. Don't guess they will offer. RSVP based on the money you actually have today.

If you want a draft that isn't syrup:

"I'm so glad you're getting married. I can't make the weekend work with what I have right now, so I have to RSVP no. I wanted you to know early. I'd love to celebrate in a smaller way after, if you want that."

Change the last line if you don't want a later hangout. Don't promise a party you can't host.

### User
I still want help with the button. I'm going to hit no. Can you tell me I'm not a bad friend if I do it in the next ten minutes.

### Assistant
You're not a bad friend for sending a true no. You would be a worse guest as a yes you can't fund. Hit the button. Then text them something human so the form isn't the only voice. Then close the outfit chat for the night. You already asked for help, and you still wanted it. That's the whole decision. Don't reopen the form to see if guilt changed the math. It didn't.
