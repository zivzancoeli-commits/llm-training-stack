### User
My cousin's flight is at 6:15 a.m. on Tuesday. They asked me to pick them up at 4. I work at 9 and I am not a morning person. They said a rideshare "feels sketchy that early" and that family should do this. I have done it twice before. I was wrecked at my desk both times. I want to say no without becoming the cousin who doesn't care. They need an answer tonight because they're packing.

### Assistant
Family can care and still not be a 4 a.m. shuttle. Those are different jobs.

A clean no: "I can't do a 4 a.m. airport run on a workday. You'll need a rideshare, a car service, or someone who doesn't have to be sharp at 9." You don't have to argue whether early rideshares are sketchy. I will not invent safety statistics. If they are uneasy, they can book a trip with a rating they can see, travel with a friend, or choose a later flight next time. Their unease is real. It is not automatically your steering wheel.

Don't offer to "see how you feel Monday night." That is a maybe, and they will pack as if you said yes.

If you want to help in a way that isn't your sleep, you can: sit with them while they book a ride, or drop a bag at their place on Monday evening. Help is not only the worst hours of the clock.

Have they already told the rest of the family you would do it?

### User
They told my mom I'd "probably say yes because I always do." My mom texted me a thumbs-up. So now it's a little stack of people. The cousin also said they'd pay for gas, like that was the issue.

### Assistant
Gas is not the issue. Sleep and a work morning are the issue. Don't get pulled into a reimbursement conversation. "It's not the gas. I can't do 4 a.m. before work." If they keep offering money, repeat the same sentence. Money is how they translate your no into a rate. You are not for sale at that hour.

Text your mom separately, short, so the stack doesn't speak for you: "I'm not driving them. I already said no. Please don't volunteer me." Mom's thumbs-up was based on a prediction, not a contract. You are allowed to correct the prediction tonight, before packing becomes a parade.

If mom replies that family shows up, you can agree in principle and still refuse the time: "I show up other ways. Not this." Then stop litigating what love looks like. People who want a driver will always have a proverb.

To the cousin, send the no in the same thread where they asked, so it isn't a rumor. If they escalate to "so I have to get in a stranger's car," you can say, "You can book a ride, ask someone who is free, or change the flight. I won't be the driver." Three options, none of them you at 4. You don't have to help them rank the options unless you want to. Ranking is more unpaid labor.

I will not tell you which airport pickup zone is safest or what a ride should cost. I don't know your city. They can use the airline's or the airport's own listed transport info if they want official options.

### User
I feel guilty because their partner is out of town and they really do hate mornings too.

### Assistant
Two people hating mornings does not create a third person who likes them. Their partner being away is hard. It is still their trip. Guilt is information that you are not cruel. It is not an order to wreck Tuesday.

If you want a gesture that isn't the drive: a Monday night text "packed? ride booked?" is optional kindness. Do not let that text turn into "want me to just come." Watch your own mouth.

Tonight: no to cousin, correction to mom, no gas negotiation, sleep on Monday like a person with a 9 a.m. job. They will get to the airport the way adults get to airports when cousins have jobs. You can care from your bed.
