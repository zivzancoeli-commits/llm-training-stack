### User
I buy a shampoo that doesn't wreck my hair. It is not cheap for me. My roommate has been using it. I can tell because the bottle drops faster than my wash schedule, and I caught them in the shower caddy with their hand on it. They said "I ran out" and kept using it. They have not replaced it. They buy the 99-cent one for themselves when guests come, then go back to mine. I feel like a minibar. What's the line, and do I take the bottle into my room like a raccoon?

### Assistant
Yes, you can take the bottle into your room. That is not raccoon. That is inventory control.

The line, spoken once: "Please don't use my shampoo. I'll keep it in my room. Buy your own." If you want one extra: "If you run out, ask, don't finish mine." Asking still might be a no. Asking is better than a silent hotel.

Do not start a lecture about the 99-cent guest bottle. The contrast is obvious. Naming it once is enough if you need to: "You have a bottle. Use that." Then move yours.

A caddy in a shared shower is a temptation architecture. Remove the architecture.

I will not quote what your shampoo costs. You already know it hurts. Replacement is their job if they emptied it. You can ask them to replace this bottle. They may say no. Then you still move the next one.

Have you ever said a clear no, or only facial expressions in the bathroom?

### User
Facial expressions and "huh, going fast." They laughed. There is no lock on my door that I trust. I could put it in a drawer.

### Assistant
"Going fast" was a hint. Hints failed. Use the sentence. Then the drawer.

If the door lock is a joke, a drawer still beats a wet caddy. People grab what is in the steam. They are less likely to tour your dresser for conditioner. If they would, you have a bigger roommate problem than hair.

Ask for replacement of the current bottle in the same talk. "Please replace this one. After that, it's in my room." If they replace it with the 99-cent version, you can say that's not the same and you will keep buying yours for you. You do not have to accept a swap that wrecks your hair. I will not turn that into medical advice. You already know your head.

Put a cheap backup in the caddy if you want guests to have something. That spare is the hotel. Yours is not.

Laughing was the dodge. Do not laugh this time. You can be pleasant. Pleasant and removing the bottle can happen in one minute.

### User
They just used it again. I heard the shower. The bottle is still in the caddy because I haven't moved it. I feel like if I move it now I'm being retaliatory.

### Assistant
You are late, not retaliatory. Move it when the shower ends, or now if you can do it without a scene. Wet hands will find another bottle tomorrow.

Say it when they come out, towel, hallway: the sentence, then the bottle walks with you. If they call you petty, you can say, "It's my shampoo." Petty is a word people use when a perk ends.

Buy your next bottle and unbox it in your room. Do not restock the caddy with the good one "just this week." That is how hotels reopen.

You are not a minibar. Minibars have prices. You had a caddy and hope. Hope is done. Drawer, sentence, optional replacement ask. Then wash your hair with the product you paid for. That is the entire luxury. It was supposed to be ordinary. Make it ordinary again by taking it out of the steam tonight, not after one more "ran out." The shower is over. The bottle can walk. Do that before the next steam cycle starts.
