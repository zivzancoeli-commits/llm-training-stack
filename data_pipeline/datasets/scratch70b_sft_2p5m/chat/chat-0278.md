### User
The bass is coming through my floor from 3A. It's 10:34 p.m. I can feel it in the mug on the desk. I have never knocked on a neighbor for noise. I wrote a note last week and didn't slide it under. I work at 7. They moved in in June. We nod at the mailbox. I don't know if they're having a party or if this is Tuesday. I don't want to be the building crank. I also cannot feel my heartbeat over a kick drum.

### Assistant
A mug vibrating is data. Building crank is a story. 10:34 on a work-at-7 night is a fair time to ask for less bass.

Note you didn't slide is a draft. Tonight you need a door or a text if you have the group app. First time can be one sentence, not a history of your unsent paper.

I'm not going to invent a quiet-hours ordinance. I don't know your city or lease. I can tell you bass travels through floors in a way voices don't. Asking for a floor-facing speaker to move or a sub to go down is specific. "Turn down the music" is vague and starts a debate about taste.

Is it a repeating thud, or a full song you could name if you hated yourself for knowing it?

### User
Repeating thud, like a workout mix, not a party laugh track. No voices. Lights are on. I don't have their number. Mailbox nod is the relationship. I put socks on to go downstairs. I took them off. I put them on again. It's 10:36 now.

### Assistant
Socks on. Go. 10:36 is still a human hour, not a 2 a.m. raid. Workout mix without voices is often one person with a speaker on the floor. They may not know the mug shakes.

For the next ten minutes: downstairs, knock, step back so you're not in their face. "Hi, I'm 4A. The bass is coming through my floor pretty hard. Could you turn it down or off the floor? I work early." Then wait. If they apologize, "thanks" and you leave. Don't lecture June, the unsent note, or your heartbeat.

If they don't hear the knock, knock once more, not a concert. If still nothing, the unsent note can become a sent note tonight: same sentences, your unit, today's date. Slide it. Then earplugs.

Don't call the cops on a thud at 10:36 unless something else is happening you haven't said. That's a jump. Try the door.

Mailbox nod can survive a polite knock. It dies harder if you wait until you're vicious at 1 a.m.

### User
What if they invite me in to "hear it's not that loud" in their room? It won't be loud in their room. That's how bass works. I'll look crazy standing there with no proof.

### Assistant
You don't go in. You don't need to sit on their couch for a science demo. "I believe you it sounds fine in there. It's the floor at my desk. Please turn it down anyway." Then you leave. Proof is the mug, which you don't have to fetch. Crazy is a word for people who feel vibration and speak. You felt it. You spoke.

If they refuse, you go home, note, earplugs, morning email to whoever manages the building with times. I won't promise management. You still sleep.

First time asking is allowed to be awkward. Awkward is two minutes in a hallway. The thud in the mug is the rest of the night if you take the socks off again.

10:36. Walk. Knock. One specific ask. No party accusation. No unsent-note memoir. Then your own door, fan, early alarm. You can nod at the mailbox tomorrow either way. The nod is cheaper if they slept on it and turned the sub down. Give them that chance tonight while it's still Tuesday and not a myth about cranks. Put the socks on and keep them on until you've knocked. That's the whole first time. The bass can lose. The mug can go still. You can work at 7. Go.
