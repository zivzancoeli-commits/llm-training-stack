### User
I intern at the neighborhood association and I just sat through a two-hour meeting about a "protected bike facility" on Maple. My notes look like a ransom letter. People said ROW, mode shift, level of traffic stress, daylighting, and something about a road diet that made a guy in a fleece stand up. My boss wants a one-page summary by morning. I don't want to copy the jargon and I don't want to pick a side. Translate this like I'm new, because I am.

### Assistant
Start with what would change on the street, in street words. A protected bike facility usually means a bike lane with a physical buffer — posts, a curb, parked cars as a wall — not just paint. A road diet usually means fewer car lanes so there's room for that buffer. "ROW" is right-of-way: the public strip that already includes sidewalks, planting, parking, and asphalt. They're arguing how to slice a pie they own, not seizing houses.

Daylighting: keep corners clear of parked cars so people can see each other. Level of traffic stress: would a regular person bike here without feeling hunted. Mode shift: more trips by bike or bus instead of by car. Vocabulary, not a verdict.

Your page needs four blocks: what staff proposed, what neighbors feared, what was undecided, and the next chance to talk. No adjectives like vibrant. Bosses smell intern adjectives.

What did they propose — parking removed, a lane removed, or paint plus posts?

### User
Staff drawing: one car travel lane each way instead of two, center turn lane gone, parking kept on the grocery side, parking removed on the school side for a two-way bike path with plastic posts. Fleece guy said it would "destroy level of service" and backup the signal at 12th. A parent said school drop-off already eats the curb. A shop said they'll die without the parking. A cyclist said paint-only is how her friend got doored. Nobody voted. There's a "comment period" and a "second reading" I didn't understand.

### Assistant
Write it like this.

Proposal: Maple drops to one car lane each way. Center turn lane goes. Parking stays by the grocery. School-side parking comes out for a two-way bike lane with posts, not just paint.

Staff's case, plain: they think the street is wide enough to trade car lanes for a buffered path, and posts keep bikes out of the door zone.

Pushback, plain: fewer car lanes can mean longer waits at 12th. Losing school-side parking collides with drop-off. Shops worry driving customers will skip them. "Level of service" is engineer-speak for how delayed cars are. Fleece guy was saying the light will feel worse. He might be right about delay. You don't settle that. If staff didn't show a count, write "staff said delay would be acceptable; a resident disagreed; no vote."

Dooring: a car door opening into a bike lane. Paint next to parking is where that happens. Posts are the proposed fix. Describe, don't campaign.

Comment period: people can send written opinions. Second reading: the board looks again, closer to a decision. If you missed dates, write "confirm with clerk." Don't invent Tuesday.

Skip "the community was divided." List the three objections: signal delay, school curb, store parking.

### User
Boss also wants "complete streets" in English because it was on slide one and she hates that phrase. And a sentence for when someone emails the intern account mad.

### Assistant
Complete streets, no brochure: design the road so it isn't only for cars. Walking, biking, buses, driving, crossing all get a turn in the design — not equal space on every block. Maple's fight is which turn gets the extra feet.

Mad email: "Thanks for writing. I'm an intern. I'm forwarding this so it goes in the comment record. I can't change the design from this inbox." Then forward it. Don't debate the light in a side thread. Don't promise parking back.

Keep the tone boring. Boring is professional. Last pass: no fake numbers. If staff flashed a percent and you didn't write it down, omit it.

That's the intern job: translate the pie-slicing, list the fights, flag the calendar, stay off the battlefield.
