### User
The family thread was about who brings rolls. Then auntie said "some of us make an effort to be together." Then three people liked it. Then my mom privately texted "don't start." I already said I can do Friday dinner but not the whole weekend because I work Saturday. It's Wednesday night. The thread is still going. I haven't opened the last four messages. I can see previews about "traditions" and my name.

### Assistant
You don't have to open four guilt previews on a Wednesday to be a decent relative. Friday dinner is already an effort. A whole weekend is a different product. They are allowed to want it. You are allowed to work Saturday.

Don't start a thesis in the group. That's how "don't start" happens. One message, when you choose to type: "I'll be there Friday. I work Saturday so I can't stay the weekend. Looking forward to rolls." Then you can mute.

I won't invent who "should" host or what tradition legally requires. There is no law in this thread. There are feelings and a Saturday shift.

Is the private "don't start" aimed at you answering, or at you picking a fight with auntie?

### User
Aimed at me answering auntie. Last year I wrote a long message and it became Christmas. I work 7 to 3 Saturday. I could drive over after, but I'd arrive at 4 into leftover arguments. I don't want to. I also don't want to be the empty chair people photograph.

### Assistant
After a 7 to 3 shift, a 4 p.m. arrival into leftover arguments is volunteer punishment. Skip it. Empty chairs get photographed. So do people who showed up Friday. You can be in the Friday photos and absent from the Saturday ones. That's a schedule, not a referendum.

For the next ten minutes: don't read the four previews if they'll hook you. Draft the Friday-only line in the notes app, paste it into the group, send, mute overnight. If you need to tell mom, do it in the private thread: "I'm not debating auntie. I already said Friday." That's alignment, not a secret alliance.

If someone @'s you with traditions, you don't have to define tradition. You can repeat Friday. Repeats are boring. Boring ends campaigns slower than essays, but it ends them with less shrapnel.

Don't offer a maybe-Saturday-if-I'm-not-tired. Tired will arrive. The maybe will be screenshotted as a yes.

### User
There's a cousin who will call me. She does the voice that says I'm breaking mom's heart. Mom's heart in this story is actually auntie's paragraph.

### Assistant
If the cousin calls, you don't have to pick up during the mute. A voice note of heartbreak is still a story. When you talk, keep the same sentence: Friday yes, Saturday work, not doing the weekend. If she says you're breaking mom's heart, you can say "Mom and I already talked" if that's true, or "I'm not going to argue this on the phone" if it isn't. Then hang up like a person who has a Wednesday.

I can't tell you how your mom actually feels. A private "don't start" suggests she wants less thread, not more of you on Saturday. Trust the private text more than the cousin's narration.

Put the phone on a dresser. Work Saturday exists. Rolls exist. Auntie's paragraph can live without your rebuttal. You already chose Friday. Send that, once, in writing, and let the likes collect on someone else's bubble. The empty chair on Saturday is allowed. So is sleeping after a 7 a.m. shift instead of driving toward an argument that started on Wednesday about bread.

If you want one extra kindness that is not a weekend, bring the rolls on Friday and leave at a time you name out loud: "I need to go at 9 because of work tomorrow." Named exits beat fading into the hallway while someone reloads the tradition speech. You can hug people and still go. The group text does not get Saturday just because it learned your name. Mute, send Friday, sleep. That is a complete holiday plan for a person who has a 7 a.m. clock.
