### User
I have a library book. It was due two weeks ago. I've been carrying it in my bag like a crime. The email says "overdue," "fine," "privilege blocked," and I don't know which of those are real and which are scary letterhead. I'm new here. Talk like I'm the intern who doesn't know the building yet.

### Assistant
I'll translate the email into ordinary speech, and I won't invent your library's prices or rules. Those are theirs. Different libraries mean different things by the same words.

"Overdue" means you kept the book past the day they asked for it back. That's the whole idea. Not a criminal charge. A late return.

"Fine" means they may charge money for the extra time. I will not tell you how much. It might be small, it might be more, it might be waived, it might be capped. The email is not a bill I can read from here. You find the amount in your account or at the desk.

"Privilege blocked" is extra jargon. Plain version: some libraries pause your ability to borrow more items until you return the late stuff or settle what they say you owe. It does not mean you are banned from the building. It does not mean you should hide the book. It usually means the computer won't let you check out the next thing until this one is handled.

Two weeks is late. It is also common. Staff have seen this today already. Your job is to walk in with the book, not to write an apology essay in the lobby.

Do you still have the physical book, and is the account in your name?

### User
Yes. Book is in the bag. Account is mine. I renewed it in my head and never pressed the button. I also don't know if I should go to the desk or the drop box like a coward.

### Assistant
If you only drop it in the box, the book is back, which is the main thing. The account stuff might still be sitting there until the box is emptied and the computer notices. If you want the scary words gone faster, the desk is better: they can take the book, tell you in plain language what the account says, and you can ask what "blocked" means for you, today, at this library.

You're not a coward for using the box. You're just delaying the conversation. If the building is open and you have ten minutes, desk is cleaner.

What to say, intern-plain: "This is two weeks late. Can you take it and tell me if I owe anything or if I can't borrow." That's it. You don't need the life story about the button you didn't press. They don't need "I usually never" either. Everyone usually never until they do.

Words they might use next:

"Grace period" — a short window after the due date when they don't charge. I don't know if yours has one, or if two weeks is past it. Ask: "Did I miss a grace period?" If they say they don't do that, that's an answer, not a moral.

"Lost" — sometimes after a long time they mark a book lost and charge a replacement. Two weeks is often not that, but I will not swear it isn't. If they say lost, you show them the book in your hand. The book in your hand is the argument.

"Collections" or "sent to billing" — bigger-library scare language for unpaid stuff that got old. For two weeks and a book you're holding, you're probably not in a movie. Still, don't nod at words you don't understand. "What does that mean for me, in normal words" is an allowed sentence.

I won't quote a fine per day. If they name a number, that's their number. You can pay it if you can, or ask whether they waive first-time or student or whatever category you actually are. Don't invent a category. Don't cry on purpose. Don't joke that you stole it. Jokes like that land weird at desks.

### User
What if the intern at the desk is as new as me and we both panic.

### Assistant
Then you keep it physical. Book on the counter. Due date if it's stamped. Your ID if they ask. "Please check this in." Computers are for this. If they freeze, they can get a supervisor. That's not you failing. That's the building working like a building.

Go today. The bag-crime feeling is just an unread email plus a book. Return the object. Ask what the words mean for your account. Leave with either a zero or a number they stated, not a number you imagined. That's the whole errand, without the letterhead.
