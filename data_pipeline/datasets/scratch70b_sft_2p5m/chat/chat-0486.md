### User
I came home to a wet windowsill, a damp curtain, and a paperback that is now a wave. My roommate left the living room window cranked because they like "air." It was raining sideways. This is the third time. I already put the book on the radiator like an idiot. I need a way to talk to them that isn't a laminated chore chart and isn't me slamming the sash every time the sky looks funny.

### Assistant
The window is a household tool, not a personality test. Rain coming in is a closed-window problem. You can say that without a manifesto about airflow.

Tonight, dry what you can: towel the sill, move the curtain, stand the book on its edges if you still want it. I won't pretend a radiator is a conservation lab. If the book is already waved, that's a loss. Don't spend the evening performing grief at the furniture.

Then you talk once, in person if you live together, short. "When you leave the window open in rain, stuff on the sill gets wrecked. Please close it when it rains, or before you leave." That is the whole thesis. Third time means they already had data. You are not surprising them with weather.

If they live for "air," offer a true alternative you can live with: crack it two inches under an overhang, or open it when you're home and the sky is not throwing water. Don't offer six alternatives. One is enough.

Are they usually home during storms, or do they leave and assume you'll be the weather service?

### User
They leave for work early and I get back later. The window is often how they "reset the apartment." They said last time I was being dramatic because "it's just water." The paperback was mine. Their stuff was on a higher shelf.

### Assistant
"Just water" is easy to say about someone else's paperback. You can name that without a courtroom: "It was my book on the sill. I'm not being dramatic. I'm asking you to close the window when you leave if rain is in the forecast." Forecast is free. They can look at a phone the same way they look at a window.

Because they leave first, the rule has to live at their exit, not at your return. Ask for a specific habit: last person out checks the living room window. If that person is them every weekday, then it is their check. You can offer to text a rain alert once as a courtesy this week, not as a forever job. If you become the alert system, they will never look up.

If they refuse, you still get to protect the sill. Move your things off it. That is not surrender of the principle; it is not leaving bait. You can also close the window when you leave even if they opened it, without a speech. Closing a window is allowed in a shared living room.

I will not invent whose insurance covers a warped floor. If you see actual pooling or staining that worries you, you photograph it for yourselves and you mention it once as "the sill is staying wet." I won't guess what a landlord will do. A dry sill is still the immediate fix.

Don't laminate a chart unless you both already like charts. A chart after "you're dramatic" will feel like a prosecution exhibit. A sentence plus a closed window is stronger.

### User
Do I replace the book and send them the receipt?

### Assistant
Only if you want money more than you want the next month to be livable. A receipt can be fair and it can also turn this into a tiny collections case. I will not tell you a price or that they legally owe you. If the book was cheap and you can let it go, say "please don't wreck the next one" and move your stack. If it was hard to replace and you want the cost, ask once, calmly: "That book is ruined. Can you cover a replacement?" Then accept yes, no, or a partial without a week of screenshots.

Tonight: dry the sill, close the window, one conversation tomorrow morning before they leave. Rain is not a personality. It's a latch. Use the latch.
