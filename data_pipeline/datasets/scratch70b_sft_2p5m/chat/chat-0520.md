### User
It's 12:40 a.m. A party bus is idling under my window with the door open and a playlist. People keep getting on and off like it's a nightclub with a parking brake. I have to work at 8. I already tried the closed window. I can still hear the bass and the laugh that happens every time someone almost falls. I don't want to go downstairs in pajamas and become a story. I also don't want to lie here naming songs.

### Assistant
You don't have to go downstairs in pajamas. You also don't have to memorize the playlist.

Tonight's tools, in order of how much body you spend: earplugs or a fan; a call or text to a non-emergency number if your city has one for noise; a building manager after-hours line if you have one. I will not tell you which number will care. I don't know your block. I will not promise a cop. I will say a log of "party bus, [street], 12:40 a.m., still idling" is more useful than adjectives if you do call.

Don't go argue with drunk people around a bus. That's how pajama stories happen, and not the funny kind.

If you can see a company name on the bus, write it down. Tomorrow that name is for a complaint to the company or the building, not for a midnight debate.

Is this a one-off drop-off, or has the same bus been a regular?

### User
First time this month. They've been there twenty minutes. It feels like they're waiting for one more person who is never coming. My neighbor across the hall already banged a window. The bus honked. So the war has started without me.

### Assistant
Good. Stay out of the war. A honk is them telling you they are not in a listening mood. Your neighbor chose a window bang. You can choose earplugs and a number, or only earplugs.

If you call, you stay inside. You give the location, the vehicle description, the time. You don't narrate your neighbor's bang. You don't go outside to "help" the situation. Help at 12:40 near an idling party bus is how you become a third noise.

If you don't call, that's allowed too. Twenty minutes might end. Forty might not. Set a personal cutoff: if it's still there at 1:10 and you cannot sleep, then you use the number. A cutoff stops you from checking the curtain every ninety seconds.

Don't post a photo of people's faces in a neighborhood app at 1 a.m. That's extra heat. A photo of the license plate saved on your phone is for a company complaint tomorrow if you want one.

I will not invent idling laws. Some places care, some don't. The company's dispatch might care more than a proverb.

Work at 8: if you can move to a room that isn't the street, do that even if it's a couch. Pride about your own bed is expensive tonight.

### User
If I call, will I have to go down and point?

### Assistant
You can ask the operator whether you need to meet anyone. Often you don't. If they say someone will come, you can choose to stay inside and describe from the window. You don't have to be a tour guide in socks.

Tonight: stay in, earplugs, cutoff time, number if it holds, no pajama summit. Tomorrow, if you have a company name, a short daylight message. You don't need to win the street. You need enough quiet to be a person at 8. Let the bus be a vehicle, not your midnight job. If it leaves at 12:50, you don't owe the night a sequel. If it stays, you use the number you already picked. Curtains, not combat. Sleep is the only score that matters at 8. Pajamas stay inside. The playlist can finish without you tonight, easily.
