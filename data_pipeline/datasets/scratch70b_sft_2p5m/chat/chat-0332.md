### User
Mara asked me to text her ex for her. Not a "are you okay" text. She wants me to tell him she needs space and that he should stop writing, and she wants it to come from my number so he doesn't have her unread messages sitting there. She said she's "bad at endings" and that I'm the only one he still respects. I barely know the guy. We met twice. I don't want to be the messenger, and I also don't want Mara crying on my couch because I said no. She wants the text tonight.

### Assistant
No. You are not a ghosting service.

Mara can send her own message, or she can not send one, or she can mute him. What she cannot rent is your name and your number. Once you text him, you are in the thread. He will reply to you. He will ask you questions Mara does not want answered. You will be the person who "knows what's going on." That is a job you did not apply for.

You can still be a friend. Friendship here is helping her write the message she sends, sitting with her while she sends it, or helping her block and mute if she is scared. Friendship is not laundering an ending through a third phone.

A clean no: "I'm not contacting him for you. I'll help you draft something from your number, or I'll stay on the phone while you send it. I'm not going to be the middle."

If she is unsafe, that is a different conversation — leave, document, people whose job is safety. You have not described that.

Has he been blowing up her phone in a way that feels threatening, or is this mostly that she hates being the one to say it's over?

### User
Mostly she hates saying it. He texted twice yesterday, normal stuff, asking if they were still on for Saturday. She hasn't answered. She says if I do it he'll listen because I'm "calm." She also said if I don't, she'll just leave him on read forever and I should be fine with that.

### Assistant
Leaving him on read forever is her choice. It is a messy choice. It is still hers. You do not have to improve her mess by inserting yourself.

"Calm" is flattery with a task attached. You can be calm in her living room. You do not have to be calm in his inbox.

Help her send one ordinary message tonight if she actually wants Saturday called off. Draft on her phone, from her: "I need to cancel Saturday. I'm not going to keep seeing you. Please don't text about plans." Short. No essay about growth. No "it's not you." If she cannot tap send, she can sit with the draft until she can. You can stay. You can also leave at a normal hour. You are not a hostage to her unread dots.

If she refuses to send anything and wants you to carry it, repeat the no. Do not bargain into "just this once." There is no once. There is a precedent.

Do not text him a private explanation either. "Hey this is Mara's friend, she's not coming" is the same job in a costume.

If she threatens the friendship over this, believe the threat enough to notice it, and still do not take the job. A friend who needs you to impersonate a breakup is asking you to be useful, not close.

### User
She just sent a voice note that she's drafting something mean and she wants me to "tone it down but still send it from my phone so it lands." I haven't opened the draft. I feel like if I open it I'm already in.

### Assistant
Opening a draft on her phone is not the same as sending from yours. You can look, you can say "this is too mean," you can help her cut it to the cancel-Saturday line, and you can still refuse to be the sender.

Do not rewrite it onto your device. Do not "tone it down" into a version you then fire from your number. That is how you become the author.

If the draft is cruel, say so once: "I'm not helping you send that. If you want to end it, send the short version from you." Then stop editing. You are not her PR firm.

Tonight's order: tell her you will not contact him; offer to sit while she sends a short message from her phone; leave if the loop starts again. You can care that she is sad and still keep your number out of his life. Those are not opposites. They are the boundary. Let Saturday get cancelled by the person who made Saturday.
