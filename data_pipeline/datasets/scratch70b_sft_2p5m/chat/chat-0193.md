### User
Tomorrow is my first standup at this job. I started Monday. I have sat in two of them already and mostly said "still ramping, no blockers" and then stared at my notes like they were a shield. Tomorrow they will expect a real sentence. I am sixteen on a summer internship and everyone else sounds like they have been shipping since they were twelve. I do not want to sound like a mascot and I also do not want to fake a status. How do I talk in that circle without looking lost?

### Assistant
Hold on. Before I coach the sentence, I need one fact that changes the plan.

Is this standup a round-robin where each person speaks once, or a free-for-all where people jump in when they have something?

### User
Round-robin. Camera on. We go in the same order every day. I am near the end, after two senior people who talk in tickets and after my manager who talks in "we." I get maybe twenty seconds. Yesterday someone asked if I needed anything and I said "I think I'm good" which was a lie. I did not know what I was allowed to need.

### Assistant
Then you do not invent a status. You report what is true in twenty seconds.

A standup is a traffic report, not a review. Fluent people are often just listing: what I touched, what I will touch, what is in my way. You can use that on day four.

Try this shape out loud tonight so it is not new on camera:

"Yesterday I finished local setup and read the ticket my mentor assigned. Today I will try the first small change. Blocker: I am not sure which branch. I will ask Sam after this."

Three beats: past, next, obstacle. You named a person. You did not claim you understood the whole system.

"Still ramping" is fog. Drop it. Specific is kinder because people can actually help.

If they ask "need anything?" do not say you are good unless you are. Try: "Yes. Ten minutes with whoever owns the test folder, so I do not break the suite."

### User
What if I have nothing finished? I spent yesterday fighting the login on the laptop and reading a wiki page that contradicted itself. I have no "shipped" anything. Also Sam might not be in after standup. Then I look like I promised a meeting I cannot have.

### Assistant
Then you report the laptop and the wiki. Those are work. Standup is not only for people who merged code.

"Yesterday I spent most of the day getting the laptop on the network and reading the setup wiki. Two steps did not match the screen. Today I will start the first ticket once login works. Blocker: I still cannot get past the internal login page. I will message the IT channel right after this."

That is honest. It also lets someone say "use the VPN toggle" in five seconds, which is the point of saying blockers out loud.

Do not promise Sam if Sam is a maybe. "I will ask in intern Slack" is a plan. "I will ask Sam" is a plan only if you have seen Sam exist after 9:40.

If people talk in ticket numbers and you do not have one yet, use the title. Numbers are shorthand, not a membership test. They are listening for whether you are stuck in silence.

### User
Okay. So I pick one true yesterday, one true today, one true stuck thing, and I do not say ramping. What if my voice shakes? I hate that. It makes me sound younger than I already am.

### Assistant
Voice shake is not a status. Ignore it like a cough. Keep the sentences short. Write the three lines on a sticky note and read them if you have to. A true note beats a fake improvisation.

Look at the camera, not your own tiny face. Start talking in the first second so you do not spend the pause inventing a better personality.

After you speak, stop. Do not add "sorry if that was dumb." The room will already have moved on. Take that.

Tomorrow you are not supposed to sound like you have shipped since age twelve. You are supposed to say what happened, what is next, and what is in the way. That is the twenty seconds. The rest is sending the IT message after the call, not only announcing it.
