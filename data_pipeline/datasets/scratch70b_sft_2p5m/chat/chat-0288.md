### User
We're an hour into a hike. It's warm. Dan showed up with snacks and no bottle. He said he "hydrates in the car" and he'll "just sip if someone extraed." Nobody extraed. I have one liter for me. Two other people have their own. Dan keeps joking about my "tactical water." I don't want him to faint and I don't want to be the canteen.

### Assistant
He did not forget. He outsourced his water to the group and covered it with a joke. You can still be decent without becoming the canteen.

Do not pour him half your liter in one go to keep the peace. An hour in, you do not know the rest of the trail, the heat, or whether anyone is slower than they admitted. Your job is your body first, then a plan for him that does not empty the group's supply.

I will not guess the remaining distance or whether this trail has a stream you should drink from. Untreated water is its own risk, and I am not going to bless a creek.

Is turning around still realistic, or are you past a point where going back is as long as going on? And did anyone in the group agree to be the person who carries extra, or did Dan just assume?

### User
There's a junction in twenty minutes that can loop us back to the lot in about the same time as the original loop. Nobody agreed to extra. Dan is already a little pink. He's still laughing.

### Assistant
The junction is the decision, not the summit. Tell the group before you hit it, out loud, not in a side mutter: "Dan doesn't have water. I think we should take the short loop back." You are not putting him on trial. You are naming a constraint.

If he says he is fine, you can answer, "You might be. I'm not hiking further without a plan for water." That is you, not a lecture on his character. If two people want to continue and you do not, you are allowed to split at a marked junction. Splitting on an unknown trail is worse; splitting at a signed loop back to a lot is how adults abort.

Offer a measured sip, not a donation. "Two swallows, then we turn at the junction" is a boundary you can live with. If he refuses the turn and also wants your bottle, that is the moment you stop sharing. A joke about tactical water does not entitle him to your liter.

I do not know how pink is too pink. I am not going to diagnose heat illness. If he gets dizzy, confused, or stops sweating and looks worse, you treat it as get-to-the-car, not as a pride hike. Shade, sit, sip, turn back. If you have signal, one person can check a map while another stays with him. If you do not, you use the junction you already know.

Do not shame him in front of strangers on the trail. Save "bring a bottle or don't come" for the parking lot or a text tonight. Out here, logistics beat a TED talk.

### User
If we turn around he'll sulk the whole ride home. I already hate that I'm the one who has to say it.

### Assistant
Sulk is cheaper than a medical afternoon. Someone has to say it because he designed it that way. If you hate the role, say the line once and then talk to the junction, not to his mood.

For the next ten minutes: check how much you have left, offer the two swallows if he wants them, tell the group the short-loop plan before the junction, and start walking like the decision is already made. If the others agree, great. If they do not, you still get to take the loop; invite anyone who wants shade and a car to come with you.

Tonight, a short text to the group thread: "Next hike, no bottle, no spot. I'll bring extra only if we plan it." Not a manifesto. Dan can sulk. You can drink your own water.
