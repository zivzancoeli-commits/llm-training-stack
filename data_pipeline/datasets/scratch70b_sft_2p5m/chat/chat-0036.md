### User
I just walked into a potluck. I brought pasta salad. So did Priya. Same bowl energy, same olives, same "I didn't know." I want to sink into the floor. Last year I would have laughed too loud and then not eaten. Tell me what to do that isn't a bit.

### Assistant
Two options, then a recommendation.

Option one: name it out loud, light, and then move on. "We both did the pasta. Good, people can actually eat." You put both bowls down. You do not apologize for existing. You do not start a trial about who should have used the sign-up sheet. Duplicate food is a logistics oops, not a personality reveal.

Option two: quietly pivot your bowl. If there's still time and a kitchen, you change yours into something else with what's there — extra herbs, a different dressing, chopped leftover veg, served warm if theirs is cold — or you label yours with a true difference ("mine's no onion") if that is actually true. Don't invent a difference. Don't throw yours away to make hers the winner. Waste is a worse bit than two salads.

I'd take option one unless one of you has a dietary thing the other bowl doesn't cover. Two bowls of almost the same food is a gift to a hungry table. Last-year-you treated it like a spotlight. This-year-you can treat it like extra servings.

Did anyone actually gasp, or is the gasping only in your head?

### User
In my head. Priya said "twins!" and smiled. I said "oh my god I'm sorry" immediately. There's a sign-up thread I muted. That's on me.

### Assistant
You already did the too-loud apology. You can stop now. One "sorry" was enough; a second one makes her manage your feelings while she's holding a bowl.

Recommendation stays option one, with a small repair: next time you see her in the kitchen, "I muted the thread. That's why we doubled up. Yours looks good." Then you walk the bowl to the table. You don't need a speech at the group. People came to eat. They did not come to watch a reconciliation.

Don't compete on whose looks more homemade. Don't hover to see which bowl empties first. That's a scoreboard, and you will lose on purpose just to feel polite, which is a miserable way to spend a party.

If someone teases, you can agree with the fact. "Yep. Two pastas." Then you ask where plates are. Changing the subject is allowed. Explaining the mute button to the whole room is not required.

### User
There's a third person who brought garlic bread and is joking that we should start a restaurant. I don't want to be rude to them either. Also I haven't eaten. I always wait until it looks like other people have enough.

### Assistant
The restaurant joke is small talk. You can give it one smile and not build a brand with them. "We've got the salad covered" is plenty. If you keep riffing, you'll stay in the bit and still not eat.

Eat. The "wait until others have enough" habit is how you go home hungry and remember the night as a failure. There are two pasta salads. There is garlic bread. The table is not going to run out because you took a scoop. If you're worried about appearing greedy, take a normal plate, not a martyr plate. Martyr plates are still a bit.

If you want a tiny option-two move without a full pivot: put a spoon in each bowl so both look served, not like leftovers. That's hosting, not erasing yourself.

You're allowed to be a person who muted a thread and brought olives anyway. You're allowed to stand next to Priya without merging into her. Last year you would have narrated your shame until someone rescued you. This year you put the bowl down, you let the twin joke die, and you eat some of both if you want. That's the whole social skill: don't turn extra food into a story about your worth.

When you leave, you can take your empty-ish bowl without a farewell tour. If they ask you to take leftover pasta, take a little or leave it. Don't take both bowls home as penance. You didn't fail. You brought food to a potluck. So did she. The overlap is funny for ten seconds and then it's dinner.
