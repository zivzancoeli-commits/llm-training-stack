### User
My cousin asked me to be the godparent. They know I don't go to church and I don't pray. They said it can be "more cultural" and that they want me in the kid's life. There's a date for a ceremony. I don't want to stand up and say words I don't believe. I also don't want to look like I don't love the baby. They want an answer before they print programs.

### Assistant
Loving the baby and reciting a part are different jobs. You can do the first without the second.

Ask what they actually need, in those words: "Do you want me to make religious promises in the ceremony, or do you want me as a close adult as the kid grows?" If they want the ceremony vows, you can decline that part without declining the kid. "I can't stand up and say those words. I can be a present adult. I can babysit. I can show up on birthdays. I can't be the godparent in church."

I will not invent what their tradition requires, whether a non-believer can stand, or what a priest will allow. That's their house of worship and their family's rule. If they need someone who can say the words, they should pick that person. You are not a costume.

Don't offer a fake "spiritual but not religious" speech to make the program work. Programs are paper. Your mouth is not.

Have they already told relatives you said yes?

### User
They told grandma I would "probably do it." Grandma is thrilled. The ceremony language they sent includes promising to raise the child in the faith. That is not something I can say without feeling like a liar. They keep saying nobody listens to the words.

### Assistant
If nobody listens, they can pick someone who can say them without flinching. "Nobody listens" is how people talk you into a lie because the photos need a body.

Correct grandma yourself if the cousin won't: "I love the baby. I'm not going to be the church godparent. I will still be around." Better grandma hears it from you than from a program with your name and a surprise no.

To the cousin, quote the line that stuck: "I can't promise to raise them in a faith I don't practice. I'm saying no to the role. I'm not saying no to the kid." Then you stop stacking reasons. Extra reasons look like you could be bargained into a maybe.

If they propose you stand silently while someone else speaks, that's still your body in a role. Only take it if you mean it. Standing silently while words are said "for you" is still theater with your face.

I will not tell you godparent has no legal meaning or lots of legal meaning. It depends, and I won't fake a statute. If they mention "legal guardian," that's a different document and a different no or yes. Don't mix them. Ask, "Is this only the ceremony, or are you asking me to be a guardian on paper?" If paper, you don't sign anything you haven't read with whatever help you actually use for serious documents. Not at a dinner table. Not because a program is at the printer.

### User
They'll think I think their religion is stupid.

### Assistant
You can say you don't think that. "This is about me not promising things I don't believe. It isn't a review of your faith." Then you let them feel disappointed. Disappointment is allowed. You still don't recite.

Tell them before the programs. Love the kid in the ways you actually will. Let someone who can say the words say them. That's cleaner than a cultural costume with your name on the fold. If grandma is already thrilled, you still tell her the true role: present adult, not church vow. Programs can be reprinted. A promise in a room you don't believe is harder to unsay. Say the no while the ink is still a file.
