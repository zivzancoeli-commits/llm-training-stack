### User
Saturday I'm cooking for my partner's parents for the first time. They eat meat. I mostly don't. I said I had it handled because I didn't want to look twelve. I do not have it handled. I have a recipe for roast chicken I have never made, a pan I've never used, and a timeline I copied from a blog that assumes I own kitchen twine and "an instant-read thermometer I trust." I don't. Help me not bluff my way into dry poultry and a vibe.

### Assistant
Good. You already did the hard part: you admitted the roast is theater. Don't perform competence at people who will be in your kitchen. Perform a meal you can actually finish.

I don't know your oven. I don't know if their "we eat meat" means they will be offended by a meatless main or they just don't want a sad salad. I also don't know whether anyone has allergies. So I'm not going to pretend this chicken is a guaranteed win.

Safer Saturday: cook the thing you have already cooked well once, even if it's pasta plus a vegetable, and add one meat side you can buy already cooked if you have to — a rotisserie bird you reheat with a pan sauce, or sausages you brown and don't have to guess the inside of. If that feels like cheating, it's still food. Cheating is lying that you roasted it.

If you still want to try the chicken, treat it as an experiment with a backup already in the fridge. What do you already know how to make without a timer panic?

### User
I can make a huge tray of roasted vegetables that actually taste like something, and a rice thing with lemon. I can follow a cookie recipe. I cannot tell when chicken is done without cutting it and then it looks wounded. Partner said "don't do a science fair." Parents are polite. I think they'd eat vegetables. I think I wanted the roast because it photographs like I belong in an adult house.

### Assistant
Then skip the roast. Belonging is not a carcass. A full tray of vegetables, lemon rice, and a bird you didn't have to guess — rotisserie plus a simple pan gravy, or skip meat and put a block of good cheese and bread on the table — is an adult dinner. Polite parents eat. They also notice when the cook is vibrating.

Tell your partner the truth tonight, not Saturday at 4 p.m.: "I'm not roasting a raw chicken for your parents. I haven't done it. I'm doing vegetables, rice, and [backup]. If they need a meat centerpiece, we pick one up." If your partner wants the showpiece, they can cook it. That's not a fight. That's labor.

If anyone asks why there's no homemade roast, you say "I don't cook whole chickens yet." That's a complete sentence. Sharp people respect a true limit more than a shiny lie.

Cookies at the end are plenty of theater. Do those. You said you can follow that.

### User
What if they offer to "help" and then take over my stove. I get weird about people in my drawers. Also I don't know if I should ask about food issues now or if that's last-minute rude.

### Assistant
Ask about allergies and no-go foods now. A text: "Anything you don't eat? Any allergies?" That's hosting, not rudeness. Waiting until they arrive is how you find out someone can't have lemon when the rice is already lemon.

Help: give them a job that is not command of the stove. "Can you put ice in glasses" or "can you toss this salad" or "can you taste the rice for salt." If they start rearranging your oven, you can say "I've got this part, thanks." You don't need a speech about drawers. If they keep going, your partner can intercept. That's their parents. Use that.

I would not bluff a temperature. If you don't have a thermometer, you don't magically grow one by Saturday afternoon unless you buy one, and even then a first use on company night is a lot. Uncertainty here is information: you don't have the tool, so you don't take the job.

Set a timeline that assumes you are slow. Vegetables start early. Rice later. Cookies even later or earlier and cooled. You are not a restaurant. A gap where people drink water and talk is allowed.

If the vegetables come out too dark, you say they're extra roasted and you put yogurt or a sauce on the table. You don't apologize eight times. You eat. That's the whole adult trick, and it doesn't require twine.
