### User
I got a credit card fee I didn't expect. I need to tell my parent because they're helping with rent this month and they're going to see the statement vibe even if they don't see the PDF. I'm new at this — intern-new. I don't want to invent what the fee "usually" costs or pretend I understand the whole card contract. Tight budget. Help me explain and make a plan that doesn't assume extra money exists.

### Assistant
Tell the truth in intern English, not banker English.

I will not name a dollar amount. I don't have the statement. If you guess a "typical" late fee, you might lowball and look like you lied later.

Read the line. Write down the exact words — late fee, annual, interest, whatever it actually says. If you don't understand them, say that. "I don't know this one yet" beats a fake definition.

The plan is about not stacking new damage, not a heroic payoff you can't do.

Are they a cosigner, or just sending rent? And is the fee already posted, or a warning email?

### User
Posted. It's my card, not theirs. They send a rent chunk. If I short rent to cover the card, dinner gets weird. I think it's a late fee because I paid three days after the due date. I had the money, I just... intern brain. Calendar.

### Assistant
Late because of calendar still counts. Don't dress it up as the bank being mysterious if the due date was printed.

Explain: "I missed the due date by three days. There's a late fee. I don't want to guess the number until I read it off the statement. I'm not going to short the rent you send."

That's the honesty. The tight-budget plan has to fit money you already have, not fantasy overtime.

One: pay the minimum on time from now on, on a date two or three days earlier than due, with an alert. Interns miss due dates because they sit on the busiest weekday.

Two: if you can pay the fee from your own non-rent pot without bouncing food, do that. If you cannot, say so: "I can pay the regular amount and the fee waits until payday X," and X has to be a real payday.

Three: do not open a new card to "fix" this.

I won't quote APR. Look at the PDF. If interest is also there, name that line too, still without guessing.

Don't mix "I'm informing you" with "please pay this." If you need help, make it a separate sentence: "I am asking if you can help with this fee."

### User
They'll ask why I didn't set autopay. I didn't because I was scared it would pull rent money.

### Assistant
That's a real fear. Say it: "I didn't turn on autopay because I was afraid it would hit when rent needed the balance." That's cash-flow, not intern-stupid.

If you use autopay, make it a minimum or a fixed amount you know is there after rent. I won't pick your bank setting. Manual pay the morning after payday is fine if you actually do it. The failure mode is autopay on an empty account plus another fee, which I also will not price.

This month: list rent, food, transit, the card minimum, the fee as printed. If the fee doesn't fit, say "I made a late payment, here's the line, I'm not touching your rent chunk, here's the date I can clear the fee." If they offer extra, you can accept or not. Don't refuse help you need just to look independent.

You can call the number on the card and ask if a first-time late fee can be waived. I don't know if they will. Don't promise your parent they'll reverse it. Ask, then report the actual answer.

### User
I'll read the line off the statement, not guess, keep rent sacred, set an earlier pay alarm, and only ask them for money if I'm actually asking.

### Assistant
That's the intern plan that fits a tight month: named fee, no invented prices, rent untouched, calendar moved earlier than the due date. You missed three days. You don't have to miss the explanation.
