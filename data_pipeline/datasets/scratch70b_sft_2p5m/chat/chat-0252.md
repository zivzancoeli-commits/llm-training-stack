### User
The right hinge on my glasses snapped on the sidewalk at 5:18. One lens is in my palm. The bus I need is the 14 or the 32 depending on the time, and I cannot read the headsign from the stop. It's getting dim. I have a tape measure in my bag from work, no tape. I have a hair tie. People are boarding something. I might be late to dinner at 6. I feel like a cartoon.

### Assistant
You're not a cartoon. You're a person with one lens and a dim street. Sit or stand with your back to a wall so you're not in the boarding shove.

Hold the frames on with the hair tie: loop around the temple and your head, or around the broken hinge as a crude wrap if the arm still exists. It will look odd. It may keep the left lens in front of your left eye. That's the one you use to read a number if you step closer.

Don't tape with a tape measure. That's a joke your bag played on you.

I won't invent the bus map. I don't know whether the 14 or 32 is next. Ask a human: "Is this the 14?" at the door. Drivers hear that all evening.

Can you still see well enough with the left lens on your face, or are you basically guessing at shapes?

### User
Left lens works if I hold the frame there with two fingers. If I let go it hangs. The hair tie idea might work. I don't want to ask the whole line. Dinner is at my friend's fourth-floor walk-up. I need the 32, I think, after 5:30. It is 5:22 now. I think.

### Assistant
5:22 means you have minutes, not a philosophy seminar. Hair-tie the left side so two fingers can retire. Then you only need one hand for the phone.

For the next ten minutes: put the spare lens in a pocket wrapped in a receipt so it doesn't grind. Open your transit app if the screen is readable at arm's length, or ask one person, not the whole line: "Which bus is this?" Point at the vehicle at the curb. One person. If they don't know, ask the driver as you hold the doors with a question, not with your body.

If you need the 32 after 5:30, wait rather than board a maybe-14. A wrong bus in dim light is a longer cartoon. Confirm the number out loud. "Thirty-two?" Wait for a yes.

Text the friend: "Glasses broke. Might be 15 late. Don't wait on the stairs." Fourth-floor walk-up plus a late guest is how people sit on steps. Let them know.

I don't know your stop names. If the app is a blur, use voice assistant if you already know how, or trust the driver's yes.

### User
What do I do with the loose lens until I can get this fixed? Also I'm embarrassed to board holding my face.

### Assistant
Loose lens: pocket, not palm. If you have a hard case, even better. If not, a receipt fold is enough for a bus ride. Don't wrap it in tissues that shed. Tomorrow, call wherever you got the glasses, or any optical shop, and ask about a hinge repair or loaners. I won't invent their prices or whether they can do it same day.

Boarding holding your face is allowed. Drivers have seen worse at 5:30. Hair tie plus one lens is a system. You can sit near the front if the number was hard, so you can ask "this still the 32?" if the route branches. I don't know if it branches. If you're unsure, sit where you can hear stops.

Go. Tie, pocket the spare, confirm 32, text the fourth floor. Dinner can start without your perfect face. You need the right bus more than you need to look assembled. The hinge can be a Monday problem. The headsign is a now problem. Ask the door. Get on the yes.
