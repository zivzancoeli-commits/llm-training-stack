### User
They started clapping at me in a staff meeting because I "survived" a project. I did not want a clap. I wanted to sit down. My face went hot and I smiled like a hostage. First time I have ever wanted to decline applause while it was happening. I don't want a speech about humility. I want out of the noise without looking like I hate my team.

### Assistant
You can hate the clap and still like the team. Those can sit in the same chair. Declining applause midstream is mostly body, then one line.

Hands: a small wave down, palms toward the floor, the universal "that's enough." You do not need a microphone. If you have a voice, "We're good — thanks, let's keep going" is enough. You are not scolding them. You are closing a loop. I will not invent a workplace rule about recognition. I will not invent that you must perform gratitude. You already did the project. The clap is extra theater.

If a manager is leading it, look at them and say, "I'd rather not do a round. Can we move on?" Managers can stop a clap faster than you can. Ask them to spend that power.

Hostage smile: drop it when you can. Neutral is allowed. You do not owe a bow.

Has it already ended, or are you asking for the next time they do this?

### User
It ended. I sat down too fast and knocked a water. They laughed kindly. Now someone put "clap for Sam" as a joke on the retro agenda for Friday. I want that line gone. I also don't want to be the person who cannot take a compliment.

### Assistant
Taking a compliment is "thanks." A scheduled clap is a bit. Get the agenda line off.

Message the retro owner: "Please take 'clap for Sam' off the agenda. I didn't want the applause and I don't want a sequel." If that person is a jokester, say it twice: off the agenda, not a bit. Then if it still appears, say it in the first minute of Friday: "Skip that item. I'm good without a clap." Early is kinder than sitting through a drumroll you already refused.

You are not required to enjoy being a mascot for survival. Survival was the work. Work can be thanked in a sentence. "Nice job on X" in Slack is a compliment. Hands making noise while you knock water is a show. You can take the first and decline the second.

If they say you cannot take a compliment, you can say, "I can. I don't want a round of applause." Then you stop defending your personality. Defense is how retro becomes a roast.

### User
My skip-level was in the room and clapped the longest. I don't want them to think I'm ungrateful in a packet somewhere.

### Assistant
A skip-level's long clap is still not a performance review by itself. If you are worried about a packet, you can send one dry note: "Thanks for the kind words on [project]. I'm not comfortable with applause in the meeting — I'd rather the work just go in the doc." That puts gratitude and the preference in the same place. I will not promise what they file. I will promise a hostage smile files worse than a clear sentence.

Friday: agenda line gone, or spoken skip. Sit still. Do not knock the water by bolting. If they start anyway, palms down, "We're good." Then you ask a question about the next slide so the room has a new job. A question is an exit ramp.

First time is already over. You survived the clap the same way you survived the project: you were there, it was loud, now you want it finished. Finish it in the agenda. Finish it in a note if you need the packet to hear you. You can be grateful and still not be a scene. Thanks in writing. Quiet in the chair. No encore. Let them clap for someone who wants it. You wanted to sit down. Sit.
