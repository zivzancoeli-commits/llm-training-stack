### User
Airport security line, 40 minutes easy. Phone at 4 percent. Power bank light is dead. I need the boarding pass in the wallet app and a text from my ride on the other end. It's 6:20 a.m. My charging brick is in the checked bag because I thought the bank would last. Shoes are already in the bin. I can see the scanners. I don't want to be the person holding up the belt hunting for paper.

### Assistant
You're already in it. Don't start unpacking the checked-bag regret. That's a later story. Right now: boarding pass first, ride text second, games on the phone never.

If the wallet pass won't load at 4 percent, switch the phone to low power if it isn't, drop brightness, kill the maps that are chewing the battery in your pocket. Get the pass on screen before you hit 1 percent. Screenshot it if you can; a screenshot sometimes still opens when the app is cranky. If you have an email with a barcode, open that and screenshot too.

I won't invent TSA rules. I don't know whether this airport wants phones in bins or pockets. Follow the sign in front of your face. Don't argue with a bin about policy.

Do you have the confirmation code in email without opening the airline app, or is the wallet the only copy?

### User
There's an email. Opening it made the phone hiccup. I saw the code, six characters. I didn't screenshot yet. The ride is my sibling. They said they'd wait at arrivals. If I go dark they might think I missed the flight. Bin is moving. I still have socks in my hand.

### Assistant
Socks on, phone out, screenshot now while the bin crawls. Email with barcode, wallet pass, six-character code written on your boarding-pass sleeve or a receipt in pen if you have one. Analog backup is ugly and it survives a black screen.

For the next ten minutes: low power, screenshot, tell your sibling one line before you die: "In security, phone dying, flight on time, see you at arrivals." Then airplane the phone or shut it off after the scanners if you can still walk without it. A dark phone that already sent the text is better than 1 percent refreshing the gate map.

If the screen dies at the podium, say the six characters and your name. People do this when phones fail. It is embarrassing and it works more often than standing silent. I can't promise the airline's system. I can tell you a code plus ID is the usual pair.

Don't hold the belt to rummage. Step aside after the scanner if you need to put socks on. Holding the belt is how you get the glare you were trying to avoid.

Checked-bag charger is a lesson for the return. Tonight's flight doesn't care.

### User
What if they need me to pull up an app to tap for the lounge or the wifi on the plane? Also I feel shaky, not just annoyed.

### Assistant
Lounge apps and plane wifi are extras. Boarding is the product. If the lounge requires a living phone, you skip the lounge. If plane wifi needs an app, you sit in a chair and eat a bar. I won't invent whether this flight has outlets. Look at the seat when you get there. If there's a USB, great. If not, you already sent the sibling text.

Shaky is adrenaline plus 6:20 a.m. plus 4 percent. Put your feet in the socks, breathe out longer than in, hold the bin with one hand so it doesn't look like a tremor is stealing someone's laptop. You're allowed to tell the officer "phone is dying, I have a code." That's information, not a plea.

Get through the scanner. Recite the six characters in your head so they stick. On the other side, one more send if the phone lives: "Through security." Then off. Your sibling can wait at arrivals with that. You can be a person with a dead battery and a still-valid ticket. The power bank failed. You didn't. Move with the line. Screenshot. Text. Shoes. Code. That's the whole plan. The brick in the checked bag can stay a mistake. Don't let it become a missed flight.
