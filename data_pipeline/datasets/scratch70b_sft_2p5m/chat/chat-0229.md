### User
I'm at the self-checkout with a bunch of bananas, a gallon of milk, and two boxes of cereal. Every time I put the bananas on the scale it says I scanned steak. There is no steak in my cart. A light is flashing. The screen wants a staff member. There are three people behind me and one cashier at the far end looking at a receipt. I feel like I stole something I did not steal. What do I do without making a speech?

### Assistant
Stay put. Don't lift the bananas off and on again hoping the machine will get bored. That usually retriggers the same alert. Don't start explaining to the line. They can see a flashing light. They cannot hear a produce code.

The machine is not accusing you of theft. It is matching a weight or a leftover lookup to the wrong item. I don't know this store's software, and I won't pretend I do. What I can see is a stuck line, not a crime.

Look at the screen for a "need help" or attendant button if it isn't already calling someone. If it is already calling, wait facing the lane, not facing the people behind you. Hold the banana bunch in view so when staff walks up they see fruit, not a mystery.

Do you still have the bananas sitting on the bagging scale, or did you take them off?

### User
They're on the scale. The milk and cereal already bagged fine. The steak line is still on the screen with a red box. A kid behind me said "just go" which is not helpful. The attendant is still at the other end. I have my card out like that will magically finish this.

### Assistant
Put the card away. Payment will not clear a flagged item. The kid can wait. "Just go" is how people walk out with an unpaid bunch and then actually have a problem.

Keep the bananas on the scale. If you yank them now, the weight change can add a second error on top of the first. You want one conversation with staff, not two.

For the next ten minutes, do this in order. One: stay in the lane with your cart touching your hip so nobody "helps" by sliding your milk. Two: make eye contact with the attendant when they glance this way and raise two fingers, not a whole arm wave. Three: when they arrive, one sentence: "Bananas keep coming up as steak. There's no meat." Hand them the bunch. Let them key the produce code. Four: watch the screen until the steak line disappears. Five: pay. Don't add a joke about stealing steak. Jokes at a flashing register sound like nerves, and nerves make staff slower.

If nobody comes soon, take two steps toward the manned register and say the same sentence. Don't abandon the lane with your bags sitting there. I don't know whether they void the steak line from a handheld or the tower. You are not the person who types it.

### User
If they think I scanned steak on purpose to get a cheaper price, do I need to show my receipt history or something? I just want to leave. Also the milk is sweating.

### Assistant
You don't need a receipt history. You haven't finished the transaction. Staff see wrong PLUs all week. Showing old grocery emails would look stranger than standing still.

When they walk up, let them look at the screen first. If they ask "did you hit steak?" say "no." Then stop. Don't stack a story about never buying meat. Extra autobiography is how a two-minute override becomes a performance.

If they void the steak and the bananas still won't take, they can key the produce code. That's their job. Keep your card in your pocket until the total looks like milk, cereal, and fruit.

The sweating milk will survive ten minutes. Set it in the cart's child seat if it's sitting in a puddle. Don't put it back in a fridge case while the lane is open.

If the attendant is still trapped, call once: "this lane is stuck on a wrong item." Then go quiet. Pay, take the receipt, walk out. The machine had a leftover name. Leave the flashing light for the next person.
