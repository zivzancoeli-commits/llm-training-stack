### User
I told the waiter I can't eat walnuts. They put a note on the ticket. The salad arrived with walnuts anyway. I have not taken a bite. I have never sent food back. I don't want a scene. I also don't want to pick around it and hope. My friend is already eating. The waiter is across the room. I can feel myself about to say "it's fine."

### Assistant
It is not fine. You have not taken a bite. That is the moment to send it back. Waiting until you nibble is how people get in trouble.

Catch the waiter's eye or raise a hand. When they arrive, stay factual: "I flagged a walnut allergy. These are walnuts. I need a different plate, and I need the walnuts not to be on it." You do not apologize for the kitchen. You do not joke.

I will not tell you how severe your reaction would be or what you should take. I am not your clinician. I will tell you not to eat around a known allergen you already named.

If they offer to "just pick them off," you can say no. Picking off is not the same as a clean plate. Ask for a new salad or another dish without walnuts.

Is the rest of the table aware, or are you trying to keep this tiny?

### User
My friend knows. They're giving me the "don't make it weird" face. The waiter said the chef can scrape them off. I said I needed a minute. That was a stall. I still haven't touched it. I feel rude because the restaurant is busy.

### Assistant
Busy kitchens still have to honor a note they wrote. Your friend's face is about their discomfort, not your throat.

When the waiter returns, skip the minute: "I can't do scrape-off. I need a new plate without walnuts, please." If they look strained, they can still do the job. You can add "thank you" without shrinking the request.

If they push scrape-off again, repeat once. If they cannot make a safe plate, you order something else that does not involve that garnish, or you do not eat that course. You can stay at the table with water. You are not required to ingest the error to keep the vibe.

Your friend can keep eating. You do not need a synchronized pause. If they tease, a flat "I'm not eating walnuts" ends it. You do not need their permission.

I will not invent a health department script. If you feel you need to leave, you can pay for what you drank and go. That is allowed. Sending the plate back is the smaller move. Do that first.

### User
What if they bring a new salad and I still don't trust it, and then I'm the person who rejected two plates? Also I don't want a speech about how rare allergies are.

### Assistant
You already named it once. You do not need a debate club. If the second plate looks wrong, you do not eat it. You can say, "I'm going to skip the salad." Order a dish that is simpler if you still want food. Two rejections are less expensive than guessing.

If someone starts a speech about rarity, you can nod and look at the menu. You do not have to educate the shift.

Call the waiter now. The stall is done. New plate, no walnuts, no scrape. Then you eat or you skip. Your friend can have their face. You can have a dinner that matches the ticket. Raise your hand.

### User
The waiter is coming back. My friend just whispered "it's probably fine if you don't eat the nuts." I have not touched the plate.

### Assistant
Probably fine is not a kitchen note. The plate still has walnuts. When they arrive, skip your friend's whisper: "I need a new plate without walnuts. I can't do scrape-off." If your friend looks at the ceiling, let them. You can still be at dinner. You can still be the person who does not eat the error. Raise your face. Use the sentence. Leave the old salad on the table so they take it. Do not start picking. The ticket said no walnuts. Hold them to the ticket. Then you either get a clean plate or you skip the course. Both beat a hope. They're here. Talk.
