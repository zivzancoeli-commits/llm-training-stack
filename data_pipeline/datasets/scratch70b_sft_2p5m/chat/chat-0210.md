### User
Sunday dinner at my parents'. Uncle is going to start in on the news like he always does. Last year I tried to "win" and it wrecked the pie. This year I want a smaller playbook: how to not take the bait, how to leave the table without a speech, and how to not make my mom do all the peacekeeping. I asked you because I thought I wanted a script. I might just want to survive the meal. Check if I still want help as we go, because last year I asked for a debate kit and then hated it.

### Assistant
I will keep checking. Right now it sounds like you want an exit, not a kit. If that is true, we skip rebuttals.

Last-year-you already knows the trap: you do not have to correct the first wrong sentence. You can pass a dish. You can ask someone else a non-news question. You can say I am not doing news tonight and then you actually stop. The hard part is the second sentence, where you explain why, which is how last year's pie died.

If you want help, we pick one line and one exit. If you want less, say so and I will not pile on tactics.

Do you still want a script, or do you want permission to stay quiet and eat?

### User
Permission, mostly. But I also need one line because if I go fully silent he talks to my face like I agree. Mom hates silence too; she fills it. I do not want her stuck. Keep it short. If this turns into debate club, tell me to stop.

### Assistant
Still with you, and this is not debate club. One line that is not agreement: I hear you. I am not going into that tonight. Then turn to Mom or a cousin with a real question — work, a show, the dog, the rolls. "I hear you" is not a vote. It is a closed door with manners.

If he pushes, repeat the door once: still not doing news. Twice is plenty. A third round is you getting pulled back in. Then stand up with plates, or take a trash bag to the kitchen. Bodies leaving are clearer than speeches.

Mom filling silence: twenty seconds in the car. If Uncle goes political, I am going to say I am skipping it. You do not have to jump in. If she still jumps in, that is her habit, not your failure. You cannot run her night for her. You can refuse to be the second debater.

I am not going to tell you which issues are right. That is how this becomes last year.

Do you still want me on this, or is the one line enough?

### User
One line is good. What if my cousin starts arguing with him and they look at me to pick a side. Also I drove, so I cannot leave early without taking my sister home, and she likes staying for dessert.

### Assistant
Still checking: you want the look-at-me problem, not a new ideology.

When two people hunt for a third, you do not have to enter the ring to be loyal. Say I am staying out, you two can finish. Then talk to your sister about dessert, or start clearing. If they call that cowardly, do not defend your character. Defending is entering. Last-year-you would have written an opening statement. This-year-you can be boring on purpose.

Driving: tell your sister in the driveway. I might want to go after dessert, not after a second round of news. Agree on a time — pie, then we go — so you are not hostage to a filibuster and she is not yanked mid-fork. When the time hits, you still stand up. The clock is the reason, not a speech about him.

If you notice yourself drafting a rebuttal, that is the old kit sneaking back. You can tell me to stop anytime.

Leave it as: one refusal, no third-wheel vote, pie then drive. If you text from the bathroom on Sunday, you do not need a new argument. You need the line you already have, a plate in your hands, and the pie time you set. Wash your hands, go back, pass a dish. You asked for survival, not a win.
