### User
I found my helmet on my roommate's bike in the hallway, straps still warm. I wear it because I actually bike in traffic. They "borrow" things. They said once that helmets are one-size-ish. I don't like sharing sweat and I don't like guessing whether mine will be here when I leave at 8. I don't want a TED talk about brains. I want my helmet to stay mine.

### Assistant
Your helmet is gear, not a house mug. Sweat and fit are enough reasons. You don't need a neurology class.

Tonight, take it off their bike, put it where you keep it — on your handlebar, in your room, on a hook they don't treat as communal. Then say one sentence when you see them: "Please don't use my helmet. I need it for my commute. Get your own." If they already used it, you can add, "Ask first isn't enough. I don't share it."

Don't leave it in the hallway as a test. Tests fail.

I will not quote injury statistics or claim a used helmet is "expired." I don't know the scuffs. If it took a real hit, that's a replacement question for a shop or the maker's own guidance, not a guess from me. For a borrow without a crash, the issue is still consent and availability at 8 a.m.

Do they even own a bike regularly, or was this a one-off errand?

### User
They have a bike they ride on weekends. They said mine "was just sitting there." Sitting there is how I store it. They offered to wipe it with a shirt.

### Assistant
A shirt wipe is not a rental agreement. "Just sitting there" is how people describe other people's property that was convenient.

You can answer the wipe: "I don't want it wiped. I want it unused by anyone else." Then you move storage to your room if the hallway is a buffet. Inconvenient for you is still cheaper than hunting a helmet at 7:55.

If they ask to buy a cheap one and keep borrowing yours "until it arrives," you can say no to the until. Until is how weekends become November. They can skip a ride or use a rental where that's real. I won't invent a shop price. They can look.

If they get sulky about trust, you can say this isn't about trust as a personality. It's about a thing you need every weekday. They can be a fine roommate and still not wear your foam.

Don't hide it in a way that turns the apartment into a puzzle box if you can just put it in your closet. Adult storage beats cleverness.

### User
Should I mark my name in it so they "forget" less?

### Assistant
A name in Sharpie can help a hallway of bikes. It will not fix a person who saw it and took it. You can mark it anyway. The conversation is still the point.

If they "forget" again after a clear no, you don't give a second TED talk. You take it back, store it in your room, and you repeat the same sentence. You can add, "This isn't communal. I'm done sharing it." If they need a helmet the same morning, that's a problem they can solve with a shop, a friend who actually offered, or skipping the ride. It is not a problem your commute has to fund.

Don't lock your door in a way that turns the apartment into a hostage situation over foam. Do lock your own closet if that's where the helmet lives and they treat open hooks as a library.

Tonight: retrieve, store out of the buffet, one clear no, no brain TED. They can wipe their own helmet when they have one. Yours leaves the apartment on your head at 8. If it isn't there at 7:55, you don't hunt the building. You tell them you need it now, you take it, and you go. The weekday clock is the whole argument. Weekend borrowing without asking is how 8 a.m. became a scavenger hunt. End the hunt. Keep the foam.
