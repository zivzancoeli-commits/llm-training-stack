### User
It is my first year in this city. My roommate's birthday is Saturday. I like them. I have a thin checking account. I do not want a pity party with a sad cupcake. I also do not want to pretend I can do a bar tab. I am new at adult friend stuff and I keep drafting ideas that sound either cheap in a bad way or expensive in a lying way. Help me make a plan that actually fits no-money week without looking like I forgot.

### Assistant
You can do a real birthday on a tight budget. The sad version is when the plan is an apology. The good version is when the plan has a time, a place, and one specific treat, even if the treat is small.

I will not invent dollar amounts for this city. You know what is in the account. Build from that: free or already-owned first, one paid thing only if it is a known number you can cover.

Skeleton that does not look forgotten: pick a start time, pick a place that does not require a cover, pick a thing they like that is not a generic "fun," and tell them the plan by Wednesday so it feels chosen. Example shape: Saturday late afternoon walk or park or free museum hours if those exist, then back home for a food they actually eat, then a cheap movie on a laptop or a card game. The sentence you send is, "I want to do your birthday Saturday. Here's what I can host." Host is the word. Host is not pity.

### User
They like spicy food and bad reality TV. The apartment is ugly. I thought about a restaurant and I felt sick looking at menus. There is a grocery store I can walk to. Two other friends might come if I invite them. I do not know if inviting people makes it better or makes me look like I am spreading the cost in a sneaky way.

### Assistant
Restaurant is out if the menu made you sick. Believe that feeling. Grocery store is the plan, not a downgrade.

Ugly apartment: dim the overhead, use the lamp, wipe the table, put a mug with a marker-drawn 2 and 7 if they are twenty-seven, or skip the number if that feels corny. Clean is more festive than balloons you cannot afford. I will not tell you to buy a balloon arch.

Food: cook or assemble something spicy you can share. Nachos, a pot of chili, frozen dumplings with a hot sauce you already like — pick one method you can finish without a new gadget. Ask them about allergies if you have not. If you do not cook, a grocery rotisserie plus a jar of something hot is still a meal. The point is a plate in front of them at a set time, not a chef story.

Two other friends: invite only if your roommate would want those two in the room. Say it plainly in the text: "I can host a small spicy-food and bad-TV night. Want me to ask Sam and Priya, or keep it just us?" That is not spreading the check. There is no check. You are asking about the guest list. If they want just you, you do that. Smaller is not sadder when the food is right.

Reality TV: queue the show before they arrive so you are not scrolling in silence. That costs nothing and looks like you thought.

### User
What do I say if they offer to pay me back or suggest a bar after.

### Assistant
If they offer to pay you back: "It's your birthday. I wanted to do this." If they insist on contributing, they can bring a drink they like from their own kitchen. Do not turn it into a venmo thread on the night.

If they suggest a bar after: you can say no without a fake meeting. "I'm keeping it a no-spend night. I'm in for another episode here if you are." If they still want the bar, they can go and you can hug them at the door. You do not have to follow a tab you cannot float.

You are new at this, not bad at this. Wednesday text, Saturday spicy food, one show, optional two friends, apartment made less ugly with a cloth and a lamp. That is a birthday. It is allowed to be small. It is not allowed to be an apology.
