### User
My coworker failed their driving test this morning and they're sitting in the break room like they want to disappear. They already told two people. I have ten minutes left on my break. I don't want to do the "you'll get it next time" thing because that sounds fake, and I also don't want to sit there and just stare. What do I actually say?

### Assistant
Don't open with next time. Don't open with how common it is. Those lines are for later, if they ask. Right now they failed a test that other people treat like a personality. That's embarrassing even when it shouldn't be.

Sit down if there's a chair. If there isn't, stand near them, not over them. First line can be small: "That sucks. I'm sorry it went that way." Then stop. Give them a beat to decide if they want to talk or if they want quiet company.

If they start listing what they missed, don't correct the examiner from across the table. You weren't in the car. You don't know if the stop was short or the parallel park was crooked. You can say, "I don't know what they marked. Sounds miserable either way."

Are they the type who wants a plan, or the type who wants to sit and hate the morning for a while?

### User
They want to hate the morning. They said "I just want to go home" and then didn't leave. They're stirring coffee they aren't drinking. I already said "that sucks" and they nodded. Now it's quiet and I feel like I should fill it.

### Assistant
Don't fill it with a pep talk. Quiet is not a failure. Stirring dead coffee is a person trying not to cry at work. You can stay in that quiet without decorating it.

If you need a second sentence, make it practical and optional: "You don't have to rehash it with me. I can sit here until I have to go back." That gives them an out and a companion at the same time. If they start talking anyway, listen for the actual bruise. Failed tests usually hurt in one of three spots: they think they're stupid, they think they wasted money, or they think everyone will ask. Respond to the one they name, not all three.

If they say they're stupid, don't argue with a speech about intelligence. Say the test is a set of timed moves, not an IQ score, and leave it. If they mention money, don't guess the retest fee. Say you don't know what it costs to try again and you're not going to pretend you do. If they worry about people asking, offer to change the subject when someone else walks in. That's a real favor.

Skip "at least you tried" and "plenty of people fail." Both are true enough and still feel like you're sanding off their day. Skip jokes about the examiner unless they joke first. Matching their volume matters more than matching their words.

When your ten minutes are up, say so plainly. "I have to go back. Text me if you want company after." Then actually go. Hovering past your own break can make them feel like a project.

### User
Someone else just came in and asked "how'd it go?" loud. They froze. I kind of want to intercept but I also don't want to speak for them.

### Assistant
You can intercept without stealing the story. Look at the new person and say, "Not the morning for that question." Then look at your coworker so they can add whatever they want, including nothing. If they stay frozen, you already did the job. The loud asker can sit with the awkwardness they created.

If the asker keeps going — "what did you fail on?" — repeat the boundary once: "They'll tell you if they want." Then change the room. Ask the new person about the printer, the weather, anything that isn't a score. You are de-escalating a public moment, not hosting a group therapy.

After the extra person leaves, check in with one question: "Want me to keep people off this, or are you okay?" Then follow that answer. If they want cover, you can tell the next two people "don't ask about the test" without adding details. If they want to own it, let them.

Don't promise they'll pass next month. You don't know their test date, their instructor, or whether the examiner was fair. Comfort that stays honest is: this morning was rough, you don't have to perform bounce-back in a break room, and you have somewhere to sit that isn't a lecture. That's enough for a ten-minute break. Go back to your desk when the time's gone. Leave the coffee stirring to them.
