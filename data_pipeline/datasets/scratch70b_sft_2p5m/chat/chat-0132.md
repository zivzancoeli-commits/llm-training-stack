### User
I signed up for a gym. Induction is tomorrow. I'm nervous in that last-year way — I already know I'll nod at everything and then agree to a personal trainer package I can't think about in the moment. I want you to help me get through the tour. Also can you tell them I have a knee thing so they go easy, even though I haven't seen a doctor? I don't want to look lazy.

### Assistant
No. Don't invent a knee diagnosis, and I won't help you script one.

If your knee actually hurts, you can say "my knee hurts when I do X" as a feeling, not a disease name. If it doesn't hurt and you just want an excuse to skip the hard bits, that's the lazy story you're trying not to look like, only with extra fiction. You don't need a medical plot. You need a way to say no to the upsell and no to ego lifting.

I'll talk to you like last-year-you, because that's who walks into inductions and leaves with a clipboard of yeses.

The induction is usually: waiver, tour, how to rack weights, where the water is, maybe a short machine demo. It is not a test. The trainer is partly a teacher and partly a salesperson. You're allowed to take the teaching and refuse the sale.

Lines that are nos without a fake injury: "I'm not adding a trainer package today." "I'll do the induction only." "I don't want to try a one-rep max." "That's enough weight for me."

No story about a wedding, a sick cat, or a knee you haven't had checked. "Not today" is the whole excuse, and it isn't even an excuse. It's a decision.

### User
They'll ask why. Last year I always filled the silence.

### Assistant
If they ask why, repeat the decision: "I want to use the floor on my own first." If they keep going, "Not today" again. Filling the silence is how last-year-you bought things.

If they say the package is "only" something, I won't invent the price, and you shouldn't argue the number. You don't need a counteroffer. You need an exit from the pitch. "I'll think about it" is a soft maybe. Last-year-you heard that as a polite yes. If you mean no, say no.

Tour behavior for a nervous beginner that's actually safe: shoes you can walk in, listen when they show the emergency stop on a treadmill, ask where wipes are. Try a machine at a weight that feels like you could do ten slow reps, not a hero set. If they load more "to see what you can do," say, "That's more than I want today." Get off. Don't perform competence.

If something hurts sharp, stop. Sharp is not the same as "I'm new and this is uncomfortable." I won't diagnose that through a chat. Joint-odd or sudden: stop, name it as pain, no syndrome. If you're scared it's serious, that's a clinician, not a sales floor.

Don't apologize for reading the diagram. Last-year-you thought looking lost was a failure. It was just new. Ask: "When is it least crowded?" and "What's the rule for leaving bars on the floor?"

### User
What if they put me on a hard class as part of the welcome?

### Assistant
You say no to the class if you don't want it.

"I'll skip the class today. I want the floor tour and how to start the machines." If they already signed you up, "Please take me off that class." You don't need to claim an injury to leave a room. Walking out of a class you didn't want is allowed. Awkward is cheaper than throwing up in a spin room to be polite.

If you do want a class later, pick a beginner-labeled one when you're ready, not as proof. Proof is how people get hurt.

After you get home, they don't need to like you. You need a badge and two short visits this week: walk, a simple machine, leave. Twenty minutes counts.

Blunt, because the knee idea will come back in the parking lot: a fake joint can follow you in their notes. If the knee is real, keep it light and see a clinician when you can. A fake knee is a trap for future-you.

No package, no fake injury, no hero set.

### User
Okay. Induction only, no trainer add-on, no invented knee. If they push, "not today," twice if I have to.

### Assistant
Twice is fine. You don't owe a third reason. Go, learn the stops and the wipes, lift less than your pride wants, leave without a second contract. That's the visit last-year-you actually needed.
