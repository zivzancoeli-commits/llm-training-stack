### User
My phone died Friday. Replacement was supposed to be yesterday. Carrier says "delayed" with no day. I cannot get into email, the bank app, work Slack, or the authenticator. Everything wants a code from the dead phone. I have a laptop. I am using a guest browser at a cafe because I didn't want to type passwords on a work machine. I feel like I don't exist. Walk me through the next hour without telling me to "just restore from backup" like I have the phone.

### Assistant
You exist. The accounts are gated. Those are different.

Do not type bank or work passwords on a cafe guest PC. That's the first no. Cafe is for reading help pages and making calls, not for becoming you on a public machine. If you need a private screen, go home, a friend's place, or stay on your laptop if that laptop is yours.

On your laptop, list accounts on paper: backup code, second device, or a recovery email you can still open. Check the logged-in email first, slowly, before you reset anything.

I will not invent this carrier's timeline, this bank's recovery script, or Slack's admin policy. You will ask them with specific sentences.

If the authenticator had no cloud backup, assume the codes died with the phone. Printed backup codes are the real keys. If you never saved them, use each site's lost-device flow, starting with the email that unlocks the rest.

Which laptop is this — yours, or the work one you didn't want to use?

### User
Mine. Old, slow, still has my personal Gmail logged in from last year. Work email is in the dead phone's app only. Bank has 2FA to the dead number. Authenticator was on the phone with no backup that I know of. Carrier chat is a bot. Replacement order number I have on an email in that Gmail.

### Assistant
Gmail on your laptop is the island. Use it. Don't log out. Don't "try another browser" for fun.

Email the carrier from that Gmail with the order number, the dead IMEI or last-four if you have it, and a request for a date, not a vibe. Bots are for status. A written email is for a human later. I will not guess when they will answer.

Work: you need an admin or a coworker who can tell IT "new phone delayed, 2FA on a dead device." Use Gmail to email your work address if that forwards, or email your manager from personal mail with the facts only. IT has a process for lost phones. I will not invent it. Your job is to say dead device, no codes, laptop available.

Bank: at home, on your laptop, use the posted lost-phone path. Call the number on the card or a statement, not a search ad. They will ask you to prove you are you. Don't do that on cafe wifi. Pack up for money stuff.

Authenticator with no backup: you will rebuild it after the new phone exists. Until then, every account is a separate recovery. Prioritize: email, then bank, then work, then everything else. Social apps can wait. They are not oxygen.

### User
If I email my manager from Gmail it looks unprofessional. Also I am scared the bank will lock the account if I fail the questions. I already tried the app once on the laptop and it sent a code to nowhere.

### Assistant
One failed code to a dead number is not a moral event. Stop retrying the app in a loop. Loops look like fraud to automated systems even when you're honest. Use the phone-call recovery path instead of more app guesses.

Email the manager from Gmail if that's the only path: phone dead, work mail on that device, this is personal mail, please loop IT. Silent for two days is worse than a personal address.

When the replacement ships, do not put 2FA on only the new phone. Write backup codes on paper. Keep a copy in a password manager you actually open. One-device 2FA is how this Friday happened.

Leave the cafe for bank stuff. Keep Gmail. Write the carrier. Write work. Call the bank on a number you already have. Stop sending codes into the void. You are waiting on a delayed handset, not disappearing as a person.
