### User
I bought a blender on the first floor because it was on the endcap and I was afraid it would vanish. Now I want to look at sheets on three and I don't want to haul a box that says SMALL APPLIANCE through a bedding display. I've never asked anyone to hold a bag. I always just suffer. The customer service desk has a line. A guy with a vest is standing near towels doing nothing. This is the first time I'm going to ask without apologizing for existing. What do I actually say, and to whom?

### Assistant
Ask the vest, not the longest line, unless they point you to a counter.

"I already paid for this blender. Can you hold it at the desk while I go to bedding? I'll have the receipt." Show the receipt. That's the whole pitch. You don't tell them your fear of vanishing endcaps. You don't apologize for shopping in their store. Last year's you would have started with "sorry this is weird." This year's you can have a box and a floor plan.

If they say they only hold at customer service, you join that line. If they say they don't hold, you have a true answer and you either skip sheets or you carry the box. Don't argue a policy you just heard. I won't invent whether stores "have to" hold. They don't owe you a locker. Asking is still allowed.

If they take it, get a name or a ticket or a "ask for Priya." Don't leave a blender with a shrug and a vibe.

Are you going to be in the building for twenty minutes or two hours?

### User
Maybe forty minutes. Receipt is on my phone. The box is awkward, not heavy. Vest guy already made eye contact and looked away. I feel like I'm about to ask a favor that's against an invisible rule.

### Assistant
Eye contact then look-away is not a rule. Walk over. "Hi — paid blender, can this sit at customer service while I go upstairs?" If he says he's not that department, "Where do I take it?" is the next sentence. Let him point. Then you go there.

Forty minutes is a normal hold if they do holds. If they say "we can do twenty," you either finish sheets faster or you take the box. Don't negotiate like it's a hostage. You can say, "I'll be back in forty; if that's too long I'll take it with me." Then they choose.

Phone receipt is fine. Offer to show the order number. Don't hand them your unlocked life. Show the screen, take it back.

If the desk person is stressed, you still don't need a speech. People hold bags all day in some stores and never in others. You'll find out in one question. The invisible rule is in your head. The actual rule is whatever they say next.

### User
They said yes and put a tape tag on it. Now I'm on three and I feel like I have to buy sheets so the hold was "worth it." That's a trap, right?

### Assistant
It's a trap. The hold was for a blender you already bought. Sheets are optional. If you don't like them, you go back downstairs, pick up the box, leave. You don't owe the store a second item as rent on a piece of tape.

Set a timer for thirty-five minutes so you don't become the person they announce on a speaker. Be back when you said.

You asked without the apology. That's the first-time part. The sheets can lose. Get the blender at the desk, say thanks, go. You don't have to suffer the box and you don't have to buy bedding to justify a normal question. That's the whole errand, plus a receipt on your phone.

If they already tagged it, you still don't owe a second SKU. Walk downstairs when the timer hits, even if your hands are empty of sheets. The hold was a logistics ask, not a personality test you passed by spending more.
