### User
I have roaches in the kitchen. I reported it in writing last week with photos. The landlord emailed back that pest control is "a lifestyle issue" and I should take out the trash more. I take out the trash. I don't leave food in the sink. I still have roaches, including in the daytime, which I have read is a bad sign though I am not a scientist. I don't want to start a war. I also don't want to be told my character is the infestation. What do I say next that isn't a fake legal brief?

### Assistant
You stay on the pests, not on your personality. "Lifestyle" is a dodge. You don't have to win a debate about who you are. You have to keep a record of what is in the kitchen and what you asked for.

Reply in email, short, attached to the same thread as the photos. "I reported roaches on [date] with photos. I take the trash out. I still have activity, including during the day. Please send pest control or tell me in writing what you will do and when." That is a request for an action and a date, not a character defense.

I will not quote your local housing code, a "habitability" statute, or a deadline I don't know. Those rules vary, and a fake brief can get you in a mess. If you want the actual rule, look up your city or state's tenant information from a government site, or ask a local tenant hotline if you have one. I am not that hotline.

Keep doing the ordinary cleanliness you already do. Don't perform a theatrical bleach spiral to prove a point. Don't start a pesticide war in a way you don't understand; I will not invent a spray recipe.

Have they ever sent an exterminator to this unit before, or is this the first time you've asked?

### User
First time I've asked. Neighbors have mentioned seeing them in the hall. The landlord manages several buildings and answers fast when rent is late, slow when anything is dripping. I don't want to group-chat the building into a riot. I just want the kitchen not to crawl.

### Assistant
Hall sightings are useful as context, not as a mob. You can mention it once, factually: "I've also seen reports of activity in the hall." You don't name neighbors if they didn't ask to be named. You don't rally a riot. You keep your own dated photos.

If you speak to other tenants one-to-one and they already complained, they can send their own emails. Parallel records are not a riot. A pile-on group text with memes is a riot. Skip the memes.

If the landlord repeats "lifestyle," you do not write a five-paragraph memoir of your trash schedule. You restate: "This is a pest issue in the unit. I am requesting professional treatment." Then you wait a reasonable time that you choose based on how bad it is for you, not based on a number I invent. If nothing happens, your next step is whatever local process actually exists — a housing inspector, a mediation office, a legal aid clinic — not a speech I write as if it were the law.

Document: photos with dates, copies of emails, notes of calls (time, who, what they said). Store them where you can find them. That is dull and it is how adults talk to institutions later if they have to.

Don't withhold rent because a blog told you to. I will not advise that. Rent fights have consequences I will not pretend to manage.

### User
What do I do tonight so I can sleep without feeling like I live in a nature documentary?

### Assistant
Tonight is containment, not victory. Seal food in containers you already have. Wipe crumbs. Empty the indoor trash. Don't leave pet food down if you can feed on a schedule. I am not promising that ends roaches. It reduces the buffet.

If you are willing, a glue trap from a store can show you where they travel. Read the package. I won't pretend a trap is extermination.

Sleep in whatever room feels less active. That's allowed. You are not conceding the kitchen forever.

Tomorrow: send the short email if you haven't already in that form. Keep the photos. Don't argue lifestyle. Ask for pest control and a when. Your character is not the infestation. The infestation is the infestation. Make them answer that.
