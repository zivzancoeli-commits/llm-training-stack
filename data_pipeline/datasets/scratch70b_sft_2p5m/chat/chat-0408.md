### User
A friend is going away for six weeks. They asked me to water "a few plants." Then they sent a photo of a jungle, including a fern that "gets dramatic." They have not said how I get in. Last time I used a code that changed. They have not offered money. I would do a week for free. Six weeks is a job. I don't want to be cheap. I also don't want a dead fern and a broken plan. They want an answer tonight.

### Assistant
Six weeks of entry and watering is a job, even between friends. You can say yes with terms, or no.

Do not say yes until there is a key plan. A code that changed last time is a warning. You want a physical key you can keep for the dates, a lockbox you can test before they leave, or a neighbor with a spare you have met. "We'll figure it out" is how you stand in a hallway.

On money: you can ask. "I can do it if we set a simple thank-you. Six weeks is a lot." I will not name a price. If you do not want money, you can ask for a grocery card, or you can decline. Free is your choice, not their default.

If the jungle is bigger than you thought, you can offer a smaller yes: one visit a week, or only the easy plants, or you help them hire a sitter. You are not required to become a greenhouse.

Have you seen the apartment in person, or only the photo?

### User
Only the photo. They leave in five days. They said the super can let me in "if needed," which sounds like I should not plan on the super. There is a cat in the corner of one picture. They did not mention a cat.

### Assistant
Stop. A cat is not a plant. If there is an animal, that is feeding, litter, and daily care unless they have another plan. You do not accidentally adopt a cat for six weeks because of a pixel.

Text tonight: "I need a real key plan I can test before you go. Also is that a cat? I can't do pet care unless we talk about it separately. For plants, six weeks is more than I can do unpaid / without a key." Adjust the unpaid line if you would do it for a thank-you you name.

If they minimize the cat ("it belongs to my ex, maybe"), you still need a yes or no from them in words. Maybe is how you find a hungry animal on week two.

Super-as-backup is not a plan. Supers go on vacation too. Test the entry twice while your friend is still in town.

If you cannot do six weeks, say so tonight. Five days is enough for them to find someone else. A late no is worse.

### User
I feel like if I say no they'll think I don't care that they're traveling. I do care. I don't want their fern, their maybe-cat, and a super.

### Assistant
Caring is a text that says you hope the trip is good, and that you are not the sitter. Those can be the same message.

"I care. I can't house-sit plants and a possible cat for six weeks without a key and a real plan. I need to say no so you have time to find someone." Then you stop adding soft maybes.

If you want a smaller gesture, take them to the airport or water the one succulent they bring to your place. Do not take the jungle.

Answer tonight. Ask about the cat in the first lines. Do not leave them a vague "I'll try." Trying is how ferns die and friendships get a smell. Give them the no or the terms. Then let them pack.

### User
They replied "the cat is only sometimes there." That is not a yes or a no. They still want me to decide tonight.

### Assistant
Sometimes is a no from you unless they can name who feeds the animal on the other days. "I need a yes or no on the cat, a key I can test, and I still can't do six weeks of jungle unpaid. I'm saying no to the house-sit." If they want a smaller yes, they can ask a plant-only week after the animal is someone else's problem. Tonight they need a sitter who agreed to a mammal. That is not you. Send the no. Let them use the four days. Caring is the clear message, not a maybe-cat.
