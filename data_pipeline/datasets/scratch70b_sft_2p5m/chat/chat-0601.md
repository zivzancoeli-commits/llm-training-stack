### User
The high school field is two lots over. They told the neighborhood band was Tuesdays until 8. Tonight is Thursday, 9:10, and the drumline is still doing the same eight bars. My windows rattle. I have an early shift. I don't want to be the person who hates kids and music. I want the posted night to mean something. How do I ask without a speech about civic virtue?

### Assistant
You were given a calendar. Thursday 9:10 is not on it. You can ask about the calendar without hating saxophones.

I will not invent a noise ordinance. I will not invent the school's permit. You can still call or email the number on the football flyer, the main office, or the band boosters contact if you have it. Sentence: "We were told indoor/outdoor practice was Tuesdays until 8. It is Thursday after 9 and the drumline is still going. What is the actual schedule, and can tonight wrap?" You want two things: tonight shorter, and a true schedule. Do not mix in your feelings about youth. Feelings get used to dismiss you as a grouch. Times are harder to dismiss.

Tonight, close windows, run a fan, earplugs. Then decide if you walk over. If you walk, go to a staff adult, not a sophomore with a snare. Parking lot, not the fifty-yard line.

Do you see a staff jacket on the sideline, or only kids and a Bluetooth speaker?

### User
There's a person with a lanyard and a folding table of water. I don't know if they're staff or a parent. The fight song just started again. My cat is under the bed.

### Assistant
Lanyard plus water table is as close as you will get to a desk. Walk there in shoes. "Hi — I live on [street]. We were told Tuesdays only until 8. It's Thursday after 9. Can you end soon, and who do I email for the real calendar?" If they are a parent, they can still point you at a director. If they are staff, they can blow a whistle. You do not need to know which before you ask.

Do not lecture the drumline. Kids will remember a yelling neighbor longer than a lanyard will remember your street. One adult conversation.

If they say "concert Friday so extra nights," you can say, "Then the neighborhood needs the extra nights in writing, and a stop time tonight." Extra nights happen. Secret extra nights are how rattling windows feel personal. You are asking for a stop time, not a cancelled concert.

Cat under the bed can stay there. You do not have to narrate the cat. Narrating pets is how they file you under cute complaint. Clock and flyer are enough.

### User
If I email the principal I'll look like a snitch. The booster Facebook is a pile of spirit. I just want Thursday to be quiet like they said.

### Assistant
Email is not a snitch if you were given a rule and the rule broke. You can skip Facebook. Spirit piles eat people. Principal or athletics secretary is the boring inbox. Same sentences. Attach a photo of the flyer if you have it, and tonight's time. I will not promise they answer. I will promise Facebook will turn you into a character.

If tonight the lanyard can stop the eight bars, take that win and still send the calendar ask in the morning while you remember. Morning-you will downplay it. Night-you has the rattle.

Go to the table, ask for a stop and a contact, come home, fan, cat. You can like football Fridays and still want Thursday to match the flyer. Matching the flyer is not hate. It is the deal they already advertised. Ask for the deal back. Then sleep if the drums allow it. If they do not, the email still goes. Two tracks: tonight's human, tomorrow's inbox. No civic sermon. Just Tuesday-was-the-deal.
