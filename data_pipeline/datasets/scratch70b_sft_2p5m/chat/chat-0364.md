### User
Every Sunday around 10, a man with a small amp stands on the sidewalk under my bedroom window and talks for an hour. Sometimes there is a hymn on a speaker. I work Saturday nights. Sunday morning is my sleep. I have thought about water, which is illegal in my head and also disgusting. I have thought about moving my bed. The window is the only one that gets air. I don't want to be the person who hates speech. I want quiet between 10 and 11. What can I actually do that isn't a cartoon villain?

### Assistant
Move the fight off the windowsill and onto hours, distance, and volume.

Sunday sleep is a real need. A sidewalk message is also a real thing people do. You can dislike the amp without writing a novel about beliefs. Keep your ask about the amp and the clock.

Practical indoor: earplugs, a white-noise fan, heavier curtains, bed away from the glass if the room allows. Those are not surrender. They are how you keep the only window that breathes.

Outdoor: if you can talk, you talk once, on the sidewalk, not from a dripping window. "I sleep after night shifts. The amp under this window is a lot. Could you stand down the block, or keep it unamplified until noon?" They may say no. Then you know.

I will not invent what your city allows for amplified sound, permits, or sidewalks. Those exist in many places and differ. If the amp is the issue, noise complaints go through whatever non-emergency number or 311-style line you actually have. Ask that line what they need. Do not invent a statute at them.

Have you ever spoken to him, or only to the ceiling?

### User
Only the ceiling. He has a couple of regular listeners. They are not loud. The amp is. My landlord said "that's the street" when I mentioned it. Bed can't move because of the closet.

### Assistant
Landlord may be right that it is the street. The street still has a volume knob in some cities. You will not get that from a shrug.

This Sunday, if you are up, you can go down in clothes, not pajamas rage, and try the one sentence. If you are asleep, you wait until next week and try at 9:50 before it starts, or at 11:05 when he is packing the amp. Packing is a calmer minute.

If talking fails, you try the city's noise or street-use line on a Monday, facts: address, 10 a.m. Sundays, amplified voice under residential windows, night-shift sleeper. You ask what they will and will not do. You may get "nothing." Then you are back to plugs, fan, and maybe a cheaper window fan that covers the hymn.

Do not water. Do not egg. Do not film him in a way you then post for sport. That is how you get a different problem.

"I don't hate speech" can stay true. You hate a speaker cone in a canyon of brick at 10 a.m. That is a narrower, honester sentence. Use that one.

### User
If I go down, I don't want a theological debate. I will lose. I just want the amp moved twenty feet.

### Assistant
Then you do not debate. You request geometry.

"I'm not here to argue the message. The amp under this window wakes me. Twenty feet toward the corner would help." If he starts a sermon, you say "I have to go" and go. You already made the ask. Winning a debate was never the assignment.

If he moves twenty feet, you send a boring thank-you next week. If he does not, you do plugs and the city line. You can be a person who believes in speech and also wants to sleep. Those are not opposites. They are a sidewalk and a bed. Ask for the twenty feet. Skip the theology. Keep the window open for air, not for an hour of cone. That is the non-cartoon path.
