### User
My roommate takes my phone charger off my nightstand after I go to brush my teeth. Every morning I find it on their floor, still plugged into their brick, cable stretched under the bed. I bought this cable in January. They have a drawer of dead ones. I have asked twice. Tonight I need my phone at 20 percent for an alarm at 5:40. They are in the shower. I can hear the water.

### Assistant
Take the cable back now, while the water is running. That is not theft. It is your cable. Unplug it from their brick, coil it, and put it in your room. If their phone dies overnight, that is information they already have a drawer of.

Twice is enough asking for a household object. A third "hey remember my charger" at midnight becomes a sermon. Actions work better: the cable lives with you, not on a nightstand that functions as a lending library.

I won't invent what a replacement costs. I also won't pretend this is a legal case. It is a roommate with a habit.

Do they have a working brick of their own in that drawer, or is your cable the only thing that still charges a phone in the apartment?

### User
They have a brick. The cables in the drawer are frayed or USB-A. Mine is USB-C and it is the only one that actually clicks in. I left a sticky note last week. The note is still on their mug. I feel like a landlord of a two-foot cord.

### Assistant
You are not a landlord. You are a person who needs an alarm at 5:40. The sticky note failed. Notes fail when the other person can ignore paper and still get a full battery.

For the next ten minutes: get the cable, plug your phone in on your side of the door, and set the 5:40 alarm while it is charging. Put the cable in a drawer or a bag when you leave the bathroom, not on the nightstand. Nightstands are public in a shared place even when they feel private.

When they get out of the shower, one sentence is enough: "I need my cable for the morning alarm. Use one of yours or buy a new one tomorrow." Then you go to bed. Don't stand in the hall and inventory their drawer. Don't offer to share until Friday. Sharing is how this started.

If they say they have an important thing on their phone tonight, they can sit against your doorway with their brick and a cable they own, or they can use a wall in the kitchen. Your nightstand is not a charging station with a receptionist.

### User
They'll do the sigh. The "I didn't think it was a big deal" sigh. I don't want a fight at 11 p.m. I also don't want to wake up to a dead phone again.

### Assistant
The sigh is cheaper than a new cable, which is why they use it. You can let the sigh happen and still keep the cord. "I didn't think it was a big deal" is true for them. It is a big deal for a 5:40 alarm. You don't have to debate whose feeling wins. You already know you need the phone on.

A fight at 11 is two people standing up. You can avoid that by being already in your room with the door not fully open. Say the sentence, don't wait for a verdict, close the door most of the way. If they keep talking through it, repeat once: "Cable stays in here tonight." Then you stop adding content. Silence after a repeat is not cruelty. It is bedtime.

Tomorrow, in daylight, they can buy a cable. I won't name a store or a price. Any drugstore or grocer with a phone wall will do. If they "forget," you still don't leave yours on the nightstand. That's the whole system. The object lives where the alarm lives.

You asked twice. You left a note. The water is still running. Go get the cable, plug in, set 5:40, put the cord where their wet feet don't walk. The sigh can wait in the hallway. Your phone cannot.
