### User
It's 6:04 a.m. Sunday. The person above me is vacuuming. Not a quick crumb. They're doing edges. My ceiling is a drum. I have to be a person at noon for a family thing. I don't want to bang a broom. I don't want to wait until 9 and seethe. I also don't want to be the neighbor who hates cleanliness. They're still going. I'm in a t-shirt at the door like I might actually knock. Should I?

### Assistant
If you feel safe, a knock now is more useful than a novel at 9. They're awake. You're awake. The vacuum is the topic.

Knock once. Step back. "Hi, I'm downstairs. The vacuum is really loud at 6. Could you do the rest after 9? I can hear the edges in my bed." That's a time, not an attack on cleanliness. If they say they work Sundays, you can say, "I get the chore. 6 a.m. is the problem." Then you go back down. Don't stand there for a philosophy of dust.

If you don't feel safe at the door, a note under the door now, same content, and earplugs. I will not invent quiet hours for your city. I won't tell you 6 a.m. is "illegal." You can still ask for 9.

Don't broom-bang. That's a conversation that starts stupid.

Is this the first Sunday, or a pattern?

### User
Second Sunday in a row. First time I almost knocked. Last week I put a pillow on my head and was late to brunch angry. There's a peephole. I don't know their face. I can hear the vacuum hit the same corner again.

### Assistant
Then this is a pattern, which is why you knock or note now, not after they finish and go to work.

If the peephole shows a person and a door chain, and you're okay, knock. If it shows nobody because they're in the back room, knock anyway; they may not hear. A note then. "Second Sunday at 6. Please vacuum after 9. I'm in 2B." Sign a first name if you're willing.

If they open angry, you don't match. Repeat the time. You like a clean building. You hate 6 a.m. Those can both be true. You don't need to praise their vacuum to earn the ask.

After you go back down, earplugs, not a second knock. You asked. If it continues, you log times and email the super after 9, not at 6:10. Super at 6 is how you become the story. Email: dates, 6 a.m., you asked.

Family at noon: you still need some sleep. If they stop, good. If they don't, you take the rest of the morning as damaged and you don't add a feud to the family thing. Coffee is allowed. A manifesto at brunch is not required.

### User
I knocked. They said "this is the only time I have." They're still holding the vacuum. The door is open. I have one more sentence before I go downstairs.

### Assistant
"I only have now to sleep. Please finish after 9. Thank you." Then you leave while the door is still a door. Don't negotiate a 6:30 compromise unless you mean it. If they keep going, you already spent the sentence. Downstairs. Earplugs. Super later.

You didn't hate cleanliness. You asked for a clock. That's a Sunday. Get off the landing. The edges can wait for 9. You can still be a person at noon. The drum can stop or it can be a logged second week. You did the knock. That's the move. Close your door.

If they finish the last corner anyway, you don't knock a second time at 6:20. You already spent the sentence. Earplugs. Super email after 9 with two Sundays at 6. That's a pattern in writing, not a broom. If they text an apology later, you can say "after 9 next time" and leave it. You don't need a new relationship. You need a ceiling that isn't a drum at dawn. Sleep what you can. The family thing at noon can have a tired person who asked. That's still better than a pillow and a late brunch of anger.
