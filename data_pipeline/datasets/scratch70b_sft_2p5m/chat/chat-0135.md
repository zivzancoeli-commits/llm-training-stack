### User
University library. Book was due fourteen days ago. The account page says "item recalled," "auto-renew failed," "replacement pending," and "borrowing privileges suspended." I almost understand, then the words stack. I have the book in my backpack. I don't want to walk in sounding like I need a translator.

### Assistant
You almost have it. I'll put the letterhead into normal speech. I will not invent this library's money or rules. Those are theirs.

"Item recalled" means someone else asked for the book, so your due date may have been pulled shorter than a normal loan. It's not a theft accusation. It's a queue.

"Auto-renew failed" means the computer tried to extend the loan and couldn't. Common reasons: a recall, a hold, a cap on renewals, or a block already sitting on the account. Plain version: the extra time you assumed did not happen.

"Replacement pending" is the scary one. Plain version: after enough days some systems start treating the book as lost and prepare a bill for a new copy. I will not swear two weeks is or isn't that threshold. You are holding the book. The book in your hand is the fix for "lost."

"Borrowing privileges suspended" means the computer may refuse new checkouts until this item is returned or the account is cleared. It does not mean campus security is looking for you. It does not mean you should hide in the stacks.

Two weeks is late. Staff have seen later this morning. Your job is the object, not a speech.

Do you want the desk, or are you hoping the outdoor slot makes the words vanish by themselves?

### User
I want the words gone. I can do the desk. I keep thinking "replacement pending" means I already owe a textbook price. Also I don't know whether to mention the recall or shut up.

### Assistant
"Replacement pending" is a status, not a number I can read from here. If they still have the book in the system as checked out to you, handing it over usually stops the lost-book path. If they already billed, they can often reverse it when the item is scanned in. I don't know their button. Ask in plain speech: "The screen says replacement pending. I have the book. Can you take it and tell me if anything is still on the account."

You don't need to lecture them about the recall. They can see it. If you want one useful sentence: "I didn't realize auto-renew had failed after the recall." That's a fact, not a novel. Skip "I usually never" and skip jokes about stealing.

Other words they might throw:

"Hold" — another person is waiting. Returning it is how that person gets it. You don't need to apologize to a stranger who isn't in the room.

"Grace period" — a short window after due when they don't charge. I don't know if yours has one. "Am I past grace?" is a legal question for them, not for me.

"Block" — same idea as suspended privileges. Ask: "Can I borrow again today after this is in, or is there another step?"

I will not quote a fine per day. If they name an amount, that's their amount. You can pay it if you can. You can ask whether they waive first overdue for students. Don't invent a hardship category you don't have. Don't cry as a tactic.

### User
What if the student at the desk is as lost as I am and we stare at the red text together.

### Assistant
Book on the desk. Campus ID if they ask. "Please check this in." If they freeze, they get a supervisor. That's the building working. You don't have to decode the software for them.

If the screen still says replacement after the scan, stay boring: "It's still showing replacement. Can we look at that before I leave." One sentence. You're not attacking their intern shift.

Go today. The backpack-crime feeling is an unread status plus a physical book. Return the object. Ask what each leftover word means for you, today, in this account. Leave with either a clear zero or a number they stated, not a number you invented from "pending."

You already understood the plot: you kept it too long, someone else wanted it, the computer got dramatic. The desk is where drama becomes a scan. That's the errand.
