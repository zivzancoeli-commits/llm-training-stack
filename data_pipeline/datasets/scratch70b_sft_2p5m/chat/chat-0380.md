### User
My friend Dana asked if they could get a copy of my house keys "just in case something happens." We are close. They water plants when I travel. Last time I left a key under the mat, which I already know was dumb. Now they want a permanent copy on their ring. I live alone. I don't think Dana is a thief. I also don't want a spare living on someone else's keychain forever. They framed it like I'd be ungrateful if I say no. I need language that isn't a speech about trust.

### Assistant
You can like Dana and still not mint them a house key. Those are not opposites.

"Just in case" is a blur. Cases differ. Plant-watering while you are on a trip is a dated, bounded job. A copy on their ring is a standing permission. You are allowed to offer the first and decline the second.

Do not start with a lecture about trust. Start with a policy you can repeat. Something like: "I don't give standing copies. If I'm gone, I'll leave you a key for those dates and take it back." That is a logistics sentence. It does not accuse them of anything.

I will not invent locksmith rules. You do not need a lease clause to say no. Wanting fewer keys in the world is enough.

Are they asking because you have a trip coming up, or did this come out of nowhere over dinner?

### User
Trip is in three weeks. They offered to do mail and plants again. Then they said it would be easier if they just kept a copy so I don't have to "do the under-the-mat thing." I told them the mat was a one-time panic. They laughed and said I was being intense. Now I feel like the dramatic one.

### Assistant
You are not intense for not wanting a permanent spare on a friend's ring. The mat was a bad method. A dated handoff is a better method. Those two facts can sit in the same sentence.

Before the trip, decide the method in writing so it is not a vibe. Text: "For the 12th through the 19th, I'll give you a key the night before. Please bring it back the day I land. I'm not doing a standing copy." If they call that dramatic, you can say, "It's just how I do keys." Then stop defending your personality.

For the handoff itself: meet in person, put the key in their hand, and say the return date out loud. When you land, the first errand is getting it back, not unpacking the sentimental part. If they are traveling the day you return, arrange a lockbox you control, or a neighbor you already use, or a timed pickup. I will not pick a brand or a price. Pick a method you can reverse.

Do not leave a copy "until the next trip" because that is how standing copies happen. If they push with a disaster hypothetical, name a contact you actually chose, or say you will handle emergencies without minting them a key. Do not invent a sister on the spot.

### User
I don't have a sister with a key. It's just me and this one friend who always helps. If I say no, I lose the plant person. If I say yes, I replay every time they have people over at their place with a bowl of keys on the counter.

### Assistant
Those two fears are both real. Neither requires a permanent copy.

You can keep Dana as the plant person and still refuse the ring copy. The offer is: help for this trip, key for those dates, key back. If they withdraw the help because they cannot keep the metal, that is information about the deal they wanted. It is disappointing. It is still allowed. You can hire a sitter, ask a different neighbor, or skip the plants. Dead plants are cheaper than a key you cannot see.

If you decide you do want a spare in someone else's hands long-term, choose the person and the storage on purpose. Not a bowl on a counter at parties. A labeled envelope in a drawer is still their house, still a copy you cannot audit. I will not tell you that is safe or unsafe as a legal fact. I will tell you that you already pictured the bowl, which means you do not want that picture.

For the next message, keep it boring: dates, handoff, return. No apology for being intense. No joke to make it light. Light is how the copy lands on the ring. Send the text tonight. Plan a backup plant person. You can be grateful and still keep the keys.
