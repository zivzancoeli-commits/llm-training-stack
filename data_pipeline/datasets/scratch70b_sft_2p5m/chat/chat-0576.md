### User
I bought a printer. Roommate has been running shipping labels for their weekend resale thing. Ink is low. Paper is mine. They said "it's just labels, it barely counts." It counts because I hear it at 11 p.m. and because I needed to print a form and got a red light. I don't want to be the landlord of a printer. I want my form, and I want the hustle off my hardware unless we agree.

### Assistant
It's your printer. Labels are a business expense they parked in the living room.

"I need the printer for my stuff. Your labels are using my ink and paper. Buy your own supplies or use a shop. Ask before you run a batch." Barely-counts is how ink dies at 11 p.m.

I will not invent what ink costs. "Replace what you used" is enough. If they want a standing deal, that's a written split: they buy a pack of labels and a cartridge, you share hours. If they don't want to pay, the hustle goes to a library or a store with a machine.

Don't hide the printer in your closet unless talking fails. Start with talking. Closets are a later sitcom.

Did they ask the first time, or did you come home to a warm tray?

### User
Warm tray. First time I thought it was a one-off. It's three weeks. They offered to "get paper sometime." Sometime is not a cartridge. I have a form due in the morning.

### Assistant
Tonight: you print the form first, if the red light will allow. If it won't, you need another machine tonight — work, library hours, a friend, a shop. I will not pick one. The form is the priority, not the moral victory.

Then the conversation: "Sometime isn't enough. No more hustle jobs until you replace ink and paper, and not at 11." Three weeks is a pattern. One-off is dead.

If they need labels tonight, that's a them problem. Your morning form outranks their weekend queue. You can say that without becoming a landlord. You're a person with a deadline and a red light.

Offer the standing deal only if you actually want it. You are allowed to say the printer is personal. Personal is allowed in a shared apartment. Shared is rent and kitchen, not automatically every appliance.

### User
They'll say I leave it in the common room so it's communal.

### Assistant
Location isn't a contract. "It lives here because that's the only outlet. It's still mine. Ask." If you want it less communal, you move it to your room after this talk. Outlet problems are real. A power strip in your room is boring and effective.

Form first. Then the no-batch-without-supplies rule. Then, if you want, a paid share. Hustle can exist. It can exist on their cartridge. Red light in the morning is not "barely." That's the whole printer talk. Keep it that small.

### User
I printed the form at a shop. Conversation happened. They said the printer is in the common room so it's fair game. I said ask, and no 11 p.m. batches. They have a side hustle tomorrow. I think they will try anyway.

### Assistant
Then you move it to your room tonight if the outlet allows, or you unplug it after your own use and take the cable. Common room is not a contract. Fair game was their theory. You already rejected it.

If they try anyway, you repeat once: "Not without asking, not at 11, not on my ink." If a batch is mid-run, you can let that batch finish so you don't fight over a jam, then the machine goes with you. Hustle can buy a cartridge and request hours. That's a deal you still don't have to take.

Form is done. Red light was the teacher. Landlord of a printer is a nickname. Owner of a printer is accurate. Labels are a business. Businesses buy supplies. Keep it that small when they start tomorrow. Unplug is a complete sentence.
