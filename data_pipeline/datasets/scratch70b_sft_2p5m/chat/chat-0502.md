### User
Someone upstairs is learning trombone. I support hobbies in theory. In practice it's the same five notes at 8:30 p.m. on weeknights, plus Saturday morning. The floor is thin. I have tried headphones. I can still feel the brass in my ribs. I don't want to crush a beginner and I also don't want to keep eating dinner to a fanfare. I don't even know if it's a kid or an adult.

### Assistant
A trombone is loud on purpose. Thin floors don't care about your support in theory. You can ask for hours without asking them to quit.

Find out who it is the ordinary way: a note under the door is worse than a knock at a reasonable hour. Knock after a session, not during the note, so you aren't the villain in bar three. "Hi, I live below. The trombone comes through a lot in the evenings. Could we pick a window that isn't dinner and early morning?" You are negotiating a clock, not their soul.

If you never see them, a short paper note with your first name and "evenings are tough downstairs, could we talk about practice times?" plus a way to reach you. Keep it short. No jokes about dying. Jokes land mean when someone is already embarrassed to be bad at trombone.

I will not invent quiet-hours statutes. If the building posts hours, you can mention those as a shared fact. If not, you are still allowed to ask.

Have you ever spoken to that unit, even about packages?

### User
Never. They moved in this spring. I smiled at someone with a case in the lobby once and then felt shy. Saturday mornings are the worst because I sleep in. Weeknights I could live with 7 to 8 if 8:30 stopped.

### Assistant
Then you have a proposal, which is better than a complaint. "Weeknights 7 to 8 is okay for me. After 8:30 and Saturday before 10 are hard." Specific windows are easier to meet than "please be considerate," which a beginner cannot measure.

If it is a kid, the adult who opens the door is your partner in the clock. Don't scold the kid. Kids learning brass already know they are loud. If it is an adult, same clock, less kid-glove, still not a TED talk about thin floors. They probably know.

Offer one true accommodation you can live with if they need more minutes: a midday practice if they're home, or a mute if they have one. I will not claim a mute makes trombone polite. It often helps. If they don't have one, you don't buy it for them unless you want to. This is not your instrument.

If they get defensive — "it's my apartment" — you can say, "It is. It still comes through my ceiling. I'm asking about time." Then stop. You cannot win physics with a proverb.

Don't record them for leverage. That's a different fight. Don't bang the ceiling in rhythm; that's how feuds start.

### User
What if they say they're preparing for a jury or a concert and need more hours this month?

### Assistant
Then you can grant a short, dated stretch if you mean it: "This week, okay. After the 15th can we go back to 7–8?" Dated exceptions prevent forever. If you don't mean it, say you still need evenings. Their concert is real. Your ribs are also real.

Start with the knock and the window. Headphones failed because bone conducts. Clocks work better than foam. You can support a hobby from downstairs without being the unpaid concert hall. If they never answer the door, use the short note with a time window and your first name. You don't need to know kid versus adult to ask for 7–8. The brass doesn't care. Your dinner does. Ask for the clock. Let the notes happen inside it.
