### User
A coworker is on a documented PIP. They asked me to "stay friends through this" and to keep eating lunch like nothing is happening. They also asked whether I would "put in a good word" if anyone asks. I like them fine. I am not their manager. I don't want to be a secret character witness, and I don't want to ghost them in the kitchen. The PIP is not my business and somehow it is in my inbox because they forwarded me a chunk of it.

### Assistant
Delete or archive the forwarded chunk. That document was not yours. Don't become an unofficial copy of HR.

You can stay civil in the kitchen without being a campaign. Lunch like nothing is happening is optional. Lunch like two people who still eat sandwiches is enough. You don't have to process the PIP. You are not their union, therapist, or skip-level.

On the good word: "I won't lobby about your PIP. I can keep being normal at lunch." If someone above you asks a factual question about work you actually shared, you answer facts you know, not a character commercial. I will not invent what you are required to say. I don't know your policy. I know you weren't hired as a character witness.

Don't promise "I'll always have your back" if you don't know what that will be asked to mean.

Have they asked you to hide mistakes, or only to keep the social temperature warm?

### User
Mostly the temperature. They did joke that if I get pulled into a meeting I should say they're "crushing it." That didn't feel like a joke. I haven't been pulled in. I forwarded the PIP to nobody, I just wish they hadn't sent it.

### Assistant
Treat "crushing it" as a request to lie later. You can close it now: "If anyone asks me about work, I'll say what I actually know. I won't say crushing it as a script." Then you go back to your sandwich. You don't need to report the joke to HR unless your job says you must or you believe something unsafe is happening. I won't invent a reporting duty. If you are unsure, you can read your handbook or ask HR a hypothetical without naming if that's how your place works. I won't pretend I know.

If they send more PIP pages, you reply once: "Please don't forward that to me. I can't help with it." Then you don't discuss the content. Discussing the content is how you get quoted.

Kitchen: you can still say hi. You can skip a lunch if every lunch is a strategy session. "I need this lunch to be about nothing" is a valid filter. If they can't do nothing, you eat at your desk sometimes. That's not a ghosting. That's a boundary.

Don't start a group sympathy thread. Don't ice them in meetings you both still have to do. Professional normal is the kindness that doesn't create a second HR file with your name on it.

### User
I feel cruel eating chips while they're spiraling.

### Assistant
You can feel that and still not take the PIP into your stomach. Cruel would be mocking them. Chips are allowed. If you want one human sentence, "This sounds stressful. I hope it works out. I can't be in the process." Then chips.

Stay a coworker who is decent, not a friend-shaped advocate with a forwarded document. Return the role you actually have. Let the documented plan stay between them and the people who wrote it. You can say hi. You cannot say crushing it on command. If they forward another chunk, you repeat the don't-send line and you don't open it like homework. The PIP is not a book club. Eat the chips. Be normal. Leave the document where it belongs. Lunch can be lunch. You already have a job. It is not this document.
