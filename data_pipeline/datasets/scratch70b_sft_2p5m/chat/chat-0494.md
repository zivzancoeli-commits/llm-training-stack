### User
I met this person at a group hike two weeks ago. We have texted about trails. Tonight they asked me to share my live location "so we don't miss each other" for a coffee that isn't even scheduled yet. They sent a map pin request. I don't share location with people I barely know. I also don't want to sound like I think they're a predator. I over-explain when I'm nervous. Give me a no that isn't a TED talk about privacy.

### Assistant
Live location is a lot of information. Coffee is a small plan. Those don't match. You can decline the pin without writing a threat assessment.

Short: "I don't share live location. Tell me the cafe and a time and I'll meet you there." That swaps a tracking request for a normal appointment. If they push "it's just easier," you repeat, "I still meet at a place. I don't share live location." Twice is a policy. A paragraph is a debate.

You don't need to add "no offense" or "I'm sure you're great." Softeners invite them to reassure you until you fold. You also don't need to accuse them. Accusations are a different conversation you have no evidence for. Mismatch of intimacy is enough.

I will not invent what an app stores or how long a pin lasts. I don't know the app. "I don't share it" does not require a lecture on servers.

Is the coffee something you actually want, or did the pin arrive before you even said yes to meeting?

### User
I was 60 percent on coffee. The pin dropped me to 20. They said their other friends all share and I'm being "old school." We don't even have a day. It's like they want a radar lock first.

### Assistant
Trust the percentage drop. A radar lock before a day is backwards. You do not have to earn a coffee by becoming a dot on their screen.

If you still want coffee later, you pick a public place with a time after you actually agree. If you don't want coffee, you can let the whole thing cool: "I'm going to skip hanging for now." You don't owe them a privacy seminar as an exit interview.

"Old school" is a tease meant to make you feel unsophisticated. You can ignore the tease. If you answer it, answer once: "Call it whatever you want. I still don't share live location." Then you stop defending 2026 like it's 1998. This is not a tech-history class.

Don't send a screenshot of your settings to prove you're consistent. Proof is how people keep negotiating. Don't offer a one-hour share as a compromise unless you truly want that. Compromises on tracking tend to become the new baseline.

If they keep sending pin requests, you can stop answering the pin and only answer logistics, or you can stop answering. Repeated asks after a no are the thing that should lower the percentage further, not your manners.

### User
What if they show up with "I just wanted to make sure you got home" energy after a first coffee I haven't even agreed to?

### Assistant
That's a different problem than a map pin, and you still don't reward it. You don't share your address, your route, or a live dot to soothe their worry. "I'll get myself home" is a full sentence. If that energy shows up in texts without a meeting, you can take it as a reason the 20 percent was correct.

For tonight: decline the pin, don't over-explain, decide coffee separately on a yes you actually feel. A new acquaintance gets a cafe name, not a radar. That's not a TED talk. That's a door. If they keep pushing the pin after the door, you don't owe a second privacy lecture. You can leave the thread on read. Live location is a lot of yes. Coffee never required that much yes. Keep the map off. Keep the acquaintance at acquaintance scale.
