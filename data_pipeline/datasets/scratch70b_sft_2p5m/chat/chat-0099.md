### User
My jacket zipper just split at the bottom and I have to leave in ten minutes. It's the only warm layer I own that looks okay for this thing. I don't have money to replace it tonight. I already tried yanking it. The slider is still on, but the teeth don't catch. Help me make a plan that doesn't involve buying a new coat.

### Assistant
Don't yank it again. You can finish killing the zipper in one more tug, and then you really do have a flap. Ten minutes is enough for a hold, not a repair.

You're close: slider on, teeth not meshing at the bottom usually means the insert pin popped out of the box, or the slider is bent just enough to skip. I can't see it, so I won't diagnose which.

What are you walking into — outdoor wait, indoor event, or a car the whole way? And do you have a safety pin, a binder clip, or even a paperclip in a junk drawer?

### User
Bus stop then a heated building, maybe fifteen minutes outside. I have two safety pins in a little sewing kit I never use, and a binder clip on a bag of chips. No spare jacket. Budget is like whatever's in coins.

### Assistant
Good. You're not shopping. You're closing a gap.

Minute one through three: put the jacket on. Pull the slider all the way down. Look at the bottom. If one side of the zipper tape is sitting outside the slider, feed that side back in with your fingernail. Don't force it if the metal is clearly twisted. If it seats, try zipping *slowly* once. If it climbs two inches and holds, stop. That's a win. Clip the binder clip across those two inches so it can't reverse while you walk.

If it won't seat at all: skip the zipper. Overlap the front panels like a wrap. Pin them from the inside at the chest and again a hand-span lower, using both safety pins. Points facing in, toward your shirt, not out toward a stranger. The binder clip can pinch the bottom hem so it doesn't billow at the bus stop.

You will look a little improvised. You will also be warm enough for a short wait. That's the job.

### User
What if the pins show? I don't want to look like I got dressed in a parking lot. Could I just hold it closed with my hands the whole ride?

### Assistant
Holding it closed on a moving bus is how you miss your stop and freeze your knuckles. Use the pins. If you're worried about the look, pin from the *inside* so only a tiny bump shows on the outside. Dark jacket hides that better than you think.

If one pin is obvious, put your bag strap across that spot. That's not fashion advice. That's blocking a glint.

I won't quote you a tailor price or a replacement coat price. You said coins. Tonight is overlap-and-pin. Tomorrow, if you still want this jacket, a zipper slider swap is a small sewing-shop job — I don't know what they charge in your town, so look it up when you're not running.

Leave two minutes early so you're not doing this on the sidewalk. Test the overlap with your arms down, then with one arm up like you're grabbing a bus rail. If a pin pops, move it higher, not tighter.

### User
Alright. Jacket on, try the slider once slow, if not then inside pins and the chip clip on the hem. One more thing — if the pin pokes me on the bus, do I just suffer, or is there a cheap buffer? I have a grocery receipt in my pocket.

### Assistant
Fold the receipt twice and stick it between the pin head and your shirt. That's a buffer, not a fashion layer. If you have a spare sock or a tissue, even better, but don't hunt. You're on a clock.

Go. If the slider catches even a little, leave it caught and clip it. Don't "test" it ten more times. You already almost had it. Walk to the stop with the jacket closed however it closed. You can fuss with looks on the heated side of the door.
