### User
I'm in a drugstore looking at shampoo. A clerk has been in my aisle for four minutes, restocking the same three bottles. When I move to toothpaste, they move. I have a basket with deodorant and floss I already picked. I am not stealing. I feel watched in a way that makes my hands look guilty even to me. I still need the shampoo. It's 7:20 p.m. I came from work in a hoodie.

### Assistant
You're allowed to buy shampoo in a hoodie at 7:20. A clerk shadowing the toothpaste is their choice. It isn't proof you did something.

You don't have to perform extra innocence — narrating your cart, smiling too hard, putting items back. That's how people try to win a test nobody posted. You can keep the basket, pick the shampoo, walk to the front, pay.

If you want to name it, you can, once, without a scene: "I don't need help, thanks." If they say they're restocking, you can still move to the register.

I won't invent store policy or whether this is profiling. I don't know you or the clerk. I can tell you the feeling is common and you don't have to empty your pockets to earn the aisle.

Is there only one clerk in the store, or is this person assigned to you while others exist?

### User
Two clerks. The other is on register. This one left a cart of freight to stay near me. I put the shampoo in the basket. Now they're at the end cap. I want to put everything back and leave. I also need the deodorant tonight.

### Assistant
Don't put it all back. That's letting a bad feeling run your bathroom shelf. You need deodorant. You already have it in the basket. Pay.

For the next ten minutes: walk the short path to the register, don't wander to hair dye to "prove" you're a browser. Browsing more will keep the freight clerk employed as a shadow. Put the items on the counter. Use a card or cash as you planned. Don't over-explain the hoodie.

If the freight clerk follows to the register, ignore them. The register clerk is the transaction. That's the only conversation you need: "that's all."

If you feel unsafe — not just annoyed — you can leave the basket on an end cap and walk out. Annoyed plus deodorant tonight is a reason to stay in line. Unsafe is a reason to go. You get to tell the difference. From here it sounds like surveillance irritation, not a chase. I wasn't there. Trust your body if it says leave.

You don't owe a complaint at 7:20 unless you want to. A later email to the store is optional. Tonight the job is pay and exit.

### User
I keep thinking I should film it so I'm not crazy. That feels like escalating.

### Assistant
Filming can escalate a clerk who is already performing cop. I won't tell you it's illegal or legal where you are. I don't know. If your goal is deodorant, filming is extra plot. If your goal is a record because this happens every time you come, a later daytime visit or a call to a manager is cleaner than a phone war in the toothpaste aisle.

You're not crazy for noticing a freight cart that stopped moving. That's data. Data can live in your head: hoodie, 7:20, followed from shampoo to paste. You don't have to submit it tonight.

Go to the register. Buy the three things. Leave. Wash your hair later without their eyes. The clerk can restock the real freight after you go. That's the correct order. Your hands aren't guilty. They're holding a basket. Put the basket on the counter. That's the whole proof anyone is owed. Then you're on the sidewalk, which is quieter, and the deodorant is yours. If your hands shake on the PIN pad, that is adrenaline, not guilt. Finish the tap. Walk. Hoodie and all.
