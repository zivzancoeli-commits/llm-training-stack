### User
The ferry is 40 minutes late. My bus on the other side leaves in a window that is now gone if we slip any more. I have a ticket for that bus. The next bus is three hours later, after dark. I am sitting in a plastic chair with a paper boarding pass and a phone at 22 percent. I do not know if I should wait, reroute, or start calling people who were going to pick me up from the bus.

### Assistant
Call the pickup person now, not when you are already stranded in a dark lot. A heads-up at 40 minutes late is courtesy. "Ferry delayed, I may miss the bus, I'll text when I know the next move." Then you still have work to do.

Do not assume the bus will wait. Some connections hold; many do not. I will not pretend I know this operator. Look at the bus company's own site or app, not a screenshot from last year. If there is a phone number on the ticket, use it and ask whether they can move you to the later bus or another stop.

Battery at 22 percent means screenshots and a power plan. If the terminal has an outlet, sit on it. Low power mode now.

Is the pickup a friend with a car on the far side, and is there any other way off the dock besides that one bus — another bus, a train, a ride you would actually take?

### User
Friend with a car, they already left their house to be at the bus stop on time. There is a later bus. There might be taxis when we dock; I don't know. The ferry board still says "boarding soon" like it has for twenty minutes.

### Assistant
Text the friend to turn around or pause, with a clear fork: "Don't wait at the stop yet. I'll know more when we dock. If I miss it, I may take the later bus or ask you later tonight, not now." Do not keep them idling out of guilt. Idling is how friends get bitter and still try to help.

Call or chat the bus company about the missed ticket. Ask what they can do. If they say nothing, you have a later bus as a floor. A taxi from a dock can exist and can also be scarce; I will not quote a fare or promise a line of cars. Treat a taxi as a maybe, not a plan you announce to your boss.

If work or a reservation is waiting on the far side, send that update too. People can move a dinner. They cannot move a ferry.

Stay where you can hear boarding, but do not hover the gate in a way that drains you. Eat something if there is food. A delayed crossing plus a skipped meal is how people make worse choices at the dock.

When you do board, screenshot the new arrival estimate and send it. Then close the apps. Refreshing does not load the boat faster.

### User
If I tell my friend to go home I feel like I'm abandoning the only ride I trusted. And I'm angry at the ferry like that helps.

### Assistant
Sending them home is how you keep the friendship. Asking them to orbit a maybe-bus is how you spend their gas on your helplessness. You can still ask later: "If the 9 p.m. bus is real, could you do that instead?" Later is a new ask, not a hostage situation.

Anger at the ferry is noise. Useful anger would be documenting times for a refund request after you are safe. I do not know if they owe you anything. I know a photo of the board helps more than a rant in a review while you still need the boat.

For the next ten minutes: plug in if you can, text the friend the pause/go-home message, contact the bus ticket channel, screenshot the ferry board, and eat or drink water. When boarding is real, get on, sit, send one arrival text, save battery.

You have a later bus as a floor. That is ugly and survivable. Missed connections are a logistics problem. Treat your friend as a person, not as infrastructure. The plastic chair is temporary. The texts you send now are what keep the night from turning into two people waiting in the wrong places.
