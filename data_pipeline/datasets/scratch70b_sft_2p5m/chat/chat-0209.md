### User
I started as an intern last week and I have to finish a training module that needs headphones-off concentration. My apartment is two roommates plus a dog. Coffee shops want a purchase and I am trying not to spend. Someone said the public library has quiet rooms but I have never asked a librarian for anything except a password reset on the guest Wi-Fi. I do not want to sound like I am demanding a private office. Tight budget. How do I actually walk up and ask.

### Assistant
You are not demanding an office. You are asking where the quiet seats live. Intern move: be specific and small.

At the desk: Hi, I need a quiet place to work for about two hours. I do not need a book. Is there a silent room, a study carrel, or a floor you would point me to. Then stop. Let them point.

Do not lead with my internship depends on this. They need duration and noise level. If they mention a booking system, ask how to reserve without a fee. A lot of public libraries let cardholders book a room for free. I will not invent your branch's rules. If they say there is a charge, say I am on a no-spend week; is there an open quiet area instead.

Budget plan: library card if you do not have one — usually free with ID and a local address. I will not quote a replacement-card fee. Ask. Guest Wi-Fi is often enough. Bring a charged laptop and water from home. Do not buy a drink to "earn" the chair. The chair is the service.

If the quiet room is booked, ask them to rank backups: stacks end, wall carrel, silent floor.

Do you already have a card here, and is the module a timed window or can it pause?

### User
No card. I moved in August. The module can pause but my manager wants it done by Friday. There is a university library two bus stops away that I am not a student at. I was considering sneaking in. I get twitchy in public.

### Assistant
Do not sneak into the university library. If they allow public hours, walk in during those hours. If they check IDs, do not bluff a student card. Ask: are community members allowed in the reading rooms on weekdays. Yes or no.

Public library first, today. Bring ID and a piece of mail or a lease if they want proof of address. If you are on a roommate's lease, say I just moved; what can I use. They will tell you. I will not guess their list.

Ask in order: card, then quiet space, then whether a study room needs a card to book. Sometimes you sit in the silent room the same day. You have not spent coffee money.

Twitchy is real. You can say I concentrate better away from talking. You do not need to disclose the internship. If a room has a glass door, take it. Ask when the loud kids hour is and come after. That question saves a wasted trip and a panic purchase at the cafe next door.

### User
What if I sit down and someone starts a phone call. I am not good at shushing strangers. Also my headphones are dying and I do not want to buy a charger brick.

### Assistant
You do not shush as a first move if you freeze. Move seats once. If the whole room is calls, go back to the desk: that area got loud; is there another quiet corner. Let staff remind people of the rule if they offer. That is their job, not your intern test.

Dying headphones: ask if the study tables have outlets. If not, work in battery chunks and pause the module. Skip the brick this week unless the laptop itself is dying. Then ask, do you lend chargers. If they say no, finish what the battery allows and return tomorrow. You do not buy a brick to look professional.

Friday plan: after work, ID, card, two hours in the quietest place they indicate. Water from home. No cafe. No university sneak. If a room needs a reservation, book the shortest free slot that covers the module, not a four-hour block you will feel guilty leaving.

Walk up, ask for quiet, take the pointing finger, sit, work. You are a patron, not a trespasser and not a customer. That is the whole ask.
