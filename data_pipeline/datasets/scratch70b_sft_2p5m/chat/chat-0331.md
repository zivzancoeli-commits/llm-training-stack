### User
The radiator in my bedroom started banging around 11:20. Not a little tick. A metal knock every few minutes, then a long groan, then another knock. I tried turning the valve. I tried a towel on the pipe. I tried sleeping on the couch. The living room one is quieter but the couch is a joke. I have a 7 a.m. shift. I don't know if this is air in the line, a neighbor's heat, or the building being the building. I also don't want to wake the super at midnight if this is just "old radiators do that." What is the actual next move tonight, and what waits until morning?

### Assistant
Tonight is about sleep and not flooding the apartment. Morning is about who fixes it.

Do not keep wrenching the valve if you do not know which way is fully closed. If you already bumped it and the banging got worse, leave it. A towel on the pipe will not stop the knock.

If the radiator is hissing water onto the floor, that is a now problem: towels on the floor, a bowl if it is dripping from a valve, and you do wake someone, because water is not a personality quirk. If it is only noise, you do not have to perform a 1 a.m. repair.

Earplugs if you have them. Couch if the bedroom is the only unit pounding. A fan or a podcast for cover noise. Set two alarms so the shift is not riding on one exhausted wakeup.

I will not diagnose your heating system. Banging can be air, a failing steam trap, pipes that expand, or a valve that was closed wrong. Those are all "call the people who own the boiler," not "YouTube at midnight."

Is there water on the floor, or is this sound only? And is the valve a simple round handle or something you already forced?

### User
Sound only. No water. Round handle, I turned it maybe a quarter and then stopped because I got scared I'd break it. The knocking is slower now but still there. Super's number is in the lease. They've told people not to call after ten unless it's an emergency. I don't know if this counts.

### Assistant
This does not have to be an emergency call if there is no leak, no smell of gas, and no heat so high you cannot stay in the room. Noise that wrecks a work night is real. It is not automatically a 1 a.m. boiler visit. Use the building's own rule: after ten, emergency only. Sound-only knocking usually waits.

Leave the valve where it is. You already turned it a quarter. Leave a note on your phone: time the banging started, that you nudged the valve, that there is no leak. That note is for the morning message, not for a novel.

Sleep in the living room if the bedroom is the drum. Put your work clothes by the door so 7 a.m. is mechanical. If you have a white-noise app, use it. If you do not, a bathroom fan or the cheap kitchen radio on low is fine. Do not take the radiator cover off at midnight.

In the morning, text or call during whatever hours the super actually answers. Short: "Bedroom radiator banged from 11:20 through the night. No leak. I turned the valve a quarter and stopped. Please look today." Then let them tell you if they want the valve left alone.

If the heat is also weak, say that. If it is roasting and knocking, say that too. Temperature plus sound is more useful than "it's annoying."

### User
If they say all the old radiators bang and I should just live with it, am I stuck? I can't do another week of this before shifts.

### Assistant
You are not stuck with "that's character" as a final answer. You are stuck with a process. Ask what they will actually check: the valve, the air, the unit above you, the boiler timing. "Live with it" is not a check. You can say, "I need the banging to stop enough to sleep. What is the next step, and when?" Get a day, not a vibe.

If they never come, write the same facts to whoever you pay or message for repairs, dated. I will not invent your local housing rules. I can say that a log of nights and "no leak, still banging" is the useful version of complaining.

Tonight: valve stays, no water, sleep in the quieter room, alarms set. Morning: one factual message. You do not need to become a steamfitter by dawn. You need a shift, and then a human who owns the pipes to look at the one that is punching the wall.
