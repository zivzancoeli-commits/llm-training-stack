### User
I have a delivery coming Thursday when I'll be at a training all day. It's the kind of box that looks stealable. I want to ask the neighbor in 3B to take it in. We've only said hi at the mailboxes. I keep writing a note that sounds like I already know the building rules. I don't. I'm new here. I don't want to bluff that this is "how we do it."

### Assistant
Good instinct. Don't bluff the culture of a building you just moved into. I don't know your lease, your city, or whether 3B even likes packages. Anyone who tells you "neighbors always do this" is guessing.

Ask as a request, not as a custom. A note or a knock that says: "I'm in 3A. I have a delivery Thursday while I'm out. Would you be willing to take the box in if you're home? Totally fine if not — I'll make another plan." That last sentence is the intern move that actually works. It gives them an easy no, which makes a yes more likely, and it keeps you from pretending you know the house rules.

I need one clarifying detail before we get cute with logistics: do you already know 3B's name, or is this still mailbox-hi? And is the package something you'd be uncomfortable putting in a stranger's apartment — medicine, expensive, legally weird? You don't have to tell me the contents. You do have to not ask a near-stranger to hold something you'd regret.

### User
Mailbox-hi only. I think her name is Denise from the directory but I'm not sure I should use it if I'm not sure. The box is a used monitor I bought used. Not secret, just awkward to carry and obvious from the lobby. I don't know if our building lets people take other people's mail. I'm not going to pretend I read the bylaws.

### Assistant
Don't use Denise if you're not sure. "Hi, I'm your neighbor in 3A" is enough. Wrong-name confidence is a bluff in a smaller font.

On the mail-versus-package thing: I am not going to invent postal law or your HOA. Taking a parcel from a carrier who is handing it to a person is different from fishing in a mailbox. If the carrier requires a signature, 3B may not be able to help anyway. If it's a knock-and-drop, she might just pull it inside her door. You can admit that split out loud: "I don't actually know whether the carrier will leave it with you. If that's not allowed or you don't want to, I'll use a pickup point." Uncertainty is the honest version. Fake certainty is how you get someone in a petty argument with a driver.

Don't promise cash or invent a building-standard tip. A clear pickup time beats a chocolate-bar bribe. If she says no, have the other plan in your mouth: hold at location, work delivery if allowed, or delay the shipment. I don't know which your seller offers. Look it up instead of hoping 3B is the infrastructure.

### User
What do I say if she asks what's in it? I don't want to sound shady by dodging, and I don't want a lecture about buying electronics used.

### Assistant
"A computer monitor. Used, from an online listing. It's bulky, that's why I worry about the lobby." That's specific and not a novel. If she lectures, you can say, "Yeah, it's a gamble. I still need a plan for Thursday." You don't have to defend the purchase to earn a maybe.

If she says yes, confirm the boring parts: she'll get a text from you Thursday morning, you'll knock Thursday evening, you won't assume she can sign for certified stuff. If the carrier won't leave it, that's not her failure.

If you never catch her at the mailboxes, a note under the door is fine. Don't slide into a tone that sounds like a memo from management. You're a new person asking a favor. New-intern energy is actually correct here: polite, slightly formal, ready to hear no, not pretending you've been on this hallway for a decade.

### User
Knock, 3A, delivery Thursday, no fake name, admit I don't know carrier rules, easy no, backup is hold-at-location. Monitor if she asks. Pick up the same night.

### Assistant
That's the ask. You don't know 3B, the bylaws, or the driver. You don't have to. You only have to not perform like you do. If she says no, you use the pickup point and you still say hi at the mailboxes next week. The package is a box. The relationship is optional and slow, which is how hallway trust actually forms.
