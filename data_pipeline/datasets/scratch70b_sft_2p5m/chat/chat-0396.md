### User
I came home and my bookshelf was "improved." My roommate grouped by color, stacked paperbacks horizontally, and put my grandmother's little dish on a higher shelf "so it wouldn't look cluttered." They left a note: surprise, it looks so much calmer. I can still find the books if I hunt. I cannot find the feeling of my wall. I don't want to be ungrateful. I also want my dish back where my hand expects it. They are in the kitchen proud.

### Assistant
A gift that rearranges your things is still a rearrangement. You can thank the intention and undo the work.

Go to the kitchen and say it while it is fresh: "I know you meant it well. I need my shelf back the way I had it, including the dish. Please ask before you move my stuff." Then you go put the dish back yourself if you do not want to wait. You are not required to live in their calm.

Do not spend twenty minutes praising the color gradient so they feel paid. Payment is how this happens to your closet next.

I will not invent a roommate-contract clause. Shared living still allows personal shelves. If it was clearly your shelf, it stays your system.

Is any of the shelf actually shared, or was this all your books?

### User
My books, my dish, my little photo. They use the lower cube for board games. They did not touch the games. They said my spine labels were chaotic. I used to know where the novel with the green dog was. Now I have a rainbow and no dog.

### Assistant
Then this is not a shared-cube problem. This is your wall.

After the sentence, restore the dog book and the dish tonight. You do not need their help to unsort. If they look crushed, let them look crushed for a minute. Then, if you want, offer a real gift they can do: "If you want to help, the games cube can get a bin. My shelf stays mine." Redirect is kinder than a week of tight smiles.

If they argue that it looks better, you can say, "It doesn't work for me." Taste is not a vote. They do not get a veto because they spent an hour.

Take a photo of the restored shelf if you are worried this will be a repeating hobby. A photo is for your memory, not a trial.

### User
I keep thinking I should leave one row by color so they feel included. That feels like training a raccoon with snacks.

### Assistant
Do not leave a tribute rainbow. That is a mixed message. Mixed messages invite a sequel.

Inclusion can be the board-game bin, or a plant they buy for the window, or nothing. Nothing is allowed. You already share rent and a kitchen. You do not have to share a sorting philosophy.

If they apologize, accept it once. If they sulk, you still sleep under your own spines. Tonight: talk, move the dish, find the green dog, throw away the surprise note so it does not sit there as a claim. Tomorrow the wall looks like you again. That is the point of a shelf.

### User
They're still in the kitchen. The dish is still high. If I wait until morning it will feel like I accepted the gift.

### Assistant
Do not wait until morning. Morning is how a surprise becomes the new shelf. Go now, say the sentence, move the dish, find the green dog. If they follow you into the living room to explain color theory, you can say, "I heard you. I'm putting it back." Then you put it back. You can thank the hour they spent without keeping the rainbow. The wall is yours tonight, not after a sleep on it. Walk to the kitchen. Bring the note if you need a prop. Then restore the dog. That is the whole gift exchange: intention noted, furniture reversed.
