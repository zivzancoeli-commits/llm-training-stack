### User
A coworker started a thing where they drop their personal social posts in the team chat and write "engagement helps the algorithm, team?" They watch who reacts. Yesterday they asked me in person why I never like their stuff. I don't follow them. I don't want work to be a fan club. I also don't want to be the only person with zero hearts while everyone else performs support. How do I get off this without a manifesto about surveillance?

### Assistant
Work chat is for work. A like is not a performance review, and it is also not nothing when someone is counting. You can refuse the fan club without writing a media-studies paper.

If they ask in person again, keep it boring: "I don't mix my personal feed with work. I didn't mean it as a slight." That names a rule about you. Rules about you are harder to argue than "your posts aren't good."

In the team channel, you don't owe a reaction. You can mute that thread if your chat tool allows it. You can also scroll past. Scrolling past is allowed.

Don't start a fake-like campaign to blend in. That teaches them the system works. Don't publicly dunk on the algorithm either. Dunking becomes a second team activity.

I will not tell you your company forbids this. I don't know your policy. If there is an actual social-media-at-work rule, that's HR's document, not my guess.

Are these posts harmless hobbies, or are they sales and personal brand with your work name attached?

### User
It's a side photography page. The photos are fine. The monitoring is the part I hate. They keep a little mental list. Two other people like everything in under a minute like it's a fire drill.

### Assistant
Photography is fine. A mental list of coworkers' hearts is the problem. You still don't have to join the drill.

If the drops keep hitting the main team channel, you can ask — once, privately or in a lead's ear if you have a decent one — whether personal promotion belongs in that room. Phrase it as channel purpose: "Can we keep the team chat for project stuff? Personal pages are easy to miss and awkward to score." You are requesting a venue change, not a ban on their hobby. If a lead is useless, skip that and handle only your own behavior.

Your behavior: no likes, no apology essay, same polite work face you already have. If they corner you with "it takes one second," you can say, "I'm not going to like work-adjacent personal posts on a timer." Then you go back to your actual timer, the one with tickets on it.

The two fire-drill likers are managing their comfort. You don't have to copy their nervous system.

If you actually like a photo on your own time, on your own account, that can stay your business. Don't like from the work channel as a peace offering. Peace offerings become quotas.

### User
They just posted again with my name in it: "even [me] should check this one."

### Assistant
That's a ping, not a compliment. Reply once in the thread if you need the mention to stop: "Please don't tag me on personal posts. I'll look if I want to." Then you do not look on command. If they tag you again, you repeat the same line or you ask a lead to tell them to stop tagging people. Repeated public tags after a no are the thing to escalate, not the existence of a photo of a bridge.

You don't need a manifesto. You need one rule: work chat is not their engagement squad. Say it, keep doing your job, let their algorithm live without your thumb. If they take it to a 1:1 as "you don't support the team," you can say the team is the tickets, not the photography page. Support is finishing the work you share. Hearts on a timer are a different sport. You can sit that one out without a press release.
