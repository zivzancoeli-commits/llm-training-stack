### User
Someone dumped a file in the shared drive called "comp_review_draft" and it has names and numbers. I opened it because the filename looked like a template I was supposed to fill out. I saw my number and three people I sit next to. I closed it. I didn't screenshot. Now I feel like I stole something even though it was just sitting there. I also can't unsee that one person makes a lot more than me for the same title.

### Assistant
You opened a file that was sitting in a shared place with a boring name. That is not a heist. Closing it and not spreading it is the part that actually matters.

Do not message the group chat. Do not ask "did you see this?" Do not compare notes by the coffee machine. Curiosity is how a small leak becomes a team-wide event, and then people start guessing who opened it.

I am not going to tell you what your company "must" do, because I do not know their policy, your country, or whether this file was a mistake or a test. What I can say is practical: treat it as confidential now that you know what it is.

Did you download it, or only preview it in the browser? And is the file still sitting there, or did it vanish after you closed it?

### User
Browser preview, I think. It's still there. Same folder we all dump status decks into. My manager is in a meeting until four. I keep hovering over Slack like I should confess.

### Assistant
Do not confess in Slack, and do not write a novel. If you tell anyone, tell one person whose job is to handle files, not your whole team. That is usually your manager or whoever owns the drive, not a work friend.

A clean version: "I opened a file in the shared folder named comp_review_draft because it looked like a template. It has compensation data. I closed it, didn't copy it, and I wanted you to know it's still sitting in [folder name]." Then stop. Do not add "and I saw X's number." Do not ask "is this allowed." Let them take it from there.

If you wait until four, the file may still be there for everyone else. Hovering is not a plan. Send the note now, even if your manager is in a meeting. A short async message is better than a hallway panic at 4:05.

About the pay gap you saw: that feeling is real and also not something to act on today. Same title does not always mean same work, location, years, or negotiation history. I do not know which of those applies. A leaked cell is a terrible basis for a compensation meeting. If you want to talk money later, talk about your work and the market, not about a file you were not meant to audit.

Do not delete the file yourself. You are not the owner. Moving it or renaming it can look like covering tracks even if you mean well.

### User
What if they think I went looking for it? And what if I have to keep working next to people whose numbers I now know? I already feel weird looking at one of them.

### Assistant
"I thought it was a template" is a normal sentence. A fishing expedition looks like searching "salary" or forwarding the sheet. You did neither. If they ask what you saw, "enough to know it was compensation, then I closed it" is complete. You do not need to recite names.

Working next to people is a skills problem, not a confession problem. You already knew some people were paid differently; you just have a sharper picture. Do not treat them differently. Do not over-help or go cold. Do not make a joke to "clear the air." Jokes about money never clear the air.

For the next ten minutes: write the three-sentence message, send it to your manager only, close the folder, mute the shared-drive notifications if they ping on every file change, and go do a concrete task that is not staring at Slack. Drink water. If a coworker asks whether you saw "that file," say "I flagged it to [manager] and I'm not talking about it." Then change the subject to the actual work.

I cannot promise how your company will react. Some places thank you. Some get stiff. Either way, spreading it is worse than reporting it. You did not steal a spreadsheet. You closed one. Keep acting like that person.
