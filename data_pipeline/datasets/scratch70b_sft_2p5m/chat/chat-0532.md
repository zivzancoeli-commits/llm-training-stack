### User
My aunt FaceTimed me. I picked up because last time I let it ring she told my mom I was "avoiding family." I'm in pajama pants and a stained shirt. The apartment is a Sunday mess. She immediately said "show me around, I want to see how you're living." I have the camera aimed at my face like a hostage. I do not want to walk her through my bedroom. Help.

### Assistant
You can stay on the call and still refuse the tour. Those are different yeses.

"I'm in pajamas and the place is a mess. I'm not doing a walkthrough." That's a complete sentence. You do not owe a comedy apology about the sink. You do not have to invent a roommate in the shower.

If she pushes with "just the living room," you can show a wall, a window, or nothing. A wall is not a personality. If you don't want to show anything, don't. The camera can stay on your face. FaceTime is a conversation tool, not a listing.

I will not invent a privacy law about video tours. You live there. You choose what the lens sees.

The guilt about last time's missed call is separate. You answered this one. That already closes the "avoiding" story for today. You don't have to pay extra in square footage.

Is she alone on the call, or is this going to get screenshotted into a family chat?

### User
She's alone, but she narrates to my uncle offscreen. Last Christmas she showed everyone a photo of my old apartment's overflowing trash. I don't think she's cruel. I think she treats other people's rooms like TV. I already said "it's messy" and she said "I don't mind messy, I just want to see you settled."

### Assistant
"Settled" is her word for a tour. You can answer the care without the camera moving.

"I'm settled. I'm just not giving a tour today. We can talk." Then you ask her a question about her week so the call has a track. People who want a walkthrough often have no second topic ready. That's useful. Let the silence sit if she pouts. Pouting is not an emergency.

If the narration to uncle starts — "she won't even show us" — you can say, mildly, "You can tell him I'm in pajamas and I said no." Naming it out loud reduces the later remix. You cannot control the remix completely. You can refuse to supply new footage of your unmade bed.

Do not speed-clean on camera. That's a show. Sunday mess is allowed to exist offscreen.

If you want a compromise that isn't a bedroom: one photo later, of something you actually like — a plant, a mug wall, the view — sent by text when you're dressed. Optional. Not required. A photo you choose is different from a live pan she directs.

### User
She asked if I'm hiding something. I laughed and it sounded guilty. I am not hiding a person. I am hiding a pile of laundry and the fact that I haven't hung curtains.

### Assistant
Laundry is not a secret. "I'm not hiding a person. I don't want a tour of my laundry. That's it." Deliver it flat. The guilty laugh already happened. You don't need to explain the laugh.

Curtains can stay unhung. Her curiosity is not a punch list. If she offers to "help you nest" by directing furniture over video, decline that too. Nested is her project. Lived-in is yours.

End the tour topic after that one clarification. If she asks again, "I already said no" plus a subject change. Two repeats is plenty. A third is you hanging up with a normal goodbye: "I'm going to eat. Love you. Talk later." Hanging up after a goodbye is not avoiding family. It's ending a call.

You answered. You were polite. You kept the lens on your face. That's a successful FaceTime with an aunt who likes sets. Put the phone down before you start hanging curtains out of spite.
