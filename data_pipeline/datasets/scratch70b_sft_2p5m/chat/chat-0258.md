### User
I told Priya in January I was done dating for a while. Tonight she texted a first name, a job, and "Saturday coffee, super casual." That's the third setup since March. I said no to the other two. She said those didn't count because one was a group thing. I like Priya. I don't like feeling like a project with a calendar. It's Thursday 9:12 p.m. Saturday is the day I do laundry and not talk.

### Assistant
Third setup is a pattern. "Didn't count" is her accounting, not yours. You said done. Coffee with a first name on Saturday is dating with a smaller cup.

You can like Priya and decline the coffee in the same breath. Those aren't opposites.

Don't write a manifesto about your year. One text: "I'm not dating right now, including casual coffee. Please stop setting me up. I still want to hang with you, not with a stranger on Saturday." If you want laundry to stay sacred, say you're busy Saturday without inventing a wedding.

I won't diagnose why she does this. Some friends think a person is a problem to solve. You are not a blank calendar.

Did she already tell the first-name person that you'd be there?

### User
She said she "mentioned I might be free." So now I might be the flake if I say no. The person is her coworker. I will see this human at Priya's birthday in June if I go. That's messy in a small way.

### Assistant
"Might be free" is Priya's leak, not your RSVP. You are not a flake for skipping a date you never booked. The coworker can hear "they're not dating right now" from Priya, which is her mess to clean because she mentioned you.

For the next ten minutes: reply to Priya, not to the coworker, unless the coworker already has your number. If they do, a single line: "Priya was ahead of me — I'm not dating. Hope Saturday is good otherwise." Then back to Priya with the stop request.

June birthday is months away. A coffee no in April does not require a disguise in June. You can say hello to a coworker like a person at a party. You don't have to hide in the kitchen. If that feels thorny, you still don't owe Saturday. Thorny in June is cheaper than training Priya that no means try a smaller coffee.

Don't offer "maybe in the fall." Maybes are inventory for her next text.

### User
She'll say she just wants me to be happy. That sentence makes me tired. I am happy enough with laundry.

### Assistant
"I just want you to be happy" is a motive she likes. It can still run over a boundary you already set in January. You can answer the motive without surrendering Saturday: "I know. Happiness this month is no setups. Laundry counts." If that sounds dry, good. Dry is readable.

If she keeps forwarding first names after this, you repeat once more, then you stop engaging the matchmaking thread. You can still answer her messages about movies. You don't have to answer the coworker bios. Filters exist. So does "I'm not discussing dating."

Tell her tonight. Thursday 9:12 is a fine time for a no that protects Saturday. You already declined twice. Third time you add the explicit "stop." That's not rude. That's the missing word. Priya can want you happy from a distance that doesn't include a coffee you didn't order. Send it. Put the phone on the dryer if you have to. The laundry can start on Saturday without a name tag.

If she replies with a new first name next week, you don't owe a fresh essay. Forward your own last text to yourself if you forget the wording. The point is the stop, not a debate about casual versus serious. Coffee is still a date if a coworker is waiting in a shop because Priya said you might be free. You were not free. You were doing laundry. That is a life. Keep it. June can be a hello at a birthday, not a makeup coffee. Send the Thursday no while it is still Thursday.
