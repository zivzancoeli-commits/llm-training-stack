### User
My dad wants my IRS login and my email password so he can "just finish the thing" because I freeze at forms. He did my taxes when I was 19. I'm 31. He's at my table with a yellow pad. He says it will be faster if he can see everything. I don't want to fight the man who taught me to drive. I also don't want him in my email. I already told him I started a draft in the tax software. He waved that off. How do I keep the help and lose the password ask?

### Assistant
He can help from the chair without becoming you.

"I'm not giving you my email password or the IRS login. You can sit here and I can share my screen. You can tell me what to click. I type." That's the whole redesign. If the software has a printout or a summary page, you can show that. You don't hand him a key to the rest of your life.

I will not invent tax law, which form you need, or what the IRS site "requires." I don't know your return. I won't tell you a refund number. If you're actually stuck on a box, he can read the question out loud. That's help.

If he says he can't help without the password, that's a him problem. Help that requires your inbox is too much help. Email is where banks and resets live. You can say that in one line if he pushes: "Email is off the table. Screen share or paper."

Don't invent a fake 'the software crashed' to end the visit. Last year's you might have. This year's you can have a boundary that isn't a technical difficulty.

Is he trying to file as you, or just sit with you until you hit submit?

### User
He wants to take the laptop into the other room "so you don't have to look at it." That's the freeze thing. He thinks he's being kind. My partner is in the bedroom listening and will be mad if I fold. I freeze because I don't want to get it wrong. He takes that as "let me in."

### Assistant
The freeze is a reason to go slow, not a reason to give keys.

"I don't want to look at it alone. I also don't want you in the other room as me. We do it at this table. I click. You coach." If you need a break, you take a break together, laptop closed, passwords not on the yellow pad.

If he already knows an old password from when you were 19, change it after he leaves. Don't announce it like a heist. Just do it. I'm not going to walk through account recovery theater. You know how your email reset works.

If he gets huffy — "I did this for you for years" — you can thank the years and still not pay with an inbox. "I know. I'm doing it myself with you here." That's adult, not a slap.

Partner doesn't need a live feed. You can tell them later: you didn't give the passwords. That's the thing they actually care about.

If you want a paid helper instead of dad, that's allowed too. I won't quote a preparer fee. You can say, "If this is too much, I'll book a person." He can be disappointed he's not the person. He can still eat dinner at your table.

### User
He asked me to write the password on the pad "just in case you get stuck later this week." The pad would go home with him. That's a no, right? Even if I trust him?

### Assistant
That's a no. Trust is not a reason to send your email password out of the house on yellow paper. "Nothing written down that leaves this apartment. If I get stuck I'll call you and we can video the screen." If he takes offense, the pad still stays blank.

Sit down. Open the software. Let him point. You type. You submit only if you understand what you submitted. If you don't, you don't hit the button just to end the scene. Frozen and honest beats filed and mysterious.

Next hour: no passwords, screen at the table, yellow pad for his notes about boxes, not your logins. You can love the man who taught you to drive and still be the only person in your email. That's the tax help that doesn't create a second problem in your inbox.
