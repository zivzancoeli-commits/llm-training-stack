### User
We're traveling for a two-day onsite. My lead, Chris, said "we can share a queen and save the team money." I do not want to share a hotel room with a coworker. I have never said that out loud. I don't want a reputation as high-maintenance. I also don't want to sleep in a room where I cannot lock a door between me and my inbox. First time. Help me decline without a policy lecture I cannot cite.

### Assistant
You do not have to share a bed or a room to be a team player. Saving money is a real wish. It is not a voucher for your night.

Say: "I'm booking my own room. I'm not sharing." If you need a reason you will share, "I sleep badly with anyone else in the room" or "I need my own lock" is enough. You do not owe a medical story. You do not owe a gender theory. I will not invent the company's travel policy. If there is a booking tool that allows singles, use it. If Chris books a double anyway, you can still book a second room and tell them you will not use the double.

Do not offer "I'll take the floor." Floor is still the room. Do not offer "only if there are two beds" if what you want is a door. Two beds is still one key.

First time will feel like you are making a fuss. Fuss is often just a lock.

Has Chris already entered a shared reservation, or is this a Slack idea?

### User
Slack idea, but they cc'd finance with "trying to be efficient." There is a queen screenshot. I have a card I can put a room on and expense if that's how we do it. I don't actually know if singles get rejected. I don't want you to guess the policy.

### Assistant
Do not guess it either. Book the single if the tool lets you. In Slack, not only in your head: "I'll take a separate room. Please don't book me on the queen." Reply all if finance is already in the thread, so efficiency does not get recorded as your yes. If you reply only to Chris, finance may still book the screenshot.

I will not promise the expense clears. I will not invent a cap. If finance later asks why two rooms, you can say, "I don't share hotel rooms." That is a complete why. You do not have to write a memo about safety. If you want the word safety in it, you can use it because it is true for you. You do not have to prove a past incident. Needing a door is allowed without a crime story.

If Chris jokes about being a blanket hog, do not joke back. Jokes are a handshake. Repeat the separate room. Then go book it so the conversation is behind the reservation, not in front of it.

If the only remaining room is a double with Chris, you look at other hotels on the block. I will not invent their rates. A different lobby is still a door.

### User
I'm scared they'll discuss it in the van. There's four of us. I can already hear the tease. Also I don't want Chris to think I think they're a creep. They're probably just cheap.

### Assistant
Cheap and creep are not the only two settings. You can decline a cheap plan without charging them as a threat. In the van, if it comes up, "I booked my own, boring sleeper" is a shrug. If the tease continues, you can look at your phone. You do not have to entertain a referendum on your sheets.

If you want one private line to Chris to protect the relationship: "This isn't about you personally. I don't share rooms on work trips." Then drop it. Reassurance is optional. The booking is not.

Do not wait to see if they "find a suite with a pullout." Pullouts are still the room. First time means you do the booking tonight, while it is still Slack, before a confirmation email glues you to a queen. Own lock. Own invoice if that is how your card works. Let efficiency live in the airfare, not in your sleep. You can be easy on the trip and still hard on the door. Those can both be true. Book the single. Go to sleep without an audience.
