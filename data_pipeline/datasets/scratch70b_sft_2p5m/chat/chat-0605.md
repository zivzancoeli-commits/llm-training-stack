### User
I bought an ADA seat months ago. Tonight the usher said that row was resold and I should "try the overflow." Overflow is a folding chair behind a speaker. I have a ticket in my email with the seat. The show starts in twenty minutes. I don't want you to invent the ADA rulebook. I want the next twenty minutes: who to talk to, what to say, whether I eat the folding chair so I am not in the lobby for the opener.

### Assistant
You have a ticket with a seat. Overflow behind a speaker is not that seat. Start with the person who can actually move tickets, not the usher who already said try overflow.

Twenty minutes: ask for the house manager or guest services, not a second usher with the same script. Sentence: "I have an ADA ticket for [seat]. It was resold. I need an equivalent accessible seat, not a folding chair behind a speaker." Show the email. Stay standing at the desk. I will not invent what they owe you. I will not invent a refund amount. I will not invent a statute. Equivalent is the word to keep using. Folding chair is the word to refuse until they say they have nothing else.

If they offer a different accessible location with a line of sight, look at it if you can get there in time. If they only have the speaker chair, you decide whether the music matters more than the view tonight. That is your call. It is not you being easy. It is a clock.

Do not cry on purpose. Do not apologize for the ticket you bought.

Is guest services a real booth, or only this usher in the aisle?

### User
There's a booth on the concourse. One person, a line of four. I can still walk. I use the ADA seat because standing too long and stairs are bad, not because I cannot walk at all. People already look at me like I am faking. I hate that part.

### Assistant
You do not owe the line a performance of disability. You owe the booth the ticket. Walk there now. If standing the line will wreck you, say at the front: "I cannot stand long. I have a resold ADA seat and the show is soon." They can pull you to the counter or not. Ask. I will not promise they will.

Do not explain your stairs to the four people behind you. Their looks are not a tribunal. The email is.

If the booth offers a refund and goodbye, you can take the refund and leave, or you can say, "I came to see the show. I need a seat I can use." Pick one. Bargaining both at once is how you miss the opener in a hallway. If they find a seat at the back of a platform with an armrest and a view, go look. If they point again at the speaker, you can say no once more, then choose show versus exit. I will not shame either.

Twenty minutes is already fifteen. Move.

### User
They offered a "companion seat in the back" that is a regular chair with no extra space, or the speaker fold-out. Manager is "on a walkie." Opener is probably starting.

### Assistant
Wait for the walkie if you can still stand it, with a time cap. Five minutes. Then you pick among real options, not hoped-for ones. Regular companion chair with no extra space may be as bad as stairs. Only you know. Speaker fold-out may be loud and useless. Leaving may be the night. None of those is you faking. The resale already happened. You are shopping leftovers.

If the manager appears, repeat the emailed seat and "equivalent accessible," then stop stacking your medical history. History is not a ticket. The ticket was the ticket.

I will not tell you to record them in a state where that is a problem. I will tell you to keep the email and the names if they give them. Tomorrow is a complaint if you want one. Tonight is a seat or a door.

Cap the walkie wait. Choose. Sit or leave. Do not spend the opener proving you deserve the chair you already bought. You did deserve it. They resold it. Get the best remaining thing you can actually use, or get your night back. That is the twenty minutes. After the show, paper. Right now, booth, walkie, cap, choose.
