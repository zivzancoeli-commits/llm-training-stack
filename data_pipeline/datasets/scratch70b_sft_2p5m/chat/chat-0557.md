### User
I have a second date tomorrow. I cannot do it. Work dumped a thing that will run late, and my brain is fried in a way that would make me a bad time, not a mysterious one. Last time I cancelled plans I just stopped answering for two days and the person thought I died. I don't want that. I also don't want a paragraph that sounds like a breakup. First time I'll reschedule out loud like an adult. They're a texter who reads tone into punctuation.

### Assistant
Text today, not tomorrow an hour before. Early is the adult part.

"I need to move tomorrow. Work ran over and I wouldn't be good company. I still want to see you. Are you free [two actual options]?" That's a reschedule. It has a want and a date. Vanish is the thing with no dates.

Skip the novel about your brain. Fried is yours. "Wouldn't be good company" is enough without a diagnosis or a trauma tour. I will not diagnose you. Don't diagnose yourself in the text as a gift.

Punctuation: one period is fine. Don't send four apologies. Don't send a voice note if you hate your voice when you're tired; a short text is cleaner. Emoji optional. If they read cold into a period, the content still has "I still want to see you." That's the load-bearing wall.

Have you already been a little slow to reply this week, or is tomorrow the first wobble?

### User
A little slow. I left one message on read for six hours because I didn't know how to answer a "how was your day." So they might already think I'm cooling off. That's why I'm scared a reschedule looks like a fade.

### Assistant
Then the reschedule has to include the still-want and the new time. Fade is open-ended. You're closing the loop.

You can also answer the leftover "how was your day" in the same bubble so it doesn't sit as evidence. One line: "Day got eaten. Sorry I went quiet." Then the move request. Two topics, one message, not a stack of delayed guilt.

If they offer "we can just skip it," you take that as a question, not a verdict. "I'd rather move than skip unless you want out." Some people test. Some people mean it. You won't know until they answer. You still don't vanish while you wait. If they go quiet, you wait a normal amount, not two days of imagined funerals.

Don't over-explain work. A dumped thing is enough. Mystery plus details is how people smell a lie even when you're honest.

### User
What if they say "it's fine" and nothing else. No new day. I will want to write a second essay.

### Assistant
One follow-up, not an essay. "If you'd rather not reschedule, say so. If you would, Thursday or Sunday work on my side." Then you stop. "It's fine" is sometimes hurt and sometimes low-text. You cannot fix hurt with a paragraph about punctuation.

If they don't answer, you don't pile on at midnight. You did the opposite of vanishing: you named a conflict, you offered times, you asked them to pick. That's a complete first-time adult cancel.

Tomorrow you do the work thing. You don't show up fried to prove you're not a ghost. Showing up wrecked is another way to disappear while sitting in a chair.

Send the message before you open a spiral. Options in it. Still-want in it. No two-day hole. That's the whole skill.

If they accept Thursday, you show up on Thursday like a person who meant it. That's how reschedule differs from vanish. If they pick Sunday, same. Don't add a third apology on the morning of the new date. The first message already carried the want.

Slow replies this week get better after you send this, not by writing a memoir about punctuation. Answer short things short. Leave "how was your day" with one true clause. You don't have to be a sparkling texter to not be a ghost. Tomorrow's work dump can exist. The second date can move. Both can be real. Send it.
