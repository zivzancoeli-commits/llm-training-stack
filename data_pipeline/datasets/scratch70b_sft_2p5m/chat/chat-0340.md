### User
Someone is riding a skateboard in the third-floor hallway. It is 1:08 a.m. I can hear the wheels hit the metal threshold at my door every loop. I have work at 8. I don't know if it's a neighbor's kid, a drunk adult, or a visitor. I don't want to open the door in pajamas and start a fight. I also cannot sleep through the clack. Building group chat exists. Super is asleep. What is the move that gets quiet without me becoming the hallway cop?

### Assistant
Do not open the door into a moving board at 1 a.m. if you can solve it with a wall and a phone.

First pass: a firm knock from inside, not a full open. Two knocks, then your voice through the door: "It's after 1. Please stop riding in the hall." You are talking to a behavior, not hunting a face. If they stop, you won. Go back to bed. Do not give a lecture through the peephole.

If they do not stop, building chat is better than you in socks in a dark corridor. One line: "Someone is skateboarding on 3 at 1 a.m. Please take it outside or wait until morning." No theories about whose kid. Theories start wars.

I will not invent your building's quiet hours. Many places have them; yours might. The chat and a note to the super in the morning are the paper version. Tonight is noise abatement, not an investigation.

Is it looping past your door only, or does it sound like they're doing the whole floor?

### User
Whole floor. I looked through the peephole. It's a teenager, maybe, hoodie, no helmet, board is loud on the tile. They didn't hear me the first time I thought about knocking because I didn't actually knock. I just stood there. There's a stairwell at the end.

### Assistant
You stood there. Fine. Now knock. Door closed. Voice: "Hey — please stop. People are sleeping." Then wait. Teenagers sometimes stop when a real adult exists. Sometimes they do a last trick. If they do a last trick, do not open the door to block the board. You are not a cone.

If they keep going, send the chat message. Then put in earplugs or a fan. You have an 8 a.m. job. A perfect capture of justice is not the assignment.

In the morning, a note under the super's door or an email: time, floor, skateboard in the hallway, you asked through the door. Ask them to remind residents that hallways are not a park. You still do not need a name. If you recognized the kid from 3C, you can say 3C. If you are guessing, do not guess.

Do not post a photo from the peephole in the group chat. That turns sleep into a trial. Text is enough.

If this is a one-off, let it be a one-off. If it happens again this week, the second report is what makes it a pattern. Patterns get handled. One night of wheels is often a bored visitor.

### User
If I knock and they film me, or they get rude, I will replay it all day at work. Maybe I should just sleep on the floor of the living room.

### Assistant
You can sleep in the living room if the bedroom is the drum. That is a valid tonight move. It is not a permanent policy. A closed-door sentence is still worth doing once so you did not only flee.

If they film, you can close your mouth after the one sentence and shut up. You do not owe a second act for their camera. Rude is noise. You can not collect it.

Do not chase them down the stairwell. Tile plus a board plus a tired adult is how ankles get involved.

Tonight: knock and speak once, or skip straight to chat plus living-room sleep if your nerves are already shot. Morning: one factual note to the super. You do not have to win the hallway. You have to be functional at 8. Quiet is the goal. Being the cop is optional, and tonight it is mostly optional. Get the loops to stop, then get the hours you can.
