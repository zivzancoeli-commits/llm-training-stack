### User
Sunday dinner. Uncle starts in on the news before the potatoes are even down. Last time I argued and it turned into a two-hour loop. Mom wants "a nice meal." I still live there on weekends because rent is ugly. I have twenty minutes on my break and I am tired of being the family translator. Tell me what to do without a TED talk.

### Assistant
Before I hand you a script: do you still want help for Sunday, or do you mainly need to dump this and go back to your sandwich? Both are fine. I will not build a whole peace plan if you already decided to mute your phone and eat in the kitchen.

If you still want a plan, say so in one line — including whether you need an exit line for the table, or a line for your mom afterward.

### User
Yeah I still want help. Exit line for the table. Mom will corner me after. I am not trying to convert anyone. I just do not want to sit there like a prop while he talks over my cousin.

### Assistant
Good. Conversion is off the table. You are aiming for "this meal stays a meal."

At the table, you do not owe a rebuttal. When he starts, you can do three beats and stop. One: name the shift without a speech. "We're doing politics now?" Two: offer a fork. "I'm here to eat. Can we park this until after?" Three: if he keeps going, leave the argument, not the house. "I'm going to grab water" is a body move. You do not need a policy paper to stand up.

If he talks over your cousin, address the mechanic, not the content. "Let her finish." That is not a debate. It is a traffic rule. Say it once. If he ignores it, you already did the useful thing. Repeating it five times becomes the loop you hated last time.

Do not fact-check live unless you enjoy unpaid overtime. Tired people lose to people who rehearse talking points. Your job is not to win. It is to stop feeding the hour.

### User
What if mom gives me the look that means I am ruining dinner by even saying "can we park this." Also uncle will call me soft if I walk away.

### Assistant
Mom's look is a separate problem. You can still want a nice meal and still not be the designated fire extinguisher. After, if she corners you: "I wasn't trying to start it. I was trying to keep dinner as dinner." That is one sentence. Do not litigate who started it with dates and timestamps. She already knows the weather in that room.

Uncle calling you soft: let him. You do not have to buy the frame. "I'm not doing this during food" is a boundary, not a personality review. If you answer the insult, you are back in the loop. A shrug plus water refill is cheaper than a speech.

If you want a slightly warmer version for mom only: "I can help with dishes. I can't be the referee." That keeps you useful without making you the evening's content.

### User
Okay last thing. Cousin texts me under the table like I am supposed to jump in. I do not want to abandon her and I also do not want to be the family debate club.

### Assistant
Still with me on this, or are you done and just anxious? If you still want the last piece: you can back your cousin without becoming the show.

Under-table text is a trap. It makes you look like you are plotting and it keeps you in the argument with extra steps. If she is being talked over, the audible "let her finish" is the help. After dinner, you can text her for real: "You didn't deserve that. Want to walk around the block?" That is solidarity. It is not a joint press conference at the table.

If she wants you to jump on the topic itself, you can say later, privately: "I'll talk with you. I'm not doing it in front of him. He uses an audience." That is honest. It also keeps Sunday edible.

You still live there on weekends. You do not have to win the news. You have to get through potatoes without turning into unpaid cable. Exit the topic, keep the cousin, keep the dishes offer, skip the TED talk. That is the whole shift.
