### User
My bike is locked to the rack outside the library. Combination lock, four digits. I thought it was my old apartment number. It isn't. I've tried three close numbers. The library closes at 8. It's 7:41. I have a meeting across campus at 8:10. If I leave the bike I don't know if it will be here at 10. If I keep spinning I look like I'm stealing my own bike. A security person already walked past once.

### Assistant
Stop spinning random cousins of the apartment number. Three tries is a pattern. Twenty tries is how people get a conversation with security you don't want, and how some locks seize. I don't know this lock's reset or attempt limit. I won't invent it. I will say: pause.

Tell the security person, if they pass again, that it's your bike and you're stuck on the combo. That's better than looking like a thief who froze. Student ID or a photo of the bike in your camera roll helps if you have it.

I won't guess the combination. Birthdays, old apartments, 1-2-3-4, the year you bought it — you can try two more that you actually remember as yours, not "close." Close is a trap.

Do you have the combo written in a notes app, a photo of the receipt, or an email from the lock brand?

### User
There's a photo in my camera roll from September of the lock on the fridge with a sticky note. I can't tell if the glare is a 6 or an 8 on the third digit. I have my ID. The bike has a sticker I put on the stem. Meeting is with a professor who hates lateness.

### Assistant
Open the photo, pinch, turn down the brightness if glare is the issue, or step under the library lamp. 6 versus 8 is a binary you can solve with a better look, not with more spins in the dark.

For the next ten minutes: one look at the photo, two tries max if you can actually see a number, then decide. If it opens, go. If it doesn't, talk to security, leave the bike, walk to the 8:10. A professor who hates lateness will hate a 8:25 story about a lock even more if you're still at the rack.

Text the professor now, not after you're late: "Locked out of my bike at the library, walking, might be 10 minutes behind." Send it while you still might be on time. Don't write an essay.

If security can log the bike or watch the rack, ask. I don't know their policy. If they can't, you still go to the meeting. A bike is property. A no-show is a relationship with a person who already tracks the clock.

Don't cut the lock tonight unless you own bolt cutters you don't have. Library sidewalk at 7:41 is a bad workshop.

### User
If I leave it, I'll replay the 6 and 8 all through the meeting. Also what if I remember the combo at 8:05 while sitting in the office?

### Assistant
Then you still stay in the office. Walking out at 8:05 to test a lock is how you become late twice. Write 6 and 8 on a scrap so your brain can stop chewing. You can test after the meeting. 10 p.m. bike is a problem. 8:10 professor is the nearer one.

If you remember a fourth digit with certainty, that's for 9:00, after you said goodbye. Not for a mid-meeting sprint.

Show security the stem sticker and the fridge photo if they want proof. Then go. Sit in the meeting with your ID in your pocket and the two candidate numbers on paper. You already tried apartment logic. The sticky note is the actual clue. Use your eyes under a light, not your panic. If the rack is empty at 10, that's a police or campus report with the serial if you have it. I won't script that unless it happens. Right now: photo, two tries, honesty with security, text, walk. The lock can wait on the rack. The professor will not wait on the rack. Move your feet toward the office while you still have 20 minutes of sidewalk.
