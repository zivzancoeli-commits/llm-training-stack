### User
I have a written offer from Place A. They want an answer by Friday 5 p.m. Place B, the one I actually want, said their committee "should" meet Thursday. Should is doing a lot of work. It's Tuesday 8:15 p.m. I haven't told Place A I have another process. I don't want to look greedy. I also don't want to accept A and ghost a Thursday that might be real.

### Assistant
You're not greedy for wanting a calendar that matches. Friday 5 is a deadline someone chose. "Should meet Thursday" is not a deadline. Treat them as different species.

I won't invent what A will do if you ask for time. Some teams can move Friday. Some won't. I also won't promise B meets on Thursday. Committees slip.

You can tell A you have another process without a speech about your worth. "I have a remaining interview process wrapping this week. Can you hold until Monday?" is a normal sentence. Looking greedy is a feeling. Asking for four days is a request.

Have you already signed anything, or is this still email and a PDF?

### User
PDF, unsigned. Place A called me at 4:50 to "talk through excitement." I said I needed to read it. Place B's recruiter sent a thumbs-up emoji when I asked about Thursday, then went quiet. I printed the A offer and it's on the table next to takeout.

### Assistant
Unsigned PDF is still a maybe. Leave it unsigned. Excitement calls are their job. Reading is yours.

For the next ten minutes: email A, not a 9 p.m. phone call. Thank them, name Friday, ask if Monday noon is possible because you have one process to close. Don't name B. Don't name salary theater. I won't invent numbers and you shouldn't open a bidding war you can't see.

Separately, email B's recruiter in the morning, not tonight at 8:15 with panic: "I have another offer with a Friday decision. Any update on the Thursday committee?" That's information they can use. A thumbs-up emoji is not a timeline. Quiet after emoji is common. Follow up once in writing.

If A says Friday is firm, you decide with the information you have Friday afternoon, not with a fantasy of B. If B hasn't moved, A is the real job. Regret is possible. That's honest. Accepting A while secretly waiting for a Thursday that never confirmed is how people start jobs already half out the door.

Takeout can get eaten. The PDF can stay in a folder labeled with the date, not on the sauce stain.

### User
My dad will say take the bird in the hand. My friend will say play hardball. I don't know which voice is mine.

### Assistant
Neither voice has to be yours tonight. Bird in the hand is a proverb. Hardball is a TV word. Your version can be: ask for Monday, follow up with B once, decide Friday if you have to, don't sign hungry at 8:15.

I can't tell you which job is better. I don't know the work, the managers, or your rent. I can tell you not to ghost A and not to lie that B is a signed competing offer. "Remaining process" is true. "I have an exploding better offer" if you don't is not.

If Monday is denied and Thursday is still fog, pick A's work if you would do that job without B in the picture. If you would not — if A is only a placeholder — then declining A is allowed, with the risk B vanishes. That's a risk with a name, not a vibe.

Write the A email tonight if you can do it in ten minutes. Keep it short. Sleep. In the morning, ping B. Put the printed offer in a bag so it isn't a centerpiece. Friday 5 is real. Thursday's "should" is homework for B, not a promise you can spend. Treat it that way and the greedy feeling gets smaller.
