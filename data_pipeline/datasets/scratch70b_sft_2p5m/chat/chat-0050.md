### User
I have a first therapy appointment Thursday after work. I keep rehearsing small talk like I am about to sit next to a stranger on a plane. What do I even say in the first five minutes without dumping my whole life or sounding like I am shopping for a diagnosis. I am tired. I booked this three weeks ago and now I am not sure I still want to go. Maybe I just need a nap and a cheaper hobby. Talk like we are on break by the vending machines, not like a pamphlet.

### Assistant
Do you still want help with this, or do you want help canceling without feeling like a coward. If you want to cancel, cancel. First sessions are allowed to be declined. If you want to go and you are just fried, we can script the small talk so it does not eat the hour.

I am not going to name what is wrong with you. I do not know. Small talk in that room is logistics and consent, not weather. They will probably ask what made you book. One sentence is enough: I booked because sleep and work have been ugly and I wanted a place that is not my coworkers. You do not owe a childhood timeline in minute one.

If they ask how are you, you can say tired, and I almost canceled, but I am here. Ambivalence is useful, not a failure. It is not a diagnosis.

Skip ranking your pain, quoting articles, asking them to confirm a label you read online. Thursday is a fit check: can you stand talking to this person twice.

A simple open: parking was annoying, I came from work. Then: I have been snapping at people I like and I cannot tell if I am burned out or something else. I do not need a name today. I need to know if this is a fit. Then shut up and let them work.

If you want to leave, say I think I need to stop for today and I will email about rescheduling. You are not trapped.

Still want to go, or are we writing the cancel email?

### User
I think I still want to go. I am scared I will fill silence with jokes and then feel fake. Also they sent an intake form with checkboxes. I left some blank because I did not want to lie. Is that going to make the small talk weirder.

### Assistant
Blank checkboxes are fine. Say I left some blank because I was not sure. Guessing is worse. Forms are not sacred.

One joke is human. A streak is a wall. If you hear yourself doing a bit, name it: I joke when I am nervous. Then answer the actual question, even badly. A bad honest sentence beats a good gag.

Silence in that room is not vending-machine silence. They are used to it. Count to three. If nothing comes, say I do not know how to start that part. That still gives them a handle.

Ask practical things: how long sessions are, what happens if you cancel with short notice. That calms the pop-quiz feeling and tells you if they are decent about real overtime.

If they ask what you want, do not invent a five-year plan. Say I want to stop taking work home in my body, and I want to check whether talking helps. If it does not, I will say so. Keep that honesty so you do not perform wellness.

You still in, or is Thursday a costume again?

### User
I am in. Give me a closing line if the last ten minutes get too personal too fast. I do not want to be rude. I also do not want my whole family history on the table when I only meant to test the chair.

### Assistant
Use a brake, not a joke. Try: I can feel this getting bigger than I planned for a first meeting. Can we stop on this piece and pick it up next time if I come back. The if I come back is true. You have not signed a season pass.

If they push, repeat once: I want to keep today smaller. Repeating is not rude. Rude would be insulting them.

If your voice goes thin, say I am getting tired and I should not drive home spilled open. I am not diagnosing you. Tired is an exit ramp.

After you leave, do not recap to five friends. Water, food, home. Raw is not proof it worked or failed.

Thursday: one honest why, one almost-canceled line, blanks left blank, one joke max, brake if it gets big. If Wednesday night you do not want that, cancel then, not in the parking lot.
