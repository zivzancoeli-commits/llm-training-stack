### User
I have two blue towels on a hook on my door. They're mine. I came home and one was wet on the bathroom bar, hair on it that isn't mine. Jordan showers at 6 a.m. I shower at night. We've never talked about towels. I bought these in January because the house ones feel like sandpaper. I don't want a germ speech. I want my towel back as a concept. They're in the kitchen making eggs. How do I say this without sounding like a sitcom neat freak?

### Assistant
You name the object and the rule. You skip the microbial TED talk.

"The blue towels on my door are mine. Please don't use them. You can use the hall ones or get a set." That's it. If you want, "I found one wet with hair that isn't mine" — one piece of evidence, not a crime board.

Don't lead with disease. I will not diagnose a risk from a towel. You can want a towel that only you use because it feels like yours. That's enough. Last year's you would have bought a third towel and seethed. This year's you can have a hook that means something.

If they say they "grabbed the closest," you don't need a diagram of the apartment. "Grab the hall ones. Leave the blue." Then you take the wet blue one to your laundry.

Are the hall towels actually usable, or is sandpaper the only other option?

### User
Sandpaper is real. That's why I bought blue. Jordan hates shopping. They asked if we can "just share and wash more." That's the sitcom line I was afraid of. Eggs are burning a little. I haven't spoken yet. I'm standing in the bathroom doorway like a ghost.

### Assistant
Come out of the doorway. Turn the eggs down if you're going to be a person in the kitchen, or ignore the eggs. Don't negotiate from a haunt.

"I don't want to share these. I'll keep washing mine. You can order a two-pack or use the hall set." If they hate shopping, you can send them a link later. That's optional kindness, not a requirement that you keep sharing skin towels. If you send a link, it's "here's a set," not "I'll buy yours too" unless you want to. I won't invent a price. They can pick.

"Just share and wash more" still means your blue ones in their 6 a.m. rotation. If that's what you don't want, don't compromise into a laundry schedule that still uses your hook. A compromise could be: they get a color, you keep blue, both hang in the bathroom with names if you need labels. Labels are not a sitcom if they prevent this talk every month.

Take the wet one to wash. That's the physical end of this cycle.

### User
They said "okay sorry" and then "you could've just said." I did not say for five weeks. Do I apologize for the delay or is that a trap?

### Assistant
You can say "yeah, I should have said sooner" if that's true. That's not a trap. It's one sentence. Don't add "it's fine." It wasn't fine. Don't add a joke about hair. Don't write a manifesto about five weeks.

Hang the remaining dry blue on your door, not on the shared bar, if the door hook is how this stays yours. If they "okay sorry" and then use it again, you repeat once, then you keep the towels in your room. That's not a personality. That's a door.

If they ask later whether the hall ones "really" feel that bad, you don't have to rank fabrics. "I bought these for me. Use the hall set or buy your own." That's the whole textile conversation.

Next five minutes: sentence, laundry, eggs are their problem unless you want to be nice about smoke. You don't have to be a neat freak. You can be a person with two blue towels. Get them off the bar. That's the concept, returned.
