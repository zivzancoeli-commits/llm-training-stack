### User
There's a required safety briefing before we go on the floor. Dana said we can skip it because "we know" and the trainer is late. Dana wants me to start with them so we look busy. I do not want to skip a safety briefing because we know. I also don't want to dummy a rebel crew. How do I stay without a sermon?

### Assistant
Stay for the briefing. "We know" is how people skip the part that changes. Trainers being late is not a waiver. Three minutes plus a bag in the room is still a briefing, not a suggestion.

Say: "I'm waiting for the briefing. I'll come on the floor after." If Dana starts walking, you do not have to chase. Looking busy is not a safety credential. I will not invent OSHA language. I will not invent your site's write-up rules. I will tell you the practical split: you can look idle for ten minutes or you can look clever and skip. Take idle. Idle is cheaper than a story you do not want attached to your name if something dumb happens.

If the trainer is actually cancelled, a supervisor should say so. Dana is not a supervisor in this story. Do not accept a peer as the all-clear.

Do not write a manifesto in the group chat. One sentence, then you sit on the chair the briefing uses.

Is the trainer late by five minutes, or did Dana decide "late" at the start time?

### User
Start time plus three. Trainer's bag is in the room. Dana is already tying on a vest and telling two new hires to follow. The new hires looked at me.

### Assistant
Bag in the room means a human is coming. Three minutes is not a cancellation. The new hires looked at you because you look like a person who might know. You can say to them, not as a cop, as a fact: "Briefing first. Trainer's bag is here." Then you stay. If they follow Dana, that is their mistake and Dana's. You still stay. You do not have to grab their vests. You do not have to dummy a rebel crew or a hall monitor. You model the wait.

If Dana calls you chicken, you can say, "I'm waiting." Chicken is a word. A briefing is a door to the floor. You want the door.

When the trainer arrives, you do not tattle as an opening. You attend. If asked where the others went, you tell the truth: they went to the floor. Truth is not a sermon. It is a location.

New hires will remember who waited. That is enough social risk to take.

### User
Dana will say I made them look bad in front of new people. I might have. I still don't want to skip.

### Assistant
They made themselves look like they skip. You stood still. If Dana corners you later, "I wasn't going to skip a required briefing, especially with new hires" is one line. Then you work. You do not need a rest of the week apology tour.

If a supervisor asks why the crew split, same location sentence. You waited. They did not. I will not guess who gets the lecture. I will guess that "because we know" ages badly if something dumb happens on the floor. You do not need that on your day.

Sit. Briefing. Then floor. Vest can wait three more minutes. Looking busy can wait. Knowing can be refreshed. That is the point of a briefing you have heard before: the one slide that changed. You will not hear it from Dana's vest. You will hear it from the bag that is already in the room. Stay in the room. Let the rebel crew be a story they tell. You be the person who went on the floor after the required part. No sermon. A chair. A wait. Then the work. The floor will still be there. The briefing is the door. Use the door.
