### User
Rent is due tomorrow. Payroll is late. HR said "early next week" and would not give me a day. I have enough in checking to cover groceries and gas. I do not have the rent sitting there. I have paid on time for two years. I need to tell my landlord today and I keep writing emails that sound either like a sob story or like I'm announcing a vacation. I am not asking you to tell me the law. I am asking how to say this without making it worse, and without doing the fake-smile "hope you're well!" thing.

### Assistant
Say it once, in writing, short, and without decorating it.

Lead with the fact, the date you can actually defend, and how they want the gap handled. Do not open with weather. Do not call it "a slight delay on my end." Do not write "as you know I am a great tenant."

Something in this shape: "I will not have rent in hand tomorrow. My paycheck is delayed and I was told it should arrive early next week. I don't have a confirmed deposit date yet. I will send rent the same day it hits. If you want a partial payment tomorrow from what I have, tell me the amount that actually helps." Then you stop.

That is not cheerful. It is also not a collapse. You are telling them the payment will miss the due date, and you are offering a path.

I will not invent what they can charge, what a "grace period" is where you live, or what happens after three days. Those rules vary. If they reply with a late fee or a notice, read the paper. Don't argue the fee in the first message.

You can mention on-time history in one clause: "I've paid on time for the last two years." Facts, not a medal. A skeptical person can smell a speech.

If you only ever email, stay on email so there is a record. Don't call and email with different dates.

### User
She's going to think I'm lying because "payroll is late" sounds like a story. I could show her the HR email. Does that make me look like a child, or does that make me look serious?

### Assistant
Showing the email is serious if the email actually says the pay date moved. Forwarding a vague "thanks for your patience" note that never mentions payroll will look like theater. If HR named the delay, attach or forward that, plus one sentence: "This is what I have from work. I will update you when I have a deposit date." You are not asking her to grade your job. You are reducing the chance she fills in a worse story.

If the HR note is messy, don't send a screenshot of a chat full of other people's business. Ask HR for one sentence you can share: pay is delayed, expected window. I don't know if they will give you that. If they won't, you still send the landlord the truth you have, not a novel.

What you should not do is offer a date you cannot hit. "I'll pay Friday" when HR said "early next week" is how you burn the two years of on-time history in one weekend. Better to be late by a few days you announced than early in your email and late in the bank.

Don't volunteer extra drama: medical bills, a roommate fight, how unfair the company is. Extra plot points make a skeptical landlord hunt for a worse reason. Keep it boring. Payroll missed. Money isn't there. You will pay when it is.

### User
Partial payment. If I send her everything except grocery money, I eat rice until payday. If I send nothing, she thinks I'm not trying. I hate both.

### Assistant
I can't tell you a dollar amount. I don't know the rent, and I will not invent one.

Ask her which partial, if any, actually matters to her bookkeeping. Some landlords want a token. Some would rather wait and get the whole amount in one transfer. You offering a menu she didn't ask for can look like you are negotiating against yourself. One question: "Do you want a partial tomorrow, or the full amount when payroll lands?" Then you do the answer you can actually fund. If the answer is partial, send a number you can live with, and write the remainder as still owed, not as a vibe.

You sound like you want this sent today. Write the short version, attach the HR note if it is clean, and go. Don't wait until 11 p.m. hoping the paycheck appears in the app.
