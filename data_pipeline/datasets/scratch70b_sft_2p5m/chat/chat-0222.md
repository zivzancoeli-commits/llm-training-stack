### User
I think I left my wallet on the bench by track 4. I already walked back. It's gone. Cards, ID, like eleven dollars. A guy in a vest asked if I needed help and I froze. I don't want to get lectured and I don't want to stand here looking like a target. What do I do in the next few minutes without making this worse?

### Assistant
Stop hunting the same bench. If it was there, it isn't. The next few minutes are: freeze the cards, tell the station, get yourself onto the next train or into a staffed room, not a third lap of the platform.

I am not going to invent which number to call for your bank. You know which apps you have. Open the first one and lock or report the card while you're still standing somewhere with light and cameras, not while you're walking with your head down.

If the vest guy is still nearby and they look like actual station staff, you can say yes to help for a lost wallet. That's their job. If you're not sure they're staff, you can say no. You do not owe a stranger your full name because you're scared.

Do you have your phone, and can you still pay for a ticket another way, or are you actually stuck here?

### User
I have my phone. Ticket is already on the phone. I can get home. The vest guy left. There's a window that says Lost Property but the line is long. Someone behind me just asked if I need cash for a cab. I don't. I feel rude.

### Assistant
You can say no without a story. "No thanks, I'm okay." That's a complete sentence. You don't have to explain the phone ticket, the eleven dollars, or that you don't take cash from people you just met. Last year's you might have invented a reason so nobody felt rejected. This year's you can let them feel a small no. It's cheaper than a new problem.

Join the lost-property line if you can stay for it. Give them the bench, the time, and a description of the wallet. Don't perform a whole panic. Staff hear this all day. If the line is too long and your train is the last one you can make, get on the train. You can call the station from home. A wallet is worse than missing the last train, but standing in a station broke and tired is how people accept help they didn't want.

On the phone, in order: lock debit, lock credit, glance at whether your ID is replaceable tomorrow. I don't know your state's ID office hours and I won't fake them. I do know you shouldn't post "lost wallet, track 4" in a public feed with your full name. If you want to ask friends, do it in a group chat, not on the timeline.

The cash-for-a-cab person might be kind. They might not be. You don't have to solve that puzzle. "No thanks" covers both.

### User
What if I find out later someone used a card? And should I go back tonight after I get home?

### Assistant
If a charge shows up, that's a bank conversation, not a midnight return to track 4. Use the app's fraud or "I don't recognize this" path. Don't try to out-think a thief in a station after hours. The window will still exist in the morning.

Don't go back tonight. The bench will still be empty and the station will be thinner. Go home on the ticket you already have. Text one person that you're on the train so somebody knows you're moving.

Tomorrow: lost property call or visit, card replacements, ID plan. Tonight: locks, train, door lock, water, sleep. You already did the hard part, which was noticing it was gone instead of hoping it would appear if you stared at the bench long enough.

Say no to the cab cash. Say yes to the staff window if you can without missing the train. Don't invent an excuse for either. That's the whole script. Get on the train.
