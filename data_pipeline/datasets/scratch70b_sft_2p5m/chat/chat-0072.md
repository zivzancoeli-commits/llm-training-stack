### User
My desk is in the middle of the open floor. A client just emailed that they want to "hop on a quick call" in ten minutes. My manager pinged "take it at your desk, everyone does." Everyone does not. Everyone pretends the whole room cannot hear their pipeline numbers. I do not have a booked room. I do not want to invent a doctor's appointment or a dying laptop. Last year I would have just taken the call and then hated myself for forty minutes. I need the sentence that is a no, and then what I actually do with my body so I am not standing there looking insubordinate.

### Assistant
Say no to the desk. Do not build a medical subplot.

To your manager: "I am not taking this client call at my desk. It is too loud here and I will be talking about their numbers in an open room. I am going to the stairwell / the empty phone booth / outside the building. If none of those work I will ask to reschedule."

That is a no plus a next action. It is not "I can't because my throat" and it is not "everyone else is wrong" as a speech. You are declining a location, not the job.

If they push "everyone does," you do not need a TED talk about open offices. Repeat the constraint: "I am not discussing this client at my desk." Then you stand up and go to the least-bad private-ish place you already named. Looking insubordinate is mostly a feeling you get while you remain seated, waiting for permission to have ears. Walking while you send "calling in two from a quieter spot" to the client is the job.

To the client, do not apologize for the architecture. "I am moving to a quieter place and will call you from there in a few minutes." If you cannot find quiet in ten minutes, tell the truth: "I cannot get a private space in the next ten. Can we do thirty minutes later, or can this be email." That is also a no, still without a fictional excuse.

Last-year-you took the call because "quick" sounded cheaper than a boundary. It was not cheaper. You paid in concentration, in leaking someone else's business, and in the slow training of your manager that your desk is a conference room. This year's version is shorter.

If there is truly nowhere — no booth, no stairwell that is not an echo, no outside that is not a bus — then the honest no is to the live call, not a fake calendar conflict. "I don't have a private place to take this. Let's move it." People survive a delay. Clients do not need your cubicle soundtrack.

Body: headphones on if you use them, laptop or phone in hand, you are already moving when you send the manager message. Do not wait at the desk for a debate. The debate is what keeps you in the noise. You can be polite and still leave.

You asked for the sentence. Use it once. Then go call from somewhere that is not a stage.

### User
Manager already replied "just this once, they are waiting." The client is a person I actually like. I can feel the old move: take the call, whisper, hope the row behind me is on lunch. If I say no again I sound difficult. If I invent a meeting in another building I am back to the excuse problem.

### Assistant
"Just this once" is how last year started. You do not need a second excuse. You already told the truth.

Reply: "I am still not taking it at my desk. Calling them from the stairwell in two minutes. If the line is bad I will reschedule." Then stand up. You are not refusing the client. You are refusing the desk. Difficult would be disappearing. Walking ten meters with a phone is the work.

Do not invent a meeting in another building. If the stairwell is terrible, say the stairwell is terrible and ask the client for a later slot. People who like you can wait twenty minutes. People who need secrecy should not have their numbers read out next to accounting.

Last-year-you whispered and then spent the afternoon half in the call and half in other people's keyboards. This year's no is one sentence, no costume, no doctor. Send it. Move. If your manager wants to talk about culture, that conversation can happen after the client is off the phone and you are not leaking a pipeline into the carpet.
