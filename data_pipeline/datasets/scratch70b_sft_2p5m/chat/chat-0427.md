### User
I have an appointment tomorrow for a follow-up. Last visit they listed tests in letters. I nodded. I still do not know what they are checking or when results show up. I am not asking you what the tests mean or what I have. I am asking how to slow a doctor down without looking like I didn't listen. I want plain words, next steps, and a way to write it down. I freeze when they have the mouse.

### Assistant
You can ask for English in the room. That is allowed even if you nodded last time. Nodding was a freeze, not a contract.

Tomorrow, start early, before the mouse race: "I need you to explain the tests in plain language, no acronyms, and I need to know when and how I get results. I'm going to write it down." Then you take out a notebook or a notes app so they can see you are recording, not challenging.

If an acronym slips, interrupt once: "What does that stand for, in regular words?" If they look impatient, you can say, "I want to follow the plan. I need the words." You do not need to apologize for last visit.

I will not interpret your labs or name a condition. If you want a second explanation, you can ask if a nurse can repeat it after. Some clinics will. Some will not.

Do you have a person who can sit with you, or are you going alone?

### User
Alone. I can record on my phone if that's allowed — I don't want you to invent the law. I brought a list: what, why, when results, what I should watch for that is urgent versus wait. I worry the last one makes me sound like I want a Web search.

### Assistant
Your list is a good visit. Urgent versus wait is a safety question, not a hobby. Ask it as: "What should make me call this office, versus what can wait for the result?" You are asking their threshold, not the internet's.

On recording: ask them. "Is it okay if I record so I don't mix up the names?" If they say no, you write. If they say yes, you record. I will not tell you the legal default.

When they grab the mouse, you can say, "Before we look at the screen, can we do my four questions?" Put the list on the table. Shared paper slows people more than a speech.

If they answer in letters again, repeat the expansion back: "So that's the test that looks at X, results in Y days, through the portal." Teach-back is a normal trick. It is not you being difficult. It is you closing the loop.

Leave with a written name of the test in words, not only letters, even if you have to ask them to write it.

### User
What if they say "it's all in the after-visit summary" and stand up? Last time the summary still had the letters.

### Assistant
If they stand, you stay seated ten more seconds: "The summary used letters. I need one sentence in words and the result timing." If they still go, you ask the nurse or the checkout person the same two items. You can also portal-message: "Please spell out the tests from today's visit in plain language and when results appear." Written, dated.

You are not required to understand a letter salad to be a good patient. You are required to leave with a next step you could explain to a roommate.

Tomorrow: list on the table, no-acronym ask, teach-back, write the words. If the mouse starts early, park it with the four questions. Freeze can happen. The list is there for freeze. Read the list out loud if your voice goes. That still counts. Go get the plain words. You already know you need them. That is enough of a reason.
