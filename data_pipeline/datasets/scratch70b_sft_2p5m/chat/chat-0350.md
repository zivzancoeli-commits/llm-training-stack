### User
The party is at 2. It is 9:15. The bakery just called: the cake "isn't going to be ready until 4, maybe 4:30." I ordered it ten days ago, chocolate, name on it, pickup at noon. Twelve people are coming, including a seven-year-old whose name is on the cake. I am standing in my kitchen with streamers. I don't know whether to yell, cry, or start baking a sad sheet cake. What is the actual sequence so 2 p.m. still has dessert?

### Assistant
Do not bake a panic cake yet. Sequence first.

Ask the bakery three questions on this call, before you hang up: Can they do a simpler cake by 1? Can they transfer the order to another store in the chain if they have one? What exactly will be ready at 4 — the written cake, or a blank? Write the answers. Then you decide.

If they can do a plain chocolate round by 1 with the name in a piping bag you finish at home, take it. Kids care about chocolate and their name. They do not care about your original sketch.

If they cannot, you need a 2 p.m. dessert that is not this bakery. Grocery bakery, second shop, ice cream cake from a freezer case, cupcakes. I will not quote prices or pretend I know what is on the shelf. Call the nearest two places now, while you still have hours. Ask "what can I pick up before 1:30 that feeds twelve."

Yelling at the first bakery does not produce ganache. A short "I needed it at noon for a 2 p.m. party; what can you actually give me before 1:30" is the useful heat.

Is the name decoration the non-negotiable, or is chocolate the non-negotiable?

### User
The kid knows there is a cake with their name. Chocolate was the favorite, not sacred. I have a car. There's a grocery with a bakery twenty minutes away. I haven't called them. I already posted "cake at 2" in the family thread.

### Assistant
Call the grocery bakery now. Ask for a chocolate cake, any round or sheet that feeds a dozen, and whether they can write the name. If they can write it, you are done. If they cannot, buy the cake and a tube of icing. You can write a name. It can look like an adult did it in a hurry. That is still a name.

Tell the original bakery you will pick up the fancy one at 4 if you still want it, or cancel the 4:30 if you do not want two cakes. Two cakes is allowed. Leftover cake is not a failure. It is a freezer.

Edit the family thread: "Dessert is happening; pickup shifted." You do not need to live-blog the bakery's failure. People need to know they should still come at 2.

Do not start a from-scratch cake at 9:30 unless you already bake under stress and have ingredients. A grocery cake is the professional move. Streamers can wait twenty minutes.

If the grocery is sold out, ice cream plus cookies is a party. Say "we're doing sundaes" without shame. The seven-year-old will survive a pivot. What they will not enjoy is a host who is crying into a mixer while guests stand in the hallway.

### User
The bakery offered 20 percent off the late cake if I still take it at 4:30. I don't know if that's a trap. Guests may linger.

### Assistant
It is not a trap. It is a coupon for a cake that missed the party. Take it only if you want extra dessert at 5, when people might still be there, or if you want a freezer cake. If the afternoon will be done at 4, skip it and stop paying attention to that shop.

I will not tell you whether 20 percent is fair. Fair would have been noon. A discount is a leftover. Use it or don't.

Right now: grocery call, car, name on something before 2. Put candles in your bag. Put a knife on the table. You can be angry at 3 p.m. on the porch. Anger is allowed. It is not the 9:15 task. The 9:15 task is a cake that exists at 2. Make the second bakery real. Then tape the streamers. Then let the kid see their name, even if the handwriting is yours.
