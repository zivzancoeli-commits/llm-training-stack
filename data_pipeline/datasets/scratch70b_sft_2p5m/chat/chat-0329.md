### User
The gray bin with my unit number is at the curb every Thursday, and it is always heavier than what I put in it. I started watching. The guy from 4B walks over with a kitchen bag around 9:30 the night before pickup, dumps it, and walks back like this is a shared dumpster. It is not. Last week it overflowed and I had to keep my own bag in the kitchen overnight. We nod in the hallway. I do not want a feud over garbage. What do I actually say, and when?

### Assistant
You say it once, early, and you make it about the bin, not about his character.

Catch him at the bin if you can, not in the hallway with an audience. Keep your own bag in your hand so it is obvious you have trash too. Then one sentence: "Hey — this one is 4A's. I need the space for pickup tomorrow." You do not need a speech about respect.

If you never catch him, try a face before a text, and a text before a note on the lid. One line is enough: "I've noticed bags in 4A's bin on Wednesday nights. I need that capacity. Can you use 4B's?" No emoji. No "hope this finds you well."

I will not invent your building's rules or extra-bag fees. Those vary. A lid that will not close is still your Thursday problem.

Have you actually seen 4B's own bin at the curb, or is it possible theirs is broken, missing, or already full?

### User
Theirs is at the curb every week and it is half empty. I watched last Thursday. He walked past his own bin to use mine. I think mine is closer to the stoop. I have a photo from my window. I have not sent it. That feels like starting a case file.

### Assistant
Do not send the photo. You are not building a case. You are asking a neighbor to walk twelve extra feet.

Keep the photo so you stop telling yourself you imagined it. Do not attach it. Attachments make people argue about cameras instead of bags.

Talk tomorrow night if you can, before he does the walk. If you miss him, talk when you both take bins back in. Script: "I need to use my own bin. Yours is out too. Please don't add bags to 4A." Then stop. Silence is the second sentence. Let him fill it with excuses. You can nod at "it was just one bag" without agreeing. One bag every week is a habit.

If he laughs it off, do not laugh with him. "I'm serious — mine overflowed" is better than matching the joke. If he claims he thought they were shared, "They're numbered" is enough. You do not have to teach municipal waste. You have to keep the lid closable.

If it happens again, repeat once: "Same as last week — 4A's bin is mine." Second time you can add, "If yours is broken, ask the super. I'm not a second bin." Still no photo. Still no building-wide message.

### User
What if he gets nasty, leaves bags beside the bin, or talks about me in the group chat? I keep thinking I should just buy extra bags and live with it because I hate this kind of talk.

### Assistant
Living with it is quieter this week and louder every Thursday after. Extra bags are you paying, in money and kitchen smell, for his shortcut. That trains him.

Nasty is possible. You still do not run a public trial. Bags beside the bin are not yours to absorb. Do not take them into your kitchen. Do not stuff them into your bin as a peace offering. A second in-person line: "Those aren't mine. Please take them." If you use the group chat, stay short and only if that thread already handles building junk. "4A's bin is for 4A. Please don't leave bags by it." Skip his name if you can. Naming him is how you get a feud.

If he talks about you, skip the defense essay. One correction if someone asks you directly. Otherwise let it die.

If this becomes harassment rather than garbage, that is a different track: dated notes to whoever manages the building, without speeches. I will not pretend I know your lease. A paper trail of "I asked twice, it continued" is more useful than a photo dump.

For this week: catch him at the bin. One sentence. No photo. You do not need an apology. You need a lid that shuts. If you freeze, read the sentence off your phone. Charm is not required. Then take your bin in and go upstairs.
