### User
Someone kicked in a back door two buildings over on Tuesday. I asked my landlord to change our building's lock. They texted "we'll see" and a thumbs-up. I live on the first floor. The deadbolt sticks. I don't know if "we'll see" means next week or never. I don't want to start a war. I also don't want to sleep behind a lock that feels like a suggestion. What do I actually say without pretending I know the housing code?

### Assistant
You do not need the housing code to ask for a clearer answer than a thumbs-up. "We'll see" is not a date. Your job is to turn a shrug into a written question with a time attached.

Text, not a speech in the hallway: "Following up on the lock. After the break-in two buildings over, I need to know if you will rekey or replace the first-floor lock, and by when. The deadbolt already sticks." Short. Factual. No essay about your anxiety. Anxiety is real; it does not need to be the first paragraph.

I will not invent what your city requires after a nearby crime. Buildings differ. Owners differ. I also will not promise that a new cylinder makes you safe. A lock is one layer. You can still ask for it.

If they ignore the follow-up, send once more in 48 hours: will you change it, by what date. Two questions. Not a lecture.

Tonight, test whether the bolt throws fully. If you can throw it with a firm lift on the door, do that every night. If you cannot, say that in the text. "It does not throw" is clearer than "it feels like a suggestion."

Have they ever changed a lock for you before, or is this the first time you have asked?

### User
First time. They were fast about a leaking faucet last spring. This feels slower because nobody in our building was the target. My roommate wants to buy a cheap extra latch from the hardware store. I don't know if that gets us in trouble. I also don't want to wait for "we'll see" until someone tries our door.

### Assistant
Fast faucet, slow lock is a pattern you can name without a lawsuit voice. In the text you can say, "You handled the faucet quickly last spring. I need a timeline on the lock, not we'll see." That is a comparison, not a threat.

I will not tell you whether an extra latch violates your lease. I do not have the lease. Read the alterations clause if you have it. If you are unsure, ask in the same text: "If you are delayed, do you object to a temporary interior latch I can remove?" Silence is not permission. It is also not a reason to freeze on the deadbolt you already have.

Agree with your roommate who buys, who drills, who restores the door. Do not surprise the wood. A chair under the knob is ugly and temporary. I am not going to rank your fear. Keep the facts: bolt throw, written date.

If they say the lock is "fine," ask them to try the stick in person. A stuck deadbolt is a maintenance item even without Tuesday's kick-in. Put both issues in one note so they cannot answer only the vibe.

### User
They just replied "building is secure, maybe next quarter." Roommate is already at the hardware store. I'm sitting here drafting a paragraph about feeling unsafe and I know that's going to sound like a novel.

### Assistant
Delete the novel. Keep the facts. Next quarter is a date-shaped object that is not a date. Reply: "I need a calendar date for the lock, not next quarter. The deadbolt sticks and does not throw cleanly. Please confirm whether a temporary interior latch is okay if we install and later remove it. I can be home Thursday after six for you to see the stick." Then you stop.

If they refuse a date, save the thread. I will not tell you which agency to call or that a nearby crime forces a rekey. That would be invented procedure. A tenant clinic where you live can read your lease later. Not tonight's paragraph.

Text the roommate: "don't drill until we have a yes or we pick a removable option." A chain on existing screws is a different risk than a new hole. Hardware can sit in the bag overnight.

Tonight: throw the bolt, chair if you need it, do not post your unit number. Tomorrow: one factual text. You asked for a lock, not a war. A date is the middle. "We'll see" was the dodge. Answer it with a date request, then sit with the door you have.
