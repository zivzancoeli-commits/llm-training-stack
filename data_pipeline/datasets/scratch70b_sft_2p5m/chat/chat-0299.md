### User
Sam texted me: if their partner asks, I was with them at my place last night watching a movie. I was not. I was home alone. I don't know where Sam was. The partner and I are friendly; we have a group chat. I haven't answered Sam. I don't want to be the cover story and I don't want to blow up their relationship by texting the partner first.

### Assistant
Do not lie for them. Do not pre-blow the relationship by messaging the partner a reveal. Those are not the only two buttons.

A third option: tell Sam no, quickly, before the partner asks you and you freeze. "I can't say you were here. Don't use me as the alibi." That is a complete message. You do not need to demand where they were. You do not need to play detective.

If the partner asks you first, you are allowed to be dull: "I wasn't with Sam last night." You are not required to add theories. You are not required to protect a story you did not agree to.

I will not tell you what their relationship "should" do, or whether this is cheating. I do not know. I know you were asked to invent a movie night.

Are you likely to see the partner in person soon, or is this mostly phones?

### User
There's a dinner on Friday with both of them. If I say no to Sam, I think they'll still tell the partner I hosted. Then I walk into dinner as the liar or the snitch. I feel trapped.

### Assistant
If Sam uses your name anyway, that is Sam putting you in it, not you choosing a personality. Your job is to not confirm the fake movie. If Friday happens and the story is already in the air, you still do not have to perform it. Dull and true beats a scene: "That's not what happened. I was home." Then you let the table be awkward. Awkward is not your crime.

You can also skip Friday. "I'm going to sit this one out" is allowed when someone tried to draft you into a lie. You do not have to sit through a meal as a prop.

Do not write the partner a midnight essay. If you want to be ahead of a false story, a narrow message to Sam is still first. If you later owe the partner anything, it is a refusal to play along, not a dossier.

If Sam says "you're a bad friend," they mean you are a bad accomplice. Those are different jobs.

I am uncertain how explosive Friday will be. People surprise you. Your control is your sentences, not their fallout.

### User
What if the partner asks me in a hallway, quietly, "was Sam at your place?" I am a bad liar and also a bad confronter.

### Assistant
You do not have to be a good confronter. You have to not say yes. "No" is a full answer. If they wait, "I wasn't with them" is the extra clause. If they cry, you can say "I'm sorry this is messy" without filling in Sam's night. Filling it in is how you become the narrator of someone else's mess.

If you go blank, "I don't want to be in the middle" plus "I wasn't with Sam" is still true.

For the next ten minutes: reply to Sam with the one-line no, do not draft a message to the partner, mute Sam if they start explaining, and decide Friday with a simple test: can you sit there without acting? If no, cancel with a short "can't make it." Put your phone down after the no. The trap is the delay. Every hour you do not answer, Sam assumes you are in.

You were asked to be a movie. Decline the role. The relationship may shake because of what Sam did, not because you refused to be furniture. Let that distinction stay in your head when Friday tries to rewrite it.
