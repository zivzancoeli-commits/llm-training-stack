### User
I was supposed to pick up my niece at 3:15. My sister texted at 2:50 that her shift ran late and could I grab her. I got to the loop at 3:22 because of a train. The kid was not there. A teacher said she went with "the usual person." I am not the usual person. I am the uncle who said yes on a Tuesday. Now I am in the parking lot looking like I lost a child I never had in the car. I need two real options and which one you would actually pick. Do not sell me a parenting seminar.

### Assistant
You did not lose a child. You arrived late to a handoff that already happened. Still ugly. Still fixable.

Two options.

Option A: stay on site, go inside, ask the office to confirm who signed her out and to call that person while you wait. You want a name, a time, and a phone. If they will not tell you details because you are not on a list, ask them to call your sister and authorize you on the spot. Stay put so you are not driving around guessing.

Option B: leave the loop, call your sister first, then the "usual" pickup if she has that number, and only after live confirmation that the kid is with a known adult do you drive toward that house. No cruising neighborhoods on a hunch.

I would pick A if you are still in the lot and the office is open. Guessing from a moving car creates two adults in two places. Sitting in the office makes you a person they can point to.

I am not reciting a law about who may collect a kid. Schools have lists. I do not know yours. Treat the list as real even if it feels bureaucratic.

### User
Sister is not answering. Probably still on the floor at work. The teacher said "grandma" like I should have known. Grandma lives twenty minutes south. Traffic is ugly. If I drive south and the kid is still in an after-school room I look like a maniac. If I sit here I am wasting time.

### Assistant
Then A still wins for the next ten minutes, not forever.

Go inside. "Hi. I was asked to pick up [name] at 3:15. I arrived late. A teacher said she left with grandma. Can you confirm that from the sign-out, and can you call the number on file while I wait?" That is specific. It is not a speech about trains.

If they confirm grandma and a time, ask the office to call grandma and say you are the uncle who was supposed to take over, or get a number from the emergency card if they will share it. Then you drive south with a destination, not a rumor.

If they cannot confirm, you do not leave. Wait while they try the parent again. Driving south on an unconfirmed "grandma" is how people pass each other on a highway with a sick feeling.

Sitting with a confirmed name is not waste. Driving without one is.

### User
What do I tell the kid later? And my sister? I am annoyed at the late text, the late train, and the assumption that grandma is default. I do not want to dump that on a nine-year-old.

### Assistant
You tell the kid almost nothing about the adult mess. "I came to get you. There was a mix-up about who was picking up. Glad you're with grandma. Love you." If you see her tonight, that is enough. She does not need the train, the shift, or your ranking of relatives.

Sister gets a factual recap when she answers: you arrived at 3:22, staff said grandma, office confirmed or did not, you did X. Then one request for next time, not a lecture: "If I am pickup, put me on the list and tell the kid and the office my name before 2:50." You can be annoyed and still keep it to one ask. Multiple sins in one text makes people defend instead of change the card.

If grandma already has her and everyone is safe, you can go home. You do not need to extract the kid from grandma to prove you showed up. Showing up was the job. Confirming she is with a known adult is the rest of the job.

Two options were: stay and verify on site, or leave and chase by phone. Recommendation stays: verify on site first. Then drive with a name. That is the whole plan, uncle. No seminar.
