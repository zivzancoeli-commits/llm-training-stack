### User
A delivery truck has been reverse-beeping in the lot behind my bedroom for fifty minutes. It's 11:10 p.m. Beep, roll, beep, roll. I thought it would be five minutes. I can see the trailer from the window. One driver. Hazard lights. I don't know if I should go down, call a number on the truck, or put a pillow on my head and hate myself. Work is early. Talk me through this like I live here, which I do.

### Assistant
Pillow is allowed. Going down in pajamas to instruct a truck is usually not.

Fifty minutes of reverse-beep is a long dock dance. Sometimes they're stuck between cars. Sometimes they're lost. You cannot tell from the ceiling.

Look once: is the truck blocking a drive, or looping the same ten feet? If it's looping, the driver may not know they're under bedrooms. If you have a building after-hours number, that's the cleaner call than you in the lot. If you don't, a non-emergency city line is for ongoing night noise you can describe: lot, reverse beep, since when, one truck. I will not invent what they will do or how fast.

A number on the truck is often a daytime dispatch. Calling it at 11:10 might reach a voicemail. Still can be worth a message: location, time, that it's been nearly an hour. Dispatch in the morning is a record if this is a pattern.

Do not stand behind a reversing truck to "just tell them." That's how people get hurt. If you go outside at all, stay on the sidewalk, well aside, and only if you actually have a sentence. Most nights you don't need to go outside.

Is this a one-off, or have they done the night dock thing before?

### User
First time this late. Daytime trucks I ignore. The driver just got out, slammed a door, got back in. Still beeping. I think they're trying to miss a low garage lip. There's no manager on site. Building group chat is asleep except one person who said "welcome to the city."

### Assistant
Welcome-to-the-city is not a plan. You can ignore that comment.

Garage lip plus a long reverse is a driver solving geometry. They may be another twenty minutes. Earplugs, fan, other room if you have one. That's the body plan.

If you still want a human: non-emergency, or the property number in the morning with the company name off the trailer. Night photos from your window of the logo help a morning email. You don't need to go downstairs for a logo if you can read it from bed.

Slammed door doesn't make them a villain. It makes them stuck. You can be annoyed at the beep and still not start a lot confrontation. Stuck drivers sometimes get shouted at and then take longer.

If the truck is going to idle all night, that's different from a long reverse. Idle all night is worth the non-emergency description. A long reverse that ends is a bad hour. Try to tell which.

### User
If I call non-emergency I feel like I'm sending cops to a guy missing a curb.

### Assistant
You can skip that call. Pillow and a morning email is a complete adult path. Non-emergency is for if this is still going when you would have been asleep an hour ago and you cannot move rooms. It is not mandatory to "win."

If you do call, you're reporting noise from a truck in a lot, not asking for a takedown. Stick to beep, duration, address. No speech about curbs.

Tonight: stay out of the reverse path, earplugs, photo of the name if you can, no group-chat argument with welcome-to-the-city. Tomorrow: one note to the building or the company's daytime number if it repeats. First late hour doesn't require you to become traffic control. It requires you to sleep as soon as the geometry ends.
