### User
Sam wants me to tell their parents we were at the library until 11 if anyone asks. We were at a party in a basement with a speaker that kept cutting out. Sam is twenty. Their parents still do check-ins. They already texted me a script: "We studied, lost track of time, Sam drove me home." None of that is true. I don't want to be the friend who ruins their night. I also don't want to be on a group chat as an alibi. They said it's "one sentence." Help.

### Assistant
You can refuse the sentence. You don't have to turn Sam in. Those are different jobs.

Don't memorize the library. If a parent texts you, you don't owe a story. "I can't speak for Sam's night" is enough. If they call while you're standing there, you can say you need to go. You are not a character witness.

Tell Sam before the parents ever appear: "I'm not covering. If they ask me, I won't invent the library. I also won't call them." That's the boundary. If Sam says you're a bad friend, let the air sit. A friend who needs you to lie to their parents is asking you to join a household you don't live in.

I won't tell you what Sam "owes" their parents. I don't know that house. I won't invent a law about adult children and check-ins. I will tell you that a scripted alibi is still a lie with formatting.

Did they already send the script to a group, or just to you?

### User
Just me. They're in my kitchen drinking water, waiting for me to thumbs-up the text. Their mom sometimes texts me because I "seem responsible." I hate that. If I say no, Sam will take an Uber home in a mood. If I say yes, I have to remember a library that wasn't there.

### Assistant
Don't thumbs-up. Say the no while they're in the kitchen so they don't find out later from a parent.

"I'm not doing the library story. If your mom texts me, I'll say I don't want to get in the middle, or I won't answer tonight." Then you stop offering design options for a better lie. They came for a cover, not a workshop.

If they take an Uber in a mood, that's transportation, not a moral verdict on you. You can still offer water and a charger. You don't have to offer fiction.

If the mom texts while Sam is still there, you don't hand Sam your phone to ghostwrite. That's how you become the alibi with extra steps. Put the phone face down. Answer tomorrow with something that isn't a novel: "Hey — I'm not going to narrate Sam's night. Hope you're both good." If that feels too sharp, you can not answer. Silence is not the library.

You seem responsible because you don't like lying. Don't spend that on someone else's check-in system.

### User
What if their mom asks a yes-or-no: "Was Sam with you at the library?" That's the trap. There's no room.

### Assistant
There is room. "I wasn't at the library" is true. "I'm not going to answer questions about Sam" is also allowed. You don't have to fill in the basement. You don't have to volunteer the party. You are not on the stand. A yes-or-no from someone else's parent is still optional.

If you freeze, you can say, "I need to go, I'll talk later," and then you actually go. Later you can be boring. You don't owe a reconstructed timeline.

If Sam keeps pressing for "just if they ask," you can say, "I'm not going to volunteer anything. I'm also not going to build a library." That's the last version of the no. Then you go back to your own night: water, charger, door.

Next minute: tell Sam no, no thumbs-up, they can Uber mad. You keep your night without a fake building. That's the whole cover you actually needed — covering yourself. If their mom never texts, you still did the right thing by not memorizing a floor you weren't on. The script can stay in their drafts. Yours can stay empty.
