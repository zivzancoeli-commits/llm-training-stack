### User
Roommate pressed the test button on the hallway smoke alarm at 12:10 a.m. because a video said you should test monthly and they "were already up." The whole unit screamed. I thought it was a fire. I was in the hallway with a mug like an idiot. They said chill, it's a test. I need this never to happen at midnight again. I also don't want to be against safety.

### Assistant
Monthly tests are real. Midnight is a choice.

"Test in daylight. Never at midnight unless it's an actual emergency." Safety and sleep are not enemies. The video did not require 12:10.

If they were already up, they can still wait for morning. Being awake is not a permit to dump adrenaline into a roommate.

I will not invent the building's alarm policy. If this unit's detector is wired to a panel, a test might have already pinged someone. Ask in the morning whether the building wants notice. That's a separate call. Tonight's rule is time-of-day.

You're not against safety. You're against a drill with no warning.

Did they also take the detector down, or just mash the button?

### User
Just the button. They held it long enough that neighbors knocked. They waved through the peephole like it was funny. I am still shaky. I have work at 7.

### Assistant
Neighbors knocking is the extra cost. Funny is not available.

Tonight: you tell them, now, while it's true. "That wasn't funny. Don't test at night. I thought we were on fire." Shaky is allowed. You don't have to joke to regulate them.

Tomorrow you can put a note on the fridge: test on the first Saturday morning, both people home if possible, warn the other person first. A calendar reminder beats a viral video's vibe.

If the building has a rule about not testing wired alarms yourself, follow that instead of the video. Some systems are not "press for fun." I will not guess yours. A super or a sticker on the device is the source.

Peephole wave to neighbors: they can apologize in the hallway in daylight. You don't have to manage their PR tonight.

### User
They said I was overreacting because "it was only a test."

### Assistant
Tests at midnight are designed to feel like fires. That's the point of the sound. Overreacting would be calling them a criminal. Wanting a warning and a daytime hour is the normal size.

"Call it a test in the morning. At night I will always treat the sound as a fire." That's your nervous system policy. They can put it on the calendar.

Work at 7: earplugs now, lights down, no further debate. Safety next Saturday, announced. You're allowed to hate the adrenaline and still like working detectors. Put the mug down. The hallway can stop being a drill ground after dark.

### User
I said it wasn't funny. They apologized to me, not to the neighbors. I still have work at 7. The alarm is quiet now. I keep hearing it anyway.

### Assistant
That's leftover adrenaline, not a second test. Lights down, earplugs, no replay of the video on your phone. You don't need to audit monthly-test content at 12:40.

Tomorrow they can knock on the two doors that knocked, or a note under those doors: sorry, test, it won't be at night. You can remind them. You don't have to be the building's apology desk if your body is done.

Fridge calendar: first Saturday morning, warn first. Wired versus local is a daylight question for a sticker or a super. You're not against safety. You're against a surprise scream. Mug in the sink. Hallway is just a hallway again. Sleep as much as the leftover ringing in your head allows. Morning you can be boring about Saturday. Tonight you already spent the words. Set a morning alarm so Saturday's test actually gets a warning text first.
