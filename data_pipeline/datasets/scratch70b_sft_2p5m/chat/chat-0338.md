### User
I went to the photo place at lunch. They took the shot, I blinked, they took another, I looked stunned, they took a third and said it was fine. I paid and left. An hour later I actually looked at the print. Eyes half closed, mouth like I'm about to object. The passport application is due at the acceptance facility on Friday because I'm trying to make a trip window. I don't know if they will reject this photo, if I should go back tomorrow, or if I'm supposed to pretend it's fine because the clerk said it was fine. I feel stupid for not looking before I walked out.

### Assistant
Looking before you walk out is a skill you now have. It is not a personality defect. It is a lunch-hour rush.

Do not mail or submit a photo you already distrust. "The clerk said it was fine" means they thought it met their checklist in fluorescent lighting. Acceptance people sometimes disagree. I will not invent the current photo rules for your country. Those change, and they are picky about eyes, glasses, shadows, and expressions. Your job is to compare the print to the official photo list on the government site, not to my memory.

If the eyes are visibly not open, retake. A stunned mouth can also fail. Blink and "about to object" are exactly the kind of things that bounce.

Call the photo place today. Ask whether a retake is included or another sitting. Ask how fast they can print. Then look at Friday's facility hours and whether they take walk-ins. Build the sequence backward from Friday, not from shame.

Did you already attach this photo to an online form, or is it still just a print in a paper bag?

### User
Still in the bag. Nothing submitted. The trip is not booked yet — I'm trying to get the passport before I buy the ticket. Friday is the only morning I can get to the facility without missing a shift. The photo shop closes at 6. I work until 5:30 tomorrow.

### Assistant
You have a print, a Friday morning, and a shop that closes at 6. That is a tight but ordinary puzzle.

Go tonight if they are still open. A same-day retake beats a heroic Thursday. If tonight is impossible, Thursday is your real slot: leave work on time, do not run extra errands, sit for the photo, look at the screen before they print, look at the print before you pay. Open eyes. Neutral mouth. You are allowed to say "that's another blink" and make them do it again. You are the customer. Awkward is cheaper than Friday with a bad print.

If they charge again, pay or go somewhere else that can print before Friday. I will not quote a price. Ask. If two places can do it, pick the one that will still be open after your shift.

Do not buy the ticket until the passport process is actually in motion the way you need it. You already know that; keep knowing it. A blinked photo does not become better because a flight is cheaper on a Tuesday.

Bring the failed print with you so they can see what to avoid. Bring the same shirt or a plain one that matches whatever the official list allows. Check the list tonight for glasses, backgrounds, and smiles. Then follow the list, not the clerk's "it's fine."

### User
If I go back, do I have to explain that I think they messed up? I don't want a fight at the counter. I also don't want them to reuse the stunned file and print it darker.

### Assistant
You do not need a fight and you do not need an apology speech. You need a new capture.

Say: "I need a new sitting. The eyes aren't open enough on this one." Put the print on the counter. Ask them to take a fresh shot, not a reprint. Watch the screen. If they pull the old file, say "new photo, please." Calm, like you're asking for a different sandwich.

If the person at the counter gets chilly, stay on the request. You are not accusing them of ruining your life. You are declining a blink.

Tonight or Thursday: new sitting, eyes open, you inspect, then Friday morning with a photo you would accept on a government website. Leave thirty extra minutes on Friday for a line. Put the print in a hard folder so it does not bend in a bag. You blinked. People blink. Redo the picture. That is the whole recovery.
