### User
The person above me started tap dancing. Not a kid. An adult with a board and metal on the shoes. It started around 7:40 tonight and it's now 9:12. My ceiling lamp is rattling. I have a 7 a.m. shift. I don't know if this is a one-night spiral or their new personality. I don't want to be the neighbor who bangs a broom. I also cannot listen to this until they "find their rhythm." Help me talk to a human through a floor.

### Assistant
You talk like a person with a morning, not like a building inspector.

First fact-check: is this the first night, and do you know their name or unit number, or only the noise? If you've never spoken, a note or a knock is a first contact, not a complaint history. Keep it that size.

I won't invent quiet hours for your city or what your lease "must" say. Those differ. I also won't tell you a legal decibel. You know it's metal on wood over your head at 9 p.m. That's enough to ask them to stop for tonight.

If you're going to knock, do it once, not as a drum solo. Stand back from the door. "Hi, I'm downstairs in 2B. The tap is really loud in my place and I have an early shift. Could you stop for tonight and we can figure out a time that isn't this late?" That's a request plus a later conversation. It isn't a verdict on their art.

If you don't want a door conversation after 9, a slip under the door or a text if you already have one: same content, shorter. Time, unit, the ask.

Are you trying to get sleep tonight, or are you trying to set a long-term hobby schedule?

### User
Tonight first. I have to sleep. I think they're in 3B. I saw a tap board go up the stairs last Sunday and I told myself it was a plant stand. There's no group chat for the building. Super only answers email. I'm in socks holding my phone like it's a weapon.

### Assistant
Then tonight is: knock or note, one ask, then earplugs or a white-noise app, then sleep. You don't wait for the super's inbox to become a person.

Knock if you can do it without shaking. If the idea of a stranger at 9:12 makes you feel unsafe, skip the knock. Note under the door, or tape it at their eye level if the hallway is lit. "3B — tap is coming through my ceiling after 9. I work early. Please stop for tonight. I'm in 2B, we can talk times this weekend." Sign a first name if you're willing. Anonymous notes get ignored or turned into a mystery villain.

Don't bang the ceiling. That's a conversation that starts already stupid. Don't film them under their door. Don't post in a neighborhood app with the unit number tonight. You want the dancing to stop, not a building trial.

If they open the door defensive — "it's my apartment" — you don't debate property. "I hear it in my bed. I'm asking you to stop for tonight." Then you leave. You already said the useful part.

If they don't answer, you still put the note. Then you go to bed. The lamp rattle is real. Your job in the next twenty minutes is not to win. It's to ask once and protect the shift.

### User
What if they keep going after I ask? I don't want to call anyone like it's an emergency. It isn't. It's just metal.

### Assistant
If they keep going after a clear ask, you don't escalate to cops for tap shoes unless something else is actually dangerous. It isn't. You log it: time it started, time you asked, that it continued. A short email to the super with those times is enough for a paper trail. I won't pretend I know whether your building will do anything. Some supers care. Some send a form letter. The email is still useful if this becomes a pattern.

Tomorrow, not at midnight, you can try a daytime hello: "Last night ran late. Can you keep the tap before eight, or on a mat, or not over my bedroom?" I don't know if a mat will help. You can ask if they have one. You cannot redesign their floor from 2B.

If this is three nights a week, that's a pattern, and you repeat the same ask plus the super email. You don't need a new personality. You need dates.

For the next half hour: note or knock, one paragraph, earplugs, lights out. You asked for tonight. That's the adult version of the broom.
