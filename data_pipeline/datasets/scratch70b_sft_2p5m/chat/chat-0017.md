### User
I have a parent-teacher conference Thursday about my kid. The email says there is one missing assignment in science, a lab write-up from two weeks ago. Everything else is turned in. I am a new intern at work and my brain is treating this like I am about to be performance-reviewed. I don't know if the assignment was never done, never submitted, or sitting in a backpack. The portal just shows a blank. I was going to walk in and say we take education very seriously. That sentence already sounds fake in my mouth. Help me not bluff.

### Assistant
Do not say you take education very seriously. Everyone says that. Teachers hear it the way managers hear "I'm a hard worker."

I also will not pretend I know what that blank in the portal means. I don't. A blank can be not started, lost, submitted to the wrong folder, or not entered yet. Guessing in a conference is how you accidentally accuse the kid or the teacher.

What I would admit out loud, even as an intern-flavored human: "I don't know yet whether this was never written or never turned in. I want to find that out with you rather than defend a story I made up."

That is the whole spine. Uncertainty is allowed. The conference is a fact-finding meeting about one lab, not a referendum on your household.

Before Thursday, do the small homework you can actually do. Ask your kid, without a courtroom tone: "The science lab from two weeks ago shows as missing. Do you remember writing it? Is it on paper, in the laptop, or did you skip it?" Then listen. If they say they turned it in, ask where — tray, Google Classroom, email to the teacher. If they say they didn't do it, you already have the truth and you do not need a speech.

I do not know your school's late-work policy. I will not invent one. Ask the teacher: "If we find it, can it still be accepted? If it was never done, what's the make-up path?" Those are questions. They are better than nodding as if you already know the handbook.

### User
My kid said they "think they did it on the chromebook" and then went quiet. I opened the chromebook and found a document titled Lab 4 that stops after the hypothesis. No data table. No conclusion. So it exists as a ghost. I still don't know what the teacher expects me to promise.

### Assistant
Now you know more than a blank and less than a finished lab. Say that. Do not upgrade it into "we're on top of it" and do not downgrade it into "this child never works."

A sentence that is true: "There's a Lab 4 file that stops after the hypothesis. I didn't know that until last night. I don't want to promise a deadline I haven't heard from you."

You are not required to know whether this teacher wants a full rewrite, a completion of the same file, or a zero that stays a zero. Intern instinct is to fill silence with a plan so you look competent. In a conference, a fake plan is worse than "I need the next step from you."

If they ask why it is late, you can say you don't know the full why. Maybe time, maybe confusion, maybe avoidance. You are not a psychologist in a folding chair. "I'll ask more at home" is honest. "They were overwhelmed by a rigorous curriculum" is a bluff in nicer clothes.

What you can promise without lying: you will sit with the kid and the file; you will look at the missing sections; you will follow whatever make-up window the teacher states. Write the window down. Repeat it back. "So if it's in by next Wednesday with data and conclusion, it counts as late credit — did I get that right?" If they say they don't offer late credit, believe them. Do not argue a policy you have not read. Ask where the policy lives if you want to read it later.

Skip the intern urge to mention your job or how you also submit things at 11:58. It steals time from the lab.

If the teacher is kind, thank them for flagging one assignment instead of a pile. If they are brisk, stay on the document. Bring the chromebook if that helps them see the ghost file. Leave with a date, a format, and no new personality for your kid. You can be new at work and still say "I don't know" in a school. That is how the assignment actually gets finished.
