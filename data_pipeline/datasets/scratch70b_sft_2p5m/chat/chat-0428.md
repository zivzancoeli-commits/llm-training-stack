### User
The neighbor's side yard has an open compost heap. Food scraps on top. I have seen rats at dusk going between that pile and our fence. I like compost. I do not like rats at the trash cans. I waved last month. They are proud of the heap. I need to talk without becoming the person who hates the planet. I also need a next step if they smile and do nothing. I don't want invented city fines.

### Assistant
You can like compost and still object to an open buffet. Rats are the topic, not their values.

Talk in daylight, not during a dusk rat tour. "I've seen rats moving between your pile and our fence. Can you cover the food scraps or use a closed bin? I'm worried about the cans." Offer a specific fix, not a vibe. Cover, hardware-cloth, a lidded tumbler, moving food waste to a municipal bin if they have one. You do not have to buy it for them. You can say you are happy to share a link if they want ideas. I will not pick a product or a price.

If they are proud, let them be proud of composting and still ask for a lid. Pride and a lid can coexist.

I will not invent rat-control law or a fine. If your town has a sanitation number, that is a later channel after a neighbor ask.

Have you seen rats on your side of the fence, or only on theirs?

### User
On both sides. Our dog goes nuts at night. They once said "rats are part of the ecosystem." I almost laughed. I did not. I have a photo from my porch. I don't want to start with a photo like a cop.

### Assistant
Do not lead with the photo. Lead with the dog, the cans, and the ask. The photo is for you, and for a later message if they deny seeing anything.

Ecosystem can get a short answer: "They may be, and I still need the food covered so they don't nest in the fence." You do not have to debate urban ecology. Debates keep the pile open.

If they agree, ask when. "This week" is better than "we'll think about lids." If they offer a tarp, a tarp that blows off is a maybe. Ask for something that stays shut.

If they smile and do nothing, you send a follow-up note after a few days: same ask, dated, plus that you have seen rats on both sides. Then you can call the building, the landlord if they rent, or the city pest/sanitation line and describe an open food pile. I will not promise a visit. I will say a dated ask-first keeps you from looking like you skipped the human.

Secure your own cans: tight lids, no bags beside the bins. That is your side. It does not excuse their heap. It keeps the argument honest.

### User
What if they get angry and the fence line becomes a cold war, and I still have rats? I feel stuck between manners and a public-health feeling I am not qualified to name.

### Assistant
You are qualified to say you have seen rats and you want food covered. You do not need a credential for that. I will not name a disease. I will not escalate your feeling into a diagnosis.

Anger at the fence is possible. A lid is still worth the try. If they go cold, you stay civil in the driveway and you use the official channel for the pile. Cold war plus rats is worse than a frank Tuesday.

If you share a landlord, the landlord may care about a fence line. If you own, you still can speak and then call the town.

This week: daylight talk, specific lid, a when. Your cans shut. Dog in at dusk if that reduces the frenzy. Photo stays in your phone unless you need it. You are not against the planet. You are against an open kitchen on the ground. Say that, plainly. Then give them a few days to cover it. If the scraps stay in the weather, you make the next call with the dates. Manners already had a month. Use the next conversation for a cover, not for a worldview. Go before dusk so you are not pointing at live rats like a tour. Talk. Then watch the pile, not the ideology.
