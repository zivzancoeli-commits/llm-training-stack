### User
I came here to write. The playlist is a concert in a room the size of a garage. I have headphones, and I can still hear the snare. I have been here an hour and bought two drinks. I have never asked a cafe to turn music down. I feel like I will be the customer they talk about after I leave. My deadline is this afternoon. There is one barista and a speaker over my table.

### Assistant
You bought two drinks. You are not a loiterer inventing a problem. You can still ask without becoming a character.

Go to the barista between orders, not during a rush of six drinks. One sentence: "Could you turn the music down a bit? I'm trying to work and it's loud at this table." Then stop. Do not critique the playlist. Do not mention your deadline as a moral argument. They can say no. A no means you move tables or you leave.

I will not tell you they have to. It is their room. Asking is still ordinary.

Is there a quieter corner you have not tried, and is the speaker actually over you or just seems like it?

### User
I moved once. The other wall is the milk steamer. The speaker is really over this table, I checked. There are two other people with laptops who look fine. I feel like that means I am the sensitive one.

### Assistant
Two laptops looking fine is not a vote. They might like it, they might be watching videos, they might be about to leave. You only need your brain for an afternoon.

If the barista lowers it, say thanks and sit down. If they lower it one click that does nothing, you can ask once more: "A little more?" Twice is the ceiling. After that you are negotiating a personality.

If they say the owner wants it like this, you believe them. Then you have the real choice: stay and write worse, or go home / a library / a quieter shop. An hour already spent is a sunk cost. Two drinks do not obligate you to fail a deadline as payment.

Do not reach for the speaker. Do not unplug anything. Do not start a petition with the other laptops. If you talk to them at all, it is "is it loud for you too," not a coalition.

Headphones plus brown noise can still help if they drop the volume even a little. The snare is the problem. A lower master volume often fixes snare more than a new playlist would.

### User
If they look annoyed I will overtip and then I will not write anyway because I will replay the look. That is also a pattern.

### Assistant
A look is a face doing a job. It is not a review of your soul. Tip what you already tip. Do not buy a third drink as an apology. Apologies are how you stay in a loud room.

For the next ten minutes: wait for a lull, ask the one sentence, sit down if it drops, and write the ugly first paragraph of the deadline before you judge the room again. If it does not drop, pack in five minutes and finish the work somewhere the snare is not a roommate. Put on the street, you can be annoyed. On the page, you only need sentences.

You are allowed to think in a cafe. They are allowed to run a vibe. The adult move is one ask, then a decision. Sensitive is a word people use for ears. Ears are how writing happens. Ask, then work or leave. Do not spend the afternoon as a story they might tell. Spend it as pages. If you leave, take the second drink in a cup and do not apologize to the speaker on the way out. The deadline is the customer you actually owe.
