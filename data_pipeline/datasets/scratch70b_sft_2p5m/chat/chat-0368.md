### User
The GPS dumped me at the wrong gate. The funeral started at 11. It is 11:12. I can see a service in the distance and another down a different road. I am in a dark jacket in a borrowed car. I do not want to walk across graves to guess. I do not want to call the grieving person and ask "which lawn." There is a small office that looks closed. What do I do in the next five minutes that is respectful and actually gets me to the right cluster of people?

### Assistant
Do not cut across graves as a shortcut. Use roads and paths.

Drive slowly toward the nearest group only if you can see a sign or a parked line of cars that matches what you were told — funeral home name on a hearse, a particular color of tent, a last name on a marker card. If you cannot tell, go to the office even if it looks closed. Try the door. Try a phone number on the door. Cemeteries often have a working number for "where is the Smith service."

If a grounds worker is in a truck, that is your best human. Wave, don't block them in a dramatic way, ask: "Funeral for [name], 11 o'clock — which section?" They know the map. Family does not need your late call yet.

Calling the grieving person is last, not first, unless they told you to call on arrival. A text to a cousin who is already there is better than a live question to the person in the front row.

I will not invent cemetery rules. Walking on graves is widely considered disrespectful; roads exist for a reason. Follow pavement.

Do you have the section name or only "the cemetery on the hill"?

### User
I have "west chapel" in an email I didn't open until the parking lot. I opened it now. There's a chapel building I passed. I thought the service was graveside. I might be wrong.

### Assistant
West chapel means you probably passed the right building. Turn around. Chapel services are often inside first, graveside second. Being late to a chapel is a side door, not a hunt across lawns.

Park where other funeral cars are. Enter quietly. Sit in the back. Do not wave. You are twelve minutes late, not a spectacle unless you make one. Phone on silent before the door.

If the chapel is empty and people have already gone out, ask anyone in a suit near the door where the procession went. One sentence. Then follow the last cars, not a diagonal across plots.

If you are still lost at 11:20, text the cousin: "At west chapel — is the service inside or already going out?" That is a logistics ping. It is kinder than calling the primary.

Do not speed on cemetery roads. Late plus reckless is worse than late plus two minutes.

Borrowed car: lock it, take the keys, do not leave it in a lane. Staff hate lane parking more than they hate a late cousin.

### User
I found the chapel. Doors closed. I can hear something. I don't know if I should wait or crack the door.

### Assistant
Crack the door. Slide in along the wall. If a usher is there, they will point you. If not, last pew. Do not stand in the doorway silhouetted like an arrival scene.

If it is a prayer or a name, wait for a shift in sound — a hymn ending, a pause — then enter. If you cannot tell, enter anyway, small. Remaining outside for ten more minutes is how you miss the whole thing you came for.

You are late. Late people still sit down. You do not owe a whispered speech to the row. Afterward you can find the family at the door or at the graveside and say the name of the person who died, and that you are sorry you were late. Short. Then you are a person who showed up, which is the assignment. The wrong gate already happened. The back pew is the recovery. Go in.
