### User
The unit next door has a garage that slams at 11:40, sometimes twice. Metal to concrete. My headboard shares that wall. I have mentioned it once in the hallway and they said "that's just how it closes." I don't want a feud about cars. I want the slam to stop being a full-body event. How do I ask without inventing an HOA rule?

### Assistant
"That's just how it closes" is a shrug with a hinge. You can ask for a slower close or a pad without a bylaws lecture.

I will not invent an HOA fine. I will not invent a quiet-hour statute. You can still describe the slam as a time and a wall. Note tonight if it happens: 11:40, twice, shared wall, headboard. Then a daylight text or note: "The garage door slams around 11:40 and hits our shared wall. Can you lower it slower or add a bumper? I can hear it in the bed." Daylight is less of a fight than a 11:41 doorway in socks.

If they have a opener with a soft-close, they may not use it. Asking them to use it is smaller than asking them to move. If the door is old and heavy, a rubber stop is a hardware-store object. I will not name a price. I will not install it from here. You are asking them to try.

Tonight: plugs, maybe the couch if the wall is the problem. Do not go out in the dark to hold their door. Hands on a moving garage is how a complaint becomes an ER.

Do you know which person in that unit drives late, or is it a rotating slam?

### User
Mostly one guy, gray hoodie, sedan. Sometimes a second car. Hallway mention was three weeks ago. Slam is back. There's a rubber strip hanging off their door like it already failed. I work 6 a.m.

### Assistant
Failed strip is useful. You are not asking them to invent physics. You are asking them to replace a strip that already existed. Photo from your property, not theirs, if you can see the hanging rubber from your driveway. Then the text: "The bumper strip on your garage door is hanging off. The slam is waking the shared wall around 11:40. Please fix the strip or close it slower. I start at 6." Times, object, ask. No character study of the hoodie.

If you have a building manager, copy them once. If you do not, it is neighbor to neighbor. One copy, not a group chat pile-on.

If he says he cannot hear it, you can say, "I can. Headboard wall." You do not need him to feel it. You need him to change a motion. Slow the last two feet. That is the whole skill.

6 a.m. is why you do this in writing, not as a groggy speech. Groggy speeches get remembered as crazy. Timestamps get remembered as 11:40.

### User
If I text he'll think I'm the neighbor who hates cars. He has to get home when he gets home. I don't actually want him to park on the street. I want the last two feet to be quiet.

### Assistant
Then say the last two feet. "I don't need you to park on the street. I need the door not to slam the last two feet. The strip is hanging off." That sentence protects his car and your wall. Hating cars is a story. A hanging strip is a part.

If he ignores the text, one follow-up in a week with the new times. If it stays, manager or whatever daylight process your building actually uses. I will not invent that process. I will not tell you to disable his opener. Sabotage is a feud. You asked for quiet, not a war.

Tonight if it slams: note the time, plugs, do not go out. Tomorrow: one text, photo of the strip if you have it, last-two-feet language. Let him come home. Let the door finish like a door instead of a dumpster lid. You can live next to a sedan. You do not have to live inside the slam. Ask for the strip. Sleep on the other side of the bed if that wall is the drum. Then send the note before you forget, because 6 a.m. you will downplay it. Night-you has the headboard data. Use it.
