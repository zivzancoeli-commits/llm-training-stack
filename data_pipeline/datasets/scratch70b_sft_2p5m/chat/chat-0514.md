### User
My sister wants me to host her baby shower. I live in a one-bedroom. I have four chairs if I count the desk chair. She sent a guest list that is more people than I own plates. She said it's "cozy" and that I'm the only one with a "cute place in the city." I don't want twenty people in my bathroom line. I also don't want to look like I won't celebrate her. She needs a venue yes this week to send invites.

### Assistant
Cute is not capacity. You can celebrate her without turning your bathroom into a lobby.

A clean no to the venue: "I can't host that many people here. The apartment is too small. I'll help with food or a game, or I'll chip in on a rented room if that's the plan. I won't do the shower in my one-bedroom." You are declining a floor plan, not a baby.

Don't pad the no with a fake leak or a fake out-of-town weekend unless those are true. She will smell a prop, and then cozy will come back as a dare.

If you want to host a tiny thing — sister, two friends, cake — say the number out loud. Numbers prevent cozy from meaning twenty.

I will not guess what a rental hall costs. I don't know your city. If money is the reason she's eyeing your living room, you can say you won't spend a number you don't have, and you still won't host twenty. Cheap and impossible is still impossible.

Has she already told guests it's at your place?

### User
She told two aunts "probably at [my] apartment." The aunts are now excited about my "little kitchen." My little kitchen is a sink and a hope. I feel the trap closing in the family chat.

### Assistant
Correct the aunts before the invites. A message in the same place the rumor lives: "Heads up — I can't host the shower here. Space is too small. We're looking at other options." You can let your sister be annoyed that you spoke. Annoyed is better than twenty bodies and a rumor that became a map pin.

To your sister, privately: "Please don't list my address. I already said no to hosting." If she says you're embarrassing her, you can say, "Invites to a place I refused is more embarrassing." Then you stop. You don't need a slideshow of your square footage.

Help you can actually give: a dessert you know how to make, a ride for an aunt, a playlist, an hour of setup at a park picnic shelter if the weather and the rules allow. I won't invent park permit rules. She can check the park's own page. Parks and back rooms of restaurants exist so one-bedrooms don't have to pretend.

If she cries, you can be kind and still not move furniture into a dimension you don't have. Kind is "I want you celebrated. Not here."

### User
What if she shrinks the list to eight and asks again?

### Assistant
Then you decide whether eight is a party you want in your home, including bathroom math. If eight is fine, you say yes to eight and you write the number. If eight is still your whole life in one room, no remains no. Don't let shrink-the-list become a weekly auction.

This week she needs a venue. Give her the true one: not your address. Celebrate the baby in a place that has enough chairs. Your cute city place can stay a place you sleep, not a venue with a hope for a kitchen. Correct the aunts if the rumor is still moving. Invites should point at a room that exists. Four chairs is not a shower. Love her in dessert, in a park, in a rented back room — not in a bathroom line you already counted. Say it this week, while programs are still a file.
