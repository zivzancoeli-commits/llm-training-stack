### User
The rec center court is across the alley. They leave the windows cranked. Tonight a game started at 10:20. Sneakers, a whistle, someone yelling "box out" like it's March. My bedroom faces it. I have work at 7. I don't know if I should go over there in pajamas or send a portal message like a snitch. I just want quiet, not a feud with the night league.

### Assistant
You want the windows and the clock, not a personality war. 10:20 basketball with open glass is a noise path. Treat it as a path.

I will not invent a city quiet-hour law. I do not know your town. I will not invent the rec center's closing time. You can still ask a staff person what the posted hours are, because posted hours are a thing you can point at without becoming a lawyer.

Tonight, do the small stack first: close your own window, run the box fan you already have, earplugs if they live in the drawer. That is not surrender. That is you buying twenty minutes to decide whether to walk over.

If you go over, go dressed, not in pajama diplomacy. Front desk if there is one. One sentence: "The court windows are open and the game is loud in the alley bedrooms. Can you close them or end the run?" You are talking to staff, not to the guy yelling box out. Players are not your customer-service counter.

If there is no desk, you can still knock the office door or wait. Do not walk onto the court mid-play to lecture. That is how a feud starts.

Portal message is for the trail if tonight does not resolve. A short note with time, open windows, alley bedrooms. Not a manifesto.

Is there a light on in the rec office, or is it just the court?

### User
There's a kid in a staff shirt at the front, scrolling. The game looks like adults. Windows are definitely cranked on the alley side. I already shut mine. Fan is on. Still getting the whistle.

### Assistant
Then you have a staff shirt. Use it. Walk in with shoes on. "Hi — I live across the alley. The court windows are open and the whistle is hitting my bedroom. It's after 10. Can you close those windows or wrap the game?" Then pause. Let them do the job they are scrolling through.

If they say they cannot control the league, ask them to close the glass anyway. Glass is a lever they probably can move without a board meeting. If they say the league has the gym until 11, you still ask for the windows. You do not need to win the whole schedule on a Tuesday.

If the staff kid looks scared of the adults, you can say, "I can wait while you ask them to close the windows." Waiting is less explosive than you becoming assistant coach of volume.

Do not film the players' faces from your bedroom and post it. A time-stamped note to the rec email tomorrow is enough: night, windows, alley, staff on site. I will not promise that email gets you silence. It makes a trail.

If they shut the windows and the game continues, you may still hear sneakers. That is a different problem than open-window theater. Take the win you can get tonight. Schedule talk is for daylight.

### User
What if they tell me to call the non-emergency line instead of doing anything? I don't want cops over basketball.

### Assistant
Then you say, "I'd rather you close the windows. I'm not looking to call anyone." That keeps your preference on the table. I will not tell you that you must or must not call a non-emergency number. I do not know your block. A bouncing ball is often a staff problem first. If staff refuses and the noise stays wild, you can decide in the morning whether a rec supervisor email is enough.

Do not threaten the kid with a phone. Threats turn a window request into a scene. If you leave without a closed window, go home, fan, earplugs, write the time down. Tomorrow: email the center, not a neighborhood pile-on.

For the next ten minutes: shoes, front desk, windows, clock. You asked for quiet, not a feud. Closing glass is the smallest version of that. Let the whistle fight wait until someone who owns the gym is awake.
