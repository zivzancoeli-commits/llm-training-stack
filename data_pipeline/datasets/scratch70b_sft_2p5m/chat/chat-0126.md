### User
There's a guy on my team, Dan, who hits reply-all on every thread. "Thanks!" to 40 people. "Got it!" on a calendar invite that was already accepted. I used to be a little like this last year and I still cringe. I want it to stop without making him feel stupid. Two paths, please, and tell me which one you'd pick.

### Assistant
Two options, then the one I'd pick if I were talking to last-year-you.

Option A: private, one-to-one, specific thread. You wait for the next "thanks!" blast, then you message Dan only: "Hey — for these big threads, reply-all hits everyone. If it's just 'got it' or 'thanks,' you can reply to the sender or skip it. Saves a lot of pings." You point at one example, not his personality.

Option B: change the environment, not the person. BCC announcements, or add a line at the bottom of your own mails: "Please reply to me only unless the whole group needs it." Ask the thread starter, if that's your manager, to set the norm once. Mute the thread. Don't make Dan the project.

I'd pick A first if you actually work with Dan and the relationship isn't brittle. Last-year-you didn't need a public shaming. You needed one person to say the quiet rule out loud: reply-all is for when everyone must see the answer. "Thanks" is not that.

If Dan is senior to you or politically sharp, I'd lean B, because A can look like a junior person policing manners. That's a real constraint, not cowardice. You can still do a lighter A: "I used to reply-all on thanks too and it blew up my inbox. I try to reply to one person now." That's you confessing, which is what last-year-you would have heard without bristling.

Don't do the sarcastic "congrats on the all-staff thank you" in the same thread. It feels clever for ten seconds and then you're the other problem.

### User
He's not senior. He's peer, a bit eager. I'm afraid he'll think I'm calling him unprofessional.

### Assistant
Then A, with last-year softness.

Open with the thing you already know: you did the same move. "I used to hit reply-all for thanks because I thought it showed I was paying attention. It mostly created noise. If you want to confirm, one-to-one is enough." That gives him a better motive than "you're annoying," and it gives him the replacement behavior.

Keep it to one message. A style guide makes him feel managed. Three old threads make him feel surveilled. One recent thread is plenty.

If he says people like visibility, you can answer, "Visibility is for decisions and blockers. Thanks is optional." That's the actual rule in most offices, even when nobody writes it down.

If he keeps doing it after a clear private note, mix in B: mute, and if you're the sender, put "reply to me only" once. You don't send a second essay. Repeating the lesson in public is how it becomes a personality conflict.

There's a third fork only if the emails include customers: then it's leak risk, not etiquette. If that's happened, say it plainly: "This one went wider than our team. Let's keep replies inside." Use it only if it's true.

You're allowed to be slightly annoyed. Annoyance doesn't have to become a campaign. Last-year-you wanted to look diligent. Diligent is doing the work and confirming to the person who asked. It is not a receipt to forty inboxes.

### User
I'll talk to him tomorrow after standup, not over email. Is that better?

### Assistant
Yes, if you can keep it to sixty seconds in a hallway, not a meeting-after-the-meeting.

Hallway version: "Quick thing — on the big threads, I skip reply-all unless everyone needs the answer. I used to 'thanks' everyone. Just a heads up." Then you walk. Don't wait for him to process his shame in front of you. Give him an easy out: "No big deal, I did it too."

If standup is public, don't do it there. Public is the option you didn't pick.

Send nothing in today's thread. Interrupting a "got it" with a manners note is the sarcastic path in disguise.

Two options were: private coaching with a replacement behavior, or change the mail pattern and mute. Recommendation: private coaching first, because he's a peer and eager, and because you already know how that person hears a kind version. Environment change is the backup if the habit survives one clear ask.
