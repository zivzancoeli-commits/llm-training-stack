### User
Reservation at 7:30 under my partner's last name. I said my last name at the stand. The list has neither. It's 7:34. The place is packed. The host is scanning a tablet like it personally betrayed them. We booked Tuesday on the phone. I have the confirmation email with a number that looks like a license plate. People behind us are sighing. I don't want to eat standing, and I don't want to be the couple that makes a speech.

### Assistant
Show the email, don't make the speech. Phone screen, confirmation number, time, party size. Last names being wrong is a filing error. It's common when two people book and one shows up talking.

Stay at the stand, not in the doorway blocking the sighers. One of you can step aside with the bags. Let the host look. Don't stack theories about the phone call yet.

I won't invent whether they have to seat you. A confirmation is strong. A full Friday is also real. You may get a wait, a bar rail, or a table. You won't get it faster by narrating Tuesday.

Is the confirmation clearly for this restaurant's name and tonight's date?

### User
Yes. Same restaurant, tonight, 7:30, party of two. The name on the email is my partner's, spelled right. I just said the wrong one out loud. The host asked if we had a 7:00 that we missed. We didn't. There's a tiny bar with two stools that both have coats on them.

### Assistant
You didn't miss a 7:00. Say that once: "We didn't have a 7:00. Here's the 7:30 confirmation." Hand the phone over. Let them search the number, not the sound of your name.

For the next ten minutes: one of you stays as the contact at the stand, the other waits where you're not a cork in the entrance. If they offer the bar when a stool frees, take it only if you'll actually eat there. Coats on stools are placeholders. Don't hover over strangers' jackets. If they offer a 8:10 wait, decide if 40 minutes is acceptable before you get hungry-mean. If it isn't, you can leave. That's allowed. Friday has other kitchens.

Don't split up to "just look at the dining room" like you're inspectors. Hosts hate that. Don't name-drop if you have nothing to drop.

If they find the reservation under the partner's name, smile with your mouth, not a victory lap. Sit. Order water. The sighers can have the stand.

If they cannot find it, ask them to honor the email as a walk-in with proof of intent. I don't know if they will. If they won't, take a photo of the host stand clock and the email, and go. Arguing a full room into a table rarely works and always sounds like a speech.

### User
My partner wants me to mention that we came here for their birthday last year. I think that will sound like a coupon. Also I'm the one who said the wrong name so I feel like I have to fix it with charm.

### Assistant
Skip the birthday. Last year is not a coupon, and using it as one makes it smaller. Charm is how people talk longer at a stand while the tablet is already searching. You don't owe a performance because you misspoke a surname. The email has the right one. That's the fix.

Let your partner be the name on the reservation since it is their name. One speaker is cleaner. You can be the person who holds the umbrellas.

If you get seated, let it go at the table. If you don't, pick a second place within a short walk while you still have blood sugar. Text them from the sidewalk, not from the host's elbow.

7:34 is only four minutes late. You are not a missed 7:00. Show the number, step aside, take a later slot or leave without a story about last year's cake. The wrong name was a sentence. It is not the night unless you keep giving it oxygen.
