### User
Friend asked me to be their plus-one at a wedding of people I barely know, not because they want me there, but so an ex in the room thinks they're "doing fine." They said I wouldn't have to stay long. I would have to be a prop. I don't like fake relationships, even for three hours of cake. They'll get the ask in a voice note if I don't answer the text. Help me decline without a lecture about authenticity.

### Assistant
You can skip the lecture. You can still skip the role.

"I don't want to play plus-one for the ex. I hope the day is fine. I'm not going as a dummy date." That's the no. Busy is not a costume you have to wear.

If they want company for nerves, that's a different ask: "I'll help you pick an outfit / I'll text you after." Optional. Not a seat at table 9 under a fake title.

I will not invent wedding plus-one etiquette. If they already submitted your name, they can unsubmit it. That's their paperwork.

Don't offer a maybe while you think. Maybe is how you end up in a dress you resent.

Have they already told the couple you exist as a date?

### User
They put "and guest" on the RSVP. They didn't put my name. So I would appear as a surprise body. The ex is in the wedding party. I feel mean. They sounded small in the text.

### Assistant
Small is real. Mean is not the same as no.

"And guest" without your name means you can still not be the guest. They can go alone or take someone who wants the job. Surprise body is worse for you, not better.

You can answer the smallness: "I care that you're nervous. I won't pretend we're a couple in front of your ex." Care and role are different. If they cry, you can still not rent your face.

Voice note incoming: you can reply in text anyway. Voice notes pull you into a tone-performance. A written no survives.

If they say "just stand near me," that's the same job with less dancing. Still no if the point is the ex's eyes.

### User
What if I already said "I'll think about it."

### Assistant
Think-about-it ends now. "I thought. I'm not going as a dummy plus-one." Late clarity beats a silent arrival.

You're not in charge of whether they look busy. That's a story the ex will write anyway. Three hours of cake as a prop is a long time to hate your chair.

Text before the voice note if you can. No authenticity sermon. No fake yes. Friend can be small. You can be absent from the seating chart. That's allowed.

### User
I sent the no. Voice note arrived anyway, eight minutes of small. I listened. I didn't change my mind. I also feel like I kicked someone who was already on the floor.

### Assistant
You declined a costume. Floor is their ex and a wedding, not a reason you have to be furniture. Eight minutes of small can get one text: "I heard you. I'm still not going as a dummy date. I'll text after if you want that." After is optional and only if you'll do it.

Don't listen to a second voice note tonight if it arrives. The no is written. Thinking-about-it is closed. And-guest can be someone else or an empty chair. Empty chairs are allowed at weddings. Props are optional.

You're not the villain of cake. You're a friend who won't fake a couple in front of a wedding party. That's a clean no without an authenticity lecture. They can still be small. You can still not go. Both true. Put the phone face down. If they RSVP you anyway, email is not required; a second text repeating no is enough tonight.
