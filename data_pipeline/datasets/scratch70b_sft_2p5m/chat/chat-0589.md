### User
The pharmacist called my name. I have a paper bag in my hand already. I think this is the brand version. I want to ask if there's a cheaper generic, but I have never said that out loud. I don't want to sound like I'm bargaining at a market. I also don't want you to guess a price or tell me what I have. I just need the plain sentence and when to say it, because the line is behind me.

### Assistant
Say it before you walk away from the counter. The bag in your hand is not a closed deal until you leave. The plain sentence is: "Is there a generic for this, and can you fill that instead?" If they already filled it, "Can this be switched to the generic?" still works. You are not bargaining. You are asking for the same molecule in a different label. Pharmacists hear that all day.

I will not guess the cash price. I will not name a discount card as if I know your pharmacy. I will not guess what the prescription is. If they ask why, "I need the lower-cost version if it exists" is enough. You do not owe a story about your month.

If they say the prescriber wrote "dispense as written," you can ask them to call the clinic for a substitution. That is a normal call. You do not have to perform poverty. You can wait in the chair they already have, or you can take the brand today and ask them to note it for next time. Next time is worse if this fill is huge and you will not use it. Ask now.

Is the bag still unopened on the counter, or are you already holding it by the door?

### User
Still at the counter. Unopened. There's a person behind me with a crying toddler. The tech looks busy. I paid last time without asking and it bothered me all week. I don't have the clinic number memorized.

### Assistant
The toddler is not your timer. Two extra sentences is not a crime against a line. Look at the tech, then the pharmacist if the tech defers. "Before I take this — is there a generic I can get instead?" Point at the bag. Then stop talking. Busy people fill silence with the answer.

If they need the clinic, they have it on the hard copy or in the computer. You do not need it memorized. "Please contact the prescriber if you need a switch" is the line. Then you can step aside so the toddler can move up. Stepping aside is how you stay polite without abandoning the question. They can call you back to the same register.

If they quote two numbers, pick with the number you can actually pay. I will not invent those numbers. If they say insurance prefers the brand, ask them to run the generic anyway so you can see the difference on the screen. Seeing a screen is allowed. You do not have to understand DAW codes. You have to hear whether a switch is possible today.

Last time you paid quiet. This time you ask, then you step aside. That is the whole first time.

### User
What if they say the generic is out and they can order it for Monday? I need this tonight. I don't want to choose wrong in front of a crying kid.

### Assistant
Then you say the constraint out loud: "I need a dose tonight. If you can fill the brand now and switch the rest later, what does that look like?" Let them propose the split. I will not invent whether they can partial-fill. They will know their stock. If they can only do brand tonight, you can take tonight's amount and leave a note for the generic on the next fill. That is not failure. That is a sequence.

If they cannot split, you pick the thing that gets you through tonight without pretending you like the cost. You can still ask them to order the generic for the refill. Ask them to write that on the bag or in the profile while you are there. Memory is how last week happened.

Do not apologize to the toddler's adult. Do not rush into "whatever, it's fine" because the line hums. Fine is how the brand walks out again.

Tonight: generic question, step aside if they need to type, tonight's dose first, refill note second. Plain words. No market voice. The first time is just asking before the door.
