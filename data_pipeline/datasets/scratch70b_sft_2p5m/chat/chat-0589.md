# Assistant

The Eiffel Tower is a wrought-iron lattice tower located on the Champ de Mars in Paris, France. It was designed by Gustave Eiffel and his team of engineers and was built for the 1889 Exposition Universelle (World's Fair) to celebrate the 100th anniversary of the French Revolution. The tower was initially met with criticism from some of France's leading artists and intellectuals, who considered it to be an eyesore. However, it has since become a beloved symbol of Paris and a popular tourist attraction, attracting millions of visitors each year. The tower stands at 324 meters (1,063 feet) tall and was the tallest man-made structure in the world until the completion of the Chrysler Building in New York City in 1930. Today, the Eiffel Tower is not only a symbol of Paris, but also a testament to the ingenuity and creativity of the engineers and architects who designed and built it.
