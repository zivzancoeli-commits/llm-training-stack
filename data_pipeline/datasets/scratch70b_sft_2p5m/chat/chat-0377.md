### User
I came home. There is a dog. Medium. Leash still on. My roommate Kai said "surprise, this is Bean, I couldn't leave them at the event." There was no house vote. Our lease has a line about pets I have not read this year. I like dogs. I do not like a surprise roommate with fur, extra rent maybe, and my allergies which are real in spring and maybe year-round. Bean is currently on my couch. What is the conversation in the next hour that isn't me becoming the villain of a rescue story?

### Assistant
You are allowed to like dogs and still need a vote. Those are not opposite.

Read the lease line now, on your phone, before the talk. I will not invent whether the building allows Bean. The paper might. If pets need approval, that is Kai's problem to solve today, not a vibe.

Conversation, without the dog as a prop if you can put Bean in Kai's room: "You can't add a pet without asking. I need time, the lease, and a plan. Bean can't be a surprise couch." If allergies are real, say that as a fact: "I get symptoms. I don't know how this will go. That's why you ask first." I will not diagnose your allergies. You already know your body's history.

Do not let "couldn't leave them at the event" be the whole moral. Events end. Texts exist. A photo in a group chat exists. Kai skipped those.

Tonight's ask: where Bean sleeps, who pays deposits if any, who walks at 6 a.m., what happens if this doesn't work. You do not have to answer "can Bean stay forever" in an hour. You do have to stop the fait accompli.

Has Kai ever mentioned wanting a dog, or is this a complete bolt?

### User
They've joked about a dog. I said "not while we have this carpet." Carpet is still here. Kai said the event was a pop-up adoption and Bean would have "gone back." I feel like I'm arguing with a commercial.

### Assistant
Carpet joke was a no. Pop-up pressure is how people skip the no.

Stay on process. "We said not with this carpet. You still have to ask. If the building says no, Bean can't stay. If my allergies flare, we revisit immediately." Immediate is not villainy. It is a house.

If Kai wants you to meet Bean as if meeting equals consent, you can pet the dog and still not vote yes. Those can be the same hour. Softness toward an animal is not a signature on a lease addendum.

Ask Kai to call the landlord or follow whatever pet process exists, tonight, not next week. A dog already here is a compliance clock. You should not be the secret.

If you need air, take a walk without Bean. Come back to a list: sleep place, food, deposits, walks, trial period of a week with a real conversation next Sunday. Trial is not a trick if you mean it. If you already know you cannot live with a dog, say that tonight. Delayed nos are crueler to Bean too.

### User
Bean is fine. Kai is crying. I haven't said yes. I also haven't said take them back. The couch is covered in hair after forty minutes.

### Assistant
Crying is real. Hair on the couch in forty minutes is also data.

You can comfort Kai without signing. "I'm sorry this is messy. I still need the lease checked and a real yes from me, which I don't have tonight." Then you get the hair off your couch with a lint roller because it is your couch, and Bean's bed is Kai's room unless you say otherwise.

If "take them back" is possible tonight, that may be the honest path. If it is not — shelters closed, event over — then tonight is containment: Kai's room, walks, you behind a closed door if you need it. Tomorrow is landlord and a decision with a date.

You are not the villain of a rescue story. You are the other person on the lease. Rescue stories skip that character. Don't skip yourself. Check the paper. Keep Bean off your bed. Let Kai cry without using it as a vote. The vote is still yours. Use it when you have facts, not when you have fur on your mouth. That is the next hour, and it is enough.
