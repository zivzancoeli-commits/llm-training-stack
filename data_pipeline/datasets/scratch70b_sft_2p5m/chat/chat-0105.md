### User
I haven't been to a dentist in three years and I finally opened the booking page. It wants me to pick "hygiene visit," "periodic exam," "new patient comprehensive," and something called "perio" with a warning. I don't know what any of that is. I also don't want to walk in and get a speech. I almost booked last month and then the words got me. Can you just translate this so I can click something?

### Assistant
You're closer than you think. The page is using clinic shorthand, not a test.

Plain version. A hygiene visit is a cleaning: someone scrapes and polishes, usually with a checkup if they pair them. A periodic exam is the dentist looking around at a patient they already know. A new patient comprehensive is the longer first visit for someone they don't have records on — that's probably you after three years, even if you went there as a kid. "Perio" is short for periodontal, which is gums and the bone under them. The warning is their way of saying "if your gums are the main issue, don't book a regular polish and expect that to cover it." I can't tell from here whether your gums are the main issue, and I won't diagnose you.

Which of those four is showing as available first, and does the site ask if you're a new patient before the list?

### User
It asked new patient and I said yes. Then it still showed all four. There's also a note about "FMX" and "BWX" as add-ons. I don't want surprise extra stuff. I just want my teeth looked at without feeling like I failed a class.

### Assistant
FMX is a full set of x-rays, lots of little pictures of the whole mouth. BWX is bitewings: the ones where you bite a plastic tab so they can see between back teeth. That's the translation. I'm not telling you which set you "need." That's their call after they look, and it depends on what they already have on file. After three years they may want new pictures. You can still say, "Can you tell me what those are for before you take them?" That's not failing. That's asking.

For the click: as a yes-I'm-new person, the comprehensive new-patient slot is the one that matches the situation. Hygiene-only can bounce you if they want the exam first. Periodic exam is for people already in their system. Perio is a specialized path you shouldn't self-select because a word looked serious.

When you get the confirmation email, read whether it says "exam and cleaning" or "exam only." If it's exam only, that's still a booked appointment. Cleaning might be a second date. Annoying. Not a trick.

You don't have to explain the three years at the front desk. "I've been away from care and I'm here for a new-patient exam" is enough. If they ask about pain, answer about pain if you have it. If you don't, say you don't. Don't invent a toothache to sound legitimate.

### User
No pain, just guilt. I'll book the new-patient comprehensive. If they try to add FMX on the phone, I ask what it's for first. What about "prophy"? That was on a PDF they linked. I almost closed the tab again.

### Assistant
Prophy is short for prophylaxis, which in this setting just means a routine cleaning. Fancy word, ordinary scrape-and-polish. It is not a diagnosis and it is not a verdict on the three years. If they pair "new patient exam + prophy," that's exam plus cleaning in one visit when they think it fits. If they don't pair them, you still go to the exam.

Guilt doesn't show up on x-rays. Book the slot you can actually attend, not the earliest one you'll cancel. Put it on the calendar with a reminder the day before. If the form asks for a dental history and the honest answer is "gap of three years," write that. Blank forms make more follow-up calls than honest gaps.

You're almost there. Click it, then close the tab so you don't keep rereading "perio" like it's a verdict.
