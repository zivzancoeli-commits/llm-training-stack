### User
Every evening around 9:20, the guy from 4F smokes on the landing between 3 and 4. There's a no-smoking sign at eye level. He ashes in a soda can. The stairwell is our only way down with a stroller if the elevator is broken, which it is this week. My kid noticed the smell. I don't want a feud. I also don't want to walk a stroller through a cloud while pointing at a sign like a hall monitor. He's out there now. I can hear the lighter. First move?

### Assistant
You ask once, in the stairwell, like a neighbor, not like a cop.

If you feel safe doing it: "Hey — smoke's going through the stairs. We've got a stroller and a no-smoking sign. Can you take it outside?" That's the ask. You don't need to quote a statute. I will not invent what your city or lease says about stairwells. The sign is already there. You can point at it without a lecture. If you don't feel safe, you skip the confrontation and you go to the super in writing tonight. A lighter plus a stranger plus a kid is a reason to choose email.

Don't block his exit. Don't knock the can. Don't film his face unless you have a real reason beyond anger. You want the landing clear, not a building war.

If he says "it's not the apartment," you don't debate geography. "It's still the stairs we use." Then you go into your unit or you continue down. One ask.

Is this the first time you're speaking, and is anyone with you besides the kid?

### User
First time speaking. Kid is already in bed. I'm going down for a package. Elevator still dead. I would be alone with him on the landing. He's bigger than me. The soda can is the thing that makes me madder than the cigarette, which I know is petty.

### Assistant
Then don't do the talk on a closed landing alone if your gut already tagged size. That's not cowardice. That's a staircase with one way past him.

Go down if you need the package, keep moving, short nod, no speech. If the cloud is thick, wait thirty seconds on your floor or take the other stair if you have one. When you're back inside, email the super: times (9:20-ish, repeating), 4F if you're sure, landing 3–4, sign, stroller, elevator out. Ask them to handle it. That's the job you pay someone for, even if they do it badly.

If you still want a human conversation, do it in daylight in a more open place, or with another neighbor, or not at all. The soda can can go in the email as "he's leaving a can of ash on the landing" if that's true. Petty is allowed in a fact. It's still a fact.

If you catch him with someone else around tomorrow, you can use the sentence then. Tonight's lighter is not a deadline for bravery.

Don't put a passive-aggressive note on the sign. Notes on signs start feuds and they get torn down. Super email is dull and better.

### User
He just said "hey" as I passed. I said hey back. I didn't mention the smoke. Package is in my hand. I feel like I missed the window and also like I survived. Now what, tonight?

### Assistant
You survived a pass. That was the correct window if the landing was empty of witnesses. Don't turn around and start the speech now with a package as your shield.

Tonight: inside, email, maybe a photo of the sign and the can if it's still there and you can take it without hovering over him. Don't wait in the stair to catch him. Tomorrow, if the super is a person who walks the building, you follow up once. If other neighbors are tired of it, they can email too. A pile of dull emails beats one dramatic landing.

If you want, a daytime "hey, would you mind taking smokes outside, stairs are rough with the elevator down" in the lobby is still available. You didn't miss the only chance in the universe. You skipped a bad setting.

Stroller week: use the stairs when he's not there if you can learn his 9:20 habit, or go earlier. That's annoying. It's also a stroller, not a morality play.

Next thirty minutes: door lock, email, stop rehearsing the speech you didn't give. The sign can stay on the wall. You don't have to be the person who enforces it with your body at 9:20. Get the package on the table. That's a completed errand. The cloud is a building problem now, in writing.
