### User
I bought ferry tickets for Saturday. I typed my partner's name wrong — one letter, like a nickname instead of the legal first name. The confirmation email is late too; it showed up hours after I paid. The site now says "processing." We travel this weekend. I don't know if the ticket is invalid, delayed, or fine. I don't want to panic-refresh until Friday. What do I actually do, without you inventing ferry law?

### Assistant
You don't invent ferry law, and I won't either. Names on transport are company rules, not a vibe. Wrong letter might be nothing or it might be a denied boarding. I cannot tell you which from here.

Do the boring sequence. Find the confirmation number, the exact name string you typed, and the ferry operator's own change/correction page — not a random forum. Use their contact: chat, email, phone. Say: booking number, what you meant to type, what is on the email, travel date. Ask two questions: is the booking confirmed, and can the name be corrected before sailing.

Until they answer, screenshot everything: payment, processing page, the misspelled name, timestamps. That's your folder, not a personality.

Don't buy a second full fare yet unless they tell you the first is dead. Double-buying is how people fund a glitch twice. If they tell you to repurchase, get that in writing.

Is the name on the ticket supposed to match an ID they check, or is this a ferry that never looks?

### User
I think they check ID on this route because it's longer. I'm not 100 percent sure. My partner's ID is the legal name. The nickname is what I always type in group chats. The confirmation finally arrived with the wrong name and a "have a nice trip" that does not answer my ticket.

### Assistant
"Have a nice trip" is a template. Treat the name as unresolved until an agent says it's okay or it's changed.

Call. Hold. Email in parallel so there is a timestamp. Use the name as printed on the ID when you speak. Don't joke about nicknames with a call-center clock running; jokes eat the hold.

If their policy page says names must match ID, believe their page more than my guess. I will not quote a match rule I haven't seen. If they have a self-serve name-change with a fee, I won't tell you the fee is fair or required. I will tell you to read the number they show you, not a blog.

If you cannot reach anyone today, try again at opening tomorrow, and look for a port office number. Ports sometimes fix what websites "process" forever. I won't promise the kiosk can help. I will say a human at the terminal on a day that isn't sailing day is better than a human at the gangway with a line behind you.

Your partner should be ready to show ID and the confirmation. You should be ready to show payment. That's ordinary.

Don't post the ticket QR in a group chat. That's how tickets grow extra copies you don't control.

### User
What if Saturday morning they say the name is wrong and we can't board?

### Assistant
Then you ask, at the counter, what they can do in that moment: correction, standby, or a later sailing. You accept the answer they give, not the one I invent. Have a backup way home that isn't the ferry — a later bus, a night in a town, a ride from a person you actually know. I will not price those.

Until Saturday, the job is get a human to confirm or correct. Processing pages are not boarding passes. Nicknames are for chats. IDs are for boats that check. Use the operator's words, not mine, and keep the screenshots. That's the whole adult version of not panic-refreshing: you replaced refresh with a ticket number and a question.
