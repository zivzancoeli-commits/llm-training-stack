### User
I told my cousin not to post the birthday photos from Saturday. I was in the red sweater I hate and my face was tired. Tonight I got tagged. The photo is me mid-sentence with my eyes half shut. I asked in the group chat before the cake. They replied "you look fine" and left it up. It's 8:50 p.m. I have a public-ish job. I don't want a fight in comments. I want it down.

### Assistant
"You look fine" is their taste, not your consent. You asked before the cake. That's the relevant sentence. Tired eyes in a red sweater is enough reason without a public-job thesis, though a public-ish job is extra reason to care.

Don't fight in the comments. Comments are a stadium. DM or a call: "Please take the photo down. I asked before you posted." If the tag is the problem, untag immediately on your side while you wait. Untag is not as good as delete. It's the part you control.

I won't invent platform rules or whether a family post is "allowed." I can tell you asking again is reasonable.

Did they post from their account only, or did they also send it to a family album other people can reshare?

### User
Their account, public. Three aunts already hearted it. If I untag, the photo still sits there with my name in the caption. Caption is "the whole circus." I hate that too. I haven't replied to "you look fine."

### Assistant
Don't reply to "you look fine" with a debate about attractiveness. That's their frame. Your frame is "I said no."

For the next ten minutes: untag, then message: "Please delete the post. I don't want it up, including the caption. This isn't about looking fine." If they want to keep other photos you're not in, they can. This one goes.

If three aunts already hearted it, that's social pressure on the cousin, not a vote that overrides you. You don't need to message the aunts. That's a spreading fire.

If the cousin ignores the DM, you can use whatever report/privacy tool the app gives for photos of you. I won't promise the tool works. Try it if the DM fails. Don't stack comments like "I asked you not to." That's a show.

Public-ish job: you can tell a manager later if it becomes a thing. Tonight is delete and untag, not a career novel.

### User
They'll say I'm controlling Saturday. Saturday was their house. I still didn't agree to their grid.

### Assistant
Their house, their camera, your face. Hosting a cake doesn't buy a public grid. Controlling would be you editing their whole album. One photo you asked to keep off is a boundary. Repeat that if they push: "I'm not managing your Saturday. I'm asking for this picture down."

If they refuse, you document the ask — you already have the group chat before the cake — and you stop arguing the same night. Sleep. Tomorrow you can ask once more or let the report tool grind. I can't promise an outcome.

You don't have to look at the hearts. Mute the thread. The caption "circus" can die with the post.

8:50 is a fine time to send the DM. Don't workshop a nicer version that says maybe. Maybe is how "you look fine" wins. Want it down. Say down. Untag while you wait. Then put the phone on a charger in another room so you don't refresh the hearts. You asked before the cake. That already happened. The post is the part that can still change. Ask for the change. That's the whole message. No comment-section war. No defense of the red sweater. The sweater can stay in a drawer. The photo doesn't have to stay on a public grid. If they leave it up overnight, you still sleep. Morning is a second ask, not a comment pile. One face, one request, then you stop feeding the circus caption with your attention.
