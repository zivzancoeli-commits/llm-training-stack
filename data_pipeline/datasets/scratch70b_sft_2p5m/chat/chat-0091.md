### User
I have a chemistry exam tomorrow at 9. My housemate is in the living room watching a show with the laptop speakers on, and they keep pausing to talk to someone on speakerphone. Our rooms share a wall. I already tried headphones. I can still hear the laugh track and the phone voice. I don't want to be the person who starts a fight at 10 p.m. I also don't want to fail a test because I was too polite. What do I actually say that isn't "you're the worst"?

### Assistant
Say the time, the need, and the ask. Skip the character study.

A line that is kind and specific: "I have a chem exam at 9. I can hear the show and the phone through the wall, and headphones aren't covering it. Can you use headphones or take the call in your room until 11:30?" That gives them a behavior and an end time. It does not say they are inconsiderate by nature. It does not review their taste in comedy.

Don't lead with "this always happens." Even if it always happens, tonight you need a quiet block, not a tribunal. Save the pattern talk for a weekend afternoon if you still need it.

If you have a shared-house rule about quiet hours, mention that as a fact, not a trap: "We said weeknights after 10." If you never agreed on quiet hours, don't invent a constitution on the spot. Ask for tonight.

Knock, don't text from two rooms away if they ignore messages. Stand where they can see you. One request. Then wait. If you stack three complaints in the doorway (dishes, bike in the hall, the show), they will hear a pile-on and miss the exam.

### User
They said "I'll turn it down." They turned it down for four minutes. Then the phone started again. I feel like if I go back out there I'm nagging.

### Assistant
"I'll turn it down" was not the ask. The ask was headphones or a different room, with a clock on it. Going back once is follow-through, not nagging. Nagging is repeating a vague "be quieter" until everyone is angry and nothing has changed.

Second pass, still specific: "Down isn't enough. I can still hear the call. Headphones or your room until 11:30, please. I will be done after that." Then you stop talking. Don't smile-apologize four times. Don't explain the exam syllabus.

If they get sharp, you can stay flat: "I'm not trying to ruin your night. I need the wall quiet for ninety minutes." Ninety minutes is a number a sharp person can stand. "Forever, because you are loud" is a fight.

If they refuse, you change rooms, not morals. Kitchen table, bathroom fan as noise, a laundry room if your building has one that isn't miserable, a campus building if you can get there safely and it is still open. I will not guess your campus hours. If you cannot leave, earplugs plus music with no lyrics is the ugly backup. It is not fair. Fair is later.

The later conversation, not tonight if you can help it: pick one example, one request, one agreement. "On exam nights I will text you by 6. After 10, headphones in the living room." Write it where you both can see it if this house runs on notes. Kind still means specific. "Be more respectful" is fog. Fog starts fights.

### User
There's a third housemate who is asleep. I don't want to be the only one enforcing stuff. Should I wake them to back me up? That feels evil.

### Assistant
Don't wake a sleeping person to join your enforcement squad. That trades one noise problem for two annoyed people and a story that you are dramatic. You can tell the third housemate tomorrow, once, if you want backup on a house rule. Tonight you are asking for ninety minutes, not holding a vote.

If you are scared of being "the only one," that is a real feeling and it is still not a reason to recruit at midnight. Say the line. If it works, study. If it doesn't, move your body to the quietest place you can get without a lecture. After the exam, you can ask both of them, in daylight: "Can we set exam-night quiet from 10 to midnight if someone texts the group by dinner?" That is a policy. Tonight is a request.

You don't need a villain. You need the laugh track off the shared wall until 11:30. Ask for that, in those words.
