### User
Jules took my bike. I didn't say they could. They left a note on the fridge: "borrowed steed, back by 6." It's 8:40. The bike is in the hallway with a flat rear tire and the lock hanging open. My pump is also missing from the hook. I need the bike at 7:15 tomorrow. I am so tired of "borrowed" meaning taken. I don't want a roommate trial in the kitchen. I want the tire usable and the habit dead. Script me.

### Assistant
Two jobs: the tire tonight, the habit in one conversation, not a courtroom.

Tonight, don't wait for Jules to become responsible in the abstract. You need a round tire by morning. Check whether the tube is shredded or just airless. If you have a patch kit or a spare tube, that's the fastest path. If you don't, a bike shop in the morning before 7:15 is the backup, not a speech. I won't invent shop hours or a price. You already know whether a shop exists on your commute.

The lock hanging open is useful. It means they didn't even pretend this was a careful borrow. You don't need to shout about that tonight if they're not home. You can text: "You took the bike without asking. It's back with a flat. I need it at 7:15. Where is the pump? Are you home to fix this or am I?" That's facts plus a fork.

If they're on the couch, don't open with their childhood. "You took my bike. It's flat. I need it tomorrow morning. I need the pump and I need this not to happen again." Then stop. Let them talk.

Did they actually come home, or are you staring at a hallway bike alone?

### User
They're in their room with headphones. I can see the light. The pump is not on the hook. I don't have a spare tube. There's a shop eight blocks away that opens at 9, which is after I need to leave. I have a portable inflator in a drawer if the valve still holds air. I haven't knocked yet because I will say something I can't take back.

### Assistant
Knock. Headphones are not a force field.

Start with the pump and the morning. "I need the pump. The rear is flat. I have to leave at 7:15." If they have the pump in their bag, you get air in the tire tonight and see if it holds. If it bleeds down in an hour, you know the tube is done and tomorrow is a walk, a bus, or a shop after work — not a fantasy that anger inflates rubber.

I won't tell you to ride a dying tube as if that's safe. If it's mushy after a fill, don't trust it on a commute. Plan the not-bike option tonight so 7:15 isn't a surprise.

On the borrow: after the pump is in your hand, one sentence about consent. "Don't take the bike again unless I say yes that day." If they say they thought it was fine, you don't debate telepathy. "It wasn't. Ask." If they offer to pay, you can say you'll tell them the shop cost when you know it. I will not invent a number. If they offer to "get it handled," give them a deadline: tonight or they're not the handler.

Don't take back a true sentence. "You took it without asking" is takeable-back only if it was false. It isn't.

### User
They said they hit glass and "thought it was still rideable." They want to fix it this weekend. That's after my morning. They asked if I'm "mad or just stressed." I hate that question.

### Assistant
You can answer the question without a personality quiz. "I'm not okay with you taking the bike. I need a working tire by 7:15, not this weekend." Weekend is their convenience. Morning is yours.

If they can get a tube into the wheel tonight, let them do the labor. If they can't, you inflate, test, and if it fails you take the not-bike plan. They don't get to rebrand a missed commute as your mood.

Put the lock on your bike, in your room if that's what it takes. That's not petty. That's how "borrowed" dies. You can also say, "Next time there's no note that assumes yes."

For the next twenty minutes: pump, fill, wait, decide if it holds. Text yourself the backup commute. Don't hold a kitchen trial. The habit dies when the bike is not in the hallway with the lock open. Get the pump, get the air, get the morning.
