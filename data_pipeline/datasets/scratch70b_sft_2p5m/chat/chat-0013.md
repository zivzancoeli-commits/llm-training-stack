### User
I've been in this clinic waiting room for fifty-two minutes past the appointment time. The TV is on mute. There's a fish tank with one fish bumping the glass. The receptionist said "they're running behind" and smiled like that was a full sentence. I have a ride at a set time. I'm fourteen and I came by myself because my parent is at work. Do I keep sitting here like a statue or do I actually say something?

### Assistant
Before I tell you what to say, I need one fact that changes the whole plan.

Is your ride a hard deadline — like a parent leaving work, a bus you cannot miss, or a sibling who will leave without you — or is it more like "someone can wait in the lot if you text them"?

### User
Hard deadline. My aunt is getting me on her lunch break. She said she can sit in the parking lot for ten extra minutes, not twenty. That ten is almost gone. The appointment was supposed to be a quick follow-up, not a whole afternoon. Nobody has called my name. There are still three people who got here after me.

### Assistant
Then you talk now, not after the fish completes another lap.

Walk to the desk. Stand where they can see you, not hovering behind a plant. Use a normal voice. Say: "Hi. I had a 2:15 and it's past 3:00. My ride has to leave soon. Can you tell me if I'm still being seen today, and about how much longer?"

That is not rude. That is information. You are asking two things: am I still on the list, and how long. Both are fair. Clinics run late for reasons you cannot see from a chair.

If they give you a number — "ten more minutes" — text your aunt that number plus the clinic name. If they say "we're not sure," ask the follow-up: "Should I reschedule so I don't miss my ride?" That puts the choice on them instead of you guessing in a plastic chair.

Do not leave without telling the desk. Ghosting a waiting room makes them call your name into empty air and then you look like a no-show. If you have to go, say: "I have to leave at 3:20. Please cancel me for today and I'll call to reschedule."

The three people who arrived after you might still get called first if they were emergencies. That is annoying and still not your job to police. You only advocate for your slot and your ride.

### User
What if the receptionist does the smile again and says "it shouldn't be much longer"? That is what she said fifty minutes ago. I don't want to sound like I'm throwing a fit. Also I didn't eat lunch because I thought I'd be done.

### Assistant
"Shouldn't be much longer" is not a time. You are allowed to notice that.

Stay calm and repeat the constraint: "I understand you're busy. My ride leaves at 3:20. I need a yes or no on whether I'll be seen before then." Then stop talking. Silence is not a tantrum. Filling the silence with sorry-sorry-sorry makes you smaller than the situation.

If she still will not pick yes or no, ask for a nurse or whoever runs the schedule. One sentence: "Can someone who knows the list tell me if I should wait or reschedule?" You are not climbing a chain of command to be dramatic. You are trying not to strand your aunt.

If there is a vending machine, grab something after you get an answer, not before, so you do not miss your name. Hungry plus delayed plus solo is a bad combo for patience.

You do not owe a speech about being fourteen. You already showed up. The adult move is a clear deadline, a clear question, and a leave-or-stay decision by 3:15, not 3:45 when the lot is empty.

If they call you at 3:12, you can still go in and tell the person in the room, "I have to be outside by 3:20." Let them decide whether to start. Yours is the clock and the aunt.

Sit where you can see the desk after you talk. You already waited fifty-two minutes. The next five are for an answer, not for more mute TV.
