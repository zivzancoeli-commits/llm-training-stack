### User
Devon from analytics just dropped a Google Sheet in Slack titled "Serious This Year" and pinged me in the thread. It's a fantasy football league. There's a buy-in. They already put my name in a cell in yellow. I never said yes. I don't even watch most Sundays. I don't want to look like I think I'm too good for the team, and I also don't want money sitting in a group chat with people who sign my peer reviews. What do I actually say?

### Assistant
You say no to the money, not to the person. Those can be different sentences.

Don't leave the yellow cell hanging. Silence reads like maybe. Reply in the thread if that's where they put you on display, or DM if you want less audience. The content is the same: "I'm going to skip the buy-in league. Thanks for thinking of me." If you watch football at all and want the social part, you can add, "I'll still talk games, I'm just not putting money in." If you don't watch, skip the extra. Don't invent a medical excuse or a fake travel season. Last year's you would have invented a wedding. This year's you can just not play.

I won't tell you whether workplace betting is allowed where you work. I don't know your HR policy or your state. I also won't invent what the buy-in "usually" is. You already know it's money. That's enough to decline.

Are they a peer, and is this the first time they've done this, or is this an annual trap?

### User
Peer. Same level. They do this every fall. Last year I said I was "thinking about it" and then they collected cash at the Friday lunch and I had to pretend I forgot my wallet. I felt like a child. They already put "Devon collecting Friday" in the sheet.

### Assistant
Then don't wait until Friday. The yellow cell plus a collection day is a machine. You interrupt the machine with one clear line before the lunch.

DM is cleaner than performing the no in a twelve-person thread, unless they keep pinging you publicly. Private first: "Hey — I'm out on the paid league. Please take my name off the sheet. I'm not bringing cash Friday." That's specific. It names the cell and the lunch so they can't "forget" and still hold a chair for you.

If they joke that you're scared or cheap, you don't have to joke back. "Yeah, not doing money this year" is a complete sentence. Matching their bit is how you end up with a Venmo request at 6 p.m.

If they offer to "cover you" and you pay later, that's still you in the league. Say no to the cover too. "Don't cover me. I'm not playing." People who like running leagues often hear hesitation as a scheduling problem. It isn't. It's a no.

You don't owe them a substitute player. If they ask you to recruit someone, you can say, "I don't have anyone. You'll have to fill it." Recruiting is still labor for a thing you declined.

### User
What if they leave my name there anyway and people start talking like I'm in? And should I say anything to my manager so this doesn't look like I'm anti-team?

### Assistant
If the name stays, one follow-up, still dry: "My name is still in the sheet. I'm not in. Please delete the row." If they still don't, you can comment once on the thread: "I'm not in the league — name should come off." Then stop. You are not the sheet admin. You documented the no.

Don't pre-brief your manager about fantasy football unless this person is actually pressuring you at work in a way that's eating your day. "I'm anti-team" is a story you're telling yourself. Skipping a paid hobby is ordinary. Managers don't need a memo about a Google Sheet. If Devon later jokes in a meeting that you "bailed," you can say, "Yeah, I don't do the buy-in," and change the subject. That's it.

Do not send money to make the awkwardness stop. That's how last year's wallet trick happens with extra steps.

Next ten minutes: DM, ask for the row to come out, ignore the Friday collection. If they collect in front of you, repeat "I'm not in" once and eat your lunch. You don't have to become the office fun cop. You just have to not be in the yellow cell.
