### User
I'm at a drugstore with a bottle of cough syrup in my hand. I don't drink, and I don't want alcohol in a medicine if I can avoid it. I have never asked a pharmacist anything except "where is aisle 8." There is a window. A person in a coat is back there. I feel like I'll say it wrong and they'll think I'm accusing the product of being a bar. I also don't want you to tell me whether to take it. I want the question and what to do with the answer.

### Assistant
I won't tell you to take it or not take it. I won't guess this bottle's ingredients from a chat.

The question is allowed and ordinary: "Does this have alcohol in it?" You can add, "I need one without alcohol if you have it." That's a preference plus a product question. You are not accusing a syrup of being a bar. Pharmacists answer ingredient questions all day.

If the window has a line, you still ask. It's shorter than your aisle-8 anxiety. Hold the bottle up so they can see the name.

If they say yes, you ask, "Is there a version without?" If they say no, you thank them and you don't stand there hoping the chemistry will change. You can ask whether a different type of product — a tablet, a lozenge — avoids it. I will not name a substitute. They can. That's their job.

Don't hide a medical condition you were told matters. Don't invent one. If you have a reason a clinician already gave you, you can say that in one clause. If you don't, "I don't want alcohol in it" is complete.

Is this for you, and do you already have a recommended product from a clinician, or are you self-shopping?

### User
Self-shopping for me. No clinician note. I just don't want to consume alcohol, including hidden. There's also a kids' version on the shelf and I don't know if grabbing that is a hack or a mistake.

### Assistant
Don't hack a kids' product because the box looks friendlier. Doses and ingredients are not a vibe. Ask the pharmacist whether a children's version is appropriate for you, if you even mention it. They may say no. Believe them. I will not tell you to take a pediatric dose as an adult strategy.

Hidden alcohol: they can read the Drug Facts with you. Some labels say alcohol, some say ethanol, some bury it in inactive ingredients. You don't have to know the synonyms. You ask, "Is there alcohol or ethanol in this, including inactive ingredients?" That sentence is the whole sophistication you need.

If they seem rushed, you can still wait for the yes or no. A wrong bottle in your bag is slower than thirty seconds at the window.

If you feel embarrassed, remember they have heard pregnancy questions, needle questions, and "what is this rash." Alcohol in syrup is a small ask.

I will not interpret your cough. If you are worse than a shelf visit — breathing, high fever, whatever would send you to urgent care on a normal day — you don't solve that with a bottle. You use the care you'd use without me. This chat is only how to ask about a label.

### User
They're looking at me because I've been hovering with the bottle.

### Assistant
Walk to the window. Put the bottle on the counter. "Hi — does this contain alcohol? I need one that doesn't, if you have it." Then you shut up and let them look. That's the first time, and it can stay ungraceful. Ungraceful and accurate beats a hover. Get the answer from the person who can read the bottle with you. Not from me, not from a kids' box as a guess. Then you buy what they point to or you buy nothing. Both are fine. Hovering is the only failure, and you can end it now.
