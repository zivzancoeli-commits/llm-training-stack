### User
We sat down and I already know I cannot do this table. We're next to the bar, a blender, and a table of six celebrating something with a speaker. My friend is fine. I am not. I do not want to be the person who makes the host sigh. I also will not hear anyone and I will fake-laugh for an hour. We have not ordered drinks. The menus just landed.

### Assistant
Menus down, no drinks: that is the easy window. Once a server has put in alcohol, moving you is a favor. Right now it is still seating.

You do not need a speech about your nervous system. You need one sentence to the host or the server, not to the table of six. "Could we move to a quieter table? This spot is loud for us." "Us" is useful even if your friend is fine. You are not required to diagnose yourself in a restaurant.

Do not glare at the celebration. They are allowed to celebrate. You are allowed to sit somewhere else.

I will not claim the restaurant "has to" reseat you. They might be full. The ask is still allowed.

Is it the two of you, and did you have a reservation under a name they can find?

### User
Two of us, reservation, we got seated anyway in this noise pocket. My friend already said "it's fine if you want to stay." Which is kind and also not helpful. A server is looking at us like we might be ready.

### Assistant
Flag the server before they take a drink order. "We haven't ordered yet. Could we ask about a quieter table?" If they say they will check, wait. Do not start apologizing in a loop. One thanks is enough.

If the only other table is by the kitchen, decide if kitchen noise is better than blender noise. For a lot of people it is. If they offer the bar rail, that is usually worse. You can say no to a worse offer: "Is there anything away from the speaker?" Specific helps. Vague "quieter" can get you a two-foot slide.

Your friend's "it's fine" is them not wanting to be a problem. You can answer them, not the whole room: "I know you can do this table. I can't hear. I want to ask once." Then ask. If they look embarrassed, that is a feeling. It will pass. An hour of fake-laughing will not.

If the house is truly full, you have three honest options: stay and use the outdoor air for a reset in ten minutes, take the food to go, or leave and eat elsewhere. Leaving a reservation after being seated is awkward and still allowed before food is in the window. Pay for nothing you did not consume, obviously. I will not invent a cancellation fee.

Do not ask the six-top to turn the speaker down as your first move. That is how a scene starts. The house can ask them. You ask the house.

### User
What if the host does the tight smile. I will fold. I always fold.

### Assistant
The tight smile is their job face. It is not a verdict on your character. Repeat once: "We'd really like to move if anything opens." If they say no, pick leave or stay. Folding is staying without choosing. Choosing is adult.

For the next ten minutes: catch the server, make the one ask, sit still while they check, and if the answer is no, decide leave versus stay with your friend in a short private sentence, not a performance. If you stay, switch seats with your friend so your back is to the speaker if that helps, and do not drink fast to cope with noise. If you leave, thank them and go. You can text an apology to the reservation line later if you feel you must. You do not have to narrate your ears to the bar.

Wanting to hear your friend is not a scene. Asking once, early, is the whole trick. Menus are still on the table. Use that.
