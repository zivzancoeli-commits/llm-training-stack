### User
There's a small skate park behind my building. Lights used to die around 10. Now they stay on, white and high, and people stay with them. Wheels, music from a speaker, the occasional yell. It's 12:40. I can see the glow on my ceiling. I like that kids have a place. I also have work at 7. I don't know who changed the lights. I don't want to be the person who kills a park. Talk me through tonight and tomorrow without a crusade.

### Assistant
Tonight is sleep. Tomorrow is a question to a human who actually owns the lights.

Tonight: blinds, sleep mask if you have one, fan or earplugs. Do not go to the park at 12:40 to negotiate with a group and a speaker. That's how you become a story, not a neighbor. If something sounds like a fight rather than skating, that's a different call, and you'd use a non-emergency line with what you can actually hear. Wheels and music are not a fight.

I will not invent a curfew, a parks department hours sheet, or whether those lights are city, school, or a private lot. You will find out tomorrow by looking at a sign on the fence, the city's parks page, or the building manager if the park is attached to your lot.

Liking that kids have a place can coexist with needing dark at 1 a.m. Those aren't opposites. Crusades happen when people skip the owner and start with a rant.

Can you tell if the skaters are mostly teens, or mixed adults, or can you not see from your window?

### User
Mostly teens, a couple of adults. The speaker is the worst part. Lights would be annoying alone but sleepable. Speaker plus lights means they treat it like noon. Building group chat already has two people typing in all caps. I haven't posted.

### Assistant
Don't post in all caps. Don't pile on. If you post at all, wait until morning and stick to hours and volume, not "these kids." Group chats turn parks into enemies.

Tomorrow: one email or call to whoever runs the park, facts only. Lights used to shut off around 10, now they don't, skating and a speaker past midnight, you live facing it. Ask whether the timer broke or the policy changed. A broken timer is a maintenance ticket. A policy change is a conversation. You cannot tell which from the ceiling glow.

If it's city, use their noise or parks contact, not a random councilmember rant. If it's your complex's lot, that's the manager. If there's a sign with a number, that's the number.

You can also ask for speaker-off after a certain hour even if lights stay for safety. I will not pretend I know what they'll agree to. Splitting lights and noise is often easier than "turn the park off." Safety lighting is a real reason people keep floods on. A bluetooth speaker is not safety.

Tonight you don't join the caps lock. You sleep as well as the mask allows.

### Assistant
You can contact the owner without joining the group-chat jury. Those are different audiences. The owner gets a maintenance question. The chat can stay without you.

"I don't want the park gone. I want the timer or the speaker dealt with so people can sleep." Write that sentence in the email so you don't sound like a shutdown campaign. Youth sports in daylight is not the complaint. Midnight speaker is.

If the chat quotes you, you still sent a calm note to the right desk. Quotes in a tenant thread are not a verdict.

Next hour: mask, fan, no park visit, no all-caps. Tomorrow: who owns the lights, one factual ask, park still allowed to exist. That's a neighbor, not a crusade.
