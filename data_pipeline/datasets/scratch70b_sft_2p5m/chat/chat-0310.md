### User
We just got news in a small room that was not good. I am not going to repeat it here. Someone said I should eat. The cafeteria is fluorescent and smells like gravy. I am holding a tray I do not remember picking up. I keep thinking if I eat I am being casual about the news. I also have a shake in my hands. There is a table by a window with a man on a phone. I do not know where to sit or what to do with the next twenty minutes.

### Assistant
Eating is not disrespect. Your body still uses fuel after a hard room. Casual is a story you are telling about mashed potatoes. It is not a moral system.

You do not have to finish a tray. A few bites, water, a pause, is a plan. If the gravy smell is too much, pick the least smelly thing and a bottle of water and leave the rest.

Sit where you can see a door if that helps you feel less trapped. You do not have to perform privacy at the man's phone table. A side table, a bench, even a step outside if weather and your situation allow, is fine. I do not know the building.

I will not interpret the news, name a diagnosis, or tell you what the next medical step "must" be. I cannot. If a nurse gave you a time to be back, that time is the spine of the next hour. If they did not, you can ask the desk when you should return, after you eat something.

Are you alone, or is someone with you who also has not eaten?

### User
My sibling is in the restroom. They said "get something." I got soup and crackers and I haven't touched them. We have to be back on the floor in about 45 minutes. I keep refreshing email like a different message will arrive.

### Assistant
Stop refreshing email. The room you left is not going to send a correction through your inbox. Put the phone face down for ten minutes. If you need a timer for the floor, set one timer, not a feed.

When your sibling comes back, you can split the soup without a conversation about meaning. "Sit. Two spoons." You can also not talk. People survive cafeterias in silence. You do not have to recap the small room over crackers.

If you cannot swallow, you still drink water. If you feel like you might faint, sit, head down, tell your sibling. I am not diagnosing you. I am saying a shake plus no food plus bad news is a common dizzy cocktail and a chair is better than a performance of toughness.

If a stranger's phone is too loud, move. You are allowed to take the tray. You are allowed to throw most of it away. Waste is not a sin today.

If you need a task, pick one: eat four crackers, or step outside for three minutes of air, or write the questions you still have on paper for the next person in scrubs. One task. Not all.

### User
I don't want to cry in the cafeteria. There are people with balloons. It feels like the wrong room for my face.

### Assistant
Balloons and gravy exist in the same hospitals as bad rooms. The cafeteria is not assigned a mood. If you cry, look at the soup. People will assume onions. If you cannot stand the balloons, a hallway chair is a valid cafeteria.

For the next ten minutes: phone down, water, two spoons or four crackers, sibling sits, timer for the floor, one question written if your brain is looping. Then you walk back. You can leave the tray. You can take a banana for later even if it seems stupid.

You are not being casual. You are staying upright. That is the assignment until the 45 minutes is gone. The news will still be there. You do not have to starve to prove you heard it.
