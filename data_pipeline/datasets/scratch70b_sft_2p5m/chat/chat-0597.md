### User
Nico asked me to "be his manager for ten minutes" on a background call. He wants me to confirm dates he worked at a cafe I have never run. He says everyone does it. I have a voice. I do not have his personnel file. I don't want to dummy as his manager. I also don't want to write him a speech about fraud. How do I say no so he stops sending me the call-in PIN?

### Assistant
Do not take the call. Do not be his manager. You are not his manager. A PIN does not change that.

Say: "I won't pretend to be your boss. Don't send me the PIN. I can be a friend reference if they want a friend, not a fake supervisor." If you were never his coworker, even the friend reference should stay inside what you actually know. I will not invent employment law. I will not invent what background checkers do. I will tell you the practical split: true reference versus theater. Decline the theater.

"Everyone does it" is how people ask you to risk your name for their dates. Your name is not a prop.

If he already sent the PIN, reply on that thread so there is a record: "I'm not calling in. I'm not your manager." Then you do not dial.

Did he tell you the company name on the caller ID, or just the PIN?

### User
He sent a PIN and a window: Thursday 2 to 4. He said they'll ask if he was assistant manager. He was a barista. I used to eat there. That's my whole connection. He thinks my "professional voice" will sell it.

### Assistant
Eating scones does not make you operations. Assistant manager is a title you will not confirm. Professional voice is how a lie sounds like a person with a spreadsheet.

Reply: "No to Thursday. I won't say you were assistant manager. I won't call the PIN. If you need a real manager, that's someone who scheduled you." If you want one kindness, "I hope the job works out" is enough. Do not offer to "just verify he worked there" if you cannot verify it. You saw him at the counter. That is not HR.

If he says you are ruining his life, you are ruining a script. He can ask the actual cafe. That is the boring path. You are the shortcut. Shortcuts of this kind put your voice on a recording. I will not scare you with invented penalties. I will say you should not be on that recording.

Delete the PIN after you refuse so you do not pocket-dial a moral injury.

### User
He's going to ask me to at least coach him on what a manager would say. That's not me on the phone. Is that still dummying?

### Assistant
Yes. Coaching him to fake a manager is still the bit, just with you as writer. Do not draft a manager. Do not role-play the call. Do not punch up his dates.

If you want to help inside truth: "Tell them the title you actually had and the months you remember. If you don't remember, say you don't." That is not a dummy. That is a friend telling him not to embroider.

If he forwards a "what managers usually confirm" blog, delete it. You are not studying for his exam. If Thursday 2 to 4 arrives and he pings "they're on the other line," do not pick up. A live panic ping is still the same no.

No PIN, no Thursday window, no assistant-manager yes, no script. You can still buy coffee from him if he works somewhere real. You cannot be the voice in the sky. Send the no before Thursday so the slot dies. Let his cafe be his cafe. Let your professional voice stay attached to jobs you actually had.
