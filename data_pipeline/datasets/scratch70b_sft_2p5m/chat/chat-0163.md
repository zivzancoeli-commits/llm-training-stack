### User
In standup my coworker said "we cleaned up the billing export" and looked at the manager like it was a duet. I wrote the export. They formatted a spreadsheet. I smiled like an idiot. I am still mad at lunch. I do not want a speech about "documenting your wins." I want to know what a sharp person would actually do next, not a LinkedIn post.

### Assistant
Before I tell you what to do, I need one thing I cannot see from here.

Was this a one-off steal in front of the manager, or have they done the "we" move on your work before?

### User
They have done the "we" thing twice on this project. Last time I let it go. Today the manager said "nice work" to both of us. The coworker did help with the spreadsheet. I am not saying they did zero. I am saying the export was mine and they walked it in like a joint invention. I keep wondering if I am being petty.

### Assistant
Petty would be if they said "we" about a five-minute favor and you filed a grievance. Twice, on the actual deliverable, in front of the person who assigns work, is not petty. It is a pattern with a witness.

I still will not pretend I know your office politics, whether this manager notices details, or what your handbook says about credit. I have not sat in that standup.

Next move should be small and checkable, not a manifesto.

Today, after lunch, you send a short follow-up that restates facts without prosecuting their soul. Something you would be fine having forwarded:

"Quick recap from standup: the billing export script is in [repo or folder], I handled the query and the job. [Name] formatted the sheet for review. If we need changes, I can take them."

That is not a gotcha. It is a paper trail that matches reality. If they truly co-built it, they can say so in the thread. If they only formatted, the sentence sits there.

If you do not write things down in this team, say it out loud to the manager once, in a 1:1 or a passing moment: "I want to be precise — I built the export; they helped the sheet." Then stop. Repeating it in every room makes you the credit guy. Once, accurate, is enough for a twice-only problem.

Do not smile-and-die in the next standup. When they say "we" about your piece, you can correct in one clause, like a fourteen-year-old who does not need a TED talk: "The export piece was me; the sheet was them." Calm. No joke that undercuts you. No speech.

If the manager actually thinks it was a duet, that one clause is the whole intervention. If the manager does not care, you still have the written recap for when reviews happen. I will not promise a review outcome. I do not know your cycle.

### User
What if they get weird and say I am not a team player. Also should I talk to them first.

### Assistant
Talk to them first if you still have to share a desk and you can do it without a courtroom.

Hallway version: "When you said we cleaned up the export, it sounded like we both built it. I built it. You helped the sheet. Please say it that way." Then you wait. If they apologize, good. If they call you not a team player, you do not debate teamwork philosophy. You say, "Team still includes accurate sentences." Then you send the recap anyway, because feelings are not the record.

If they are the kind of person who punishes a hallway, skip the hallway and do the written recap only. I cannot see which person they are. You can. Pick the channel that does not require them to be generous.

You asked what a sharp person would do. A sharp person would not write a novel, would not wait for justice to appear, and would not pretend a spreadsheet is the same as a pipeline. One clarifying fact in public, one short recap, one direct ask if it is safe. Then you go back to work. The lunch anger can ride in the passenger seat. It does not get the mic.
