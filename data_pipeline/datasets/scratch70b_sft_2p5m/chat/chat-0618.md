### User
I took notes twice because I type fast. Now Raj puts "notes: me" on every invite. Forever. I am not the secretary. I am on the same level. I don't want to dummy a gratitude paragraph. I want off the job without becoming the person who refuses to help.

### Assistant
Twice is a favor. Forever is a role. Get off the invite line. Fast typing is not a job title, and it should not show up in the calendar like one.

Say: "I'm not the standing note-taker. I'll rotate, or we use a shared doc and whoever cares fills it, or we skip notes when they're not needed." Then you stop typing in the next meeting until someone else starts. If you keep typing, the speech did not happen. Your hands will try to rescue the silence. Sit on them if you have to.

I will not invent your team's policy. I will not invent that AI notetakers are allowed. If your workplace already drops a bot in the call, that bot can be the forever. You are not a bot.

Do not thank Raj for the opportunity. Opportunity is how forever starts.

Is the "notes: me" visible to a manager, or only the working group?

### User
Visible. Manager never said I had to. Raj just types it. People now Slack me "what did we decide" instead of scrolling. I have a backlog of other work. Tomorrow there are three meetings.

### Assistant
Tomorrow you reply-all on the first invite: "I can't note this one. Please take me off the standing assignment. Who's grabbing it?" If silence, you go to the meeting and you do not open a doc. Silence plus your closed laptop is the test. If Raj looks at you, you can say, "I flagged it on the invite. Not me today." Then you participate with your mouth, not as a stenographer.

For the Slack "what did we decide," you can paste the public recap if someone else wrote one. If nobody wrote one, "I didn't take notes. Check with Raj or the thread." Redirect. You are not a search engine with a keyboard.

Manager-visible is actually helpful. If the manager wanted a secretary, they could say so. They did not. You can mention once, if needed: "I'm stepping off default notes so I can do the rest of my job." Then you do the rest of your job.

Three meetings: pick zero to note, or one if you truly own that project. Not three. Forever dies in a day of zeros.

### User
If I don't type, nothing will be written and then people will say the meeting was useless because of me.

### Assistant
If nothing is written, that is the group's product. It is not a stain with your name. A meeting that only exists because you transcribe it was already thin. Let it be thin. If someone needs a record, they will feel the gap and type. That is how rotation starts. If nobody types, maybe the meeting could have been a slack. You do not have to rescue the culture with your tendons.

Raj can want a scribe. He can volunteer. Forever was a quiet steal of your time. Take it back on the invite, then in the chair, then in Slack. Help remains available for a true one-off: "I can note Thursday's vendor call because I own vendor. Not the rest." Specific yes, general no. People can live with a yes that has a border.

Tomorrow: one reply-all, closed doc, redirect the "what did we decide." You type fast. That is not a vocation. It is a skill you get to keep for your own work. Let the minutes belong to whoever cannot stand the silence. You can stand it. Sit there. Speak. Do not transcribe your own career into a sidebar. Off the line. On the work.
