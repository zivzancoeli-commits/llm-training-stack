### User
Zipper on my jacket just died. The little pull tab is still in my hand and the teeth are open down the front. I have to leave in ten minutes for a thing I cannot skip. I am fourteen and I do not have a spare winter coat that fits. I also do not have money to throw at a tailor tonight. I need a plan that costs basically nothing and does not make me look like I got dressed in a storm. Be straight with me. I can handle a dumb-looking fix if it works.

### Assistant
Ten minutes. No shopping. No magic.

First, try the zipper as a machine, not a fashion problem. If the slider is still on the teeth, hold the bottom together, pinch the sides so the teeth line up, and tug the slider down to the box, then up. Sometimes it jumped the track. If the pull snapped but the slider is there, move it with a paperclip through the hole, pliers, or fingernails. Free if you have a clip.

If the slider is gone or teeth are missing a chunk, stop fighting it. You will spend the ten minutes making it worse.

Cheap closures already in a house:

Safety pins down the front, hidden under the storm flap if there is one. Two or three at chest, stomach, and hem. Wear a shirt you do not mind showing if a pin opens.

Binder clips or clothespins on the inside if the jacket is thick.

A belt or long scarf wrapped so it stays shut. You look like you chose a layer.

If it is not actually cold enough, leave it unzipped and walk. I do not know your weather. If it is wet and windy, pins plus a fast walk beat an open chest.

I will not quote a tailor price. Tonight is not a tailor night.

### User
The slider is still on. The pull snapped. I have paperclips in a drawer and safety pins in a tin. It is cold enough that I need the jacket closed. I have to take a bus. What do I do in the next ten minutes, step by step, like you are in the hallway with me?

### Assistant
Minute one: jacket on a table, not on your body. Paperclip through the slider hole. Bend it into a loop so it cannot fall out on the bus. Test: slider should move up and down with the clip as a handle.

Minute two: zip it on the table. If it climbs and holds, you are done. Wear it. Keep a spare clip in your pocket because paperclips migrate.

If it will not climb: stop. Pins.

Minute three: jacket on. Hold the two sides together at your chest. Pin from the inside if you can. One high, one mid, one lower. Sharp ends away from skin. If a pin would stab you, put folded tissue or a shirt hem between pin and body.

Minute four: phone camera. If a pin is obvious, cover it with a backpack strap.

Minute five: phone, keys, bus card in a pants pocket, not the jacket.

Minutes six through eight: leave. Do not recut the zipper. Do not glue it. Glue in a panic is how jackets become trash.

Minutes nine and ten: if you miss the first bus because of pins, take the next one. A closed jacket on the second bus beats an open jacket on the first.

### User
What do I say if someone at the thing notices the paperclip? And what is the actual later fix that still is not expensive?

### Assistant
You say "the pull broke" and you change the subject. You do not owe a comedy routine. A paperclip is a handle. People who laugh can lend a coat or be quiet.

Later, not tonight: a zipper repair kit from a drugstore is usually cheaper than a new coat, but I will not name a number because I would be making it up. A parent, a home-ec room, or a dry cleaner that replaces sliders can do the small part. Until then, the clip or the pins are the plan. You are not supposed to look like a catalog. You are supposed to be warm enough to get on the bus. That is the whole ten minutes.
