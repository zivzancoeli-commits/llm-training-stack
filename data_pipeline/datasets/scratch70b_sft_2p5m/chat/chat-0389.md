### User
I took this afternoon off for a vaccine appointment at 2:15. At 1:50 the clinic texted that the provider is running 90 minutes behind. I am already in the parking lot with a book. Work thinks I am out until 5. If I leave, I lose the slot. If I stay, I sit in a car until 3:45 for a thing that might slip again. I don't know whether to call, wait, or reschedule. I also don't want medical advice. I want the logistics of a wasted half day.

### Assistant
You already did the hard part: you showed up. The delay is the clinic's clock, not a character test.

Call the number on the text. Do not only reply in the portal if a person can pick up. Ask three things: is 90 minutes still the estimate, is the appointment still happening today, and if you leave the lot can they keep you in line or do you forfeit. Write the answers down. Estimates move. You want the current one from a mouth, not only from a blast text.

I will not tell you whether you should still get the shot today, what the vaccine is for, or what happens if you wait a week. That is medical, and I will not invent it. I will tell you how to spend the next hour of a half day you already paid in leave.

If they confirm you are still on the list, decide a personal cutoff. Example: "If I am not checked in by 4:00, I reschedule." Put it on a timer. Open-ended waiting in a lot is how 5:00 happens without a shot.

Are you the only adult in the car, and do you have water and a bathroom you can actually use in that building?

### User
I'm alone. There's a lobby bathroom if I check in. They said don't check in until they text again. The lot is fine. My manager already said "hope it goes well" which makes me feel like I should produce a completed appointment. I could go back to work at 3 and look available. That also feels like I donated the leave for nothing.

### Assistant
Leave already spent is spent. Going back at 3 to look virtuous does not refund the hours. It just splits your brain.

Stay or reschedule based on the clinic's answer, not on your manager's politeness. If you stay, check in when they allow it, use the bathroom, sit in the lobby if that is more humane than the car. Bring the book. Put the phone face down between updates.

If they cannot guarantee today, reschedule before you drive off so you are not in a two-week portal maze from the highway. Ask for the next opening they actually have. I will not guess wait times.

You do not owe work a completed medical errand as a souvenir. A delayed clinic is a delayed clinic. If your manager asks tomorrow, "They ran late, I waited, it's done" or "They ran late, I had to rebook" is enough. No essay.

Do not use the extra afternoon to start a big work task from the parking lot "just in case." That is how you miss the text. If you must do something, pick a closed task that can drop at a tap: a form, a grocery list, a chapter. Not a spreadsheet that needs twelve tabs.

### User
What if they call me back at 4:50 when I already promised myself 4:00 was the cutoff? I am bad at cutoffs. I will sit here until the lot lights come on.

### Assistant
Then you need a cutoff that is physical, not motivational. At 4:00 you start the car or you walk inside and ask once more, out loud: "Am I being seen today?" If the answer is no or vague, you book the next slot at the desk if you can, then you leave. If the answer is "we're calling you next," you can stay for that next, not for a new 90 minutes.

If a text lands at 4:50, you are allowed to ignore it if you already left. You are also allowed to turn around if you are still in the neighborhood and want to. I will not pick your medicine. I will pick your method: one question, one time, then motion.

Tell your future self this in a note on the dash: "4:00 ask, then go." The half day was for the appointment, not for proving you can wait longer than a clinic. Call them now. Set the timer. Read the book until the timer. Then move.
