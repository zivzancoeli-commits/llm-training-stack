### User
The guy in 2B comes down with a folding chair and smokes on my porch. It is my porch. Ground-floor stoop, my doormat, my package box. He says the landlord told him not to smoke in the hall, so this is "the outdoors." It is 11 p.m. The smoke sits under my window. I don't want a fight on the steps. I also don't want his ash on the mat. How do I move him without a speech about lung science?

### Assistant
It is your stoop. "The outdoors" is not a free lease on your doormat. You can move him with a location sentence, not a lecture.

Tonight if he is there: open the door, stay on your side of the threshold if that feels safer, and say, "I need the porch back. Please take that to the sidewalk or the alley." Point at the chair. The chair is the occupation. Ash on the mat is extra. You can add, "Don't ash on the mat." Two facts. I will not diagnose anyone. I will not invent a building smoking rule. I will not invent a fine. If the building has a posted patio rule, you can point at the sign later. Tonight you can still claim the mat.

If you do not want the doorway conversation, a note on his door is weaker but written: "Please don't sit on 1A's porch to smoke. Use the sidewalk." Written is a trail. Spoken is faster if he is already in the chair.

Do not start with "I have asthma" unless that is true and you want it said. You do not need a medical plot. Smoke on your stoop is enough.

Is he out there now, or are you planning for tomorrow night?

### User
He's out there now. Chair, phone, cigarette. I already locked the deadbolt. There's a package I need to grab and I don't want to squeeze past his knees. We have never really talked except "hey."

### Assistant
Then you need the package and the porch. Turn the light on first so it is not a jump scare. Open the door. "Hey — I need to get that package, and I need the porch. Please move the chair off my stoop. Smoke on the sidewalk." Then you wait. Waiting is the hard part. People fill waiting with "five more minutes." You can repeat, "I need it now." You do not need to smile.

If he says the hall is the only other option, you do not have to solve his hall problem. "Sidewalk" is the option you are offering. You are not the building's designated outdoor lounge manager. You are the person whose mat he is using.

Grab the package while the chair is moving, not while he is still filling the landing. If he will not move, take a photo of the chair on your mat from your doorway for your own record, then close the door. I will not tell you that a photo wins a case. I will tell you a time-stamped picture is clearer than a vibe. Text the landlord in the morning: porch, chair, time, smoke at the window. One paragraph. No lung science.

Do not kick the chair. Do not dump water. Property fights get stupid on stoops at 11 p.m.

### User
He just laughed and said I should buy a plant if I want it to look private. The package is behind his chair legs. I can feel myself getting the joke-along voice.

### Assistant
Drop the joke-along. It trains the lounge. "I'm not joking. Move the chair. I need the box." If you can reach the package without touching him, take it and go back in. If you cannot, the sentence stays on the chair, not on a plant debate. Privacy is not a fern. It is your landing.

If he still laughs, you close the door and you write. Landlord, tonight's time, "tenant from 2B sitting on 1A porch smoking, would not move chair, package blocked." Ask for a written rule about stoops. I will not promise the owner cares. I will promise that a laugh is not a lease.

Tomorrow daylight: if you see him in the hall, one calm repeat: "Porch is mine. Sidewalk is smoking." Then you stop. You do not need a friendship to enforce a mat.

Right now: light, door, three short sentences, package if you can reach it, door shut if you cannot. No plant talk. No lung talk. Chair off the stoop is the whole request. Let the laugh die in the alley.
