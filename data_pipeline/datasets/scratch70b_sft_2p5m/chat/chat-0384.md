### User
Every Thursday around 9:30 the couple above me starts. Not music. Voices. Doors. Something that sounds like a chair. It lasts forty minutes. I work early Fridays. I have never met them. I don't want to knock during a fight. I also don't want another week of sleeping in headphones. Building has a super who only answers about packages. What's the first move that isn't me becoming their counselor?

### Assistant
You are not their counselor. You are a person who sleeps under a weekly show.

Do not knock while they are mid-argument. That puts you in the scene. First move is a note or a daytime knock on a calmer day, ideally Friday afternoon or Saturday, when you can say the thing without shouting through a door during a fight.

Keep the message about impact, not about their relationship. "Hi — I live below you. Thursday nights around 9:30 it gets very loud in my bedroom and I have early Fridays. Could you keep it down after 9? Thanks." No diagnosis of their marriage. No emoji paragraph.

If you would rather not knock at all, a slip under the door with the same text is fine. Sign an apartment number so they can reply. You do not need your full name.

I will not invent quiet-hours law for your city. If the building has posted hours, you can mention those hours as a reminder, not as a prosecution.

Have you ever heard them during the day, or is Thursday the only sample?

### User
I've passed them once at the mailboxes. They seemed fine. The super told me last month "people argue" when I mentioned it in the lobby. I didn't file anything. Headphones help until I get a headache. Tomorrow is Thursday again.

### Assistant
Tomorrow, survive first. Earplugs plus the fan you already own, if you have one, is a night tactic, not a surrender. Do not go upstairs at 9:30 unless you believe someone is in danger. A weekly argument is miserable. It is not automatically an emergency. If it ever sounds like someone is being hurt, that is a different call, and you use whatever emergency number you actually use. I will not script that from here.

Friday, do the note. The super already shrugged. Super shrugs are not a plan. You can still copy the super on an email after you try the neighbors, with dates: "Thursdays around 9:30, lasting about forty minutes, bedroom ceiling." Dates beat "people argue."

If they apologize and it happens again, you send the same note once more with the new date. If they get hostile, you stop knocking and stay with paper and the super. You do not owe them a friendship because you asked for sleep.

Do not record audio unless you already know your local rules for recording. I will not invent those rules. Written times are enough for a first pass.

### User
I'm afraid they'll know it's me and then it gets weird in the hallway. There are only four units on their floor and I'm directly under them.

### Assistant
They will probably guess. Directly under is not a secret geometry. Weird hallways are still better than Thursday headphones forever if the noise drops. You can keep hallway talk small: a nod, not a recap of their fight.

If the idea of a signed note makes you freeze, use the apartment number only, or ask the super to pass a building-neutral reminder: "Please keep voices down after 9." That is weaker but it is a start. Super already dismissed you once, so do not wait on charm.

Tonight: set out earplugs before 9. Tomorrow night: do not knock. Friday: note or email with two Thursday dates. Then you live with the result for a week. If it is quieter, you let it be quieter. If it is the same, you send the dated list to the super again. Sleep is the goal. Counseling them is not.
