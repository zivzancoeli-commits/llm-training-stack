### User
I had a letter from the city about a jury questionnaire or something. I only know because I got a follow-up. Roommate "cleared the pile" on the table and tossed anything that looked like junk. Mine was in there. They said they were being helpful. I am trying not to do a crime-show voice. How do I make this stop without a laminate mail sorter speech.

### Assistant
Helpful is not the same as yours to throw away.

"Don't throw out mail that isn't yours. If the table's a mess, make a pile, don't toss." That's the whole policy. You can say it at the table, with the follow-up in your hand if you still have it.

I will not invent what the city letter required or what happens if you missed a date. You deal with that follow-up separately, today, using the phone number or portal on the paper you have. Roommate talk is the other track.

No laminate speech. A cardboard box or a hanging file with names is optional. The rule is: other people's envelopes are not recycling.

Did they toss only obviously cheap ads, or was it a mixed pile including window envelopes?

### User
Mixed. Window envelopes, a magazine, grocery flyers, my letter. They said "it looked like junk, it was sitting for days." It was sitting for two days because I work late. They work from the table. I get that the pile annoyed them.

### Assistant
Annoyance is allowed. Disposal of your window envelope is not the fix.

You can give them a path that isn't the trash: "If it's in the way, put it on my bed or in a bag on my door. Don't decide it's junk." Two days is not abandoned. Mail is slow. Late shifts are real.

If they need the table clear for work, that's a furniture problem. A small inbox on a shelf is boring and effective. You don't need a label maker. You need a landing zone that isn't the trash can.

For the city follow-up: do it before the roommate conversation if the deadline anxiety is loud. Then you talk. Mixing "you may have wrecked a government letter" into the first sentence will make them defensive and you'll get a worse listen. Facts after: "A letter of mine got thrown out. That's why I'm saying this."

### User
They offered to "just ask me next time." Asking still means they're sorting my mail.

### Assistant
"Don't sort my mail. Move the pile or leave it." Asking is better than trashing and still not their job. You can decline the clerk role.

If they look hurt, let them. You can add "I know you were trying to clear space." That's courtesy. It doesn't reopen the trash.

Table, a move-not-toss rule, you handle the follow-up today. No crime-show voice. No laminated sorter unless you actually want one. The magazine can live. The window envelopes stay unopened by anyone who isn't you.

### User
I talked. They said they would make a pile. This morning a window envelope was in the recycling under flyers. I fished it out. It was a coupon, luckily. I am still mad. Do I repeat the rule or go straight to a box.

### Assistant
Both, in that order, short. "A window envelope was in recycling again. Mail goes in this box, not the bin." Then you put a cardboard box on the shelf with their buy-in if they will give it, and without it if they won't. You already talked. A second toss is the box chapter, still not a laminate speech.

You don't need to dumpster-dive every morning. If you missed a city letter once, that's why the box exists. If they "tidy" the box into the trash, then you move your mail to your room and the table rule becomes: their clutter, their problem. You are not required to keep a communal pile that eats legal-looking envelopes.

Coupon this time. Next time it might not be. Repeat the rule, install the landing zone, stop calling it helpful when it is a toss. Two days on a table is not abandoned. Window envelopes stay closed unless they have your name and you open them.
