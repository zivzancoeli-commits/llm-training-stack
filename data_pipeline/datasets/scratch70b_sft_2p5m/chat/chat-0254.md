### User
I ordered a grilled cheese. It arrived with a hair in the fold and the plate is cold. I'm at a two-top by the window. First time I would send anything back. My friend already took a bite of their fries and said "just eat around it." I can see the hair. I cannot unsee it. The server is slammed. I don't want a free meal speech and I don't want to chew a hair I already spotted.

### Assistant
Don't eat around a hair. "Just eat around it" is a sentence for a stray pepper, not for someone else's strand. You're allowed to send it back. Villain is a story people tell to keep kitchens unbothered. A calm return is ordinary.

You don't need a TED talk. When the server next passes, a low voice: "There's a hair in the sandwich. Could I get another one, please?" Plate still on the table so they can see it if they need to. Don't hold the hair aloft. Don't joke about health department. I won't invent inspection rules. I also won't diagnose whether this is "unsafe" in a legal sense. It's food you don't want in your mouth. That's enough.

Are you asking for a remake of the same thing, or do you want something else entirely?

### User
Same thing if they can do it without the hair. I don't want to wait 25 minutes and watch my friend finish. I also don't want the cook to spit in it, which I know is a movie, but it's in my head. I haven't drunk the water. I feel like if I drink I'm accepting the table.

### Assistant
Drinking water is not a contract. Drink the water. You're a customer at a two-top, not a hostage in a folk tale.

The spit story is a movie. I can't promise kitchen moods. I can say that a short, specific complaint is less likely to start a war than a speech about disgust. You already have the right length.

For the next ten minutes: flag the server, say the sentence, let them take the plate. If they apologize, "thanks" is enough. If they offer a free dessert, you can take it or not. I won't invent what they'll comp. Don't negotiate a discount as your opening move. The opening move is a new sandwich.

If the remake will take a long time, you can ask whether a different, faster item is easier. That's practical, not fussy. If you'd rather leave, you can ask to pay for your friend's fries and walk. Leaving is not a villain arc. Sitting with a covered hair is worse.

Your friend can keep their fries. You don't need them to validate the hair. They didn't see it the same way. That's fine. You still don't swallow it.

Sit facing the room so you can catch the server's eye without a wave that looks like drowning. One finger slightly up is enough in a slammed room.

### User
What do I say if they bring it back and I still don't want to eat here? I'll feel stuck because they already remade it.

### Assistant
You're not stuck. A remake is their second try, not a lock on your jaw. If the room still feels wrong — cold plate energy, hair still in your head — you can eat a few bites or you can say "I appreciate the remake. I think we're going to take the check." Pay for what you owe. I won't guess the bill. Don't ghost the ticket.

If the second sandwich looks fine and you still have appetite, eat. If you don't, a box is allowed, or no box. You don't have to perform gratitude with a full stomach.

Thank the server for handling it. That's manners, not a retraction of the complaint. Then walk. Your friend can finish the fries. You can get a grocery sandwich later if you're still hungry. First time sending food back does not require you to become a regular or an enemy. It requires one true sentence and a plate that leaves the table. You already saw the hair. Trust that. Call the server. Don't eat around it. Don't write a review in your head while they walk over. Handle the plate. Then decide if you still want to be at the window.
