### User
I labeled a container with my name and Tuesday. It was gone at 8 p.m. Alex said they thought it was leftover from the house stir-fry. It was not. It was my lunch for tomorrow. This is the third mix-up in a month. I buy the groceries. They buy sauces. I am done with the word mix-up.

### Assistant
Three times is not a mix-up. A mix-up is once, maybe twice if the fridge is chaos. A name and a day on a lid is as clear as household writing gets.

You do not need a trial about sauces versus groceries. You need the behavior to stop and tomorrow's lunch to exist. Tonight that might mean a cheap replacement meal, not a hunger strike to make a point.

Talk in the kitchen, container in the trash or empty in the sink as a prop if it is still there, not in a passive-aggressive note on the egg carton. "This had my name. I need you not to eat labeled food. If you're hungry, ask or eat your own." If they say mix-up, "Then we need a rule, because this is the third time."

I will not allocate blame on the lease. Food is a norm. If you want a locked box, that is a practical tool, not a moral failure. Some roommates earn a bin.

Do you share all groceries by agreement, or was labeled always supposed to mean hands off?

### User
We said staples are shared, labeled leftovers are not. They keep blurring leftovers into staples. There is still half a stir-fry that is actually shared. They didn't eat that.

### Assistant
They skipped the shared stir-fry and ate the labeled lunch. That is preference, not confusion. You can name that without a TED talk: "The shared stuff was still there. You ate the labeled one. Please don't."

Replace the lunch if you can — frozen, eggs, a store run. Then set a visible rule: a shelf or a bin that is yours. Labels plus a location beat labels on a crowded shelf. If they "cannot see" labels, they can see a bin.

If they get cute about "we're a household," you can agree about milk and still refuse your Tuesday chicken. Household is not a merger of Tupperware.

If this sits inside a larger they-use-your-stuff pattern, you still start with food because it is daily. A later conversation can be about detergent. Stacking every grievance tonight makes them pick the easiest to mock.

Do not dump their sauces. Do not eat their special thing as revenge. Revenge food is still you living in a sitcom.

### User
I'm worried I'll sound like I care more about chicken than the friendship. We otherwise get along. They even unclogged the sink.

### Assistant
Sinks and chicken can both be true. Getting along is why you use a calm sentence instead of a blowup. It is not why you fund their dinner. If they liked the friendship, they can buy a $8 lunch or eat the stir-fry.

For the next ten minutes: say the three-sentence rule, put a bin or a shelf marker in the fridge, feed yourself something tonight, and do not post in a roommate meme page. If they apologize for real, accept it and watch the next week. If they joke mix-up again, you repeat the rule once and then you buy the bin if you have not.

Labeled Tuesday was not a riddle. You are allowed to want your lunch. Mix-up can retire. Ask, bin, eat. The friendship will survive a container. It may not survive you silently restocking someone else's appetite every week.

### User
They offered to Venmo me. I don't actually want the money as much as I want the food to stay put. Do I take it?

### Assistant
Take it if the lunch is gone and you have to buy another. Money is not the whole point, but it covers tonight. Say, "Send it, and please leave labeled stuff alone after this." If they only want to pay so they can keep grazing, the bin still goes in. A transfer is not a permission slip.

If they skip the Venmo, you still eat and you still mark a shelf. Do not stand in the kitchen waiting for an apology performance. The rule is the thing. Mix-up had a good run. It is done.
