title: Talk through a job interview after a career gap

### User
Interview is tomorrow. Eighteen months out of the workforce. Caregiving, then I froze on applications. I can do the job. I know I go quiet when they smile and say "tell us about the gap." Last time I filled the silence with a joke and it died in the room. I do not want a pep talk. I want something I can say that doesn't sound like I'm selling a TED talk about resilience.

### Assistant
Skip resilience. Skip "I used the time to grow." Those lines are why the room goes polite.

A flat version: "I was out for eighteen months for family caregiving. That period ended in [month]. Since then I've been [one concrete thing: a course, contract work, volunteering with a name, even structured job search]. I'm here because this role needs [skill they listed], and that's work I did in [previous job]." Then you stop. Let them ask a follow-up. Silence after a complete answer is not a failure. Filling it with a joke is what made last time worse.

You do not owe them a medical chart or a family tree. Caregiving is enough. If they pry for drama, you can repeat the dates and return to the work. Repeating is allowed. Entertaining is not required.

I will not tell you the market is on fire or that they would be lucky to have you. I do not know that. I know interviews get easier when the gap is a dated fact instead of a mood.

What is the one skill on the posting you can prove with a story that is not about the gap?

### User
Inventory exceptions. I used to reconcile a weekly mismatch report. The posting says "exception handling" like it's a personality. I'm tired. I keep thinking they'll smell the gap on me the second I sit down.

### Assistant
They will see the dates. Dates are not a smell. You walk in, you sit, you treat exception handling as a process you already ran.

Story, tired-coworker length: "Every week the system spat out a mismatch list. I checked counts against receipts, flagged vendor errors versus our scanning errors, and closed the ones that were noise so the warehouse wasn't chasing ghosts. A typical week was a few dozen lines. The ones that mattered were the repeat vendor shorts." If they want a number, use a number you actually remember. Do not inflate. If you do not remember the exact count, say "dozens, not hundreds" rather than inventing 47.

When they smile at the gap, do not smile bigger. Nod, give the dated sentence, pivot to the mismatch report. Matching their cheer is how you get fake. Matching their calm is enough.

If your voice goes quiet, that is fine. Quiet plus a process is better than loud plus a joke. You can even say "I'll keep this short" if you feel yourself speeding up. That is de-escalation for your own nervous system, not a performance of confidence.

Tomorrow morning, do not run a mock interview in the shower that tries to sound inspired. Read the posting once. Write three bullets on paper: gap dates, mismatch report, why this role's exceptions look like that report. Put the paper in your bag. If your mind blanks, those bullets are the whole personality you need.

If they ask "why this company," answer with something specific from the posting or the work, not with how passionate you are to return. Passion is a fog machine. Specific is a chair.

You might not get this one. That is a possible outcome, not a moral. The job of tomorrow is to leave the room having said the dates and one real piece of work. Everything else is weather. Go to bed at a boring hour. Eat something with protein if you can. Wear the outfit you already decided. No new haircut theory at 11 p.m.

When they ask about the gap, you are not on trial for disappearing. You are translating a calendar. Keep the translation short. Then talk about the report. That is the whole de-escalation: fewer feelings in the air, more lines on a mismatch list.
