### User
We have numbered bins in the basement. Mine is 4. I went down for the winter coats and couldn't open it. Someone had stacked three unlabeled tubs on the lid and a broken shop vac against the door of the cage. The super said "that's the building's overflow" and shrugged. The lease sheet I have says bin 4 is assigned to my unit. I don't want a war in a damp basement. I want my coats. How do I do this without inventing a storage statute?

### Assistant
You want the lid back. Overflow is their word for using your square. You do not need a statute to ask for your number.

Photo first, while it is still stacked: lid, tubs, vac, the 4 sticker if it is visible. Then a written note to whoever you already text for repairs: "Bin 4 is assigned to my unit. It is blocked by unlabeled tubs and a shop vac. I need access by [a real day this week]. Please move the overflow off my bin." Short. I will not invent what the lease does in court. I will not invent a fee. The sheet you have is enough to quote as your understanding. Quote it. Do not decorate.

If the super is standing there, you can say the same thing once out loud, then still send the text. Shrugging people forget shrugs. They remember timestamps less often, which is why you make one.

Do not climb the stack. Do not shove a shop vac that is not yours across a wet floor. Injury in a basement is a worse story than delayed coats.

Is the cage locked with your lock, or is it an open cubby with a number?

### User
It's a wire cage with my padlock. I couldn't even get the key in because the vac is wedged against the hasp. Coats are in there plus two boxes of dishes I was going to donate. Super walked away to a boiler. I have a car this afternoon for the donation.

### Assistant
Then the hasp is the immediate object. Your afternoon car is a clock. Text now, not after the boiler friendship: "I need the shop vac moved off bin 4 hasp today. I have a donation pickup window this afternoon. Bin is mine; padlock is mine." If you have an email for the office, copy that. Basement shrugs die in email slower.

If nobody comes in an hour and you can see a way to un-wedge the vac without lifting someone else's tubs onto your back, you can move the vac a foot to open the hasp, photograph the before, and put the vac next to the tubs, not in the aisle if that blocks egress. I am not telling you to handle heavy mystery tubs. I am telling you a vac on a hasp is a wedge. If it feels unsafe, wait. Coats can miss one donation window. Dishes can miss it too. A pulled shoulder in a basement is not a win.

If the unlabeled tubs belong to "the building," the office can own them. Ask who claims them. Unclaimed stacked on your lock is still blocking you.

Do not cut your own padlock out of spite. You will pay for that twice.

### User
Office texted "we'll try to get to it." Afternoon is slipping. I can see the donation center on the map and I can feel myself getting the polite wait I always do.

### Assistant
"We'll try" is cousin to "we'll see." Reply: "I need a time today or permission to shift the vac off my hasp so I can open bin 4. Dishes are sitting for a donation I planned this afternoon." Then you set your own cutoff. If nobody appears by the time you would have left, you either shift the vac if it is light and obvious, or you skip the donation and keep the dishes one more week. Skipping is annoying. It is not you being a difficult tenant. It is a blocked lock.

If you do get in, take the coats and dishes you came for and do not use the moment to rearrange the whole basement as a lesson. Lesson is the email. Access is the bin.

I will not tell you to throw their tubs into the alley. I will not tell you the city will fine them. Keep the photos. If this repeats, the next note is "second time bin 4 blocked" with dates. Repeats are the paper.

This afternoon: one time-ask, one cutoff, hasp, coats if you can, donation if the clock still works. Overflow can live somewhere that is not your lid. Your number is 4. Make them see the number. Then get out of the damp.
