### User
I signed up. They booked me an "induction." I thought that meant someone shows you the locker code. Now the email says a trainer will "build your program" and I'm nauseous. I don't want a program. I wanted a treadmill and to be left alone. I also don't want to invent a knee injury to get out of it. I've got a break and a vending-machine coffee. Be straight.

### Assistant
You can say no to the extras without a medical story. You don't owe them a diagnosis you don't have. "I don't want a program. I want a safety walkthrough and then I'll go on my own" is a complete sentence.

Induction, in gym-speak, often means: here's how the door works, here's what not to do with the racks, and also here's a sales conversation dressed as care. The last part is optional even if they act like it isn't. You're allowed to use the first part and skip the shopping.

If they ask why you don't want a program, you don't have to justify your personality. "I'm not doing personal training right now" is enough. Not "I'm too busy," not "maybe after the wedding," not a fake flare-up. Those excuses become homework they can follow up. A no that stays a no is kinder to future-you.

Are you nervous about looking stupid on the machines, or about being sold, or both?

### User
Both. I freeze if someone watches me squat. I also freeze if someone is holding a tablet of packages. I already paid the membership. I feel like I have to be grateful and do the whole ritual.

### Assistant
You paid to use the place. That's the transaction. Gratitude doesn't mean you accept an upsell. You can be polite and still decline.

For the watching: ask for a short tour of the cardio area and how to rack weights so you don't pin yourself. Then say, "I'm going to try this on my own today." If they hover, "I learn better without an audience. Thanks." That's not rude. That's how you don't freeze.

For the tablet: when the packages come out, you interrupt early. "I'm not buying training. Can we finish the safety stuff." If they keep pitching, you repeat the same no. You do not invent a reason. "No" is the reason. If they ask you to think about it, "I already decided. It's a no." Tired-coworker tone. Flat. Not a debate club.

You can also ask, before you arrive, whether the induction is required for your key fob or just recommended. I won't pretend I know your gym's policy. Policies differ. If they say you must complete it to get access, complete the safety piece and still refuse the program. Required walkthrough is not the same as required trainer. If they won't turn on your fob without a sales sit-down, that's a gym problem, not a you-being-ungrateful problem. You can ask to speak to whoever actually controls access.

Don't skip the parts that keep you safe: where emergency stops are, how to unrack, where the water fountain is, what happens if you feel awful. Nervous beginners get hurt when they avoid all human contact and then copy a stranger's ego lift. A ten-minute how-to is useful. A twelve-week package is a different product.

### User
What do I say if they do the "just try one session complimentary" thing. That always works on me because free feels like I already agreed.

### Assistant
Free is still a yes. If you don't want it, you treat complimentary like a coupon for a store you aren't entering.

"I'm not doing a session, paid or free." If your mouth wants to add "maybe later," don't. Later is how they get a calendar hold. You can change your mind in a month without leaving them a hook today.

If they look disappointed, that's their job face. You don't have to fix it. You're not their quota. You're a person who wanted a treadmill.

Day-of plan: show up, do the lockers and the safety, say the no once, repeat it if needed, then go walk or pedal where you can see the room without performing. If you want a machine explained, pick one, ask one question, stop. You don't have to fill the hour they blocked. Their calendar is not your obligation.

You can be a nervous beginner and still be someone who says no without a costume injury. Those two things stack. The coffee can go cold. The sentence can't. Practice it once in the parking lot: "I don't want a program. Show me the basics and I'll take it from there." Then go inside and use it.
