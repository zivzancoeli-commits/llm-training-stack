### User
I opened the shared profile at 9:05 to finish a show. The continue-watching row had a title card with the dead character's name in the episode title. I hadn't gotten there. My sister watches on the TV in the living room. I watch on a laptop in my room. We said we'd stay two episodes apart. She is three ahead and she left it sitting on the home screen like a billboard. I'm mad in a small, stupid way.

### Assistant
It isn't stupid. You had a rule. The home screen broke it without anyone in the room. That's a real annoyance even if nobody died in your actual apartment.

Don't storm into the living room with the laptop. You'll sound like a cop of fiction. Text or walk in with one sentence: "The title card spoiled me. Can we use separate profiles?" That's the fix. Two episodes apart was a hope. Profiles are a wall.

I won't invent what the app charges for extra users. I don't know your plan. If you can't add a profile, you can still stop using continue watching as a shared billboard.

Are you on one profile because of the plan, or because nobody bothered to click Add Profile?

### User
Nobody bothered. Mom's email is the owner. There are already four profiles of cartoon avatars nobody uses. I could make one. I didn't because we liked talking about it at breakfast. She texted "oops" with a crying emoji. The episode title is still sitting there. I can see it from the hallway.

### Assistant
Make the profile. Breakfast talk can survive separate rows. It cannot survive a corpse in a thumbnail.

For the next ten minutes: on the TV or the laptop, add a profile with your name, switch to it, open the show from the series page instead of continue watching, and skip the row that still has the spoiler. Then reply to "oops" with: "Making my own profile. Don't leave that title on the TV." Short. Don't prosecute the emoji.

If Mom's email needs a PIN to add a profile, ask her. That's a two-minute kitchen conversation, not a trial. Unused cartoon avatars can stay. They're not the problem. The shared brain of one profile is the problem.

You can still talk at breakfast. You talk about what you both have seen, not about what her Tuesday night dumped on the tile of the home screen.

If she leaves the TV parked on her episode, go to your profile before the TV wakes in the morning. That's a habit, not a grudge.

### User
She wants to keep one profile so the algorithm "stays smart." Also I feel petty for caring about a TV show on a weeknight.

### Assistant
The algorithm is not a roommate. It does not need a group hug. Separate profiles make two mediocre algorithms, which is fine. I won't pretend I know how their recommendation engine works. I can tell you a smart row that spoils you is not a gift.

Petty is a word people use to talk themselves out of a small clear preference. You wanted to meet a character alive. That's ordinary. You don't need a philosophy of art. You need a profile with your name on it.

If she insists on one profile, you can still refuse the TV as your launcher. Laptop, your list, don't open the app in the living room until you've passed her episode. That's clunky. It's also optional. The clean version is still: new profile tonight.

Go add it. Change the continue-watching landscape. Text the one line. Eat cereal tomorrow and talk about a scene you both actually saw. The dead name on the tile can stay on her row. It doesn't have to greet you at 9:05 like a billboard you didn't buy. If the TV is still on her profile when you pass the hallway, switch it before you sit down. That's a ten-second habit, not a fight. You had a two-episode rule. A named profile is how the rule survives a weeknight.
