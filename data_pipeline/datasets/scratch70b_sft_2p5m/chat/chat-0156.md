### User
New job. There's a coffee machine and apparently a whole unspoken system. Yesterday I made a cup and someone muttered about "the good beans" and "guest mugs." Last-year-me would have either over-apologized or pretended I already knew. I want two options for how to handle tomorrow morning, and which one you'd pick. I still want coffee.

### Assistant
You can still want coffee. The machine is not a throne.

I need last-year-you's yes: do you want help navigating this, or do you want me to tell you to never drink the office coffee? Because those are different mornings.

### User
I want the coffee. I just don't want to be the person who ruined a ritual I can't see. There are two bags, a handwritten sign I didn't read because I was nervous, and mugs on a shelf that might be personal.

### Assistant
Read the sign tomorrow. That's not politics. That's literacy. Last-year-you skipped signs and then wrote a story about being hated.

Two options.

Option A: you bring your own beans or your own pods if the machine takes them, plus your own mug from day one. You use the machine as a hot-water-and-pressure tool, not as a grocery. You wash your mug. You don't touch the labeled bags. If someone says "you can use the good beans," then you can. Until then, you don't.

Option B: you use the shared stuff, but you ask one human a boring question first. Not the mutterer in the moment of shame. Someone kind-looking, or whoever does office-manager energy: "What's the rule on the coffee bags and the mugs?" Then you follow the actual rule. If the sign says "staff beans, guest beans," you use staff. If mugs have names, you don't take a name. If there's a jar for coffee money, you put in what the sign says when you can — I won't invent the amount.

I'd pick B if you want to be in the communal pot and you're willing to ask one question. I'd pick A if the vibe is precious and you don't want to play. Both get you caffeine. A is cleaner. B is how you learn the ritual without telepathy.

Last-year-you's trap is apologizing for existing in the kitchen. Don't. "Oh sorry, is this the good bag?" mid-pour, five times, makes you the main event. Ask once, then act.

Guest mugs: usually means extra mugs for visitors, or mugs that are not yours. If a mug is chipped and blank, it might be communal. If it says Dana, it's Dana's. Don't do a personality test on the shelf. Names win.

### User
The mutterer is on my team. I have to see them at 10. What do I say if they bring it up. Also I don't know if I'm allowed to be in the kitchen that early.

### Assistant
Kitchen early: if people are already in there making coffee, you're allowed to be a person with a cup. If the kitchen is locked, that's a different building rule I can't see. Don't sneak.

If they bring it up at 10: "I didn't know the bag system. I'll follow the sign. Sorry I grabbed the wrong one yesterday." Then stop. Don't add a comedy routine. Don't ask them to mentor your espresso journey unless they offer.

If they didn't say it to you directly yesterday, you don't have to open a meeting with coffee. Last-year-you would. This-year-you can let a mutter die.

Two options still stand in that meeting: A, "I'm bringing my own from now on so I don't mix bags." B, "I'll use the staff bag and my own mug." Recommendation stays B if the sign allows staff coffee for employees, which a staff bag usually does. You're staff now. That's the point of the job.

Don't buy a fancy machine for your desk to avoid humans unless you already wanted that. That's a big swing for a mutter.

Don't invent a rule like "third-years get the good beans." If nobody said it, it isn't policy. Ask, or read.

Wash what you use. Empty the grounds if that's the posted last step. That's 90 percent of kitchen peace. The other 10 percent is not taking Dana's mug.

You're not ruined. You're new. New people read signs on day two that they panicked past on day one. Get the coffee, own mug, ask one question if you're doing B, short sorry only if you actually used the wrong bag. That's the politics: it's mostly dishes and labels, not a secret court.
