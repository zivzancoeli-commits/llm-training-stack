### User
I'm at my cousin's wedding. Dinner just started. The best man just sat down without a toast, and my aunt leaned over and said, "You're funny, you do it." There is no mic yet but there will be in like two minutes. I have nothing written. I like the couple. I do not want to do a roast. Help me not freeze.

### Assistant
You can do a short toast without pretending you planned this. Two minutes is enough for a structure, not a speech. Grab a napkin or your notes app and write four lines, nothing else: who you are in one clause, one real thing about the couple, one wish, and a raise-your-glass closer. Do not invent a childhood story you do not have. Do not try to be the funniest person in the room.

Skip alcohol jokes, exes, and anything that starts with "this is going to be long." If you blank, look at the couple, not the room.

Are you seated at a family table or closer to the wedding party? That changes how loud you need to be and whether you wait for a real mic.

### User
Family table, like eight people. The DJ already handed a mic to my uncle who is walking it over to me. My cousin is looking at me like this is a gift. I have a half glass of wine. I don't even know if I stand.

### Assistant
Stand. Sitting makes you look like you are muttering into a salad. Stay at your chair, do not walk to the dance floor, and hold the mic below your mouth so you do not pop it. Put the wine down first. A toast with a shaking glass is a spill, not a moment.

Say this, almost verbatim, then stop:

"Hi, I'm [name], [cousin's] cousin. I did not know I was doing this until dinner, so this will be short. Watching you two tonight, the thing I keep noticing is how you check on each other without making a show of it. That is a good way to be married. I hope you keep that, and I hope the rest of us get to keep being around it. To [names]."

That is not poetry. It is specific enough that it does not sound like a template, and it does not require a punchline. If your voice shakes, slower is better than louder.

If your uncle tries to add a "and tell the story about," smile and say, "I'm keeping it short," then raise the glass. You are not obligated to open the floor.

I do not know this room's culture. Some families clap, some wait for the DJ. After the last line, raise, sip, sit. Let the DJ take the mic back. Do not remain standing for comments.

### User
What if I cry? I cry at commercials. And what if someone yells "speak up" because I go quiet.

### Assistant
Crying is allowed. Pause, breathe out, finish the wish sentence. People forgive a cousin who likes the couple. They do not forgive a recovery bit about how awkward you are. Do not apologize for crying. Do not joke "I'm a mess." Just continue from the next word you already wrote.

If someone yells "speak up," lift the mic an inch and repeat only the last sentence, not the whole toast. Repeating from the top makes it a performance.

For the next ten minutes: write the four lines on your phone in huge type, stand when the mic arrives, put the glass down, say the paragraph, raise, sit, text yourself "done" so your brain stops rewriting it. After you sit, eat something. Low blood sugar makes the shakes worse.

You were ambushed. A short, true toast is the honest version of that. You do not owe them a TED talk because an aunt decided you were funny. Funny can wait for the afterparty when nobody is holding a microphone.

### User
Okay. I wrote it. I used their dog's name in the "check on each other" line because they actually do that. Is that too cute?

### Assistant
A dog is fine if it is true and it stays one clause. "You two even take turns checking on [dog] like a shift change" is a detail, not a bit. Do not do a voice. Do not pretend to be the dog. One image, then the wish, then the glass.

If you feel the cute sliding into stand-up, cut the dog and keep "check on each other." Either version works. Uncertainty here is normal; you will not know which line lands until you say it, and that is alright.

When the mic hits your hand, you already have the job: stand, short, true, sit. That is the whole assignment.
