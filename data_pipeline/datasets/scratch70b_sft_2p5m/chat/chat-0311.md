### User
We had a group chat of four. I wrote something unkind about Mara, not even the worst thing in the thread, but mine. Someone screenshot it. Mara texted me "wow okay" with the image. I don't know who sent it. The other three are quiet. I feel sick. I want to explain context and I also know context will sound like I'm suing the screenshot.

### Assistant
Do not hunt the leaker first. Do not open with context. Mara has the picture. She does not have your internal footnote.

Reply to Mara, not to the group, and keep it small: "That's me. I'm sorry I said that. I shouldn't have. If you want to talk, I can. If you don't, I understand." Then stop. No "it was a joke." No "we all were." No "who sent this." Those are for later or never.

I will not tell you this is legally anything. It is a trust bomb. Treat it as that.

Are you still in shared spaces with her this week — work, a class, a dinner — or is this mostly phones?

### User
There's a birthday on Saturday she might still come to. We work in the same office on Tuesdays. The group chat is now a graveyard. One person reacted with a shocked emoji on Mara's message to me, which I think was a mistake.

### Assistant
Mute the group or leave it. A graveyard with a shocked emoji is not a repair space. Tell the three, separately if needed: "I'm dealing with Mara directly. Don't pile on in the chat." You do not need to run an investigation. If someone confesses, you still do not make them the main character.

Saturday: do not campaign for her to come so you can feel forgiven in public. If you are the host, a private "you're welcome, no pressure" is enough. If you are not, show up, be civil, do not corner her by the cake. Tuesdays: do the work, greet like an adult, do not do a desk apology in front of other people unless she starts it.

Context can wait until she asks. If she asks why, you can be honest without a defense: you were being unkind, you were showing off for the chat, you were annoyed about X if X is real and not petty invention. If X is petty, skip X. "I was being mean" is a complete why.

Do not write a longer apology in the group so the screenshot has a sequel. Do not post a vague story about "learning and growing."

If she wants distance, you give distance. That may last past Saturday. I cannot promise a tidy arc.

### User
Part of me wants to say the screenshot was a violation too. It was. I also know if I lead with that I look like the problem is being caught.

### Assistant
The screenshot was a violation of the chat's assumed privacy. Leading with it still sounds like the problem is being caught. You can believe both. In the first message to Mara, you do not collect that debt. Later, with the group, you can say "we don't screenshot each other" as a new rule if the group still exists. Two conversations. Different audiences.

For the next ten minutes: send Mara the short apology, mute the group, do not text "who did this," and write down what you will not say on Saturday — jokes about it, a toast, a recap. Drink water. If you need to tell one outside person so you do not explode, pick someone not in the four and not in the office.

You wrote the unkind thing. Someone else forwarded it. You only get to own your line. Own it in one paragraph, then do the boring Tuesday. The sick feeling is information. Let it make you quieter in chats, not a detective.
