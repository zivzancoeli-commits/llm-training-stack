### User
It's 5:50 p.m. Linh dropped a 40-page deck plus a Google Doc in Slack: "quick eyes before the 9 a.m.?" I have not looked at this project in three weeks. I already had plans to leave at 6. They're a peer. They said "you're the only one who gets the numbers." I don't want to be the person who always saves the night. I also don't want to be the person who lets a bad number walk into a room with my name in the footer. How do I answer in the next ten minutes?

### Assistant
You don't review 40 pages. You choose a smaller job or you decline.

Reply soon so they can still ask someone else. "I can't do a full review tonight. I can do a 20-minute pass on the numbers slides if you point me at them, or I can look tomorrow after 9." That's a fork. If they need a full read, they need a different body.

Don't accept "you're the only one" as physics. It's flattery with a deadline attached. If you are actually the only one who knows a specific table, you can say, "I'll check table 4 and the revenue chart. Not the whole deck." Then you set a stop time. 20 minutes. Timer. You leave.

I won't invent whether your job description requires after-hours review. I don't know your team. I won't invent what the 9 a.m. "must" include. You know if this is a real meeting or a slide habit.

Do you even know which pages they think are the numbers, or is it the whole pile?

### User
They said "especially 12–18 and the appendix." That's still a lot. I opened page 12. There's a chart that looks off by a factor of ten if I'm remembering last month. I feel the hook. If I start I'll be here at 10.

### Assistant
Write that down and don't chase it into the appendix.

Slack: "I can't do 12–18 plus appendix tonight. I glanced at 12 and the chart looks off versus last month — can you verify the units before 9? I'm offline after 6:15. If you need a full review, you'll have to pull someone else or slip the meeting." Then you actually go offline.

If the factor-of-ten thing is real, naming it is the useful part. You don't have to rebuild the workbook at night to have been useful. They can check units. That's their deck.

If they reply "please just 30 min," you can repeat: "I don't have it. I'm out." You don't owe a fake dentist. Last year's you invented a dentist. This year's you can have a calendar that ends.

Don't secretly keep working after you said 6:15. That's how you train them that your no is a loading screen.

### User
They replied "ok I'll flag you in the meeting if something's wrong." That feels like a trap for tomorrow. Should I still send the one chart note?

### Assistant
You already have the chart note if you send the Slack I wrote. That's enough. You don't need a second essay. Tomorrow, if they flag you, you can say, "I didn't review the deck. I flagged page 12 last night. I don't have the rest." That's honest. It may feel awkward. Awkward is cheaper than a footer that implies you blessed 40 pages.

If you're scared of the meeting, tell your manager in one line in the morning, not a midnight CYA: "I didn't have time to review Linh's deck; I noted a possible units issue on 12." That's optional and only if your culture actually uses managers for that. I won't pretend I know.

Next ten minutes: send the fork, name page 12, close the laptop, leave. You are not the night shift for a peer's 9 a.m. You are a person who glanced, warned, and went home. That's a complete review of the situation, even if it isn't a complete review of the doc.
