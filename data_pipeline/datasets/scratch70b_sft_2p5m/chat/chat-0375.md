### User
An ice cream truck has been looping my block for fifty minutes. Same six-second tune. I work from home. I have a call at :00. It is :48. The truck is not parked. It is hunting. Kids on the sidewalk seem done. I don't know if I should go downstairs and ask them to stop, call a noise number, or put the call on mute and suffer. I feel unhinged for hating a children's truck. I still have a client.

### Assistant
You can hate a loop without hating children. The tune is a speaker. Fifty minutes is a pattern, not a birthday.

If the truck is passing now, a downstairs ask is the fastest: "Hey, the loop on this block has been an hour. Could you park or take the next street? I have a work call." They may say they are allowed. Then you know. They may move. Then you win twelve minutes.

If you cannot leave the desk, put on headphones now, not at :00. A noise number can wait until after the client unless you already know that number works in minutes, which it often does not.

I will not invent whether ice cream trucks are permitted to loop. Ask the city line later if this is daily. Today is the call.

Is the truck actually in front of your building as you type, or two corners away?

### User
It just passed. I can hear it two houses down, turning. Headphones help until it is under the window. Client is picky about background. I have two minutes.

### Assistant
Headphones. Mute when the truck is under the window. A one-line chat to the client if you must: "Ice cream truck outside, one second." Then you are not mysteriously glitchy. Picky clients prefer a named noise to a mystery.

After the call, if the loop continues, go down. One sentence. Do not lecture about childhood. Do not film for the neighborhood page unless you want a different fight.

If you work from home often, a cheap white-noise machine or a fan is a boring investment for a summer of trucks. It should not have to be. It might be.

Do not bang on the truck. Do not block it with your body. Ask, then go back up.

The kids being "done" is useful information you can mention: "Kids have gone in — the loop is just the speaker now." A driver who is hunting may not notice the sidewalk emptied. You noticed. Tell them.

### User
Call starts in forty seconds. Truck is rounding. I didn't go down.

### Assistant
Stay. Mute, headphones, optional one-liner in chat. You cannot win the block in forty seconds.

After you hang up, decide if the hour is still going. If yes, downstairs then. If the truck is gone, you still can call the non-emergency noise line and ask what they do about looping vendors, for the next time, not as revenge.

You are not unhinged. You have a client and a speaker cone on wheels. Handle the client first. That was always the order. The truck can take the next street later. Your mute button is the tool that exists in this minute. Use it. Then, if you need to, use the sidewalk. Two different minutes. Don't mix them. Go on mute. Say hello to the client. Let the tune be a problem for :30, not for :00.

### User
We're on the call. They asked if I have a dog. The truck is under the window. I muted. I need a sentence for when I unmute.

### Assistant
Unmute with the named noise if they heard it: "Ice cream truck looping — I'm here." Then answer the dog question. Do not apologize for a minute. Do not chase the truck with the laptop.

If they didn't hear it, you can skip the confession and just talk. Mute was the whole intervention. After hangup, if the loop is still going, then the sidewalk sentence. Not during the dog question. Client first, still. You are not unhinged. You are mid-call with a speaker on wheels. Stay in the call. The six-second tune can wait until you are allowed to have a body on the sidewalk. Forty seconds ago you chose mute. Keep choosing it until goodbye. Then go down if you still need to. One job at a time. This job is the client.
