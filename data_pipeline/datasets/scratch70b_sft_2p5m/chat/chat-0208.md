### User
Interview Thursday. Fourteen months off the resume. I was taking care of my dad after his surgery and then the job hunt went slow. I can hear the interviewer already: so what have you been doing. I keep writing answers that sound like a commercial for resilience. My uncle told me to "just be positive" which made me want to walk into traffic. I do not need a pep rally. I need to talk about the gap without groveling and without fake sunshine.

### Assistant
Skip the commercial. Fourteen months is a fact, not a personality. Answer the question: unpaid caregiving, then a job search. Full stop. You do not have to sell soul growth. You do not have to grin.

A calm version: I left paid work to help my dad after surgery. That ran longer than we expected. I am looking for full-time work now, and this role matches [one real thing in the posting]. Then you stop. Stopping is what keeps it from becoming a TED talk.

Do not say I am so excited to be back. If you are tired, excited is a costume. You can say I want to get back to [the actual work]. Want is allowed. Sparkle is usually fake.

If they look uneasy, get specific, not brighter. Dates, whose care, that you are available now, one example of a skill you kept if you have one — a spreadsheet, a certification you actually finished. If you have none, do not invent a side hustle. Empty months happen. Lying about a freelance brand is worse than a gap.

I will not diagnose your dad and I will not invent a legal protection. "Surgery and recovery" is enough unless they ask something you are willing to answer.

What kind of job is Thursday, and did you leave a hole on the resume or a labeled line?

### User
Operations coordinator, warehouse-adjacent office. Resume has the last job end date and then nothing. I did keep the family calendar, insurance calls, and a shared budget spreadsheet, which my uncle says "doesn't count." I got angry in a practice interview when the person said "enjoy your break." It was not a break.

### Assistant
Your uncle is wrong about the spreadsheet if you can describe it like work: calendar, follow-ups, a budget that had to stay accurate. That is operations. It is not a warehouse system. Do not pretend it is. Do not throw it away. One sentence: I ran household operations — scheduling, follow-ups, and a budget — and I am ready to point that at a team calendar and a queue. Then you move to the posting. You do not need their blessing on whether caregiving "counts."

"Enjoy your break" is a stupid line. You do not have to laugh it off and you do not have to explode. De-escalate without cheer: It was not a break. I was caregiving. I am here because I want this job. Then ask a work question so the room leaves the picnic. How they handle same-day callouts. What the first thirty days look like. Questions stop you performing gratitude.

Practice the anger down to one clause. Pause, water, finish the clause. Do not give a speech about society.

Thursday is late to rebuild the PDF, but name the gap in the room: caregiving, then job search. I would rather you say it than hope they miss the dates. They notice dates.

### User
What if they ask if I will disappear again if he has another surgery. That question makes me want to lie and say everything is fine forever. Also I freeze when they ask salary.

### Assistant
Do not promise forever. Forever is how you get trapped. A straight answer: I cannot predict health. I have backup now that I did not have last year, and I would handle time off the way anyone here does — request it, document it, not vanish. If you do not have backup, do not invent a cousin. Say I would use the company's leave process and keep my manager informed. I am not going to quote a law. Ask HR after an offer.

Salary: do not fill silence with a number you made up in the car. Say I want to hear the range for this role. If they push, I need to see the full offer. I will not invent a market rate for your city.

Thursday: gap in one breath, not a break, one operations sentence, one question about the job, no sparkle. If your uncle texts "smile more," mute the thread until Friday. You are not auditioning for a commercial.
