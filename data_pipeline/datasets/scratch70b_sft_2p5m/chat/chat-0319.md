### User
I borrowed my neighbor's hatchback for "one hour" to grab a pickup order. The app said ready. I've been in this parking lot for 50 minutes. The order is still "being prepared." My neighbor texts "all good?" I have their keys and their insurance card in the visor. I feel like I stole a car with a sofa in the future tense.

### Assistant
Text the truth now: "Still waiting, I'll bring it back as soon as I have the stuff or I'll abort if this keeps going. Sorry." Do not say all good. All good is how you spend their second hour in silence.

Go inside or to the pickup desk and ask for a real time, not the app's mood. If they cannot give a number, you decide a cutoff. The neighbor lent you an hour, not an afternoon.

I will not invent this store's pickup rules or whether you can cancel without penalty. Ask them what happens if you leave. Then believe the human, not the spinner.

Is the neighbor on a clock — work, a kid, a trip — or was the hour just a polite default?

### User
They have a dentist at 3. It is 1:50. Drive is 20 minutes if traffic is kind. The sofa is the last piece of a move. I already returned a lamp I was going to stack on it because the lot is a zoo.

### Assistant
Dentist at 3 is the real deadline, not the sofa. Back the car by about 2:25 if you want them unstressed. That means you leave this lot with or without furniture soon.

Tell pickup: "I have to go in twenty minutes. Is it actually coming, or should I cancel?" If they say ten minutes and you believe them, wait ten, not twenty-five. If they shrug, cancel or reschedule and drive the car home. A move can slip a day. A missed dentist is a worse neighbor story.

Do not try to be a hero by keeping the car through 3. Do not send a friend to return it unless the neighbor agreed. Keys in your pocket are your job.

Park legally while you wait. Do not idle in a fire lane because the app pinged. If you need to move the car in the lot, do it. A borrowed car in a tow zone is a nightmare you do not need.

If you abort, take a photo of the empty hatch when you return it, not because you distrust them, because you want a clean handoff. Tell them thank you and that you misread the ready status. Then you can be a person who still needs a sofa, later, with a better window or a rented van.

### User
If I come back empty I will feel like I wasted their afternoon and my move. I also don't want to speed to make the dentist.

### Assistant
Do not speed. Empty and on time is a successful borrow. Full and late is a failed borrow that also has a sofa. The feeling of waste is real and not a reason to gamble their appointment.

For the next ten minutes: message the dentist deadline to yourself as a clock, talk to pickup with a twenty-minute line, text the neighbor the same line, and if the sofa is not in the car with margin for a calm drive, leave. Return the keys, tell them sorry, sit in your own place and rebook pickup for a day you have a vehicle for more than an hour.

IKEA ready is a suggestion. A dentist is a time. Treat the neighbor's car like a library book with a stamp. Bring it back. The sofa can wait in a warehouse. Your reputation in the hallway cannot. If pickup suddenly appears at 2:10, you still leave; a sofa in a hatchback is not worth a neighbor sprinting to the dentist. Rebook. Apologize once. Do not narrate the warehouse like a tragedy.
