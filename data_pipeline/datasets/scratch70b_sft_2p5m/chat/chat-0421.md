### User
There's a half-court behind my building. Lights stay on. Kids and some adults play until 12:30, sometimes later. My bedroom faces it. I moved here in winter when the court was empty. Now it is summer. I like that people have a place to play. I also have to sleep. I don't want to be the neighbor who hates kids. I need a path: city, building, a sign, or earplugs forever.

### Assistant
You can like a court and still need a bedroom. Those can both be true.

Earplugs are a tonight tool, not a civic plan. Use them. Move the bed if an inner wall is quieter. That is this week.

For a path: start with what you can see. Are the lights on a timer you could ask the city or the parks number to adjust? Lights at 12:30 are an invitation. A 10 p.m. cutoff, if they will do it, changes the game without a speech about children.

I will not invent a noise ordinance hour. If your town posts one, you can cite the posted hour. If not, you still can ask for a lights schedule.

Building management may own the lot or not. Ask. If it is city, they need the city. A sign you tape yourself will be torn down and will make you the villain faster than a request through a channel.

Have you talked to anyone who plays, or only to your ceiling?

### User
Only the ceiling. There is a guy I see with a folding chair who seems like a regular, not a kid. The hoop looks city-installed. Management said "that's the park" and shrugged. I work at 7.

### Assistant
Management shrugged is useful: they are telling you it is not their hoop. Go to the parks or 311-style number with dates and times: "Half-court behind [address], play and lights past midnight, bedroom windows, asking about light timer or posted hours." Boring. Several nights logged is better than one angry night.

The folding-chair regular can be a human ask in daylight: "I'm in the building. Any chance the late games can wrap by 10 on weeknights?" He may not own the court. He may set a tone. Do not lead with kids. Lead with weekday sleep.

If teens are there at midnight, you still do not scold children in the dark. That goes badly. Daylight, or the city, or both.

I will not promise lights will go off. Some parks are 24-hour by neglect. A ticket number still helps you feel less crazy.

### User
If I call, I become the person who tried to shut down the only hoop. That story travels. I already feel like winter-me was naive.

### Assistant
Winter-me toured a quiet lot. That is not a crime. The story that travels can be "they asked for a timer," not "they hate basketball." You do not have to campaign for removal of the hoop. Ask for hours and lights. Keep the hoop.

Tonight: earplugs, maybe the couch if it is quieter. This week: two dated notes, one parks request, one daylight sentence to the chair guy if you see him. You can still like that people play. You can still sleep before a 7 a.m. job. Those are allowed in the same person. Make the boring ask. Leave the villain story to people who need a villain. You need a timer.

### User
It's 11:50 and a game just started. Earplugs are in. I still want to go downstairs and tell them to wrap it. Work is at 7.

### Assistant
Do not go downstairs at 11:50 to scold a game in the dark. That is how you become the story and still do not sleep. Tonight is plugs, maybe the couch, maybe a fan. Tomorrow is daylight with the chair guy if you see him, and a dated note to parks about lights past midnight. You can like the hoop in daylight. You can refuse a midnight sermon. 7 a.m. is closer if you stay in the building. Put the phone down so you do not watch the court like a job. The timer ask is a weekday office task. The basketball can wait until you have a ticket number, not a confrontation in sneakers. Stay in. Sleep what you can. Ask tomorrow in the sun.
