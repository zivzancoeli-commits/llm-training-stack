### User
We rented this place in March. The listing said the tracks were "rarely used at night." It's 12:50 a.m. and a freight train is doing that long horn-and-rattle thing. It's the third night this week. I have a video from the bedroom window with the clock on my phone. My partner wants to email the listing agent like that's a court. I want to sleep and also not feel stupid. What do we do tonight versus what we do as actual tenants, without me inventing railroad law?

### Assistant
Tonight you sleep. Tomorrow you write facts. You don't email at 12:50 like a closing argument.

Close the window if it's open. Put the phone on do-not-disturb after you take one more timestamped video if you want the file. Earplugs, fan, other room if it's better. You will not win against a train at 1 a.m. I won't tell you the horn is illegal. I don't know your crossing rules. I won't invent how often "rarely" means. You have three nights this week. That's your data.

The listing sentence is useful later. Save the listing if you still have it. Screenshot. Don't threaten the agent in a midnight paragraph. Agents sleep too, and rage mail gets a template.

If this is about whether you stay, that's a daytime conversation with your partner about tolerance, not a train debate. If this is about asking a landlord or railroad contact what changed, that's also daytime.

Are you month-to-month, or stuck until a date, and do you even want to leave?

### User
Lease until January. Partner doesn't want to leave. They want someone to admit the listing was sloppy. I don't care about the admission. I care about 6 a.m. I work early. There's a crossing two blocks south. I can see the lights from bed.

### Assistant
Then the goal is mitigation and a paper trail, not a confession from an agent.

Tomorrow, one email to whoever you actually rent from — landlord, not necessarily the listing person — with dates and times of night trains this week, the listing quote, and a question: "Has the night traffic changed? Is there anything on the building side for windows or insulation, or a contact for the crossing?" You are asking. You are not accusing them of a crime. If they say they don't control trains, that's probably true. You still asked about windows.

I won't invent whether you can break a lease over a listing adjective. Don't write that you can. If you want a legal read, that's a person you hire, in daylight, with your documents. Not me, not at 12:50.

You can also try practical stuff: heavier curtains, a white-noise machine, moving the bed wall if that's possible. Cheap experiments before a personality of victimhood. If the horn is the crossing, some places have a process to ask about quiet hours. I don't know if yours does. You can search the railroad name plus the crossing in the morning, not tonight.

Partner can want an admission. You can let them send a calm note to the agent: "The listing said rarely used at night; we're getting multiple night trains. Was that based on something?" If the agent shrugs, you have a shrug. You still have a lease until January. Plan sleep inside that box unless you both decide to pay for a different box.

### User
I'll take the video and sleep on the couch tonight. In the morning, one email. Should I cc the agent and the landlord together to look serious?

### Assistant
I'd start with the landlord only. CC'ing everyone to "look serious" often looks like a scene. If the landlord says talk to the agent, then you add the agent. One thread, facts, times, listing quote, questions. No "we will pursue options." That's a TV sentence.

Tonight: couch, earplugs, don't draft. The train will pass. It always does. You were told rare. You're collecting frequency. That's not stupid. That's an adjective meeting a Tuesday. Get the file, get the couch, send the boring email after coffee. You don't need railroad law to want 6 a.m. to exist.
