### User
We have a shared backyard. The woman in 1A lets a beagle out and goes back inside. The dog uses the strip along the fence. She comes back later with a coffee, not a bag. There are three piles this week. I stepped in one in running shoes. I already put a scoop and bags on a hook by the door. They haven't moved. I don't want to be the building's feces warden. I also don't want to track this into my kitchen. She's on the stoop now with the coffee. I have to walk past her to get to the alley. First sentence?

### Assistant
Name the yard and the bag, not her soul.

"Hey — the beagle's been leaving piles along the fence. Can you bag them when you let him out? There's bags on the hook." That's the sentence. If you're walking, you can say it without stopping for a trial. You don't need a joke. You don't need "I stepped in it" unless you want one piece of evidence. The shoes can stay out of it if you want less heat. The three piles are enough.

I will not invent a city ordinance or a fine. I won't tell you what the lease "must" say about pets. You can still ask a neighbor to pick up in a shared yard. If she says it isn't hers, you don't play detective in the stoop. "Whoever it is, it's in the shared strip. I'm asking you because I've seen the beagle there." If she denies, you go to the super with dates and, if you want, a photo of the pile, not of her face.

Don't kick the pile. Don't put it on her mat. That's a feud.

If you don't feel safe doing the stoop talk, skip it and write the super. Coffee plus a walk-past is optional, not mandatory bravery.

Is the yard actually shared in the lease, or is it "everyone uses it" unofficially?

### User
Lease says shared rear yard. Super is slow. She's looking at me. I said nothing yet. The running shoe is in a grocery bag in my hand like an exhibit. That's too much, right?

### Assistant
Don't lead with the grocery bag. That's theater. Hide the shoe behind your leg or put it down inside the door if you can still turn around. If you're already committed to the walk-past, hands at your side, sentence, keep going to the alley if that's where you were headed.

If she laughs, you don't laugh. "I'm serious. Bags are on the hook." Then you're done for this pass. One ask.

If she says the dog is "just marking," that's still a pile. "Please still bag it." You don't need a biology debate.

After the alley, you can rinse the shoe. I won't tell you a product. You know soap. Don't bring the exhibit back out.

Tonight, if the piles are still there, you can bag them yourself once if you can't stand the yard, or you can leave them for the person you asked. Doing it yourself once is not a lifetime job unless you let it be. If you do it, you can still email the super: asked 1A, hook has bags, still piles, shared yard per lease. Slow super plus a paper trail is how this becomes a building problem.

### User
She said "it's fertilizer, relax." I have to come back through the same stoop in five minutes. Do I repeat?

### Assistant
Don't repeat the whole speech. If you say anything: "It's not fertilizer I want on the path. Please bag it." Then inside. If she says relax again, you don't. You email. Repeating on the stoop is how you get a character in her story.

You asked. She declined with a joke. That's the information. Super, date, lease line about the yard, bags on the hook, "fertilizer" if you want the quote. No manifesto.

Next five minutes: alley errand, return, maybe the one short line, then door. Wash the shoe. You're not the feces warden if you hand it to the building. You were a person who asked once. Get off the stoop. The beagle can still have a yard. The coffee can come with a bag. That's the whole shared-yard talk, without a science lesson on fertilizer.
