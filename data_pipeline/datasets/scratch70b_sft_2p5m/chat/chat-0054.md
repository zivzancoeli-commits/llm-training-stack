### User
I need flowers in the next ten minutes. Grocery store, not a fancy shop. I do not know names. It is for my roommate because I was a jerk about the dishes and I want the apology to look like I tried, not like I grabbed the nearest dying roses. Talk to me like I am last-year me, the one who would have bought a sad mixed bunch and written sorry on a receipt.

### Assistant
Okay, last-year you. Stop scrolling names. You do not need names. You need a color, a smell check, and a yes from the person at the floral counter if there is one. Ten-minute plan, narrated like you are already in the store.

Minute one: walk to the floral case, not the bakery, not the cards. Cards make this a performance. Flowers plus a spoken sorry is enough.

Minute two: skip tired roses. Last-year you would grab them because the word rose feels like an apology. If they look tired now, they will look worse on the counter at home.

Minute three: pick a clear color. Yellow or white looks like a reset. If you do not know their taste, white or mixed greens is safer than red. Red reads like a date. This is dishes.

Minute four: pick up the bunch. Look at the water in the bucket. Cloudy water means they have been sitting. Clearer is better. Look at the leaves. Yellow leaves at the bottom are a no. Squeeze nothing. Just look.

Minute five: smell once, close, not a huge inhale like a cartoon. If it smells like a fridge, put it back. If it smells like a plant, keep going.

Minute six: if there is a person working floral, say this exact thing: I need something that will last a few days, not a romantic bouquet, under whatever the cheap bunches are. I do not know names. What would you take home. Then take their answer. They know the case. You do not. I will not invent a price. Look at the sticker. If two bunches are close in price and one looks fresher, take fresher.

Minute seven: ask them to wrap it if they will. Paper looks like you stopped. If they will not, that is fine.

Minute eight: use a pitcher at home. Do not buy a vase. Recut the stems diagonal when you get home and change the water. Last-year you skipped that, which is why the bunch died by Thursday.

Minute nine: pay. Do not add chocolates unless you know they want them. Dishes plus chocolate can feel like a bribe.

Minute ten: at the door, say the dishes line before you hand the flowers. I was a jerk about the dishes. I will do them tonight and I will not narrate how tired I am while you cook. These are because I was rude, not because I think flowers fix the sink. Then hand them over. If they say you did not have to, you say I know. I wanted to.

That is the ten minutes. Names were never the assignment. Freshness, not-red, a human sentence, water in a jar. Go.

### User
There is no floral person, just a cooler. And the white ones look sparse. The mixed ones have baby's breath which I think is the filler that looks like I did not try. What do I actually put in my hand.

### Assistant
Put the mixed bunch in your hand if the heads are open enough to look alive and the stems are not slimy. Baby's breath is filler. Last-year you treated filler like a moral failure. It is not. It is how grocery bunches are built. You can pull some of it out at home if it bothers you. That takes thirty seconds and makes it look like you edited, which is a kind of trying.

If the white ones are sparse, they will look sadder in a wide pitcher. Sparse is a vase problem. Mixed is denser. Dense wins when you have no clerk.

Check three heads. If two out of three are brown-edged, put it back and take the next mixed bunch. If they are all tired, you still take the least tired and you lean harder on the dishes sentence. Flowers are a prop. The prop can be imperfect. The sentence cannot be I got you these instead of doing the sink.

Walk. You have maybe six minutes left. Cooler, mixed, not slimy, sticker you can actually pay, home, recut, talk. Last-year you would still be in the card aisle writing a paragraph. Leave the cards. Go.
