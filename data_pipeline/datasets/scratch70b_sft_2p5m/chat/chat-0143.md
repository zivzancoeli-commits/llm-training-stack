### User
I think I wrecked the community plot I'm assigned to. Soil is dark and shiny. Some pepper leaves look yellow. I was covering for the person who usually waters because they're away. Now I feel like I drowned a square of dirt in public. Tell me how to fix it. I'm new at this.

### Assistant
I'm not going to fire off a rescue list yet. "Overwatered" can mean a few different messes, and the fix is not the same for all of them.

When you say the plot got overwatered, do you mean you stood there with a can or hose for a long time, or did a shared timer dump on it, or did it rain after you already watered? I need that before I tell you whether to wait, shade, or actually move soil.

While you answer, don't panic-weed or dump dry dirt on top as a cover-up. Yellow leaves can be too much water, too little, or a plant being dramatic. I won't pretend I can see your box from here.

### User
Hose. I was trying to be thorough. The top looked dry even while I was still spraying. Then a timer on the neighboring bed oversprayed onto my path. So maybe me plus their spray. Peppers are the yellowest. The basil looks less sad. The shared path is slick.

### Assistant
That's useful. You stacked hose plus someone else's spray, and peppers sulk in wet soil. I'm still not going to diagnose a disease from "yellow." Yellow after a soak often means roots sat in wet, airless dirt for a night. It can also mean other things. If plants collapse, smell rotten, or stems go mushy at the soil line, that's a different conversation — ask the garden coordinator, not a chat.

What you can do without bluffing: stop adding water. That's the first move. Not "a little extra to rinse." Stop. Stick a finger or a stick down a few inches. If it's wet at depth, a dry-looking surface later today does not mean water again. Surface lies.

If the path is holding a puddle against your boards, scrape a shallow channel so water can leave instead of pooling at the peppers. Don't dig the plants up to "let them breathe." Roots hate being excavated as a vibe check.

If mulch is piled like a wet collar against stems, pull it back a couple of inches. Leave mulch on the rest if it's doing its job; just don't strangle the base.

Talk to the neighbor like an intern who tells the truth, not like you committed a crime. "I overdid the hose and then your timer hit the path. I'm not watering this week. If the path is slick from runoff, I'll scrape it." You don't need a speech about your feelings about basil.

### User
Someone at the shed said yellow means feed them. Should I fertilize as an apology?

### Assistant
Someone at the shed might be talking about a different yellow. Feeding wet, stressed roots is a common way to make a mess louder. I would not fertilize right now. Hungry and drowned can look similar on a flyer, and you already know the recent event was water, not a famine.

Give it a few dry days of weather, as much as the week allows. If the forecast is more rain, you still don't hose. If a plant is clearly dead — not yellowing, dead — you can pull it so it doesn't occupy a hole, but don't rip out every sad pepper tonight. Sad is not dead.

If you want a check that's more than vibes, the garden may have a simple moisture meter. Use it as a comparison between your box and a neighbor's, not as a magic number I don't have.

If you're covering for someone, leave a note on the plot clipboard: date, hose plus overspray, no water until it dries at depth. That's intern-good. Hiding it is how the next person doubles the mistake.

### User
I said it was me plus the timer. No more water. Finger test. Channel the puddle. Mulch off the stems. No fertilizer as a sorry gift. Note on the clipboard. Don't rip everything out tonight.

### Assistant
That's the plan. You asked for a fix; the fix is mostly stopping. Overwatering is one of the few garden problems where doing less is the skilled move. If it still looks wrecked in a week, take a photo to the coordinator and ask about peppers specifically — still without inventing a fungus name. You noticed, you told the truth, you quit the hose. That's course-correcting before you make it worse, which is the actual intern move.
