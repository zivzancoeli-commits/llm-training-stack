### User
I changed the wifi password last month because the network felt slow and I was tired of random device names. It got better. This week it is slow again. I looked at the router page and there is a laptop named something like "Dell-Marcus" that is not ours. Marcus is the guy next door who used to ask for the password "just for a night" when his was down. I never gave him the new one. I don't know how he's on it. I don't want a fight in the hallway and I also don't want to keep paying for his downloads. What do I do that isn't a cop drama?

### Assistant
Treat it as your network, not a mystery novel. You do not need to accuse him of a crime to take your wifi back. I will not invent what the law says about using someone else's network. I also will not guess how he got the new password. People reuse old ones, share them, or leave a guest network on. You don't have to solve that to change it again.

Do the boring sequence. Log into the router. Change the password to something you have not used and have not said in a hallway. Store it in your manager or on paper inside, not on a sticky note by the door. Turn off any guest network you aren't using. Remove devices you don't recognize after you know how to reconnect your own phones.

Then your wifi is yours again, whether or not you ever have a conversation.

The conversation is optional and separate. A fight is optional too.

Do you actually want to talk to Marcus, or do you only want him off the network?

### User
I want him off. I also don't want him knocking to ask why "the internet died" like I owe him an outage report. He helped me carry a couch once. I feel petty. My partner thinks I should tell him to his face so he doesn't think it's a glitch.

### Assistant
Carrying a couch is not a lifetime wifi plan. You can be grateful for a couch and still not run a cafe.

If you change the password and say nothing, he may knock. That knock is inconvenient, not proof you did something cruel. You can answer with a dull sentence: "I changed the password. I'm not sharing it right now." You do not need to present the Dell-Marcus screenshot like evidence at trial. Evidence turns a boundary into a prosecution. He can be embarrassed without you narrating his laptop name.

If you prefer to get ahead of the knock, a short text is enough: "Hey — I had to lock down the wifi again, so the old password won't work. I'm not going to share it. Hope your connection is back soon." That is an adult message. It is not petty. Petty is a speech about fairness. This is a closed resource.

I will not tell you to call the police or the landlord about wifi. I don't know your building or lease. A laptop on a list is a password problem, not a drama. If you actually feel unsafe, that is separate from a slow stream, and you handle safety with people you trust.

Your partner's "tell him to his face" can be five seconds at the mailboxes. Same sentence as the text. Then go inside. If he jokes "just this week," repeat "not sharing it." Twice is plenty.

### User
What if he says I promised him he could use it whenever his was down?

### Assistant
Then you answer the promise you actually made, not the one he remembers. If you said "tonight" last year, that night is over. If you never said "whenever," you don't have to adopt his version. "I'm not sharing it anymore" still works. You are allowed to change a household rule.

If you did say something open-ended in a weak moment, you can close it out loud: "I know I helped before. I'm not doing that now." People hate hearing that. They still hear it.

Change the password first, tonight, before the conversation, so the network isn't sitting there while you rehearse. After it is changed, reconnect your own devices. Then send the text or say the mailbox line. You are not a utility. You are a person with a router. Lock it, say it once, stop explaining the couch.
