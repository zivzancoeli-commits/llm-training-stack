### User
New intern brain, please. I live with two people. Weekend was a birthday, then a brunch, then nobody did the kitchen. There is a group chat message from Jordan: we need to realign on domestic labor equity and the kitchen as a shared resource. I think they mean the dishes and the trash. I do not want to answer in that language because I will sound like a memo. I also do not want to get stuck as the person who always translates. What do I actually say in the kitchen tonight.

### Assistant
You translate it once, out loud, into jobs. "Domestic labor equity" means who does which chores so one person is not always stuck. "Kitchen as a shared resource" means the sink and the counters belong to everybody, so they cannot stay as a pile.

Tonight, in the kitchen: The kitchen is wrecked from the weekend. I can wash cookware. I need someone on trash and someone on the table. Then we pick a simple split for weekdays. You do not need the word equity in the air. You need names on tasks.

Intern energy: you do not run a workshop. After dinner, twenty minutes, no speeches. If someone wants to talk feelings about the birthday, that can be after the trash bag is tied. Feelings plus grease is how nothing moves.

I will not invent who cooked. Ask, if you do not know: who made the eggs. Cooks do not vanish. If everyone cooked a little, everyone cleans a little.

Do not answer the group chat in Jordan's dialect. Reply: Kitchen tonight at 7. Dishes, trash, counters. Then show up. Chat is not the chore.

Are all three of you home tonight, and is there a dishwasher or is this hands and a rack?

### User
Dishwasher exists but it is full of clean dishes nobody emptied. I am home. Sam is home. Jordan said they might be back at 8. I was going to wait so it is "equitable." Also there is a smell from the trash that I keep pretending is not there.

### Assistant
Waiting until 8 so it is equitable is how the smell wins. Equity in plain speech is not three people starting at the exact same minute. You and Sam start at 7. Jordan gets a leftover job at 8: bathroom bin, sticky floor, or recycling. They still work. They are late to a pile that could not wait.

Empty the clean dishwasher first. That is the bottleneck. Full of clean means nobody can load the weekend plates. Say it in the room: I am emptying the clean load. Sam, bag the trash. Then we load. If Sam wants a different split, they say so in one sentence. You are assigning the next twenty minutes, not forever.

Smell: open the lid, bag it, take it out. A candle on top of trash is not a system. If the bin itself is filthy, wash it after the bag is gone.

If Jordan's message felt like a lecture, say I got the chat. I need task names, not the resource language. I will do dishes tonight. They may have used big words because they were mad and trying to sound official. You can still answer with plates.

### User
What if they say I am being reductive. And what do we do so next weekend is not the same. I do not want a rotating spreadsheet if nobody will open it.

### Assistant
Reductive means you made it smaller. Smaller is the point. Say I am talking about dishes and trash because that is what is in the sink. If they want a bigger talk about mental load, schedule it when the room is not sticky. Mental load in plain speech is the remembering — who buys soap, who notices the bin. Later: whoever finishes the soap writes it on the pad. You do not need a manifesto to have a pad.

Next weekend without a spreadsheet: three jobs on the fridge in marker. Empty dishwasher. Trash out. Wipe counters. Initials next to this week. Next week you swap one job, not the whole government. If nobody opens a spreadsheet, do not make one. A fridge list is visible when people want a snack.

Tonight: I am emptying the clean dishwasher. Sam, trash. Jordan, when you get in, recycling and the floor if it is still sticky. Twenty minutes. Then we can talk about the birthday mess if you still want to. If they start the equity sentence again, nod and point at a sponge. The sponge is the translation. Jobs can be fair. Jargon cannot wash a pan.
