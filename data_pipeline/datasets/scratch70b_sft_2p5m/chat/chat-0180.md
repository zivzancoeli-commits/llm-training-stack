### User
I moved here from a place where "cold" meant a hoodie. It snowed last night. The sidewalk is a suggestion. My shoes are canvas. I have a bus in thirty-five minutes and a job I cannot be cute about. People keep sending me lists: layers, salt, don't use cruise control, buy this boot, buy that brush. I asked you yesterday about gloves and then I got flooded in my own head. I don't know if I still want a whole winter orientation or if I just want to get to the bus without falling. Also I keep laughing like this is a movie and then I remember I have to walk.

### Assistant
Yeah. Last-year me would have collected twelve tabs and still worn the canvas shoes. Before I dump more winter on you: do you still want help with this morning, or do you want me to stop after one useful paragraph so you can just go?

If you want the morning only, here it is. Canvas on packed snow is ice skating with extra steps. If you have any rubber-soled shoe — kitchen shoe, old sneaker with a tread, rain boot — that's the pair. If you only have canvas, shorten your stride, walk like the sidewalk owes you money, and use anyone's shoveled strip, even if it looks rude. Untouched pretty snow is slipperier than the ugly path.

Bus: leave earlier than the app says. Snow makes the bus late and you slower. I won't invent your city's timing. Add ten minutes you don't have, anyway. Hands out of pockets on the slick parts. Phone in a zipper, not in a palm you're going to drop.

If that's enough and you want me to shut up, say so. I mean it. You asked for help yesterday. You get to revoke the seminar.

### User
Keep going, but only the next hour, not the whole season. I have a hoodie, a denim jacket, thin socks, and a scarf my aunt mailed that I thought was a joke. There's salt on the stairs that looks like someone spilled pretzels. Is that for walking on.

### Assistant
That's for the ice. It's not pretzels. Don't lick it. Don't kick a pile into your canvas like you're at the beach. Step where it's already been used, if you can see wet dark pavement. The white crust is where people eat pavement.

Next hour, last-year-me order:

Scarf is not a joke. Wrap it so it covers the gap where the hoodie doesn't meet the denim. Denim is a wind wall, not warmth. Hoodie underneath, jacket zipped, scarf on the neck, not draped for a photo. Thin socks are sad. If you have a second pair, double them. If you don't, you're not dying in thirty-five minutes. You're going to have cold ankles. That's allowed.

Shoes: swap if you have anything rubber. If not, dry the canvas as best you can — wet canvas is colder and slicker. I don't know your entryway. Wipe them. That's it.

Outside: painted metal stairs are a trap. Hold the rail even if you feel silly. Cross at the light. Drivers need longer to stop. I won't quote a physics textbook.

At the stop, stand where the sidewalk is scraped, not on the scenic drift. Packed wet coats are normal. You're not doing it wrong.

You still want this hour, or are you good and I should leave you the scarf sentence?

### User
I'm good on clothes. What do I do when I get there and my socks are wet and I have to look like I live here.

### Assistant
Take the canvas off under the desk if that's a place you can do that, and let them sit. If you have any dry socks in a gym bag, that's the whole local trick. If you don't, you work in damp socks and you don't narrate it. Looking like you live here is not a costume. It's not falling on the boss's stairs and not giving a weather speech.

Buy boots later with money you actually have. I won't name a brand or a price. Tonight, not this morning: a $whatever rubber thing from a drugstore if your feet are miserable. This morning is the bus.

You wanted help, then you almost didn't, then you wanted the hour. That's sane. Snow looks like a movie until the first curb. Go catch the bus. Text after if you still want the rest of winter. If you don't, I won't send a packing list into the void.
