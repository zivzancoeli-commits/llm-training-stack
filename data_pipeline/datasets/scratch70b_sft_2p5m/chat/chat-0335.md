### User
We have a two-bedroom. The living room is the only common space: kitchen open to the couch, one table, TV. My roommate Jules has been bringing dates over three nights a week. They dim the lights, put on a playlist, and occupy the couch like I should go to my room and pretend I don't live here. My bedroom is next to the living room. I can hear everything. I have been eating dinner on my bed. I pay half the rent. I don't want to be the roommate who "ruins the vibe." I also don't want to keep migrating with a plate. How do I say this without turning it into a purity lecture?

### Assistant
This is not a purity lecture. This is a floor plan.

You pay for the common room. A date is allowed. A date that annexes the only couch three nights a week is a schedule problem. Talk to Jules without the date in the apartment. Night-of confrontations in front of a guest make you the villain in a story you did not write.

Say the spatial fact: "The living room is the only place I can eat and sit. I need it some evenings. Can we pick nights, or can dates go to your room after a bit?" You are offering a split, not a ban.

Do not describe what you heard through the wall. That turns the talk into humiliation. Stick to time and space. "Three nights this week I ate in my room" is enough evidence.

I will not invent your lease's guest rules. If you have a written roommate agreement, glance at it. If you do not, you are still allowed to negotiate the couch.

Are these first dates that linger, or a recurring person who basically lives on the sofa now?

### User
Recurring. Same person, Alex. They've started leaving a charger. Jules said, "We'll be quiet," which is not the same as me having a place to sit. There's no door between kitchen and couch. If I cook, I'm in their date.

### Assistant
A charger is a tell. This is unpaid third-roommate drift.

Name the pattern, not Alex's soul. "Alex is over a lot, and the living room is the house's only common space. I need set nights where the couch is just ours — you and me — or just me, and nights you're hosting." Then propose a boring calendar: two weeknights plus one weekend night for hosting, or whatever numbers you can live with. Write it in the group chat after you talk so it is not a foggy "we'll be quiet."

Quiet was never the whole issue. Presence is the issue. You should be able to cook pasta without starring in someone else's evening.

If Jules wants more hosting, the overflow belongs in Jules's room. That is what bedrooms are for when there is one living room. You do not have to police affection. You have to stop being displaced.

If Alex is going to keep a charger here, that is a house conversation about keys, nights, and whether rent is still two ways. You do not have to resolve "are they moving in" in the first talk. You do have to stop the default that you vanish.

Cook in the kitchen tonight if you need food. You live there. A polite "hey, I'm making dinner, I'll be in here" is not ruining a vibe. It is using the stove.

### User
Jules said I can "join them" which is worse. I don't want a trio on my own couch. I also don't want to be eating cereal at 10 p.m. in the dark waiting for them to go to bed.

### Assistant
Decline the trio. "I'm not joining your date. I need the room sometimes without being a guest in it." Flat. If they look hurt, let them. Hurt is cheaper than a year of bed-dinners.

Set a clock in the agreement: by 10, the living room is free unless you opted in. Or: weeknights the common room is shared space, not date space after 9. Pick a time you can enforce without a spreadsheet. Then go make the cereal in the light. If they are still on the couch, you still use the kitchen. You can put on your own lamp. You are not a raccoon.

If Jules will not agree to any nights, you have a bigger roommate problem than Alex. Then you talk about who moves, or who pays for a different arrangement. I will not invent the legal path. I will say that "join us" is not a compromise. A compromise is hours and doors.

Tonight: eat in the living room. Tomorrow: calendar. You already paid for that couch. Use it.
