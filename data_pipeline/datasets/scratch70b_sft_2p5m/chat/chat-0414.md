### User
My roommate lights incense in the living room almost every evening. It is thick. My throat gets tight and I go to my room and close the door, which does not help much. I have said "wow that's strong" and they smiled. I need to say I cannot be in the apartment when it is going. I don't want to insult their ritual. I also need to work in the living room two nights a week. Tonight the stick is already burning.

### Assistant
Your lungs are a reason. You do not have to critique the ritual to ask for air.

Tonight, if it is already lit: "I can't breathe with this. Please put it out, or take it to your room with the door closed and a window." Then you open what you can. You are not required to sit in it while you workshop a policy.

For the ongoing deal: incense in their room, window, door, and not in the shared space on your work nights. If they need a shared-space time, pick hours you are out. Shared air is the roommate part.

I will not diagnose your throat or tell you it is an allergy. I will tell you to leave the room if you feel worse, and to get medical help if you ever feel like you cannot breathe. That is caution, not a diagnosis.

Have they framed this as religious, or as a vibe?

### User
They said it "clears the energy." It is not a holiday thing. They buy it in packs. I work Tuesdays and Thursdays at the table. I feel like I'm asking them to stop being themselves.

### Assistant
You are asking them to relocate smoke. They can still be themselves in their room.

Say: "The incense in the living room makes it hard for me to breathe and work. Please keep it in your room, window open, not in shared space. Tuesdays and Thursdays I need the table." If they offer a lighter scent, you can still say no to shared-space smoke. Lighter is still smoke.

If they call you dramatic, you do not have to prove a medical file. "I need air I can work in" is enough.

Tonight: the out-or-relocate line. Do not wait for the stick to finish as a courtesy. Courtesy is how your throat lost the evening.

A fan pointing out a window helps only so much. Do not pretend a fan equals a policy.

### User
If they refuse, do I buy a filter and suffer, or do I make this a house issue? I don't want a war over a stick.

### Assistant
A filter is your patch, not their permission slip. If they refuse, you repeat the house rule in a text so it is not a mood: living room is a no, their room is a yes with a window. If it still happens on Tuesday at the table, you take the laptop to a cafe or a bedroom that night and you tell them the next day the rule did not hold.

I will not invent whether incense violates a lease. If your building has a smoke rule, you can read it. Many are about cigarettes. Do not bluff a clause you have not read.

Tonight, speak while it burns. Put it out or move it. Then set the two work nights. You can respect a ritual and still need a hallway that does not smell like a closed shop. Say it. Then go drink water in the kitchen with a window up.

### User
The stick is half burned. They're in their room with headphones. I would have to knock and also sound like a smoke alarm.

### Assistant
Knock. Smoke alarms are allowed to interrupt headphones. "I can't breathe with this in the shared air. Please put it out or keep it in your room with the window." If they cannot hear, knock again. You do not need a perfect tone. You need the stick out of the living room. Open a window on your way. Then have the Tuesday/Thursday table rule when the air is clearer. A half-burned stick is not a reason to wait for the end. Ends keep coming every evening. Interrupt this one. Then drink water. Then send the text so tomorrow is not another smile. Headphones can pause. Your throat should not have to.
