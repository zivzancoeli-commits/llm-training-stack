### User
My landlord just texted "quick question, you up?" It is 11:02. Nothing is on fire that I can see. Last time a "quick question" was them asking if they could show the unit tomorrow morning. I am in bed. I do not want to be rude and I do not want to open a negotiation in my pajamas.

### Assistant
You do not have to be up because they are. "You up?" is a hook, not an emergency. If the building were flooding, they would usually say flooding.

You can ignore it until morning. You can also send one line that closes the night without opening the topic: "I'm down for the night. Text me the question and I'll answer tomorrow." That trains the channel. It is not rude. It is a clock.

Do not call. A call at 11 is how a quick question becomes a twenty-minute tour of their thoughts.

I will not invent what they are allowed to demand after hours, or whether a showing notice is valid. That depends on your lease and where you live. I do not have those papers.

Is there any active issue they already know about — a leak, a lock, a notice they posted — or is this out of nowhere?

### User
There's a leak I reported five days ago that they said a plumber would "try to schedule." No notice on the door. I already feel like if I don't reply they'll say I was uncooperative about the leak.

### Assistant
A leak they already know about is still not a reason you have to chat at 11:02 unless water is moving. Look once: is the ceiling worse, is there a new drip, is anything electrical involved? If it is the same slow leak, it can wait until morning. If it is suddenly worse, that is the rare case where you reply with facts, not availability: "The leak looks worse, water in the bucket. I'm not available for a long text. Call a plumber or tell me if I need to shut something off." Then you can stay in bed or not based on the water, not based on their loneliness.

For the ordinary case, non-reply is allowed. So is the one-liner. What is not useful is "what's up?" at 11:03, which invites the showing conversation while you are half asleep. Half-asleep you will agree to 8 a.m.

If they are trying to book a showing, morning you can answer with your lease in front of you, not with folklore about "24 hours." Read the actual clause. If you do not understand it, say you need to read it, which is honest. I will not quote a law at you.

They cannot usefully mark you uncooperative for sleeping. You reported the leak in writing five days ago. That is the record. A late-night "you up" is not a test you fail by being in bed.

### User
If I send the one-liner they'll just call. They do that. I hate the phone.

### Assistant
If they call, you are allowed to not pick up. Let it ring. A voicemail is a gift: it contains the question without you performing alertness. If they leave nothing, you still have your morning.

If you worry they will show up, that is a different fear. You do not have to pre-open the door at 11. If someone knocks, you can speak through the door: "I'm not available tonight. Text me." You do not need to narrate your pajamas.

For the next ten minutes: glance at the leak, take a photo if it looks any different so tomorrow's story is not memory, send the one-liner or send nothing, put the phone face down, and go back to the thing you were doing, which was sleep. If you send the line, mute the thread until morning.

Tomorrow, answer the actual question in daylight, attach the leak photo if relevant, and ask for a plumber window in writing. Night texts are how landlords think out loud. You do not have to be the person who thinks with them after eleven.
