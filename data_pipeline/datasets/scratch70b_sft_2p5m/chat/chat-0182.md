### User
I'm on a public library PC and it is dying. Catalog search takes a minute per click. I told my coworker Dana I'd stay after my shift and help her upload a resume before the 5 p.m. cutoff. It's 4:22. She wants me to "just keep trying" and to ask the librarian to install Chrome because Edge is "the problem." I have to be at the bus by 4:40 or I miss the only one that gets me home before my kid's pickup. Dana says I promised. I did. How do I leave without inventing a dentist appointment.

### Assistant
You leave by telling the truth: you have a bus and a pickup. That is a no, not an excuse. An excuse is a fake dentist. A no is "I can't stay past 4:35."

The computer is slow. It is also not yours to fix. Don't ask them to install Chrome. You'll burn minutes, they'll say no, and Dana will treat that as you not trying.

At 4:22 your job is triage. Save whatever is typed. If it's only on this session, copy the text into an email to herself right now, even if the page crawls. Then stand up.

One pass, not a negotiation: "I have to catch the 4:40. I can stay until 4:35. After that, ask staff. I'm not asking them for Chrome."

If she says you promised: "I promised help, not a rebuilt PC, and not missing pickup." Repeat the time. Don't add a story about the bus being weird today.

### User
She started crying at the desk. Quiet crying, which is worse. She's whispering this job would change her rent. The librarian glanced over. If I walk out I feel like the villain of the children's section.

### Assistant
You can be kind and still leave. Kind is not extra minutes you don't have. Kind is a next step she can do without you.

Three lines on scrap paper: email the text to yourself now; ask the desk about the session; try another device if this one dies. Don't invent whether the employer will take a late file. You don't know.

Out loud, low: "I'm sorry this computer is garbage. I'm still leaving at 4:35. Keep the chair. Ask the desk. I can't stay." Then pack.

Crying does not extend the bus. Wait until she stops and you miss pickup — that's two emergencies. You are not the villain for collecting your kid. You would be if you lied about a dentist.

If the librarian comes over: "Public PC is hanging, she needs the session, I have to go." Don't translate Dana's rent into a speech. Staff hear that all day. It doesn't add RAM.

### User
What if she follows me to the door and asks me to miss one bus. "It's twenty minutes, your kid's daycare will understand." I fold on the word understand.

### Assistant
Daycare understanding is not a plan. Pickup windows are. Don't re-open the math at the door.

"I'm not missing the bus. I have to go." If she repeats the twenty minutes: "Still no." Don't explain daycare policy. Extra detail is where folding lives.

If she wants you on the phone walking her through clicks, no: "I can't coach this while I'm running. Ask the librarian." That's a no with a person attached, not a fake reason.

Don't promise tonight as a guilt payment unless you mean it. "I'll call after pickup" is a second promise. "Good luck. Email it to yourself" is complete.

### User
I keep wanting to say the computer "isn't compatible" so it sounds official. That's a lie, right?

### Assistant
Yes. "Isn't compatible" is a costume for "it's slow and I won't stay." Official language makes you sound like IT, which you aren't, and then she asks you to try one more setting.

Say the true part: "This machine is too slow to finish by 4:35. I'm leaving." No dentist. No compatibility theater. No extra twenty minutes.

Walk. Text only if you already said you would. Otherwise pocket the phone and go get your kid. The resume is her job. The bus is yours.
