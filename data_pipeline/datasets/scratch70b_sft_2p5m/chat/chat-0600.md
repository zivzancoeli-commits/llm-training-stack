### User
Marta is at my desk with a laptop that "just needs a minute." It is 12:10. I have a lunch I actually packed. She wants me to skip it and click through her email freeze. I am not IT. I am the person who once restarted a printer. I don't want to be cruel. I also don't want to chew a sandwich over her keyboard. How do I keep lunch?

### Assistant
Keep lunch. A frozen inbox is not a fire just because it arrived at 12:10. You packed food on purpose. That plan still counts when someone sits in the guest chair.

Say: "I'm going to lunch. If it still fails at 1, I can look for five minutes, or you can ticket IT." Then you stand up. Standing is the sentence's body. Sitting is how a minute becomes your apple on her trackpad. If your stomach is already loud, that is not a professional failure. It is noon.

I will not invent your IT SLA. I will not invent a rule that you must help. If you want to be kind, point her at the actual help desk chat while you put your container in the bag. Kind is a door, not your stomach.

Do not start "just the restart" unless you are willing to skip lunch. Restarts grow children: updates, passwords, a second account. Printer you once saved is how you got this reputation. You can retire it at noon.

Is she blocking your drawer, or can you physically leave?

### User
She's in my guest chair with the laptop open on my calendar blotter. My lunch is in the drawer under her bag. She said IT has a two-hour queue and she has a 12:30 send. I feel the old helper sitting back down in my spine.

### Assistant
Ask her to lift the bag. That is not cruel. That is your drawer. "I need my lunch out from under your bag. I'm still going." If the 12:30 send is real, she can send from another machine, a phone, or a web client if her world allows it. You do not have to diagnose the freeze. You can say, "I don't know this error. IT will. I'm not skipping lunch to guess."

If you know the restart path and you are truly willing to spend three minutes while standing, you can do it standing, then leave. Clock it. Three minutes. If it asks for an update, you stop. Updates are how 12:10 becomes 1:10. If you are not willing, skip even the standing restart. She has a queue. You have a sandwich.

The old helper in your spine wants to be needed. Needed can wait until 1. 12:30 is her deadline, not a transfer of your break.

If she looks wounded, let her. You can eat in the kitchen, not at the blotter, so the chair does not refill. Location is a boundary.

### User
She'll tell people I wouldn't help. There's a story already about me being "too precious with breaks." I hate that story.

### Assistant
People who skip lunch write that story about people who do not. You do not fix it by donating this noon. You fix it by being boring and consistent: you eat, you come back, you help for five minutes at 1 if you still want to, you ticket if you do not know. Boring is hard to turn into a villain.

If she narrates you in the kitchen, you do not need a counter-campaign. You can say to a third person, if asked, "I went to lunch. I offered after 1." Short. True.

If she tries to follow you to the kitchen with the laptop, you can say, "After 1. Not over food." Then you sit somewhere she is not. A laptop next to a sandwich is how lunch becomes a ticket you never opened.

Drawer, bag, standing, exit. Do not debug a send you do not own. IT exists for a reason, even when the queue is two hours. Her 12:30 can be a phone email. Your 12:10 can be food. Take the container. Leave the laptop on the blotter for her to carry away. You are not the dock. You are a person who eats.
