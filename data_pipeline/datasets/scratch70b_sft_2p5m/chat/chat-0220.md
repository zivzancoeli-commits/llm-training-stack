### User
It's 1:07 a.m. and whoever lives above me is dragging furniture or dropping weights, I can't tell. This is the third night this week. I have work at seven. I already banged on my ceiling once like a cartoon. I don't want to go up there and start a fight, but I also don't want to lie here getting angrier. What should I actually do tonight?

### Assistant
Tonight is not the night you become the building detective. Tonight is sleep and a note for tomorrow, unless the noise is something that sounds like a person in trouble. Dragging and dropping, third night, that's a living-habit problem, not an emergency I'm going to send you into a hallway for at 1 a.m.

Before I stack advice: do you still want help handling the neighbor, or do you want help getting through this one night so you can decide in daylight? Those are different jobs. I'm not going to talk you into a confrontation you don't want.

If you want the night only, earplugs if you have them, a fan or a podcast at low volume for cover, phone face-down so you stop checking the time. Don't bang the ceiling again. The second bang is how people start feuds they then have to live under.

If you want the neighbor handled, we do that in daylight with a paper trail, not with you in socks on their welcome mat.

### User
I still want help with the neighbor. I'm just not going up there at 1 a.m. I don't even know their name. Building has a super who answers emails like once a week. I feel like if I wait until morning I'll chicken out.

### Assistant
Waiting until morning isn't chickening out. It's not knocking on a stranger's door in the dark. Write the email now, while you're mad enough to be specific, and send it in the morning after you reread it. Anger drafts well. Anger sends badly.

In the draft, stick to times and sounds. "Three nights this week, including tonight after 1 a.m., heavy dragging or dropping from the unit above me. I work early. Can you tell me the process for noise complaints?" Don't diagnose them as gym rats. You don't know. Don't threaten to call the police unless you already know that's the building's actual path, which I don't. Super emails that sound like lawsuits get slower answers, not faster ones.

If you still want a human conversation, do it once, daytime, after you've slept. Knock, step back from the door, and say one thing: "Hey, I'm downstairs. The late-night dragging is coming through pretty loud. Would you be willing to keep it earlier?" Then stop. If they apologize, say thanks and leave. If they get weird, you already have the email. You don't need to win the hallway.

I'm checking again: you still want this handled, not just a rant?

### User
Yeah. Handled. But if they say they weren't even home, I don't know what I'd do. And I don't want the super to think I'm the high-maintenance tenant.

### Assistant
If they say they weren't home, you don't argue from memory of the ceiling. You say, "Okay, then maybe it's something in the unit. I'll mention the times to the super." That's not a bluff. That's you refusing to litigate a floor. You might be wrong about which neighbor. Buildings carry sound sideways. I won't pretend I can map your joists from here.

The super already has high-maintenance tenants. A short email with dates is normal. High-maintenance is daily novels and hallway scenes. One written complaint with three nights listed is the opposite of a personality.

Tomorrow, in order: sleep whatever you can, send the email before work, and only knock if you still want a face-to-face after coffee. If you wake up and the fight has left your body, skip the knock. The email still exists. You can follow up later.

If the noise is still going at 2 a.m. and you can't sleep, that's a tonight problem again: cover sound, not a visit. Save your one in-person ask for a time when both of you are dressed and less likely to say something you'll hear through the floor for a year.

You asked for help, not a pep talk about being tough. Tough is sending a dated note and going to work. Leave the cartoon ceiling bang in the past. That's the whole play.
