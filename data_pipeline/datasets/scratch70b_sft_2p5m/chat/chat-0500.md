### User
Someone keeps leaving notes under my wiper. They start like parking etiquette and then become rants about "people like you," the city, and once my bumper sticker. I park legally as far as I can tell. The notes are getting longer. I haven't answered. I don't want a windshield war and I also don't want to keep finding essays on my glass after work. There are no cameras I control in the lot.

### Assistant
Don't answer on paper. A reply under their wiper, if you even know the car, is how this becomes a correspondence. Rants want an audience. You don't have to be the desk.

Photograph each note with the date, the stall if you can see a number, and the windshield. Keep the paper if it isn't disgusting. That's a record, not a hobby.

Then you decide whether this is annoying paper or something that makes you feel unsafe. Those are different. Annoying paper: you ignore, you park, you live. Unsafe: you tell people you trust, you use whatever building or lot reporting actually exists, and you don't play detective in a dark lot at 11 p.m. I will not invent a crime or a police outcome. I don't know your city. I won't tell you "it's illegal" as a slogan. Notes on cars sit in a mushy category. Your nervous system is still real.

Don't leave a sarcastic counter-note. Wit reads as engagement.

Do you know which neighbor it is, or only the handwriting?

### User
I think it's the person in 2B because of a phrase they used in the laundry room. I'm not sure enough to knock. The last note said they "know my schedule." That sentence sat in my stomach.

### Assistant
"I know your schedule" is the line that moves this out of bumper-sticker critique. Treat that as a reason to stop handling it as a parking debate.

Don't knock on 2B to confirm your theory. Maybe is not a knock. If you are wrong, you have a new problem. If you are right, you have given them a face-to-face.

Use the channels that don't require you to be a detective. If the building has a manager, email: dates, photos, the schedule sentence quoted, request that they remind residents not to leave notes on cars / that you feel targeted. Stay factual. If there is no manager, a landlord or HOA or lot owner is the same idea if they exist. If none of that exists, you still have friends, a change of parking time when you can, and the option to ask a local non-emergency line what they take reports on. I will not script a police report as if I know they will care. You can ask.

Consider whether the bumper sticker is worth the attention. Removing it is not surrender of your personality; it is reducing a handle. Leaving it is also allowed. I won't order you to hide.

Change what you can without a war: different stall if the lot allows, not posting your comings and goings in public chats, walking to the car with a phone in your hand like a person who is allowed to exist. That's ordinary caution, not a movie.

### User
Should I write one calm note that says please stop?

### Assistant
I wouldn't. You already didn't answer, which was the right instinct. A calm note still tells them the channel works. If you want a message to land, let it come from management on a hallway board or an email blast, not from your wiper as a duet.

Keep photographing. Don't knock on 2B. Don't wait at the car to catch someone. If anything escalates past paper — touching the car, showing up at your door — that's when you stop treating this as etiquette and you use emergency or non-emergency help the way you would for any other scare. I won't predict that it will.

The rants want a character in their story. You can be a person who parks, documents, reports once, and doesn't write back. Let the glass stay empty.
