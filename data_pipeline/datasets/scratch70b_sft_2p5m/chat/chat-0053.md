### User
There is a group chat from college, eight people, and it is dying ugly. Two of them dated, broke up, and now every meme is a landmine. Someone made a side chat without the ex. The ex found out. Now people are texting me privately asking whose side I am on like I am HR. I just started this job and I already feel like the intern of other people's drama. I do not know if I should mute, leave, or try to split it into two clean groups. Help.

### Assistant
Before I pick mute, leave, or split: are you trying to stay friends with both of them, or are you trying to stop being the mailbox. Those are different jobs. Which one is actually yours this week?

### User
I want to stay friendly with both if I can, but I am more tired of being the mailbox than I am scared of losing either of them. We were never that close, it was a campus housing chat that never died. I just do not want to look like I plotted.

### Assistant
Good. Then you are not HR and you are not a diplomat. You are a person in a leftover chat. You do not owe a group announcement or private briefings.

Do not split it into two clean groups. The moment you create the new one, you have picked a roster, and the roster is the plot. Someone will screenshot the member list. You will look like you hosted the breakup.

Mute if you still want the rare useful ping, like a reunion date. Leave is cleaner if the thread is only landmines. Given you were never that close, leaving is allowed. Muting for a week first is also allowed. I would not stay active. Active is how you become the mailbox.

For the private texts asking whose side: one sentence, same sentence to everyone. I am not taking a side in the breakup. I am also not passing messages. Please talk to them, not through me. Then stop answering follow-ups that are just more temperature checks. If you keep explaining, you are still the intern.

If you leave the group, do it without a speech. A speech becomes content. If you feel you must say something because eight people will notice the goodbye banner, keep it dull: I am stepping out of this chat, nothing personal, I am drowning in notifications. Dull is a gift. Dramatic is fuel.

The side chat that already exists: you do not have to join it to prove loyalty, and you do not have to snitch about it. If someone asks whether you are in it, say I am not discussing other chats. That sounds stiff. Stiff is better than a map of who is where.

You asked for advice after I asked the question, so here is the recommendation in intern-plain: mute today, send the one sentence to the people using you as a hallway, do not found a new group, leave in a week if it is still ugly. If a real-life hangout comes up, go or do not based on whether you like the people, not based on who is exiled from a thread.

### User
One of them is going to say you were in the side chat screenshot even if I was not, because my name is similar to someone else. I can feel that coming. Also the original chat is named something jokey from 2019 and leaving feels like I am throwing away the joke.

### Assistant
A similar name is annoying, not a verdict. If you get accused, answer once: I am not in that chat. Do not send a collage of your chat list. Collages look like a case file. If they do not believe you, that is their heat, not homework.

The 2019 joke is already dead. It is a name on a thread that now hosts a breakup. You are not throwing away college. You are closing a noisy tab. Nostalgia is a bad reason to stay in a fight you do not belong to.

If you want a keepsake, screenshot one old funny thread privately, then mute. Do not post it back as a we used to be fun plea. That is how people start writing essays.

You are new at work. Do not bring this into the office, even as a funny story with names. Keep the lid on. Mute. One sentence. No new group. Leave if it stays sour. You already told me the mailbox is the real problem. Act like that is the problem.
