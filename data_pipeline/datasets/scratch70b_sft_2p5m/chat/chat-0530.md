### User
I'm at my nephew's seventh birthday. Bounce house in the yard, cake in forty minutes. My uncle sat down next to me with a paper plate and started in on the news like we're on a panel. I have opinions. I also have a kid in a superhero cape ten feet away. I have never successfully said "not here" without it turning into "so you agree with them." Help me decline the debate without becoming the new topic.

### Assistant
You can leave the chair. That is the cleanest version. Politics at a seventh birthday is optional. The bounce house is not a studio.

If you stay, you need one sentence that does not take the bait. The bait is any question that starts with "don't you think" or "you must have seen." Those are not questions. They are ramps.

Try: "I'm here for the party. I don't want to do news today." Then you look at the kid, or the cake table, or your own plate. You do not add a position. A position is a debate. "Not today" is a closed door.

If he says that means you agree with the other side, you repeat the same sentence. Repetition is boring on purpose. New explanations are fuel. "I'm not debating this at a birthday" is enough. You do not owe a syllabus.

I will not script you a clever dunk. Clever dunks are how the picnic table goes quiet and the seven-year-old notices the adults got weird.

Are you sitting at a table with other adults, or is it mostly just him parked next to you?

### User
Mostly him. Two other uncles are in earshot but pretending to watch the bounce house. My sister — the parent — is inside cutting fruit. I already nodded twice because I didn't know how to stop the first sentence. He took the nods as go.

### Assistant
Stop nodding. Nodding is agreement in this dialect. Hold the plate. Look at the bounce house like the other uncles.

You do not need to rewind the two nods. You can interrupt the next claim with the party sentence. Interrupt is allowed when someone is using a child's party as a podium. You are not required to wait for a polite comma that will never come.

If you want a body move, stand up with the plate. "I'm going to see if she needs help with the fruit." That is true enough if you actually walk inside. Don't invent a phone call in the driveway. Walking toward the parent is useful. Tell your sister, privately, that uncle is doing news at the table. She can decide whether to redirect him. It is her house and her kid. You are not the referee of the whole family. You are the person who will not host a debate on a rental bounce house.

If he follows you, you still have the same line. You do not upgrade to a speech about "the children." Seven-year-olds hear tone faster than content. Keep your voice flat. Flat is safer than righteous.

The earshot uncles will not save you. Pretending to watch the bounce house is their plan. Let them have it.

### User
He just said "it's important they hear the truth." Meaning the kids. My nephew is right there. I felt my face get hot. I don't want to make a scene and I don't want this to be the story of the party.

### Assistant
Hot face is information, not a cue to perform. Put the plate down if your hands are doing too much.

To him, still one sentence: "Not in front of the kids, and not today." Then you physically move. Bounce house, fruit, kitchen, bathroom. Distance is the scene-preventer. Standing still and arguing is the scene.

If he addresses the nephew directly with a news take, you can speak to the nephew, not to the uncle: "Hey, show me the cape trick again." Kids will usually take the exit. Adults who want an audience hate a kid-directed subject change. That's fine.

Do not fact-check him in the yard. Fact-checking is the debate. You already declined.

After cake, you can tell your sister what happened in one paragraph, no manifesto. She may already know this uncle. She may not. Either way, you did the first-time job: you did not convert a birthday into a panel, and you did not pretend the nods were a lifelong subscription.

If he sulks, let him sulk with his paper plate. Sulking is cheaper than a family legend about who "couldn't handle the truth" next to a bounce house.
