### User
I have a jury summons for next week. I already put in for the day off because the notice said to show up unless I heard otherwise. I have not heard otherwise. The court's website has a "check your status" page that just spins. I called the number on the letter at lunch and sat on hold until I had to go back to a meeting. Work is annoyed that my calendar is a maybe. I don't want to skip and get in trouble, and I don't want to burn a day off if they already dismissed me in a system I can't see. What do I do with this delay without pretending I know court rules?

### Assistant
You don't guess. You don't invent a local procedure. Jury instructions are specific to that court, and I am not going to fake them.

What you can do is keep a paper trail of the attempt and follow the summons you actually have. The letter in your hand is the thing that exists. A spinning website is not a dismissal. Hold music is not a dismissal. Until you have a confirmation you can save — a posted list, an email, a person on the phone who reads you a status — you plan as if you are going.

Tell work the true shape: "I have a summons for that date. I tried the status line and the site. I will confirm as soon as the court does. If I'm released, I'll come in." That is better than "maybe jury, lol." Managers hate comedy about legal mail.

I will not tell you that you can skip, that you must appear at a certain minute, or what a penalty would be. I don't know your jurisdiction. If the letter has a reporting time and a phone number, those are your tools. Try the number again at a different hour. Some of those lines are quieter early.

Did the summons mention a website, a call-in the night before, or only a show-up date?

### User
It says to call or check online after 5 p.m. the evening before. That's the part I can't reach during work, and after 5 the page spins. The letter is from weeks ago. I already used a vacation day because my job wants coverage planned. If they cancel me at 5:01, I have a wasted day and a boss who thinks I gamed it.

### Assistant
Then the evening-before check is the official gate, according to the paper you were sent. Build your night around that, not around lunch-hour hold music.

After 5, try: the site on your phone, the site on a computer, the phone number. Screenshot the spinner and the timestamp. If a recorded line exists, listen all the way through; dismissals are sometimes buried after a menu. If you reach a person, write down their name, the time, and the words they use. Not because I know you will need it. Because memory gets sloppy when you're angry.

If the site is still dead at 5:30, you follow the default the letter already gave you for "if you cannot confirm." If the letter is silent on outages, you do not invent a loophole. You plan to report as directed and you tell work, "The status tool failed; I'm following the summons." That sentence is dull on purpose. Dull is harder to argue with than a theory.

The vacation day is a job problem layered on a court problem. I won't tell you your employer must restore the day. Ask whoever handles timekeeping, once, what they do when jury service is cancelled late. Use their actual policy if they have one. Don't promise yourself a refund.

Do not skip because you are morally sure they don't need you. Courts are not mind readers, and neither are you.

### User
Work wants a yes/no by Thursday for the staffing spreadsheet. Court won't know until the night before.

### Assistant
Give work the only honest cell for a spreadsheet: "jury — pending court confirmation the evening before." If they demand a fiction, you still shouldn't write one. You can add, "I will update after I check as instructed." Coverage can be planned with a pending flag.

If a coworker teases you for a "day off," let it go. You asked for a day because the summons told you to be available.

Keep the letter in your bag. Try the site after 5 on the night before. If you get a clear released message, save it and tell work you can come in. If you get nothing, you go where the letter said. You already did the adult part: you didn't pretend to know the law. Don't start now. Wait for the court's words, not mine.
