### User
There's a photo from a rooftop last summer. I'm in it. They're in it. It is my favorite picture of my own face in years. They messaged: please delete it everywhere, they look awful, they hate that it is still in my grid. It is on my profile. I don't want to be cruel. I also don't want to erase myself because they had a bad hair day. We are still friends. I need a reply that isn't a legal brief about whose photo it is.

### Assistant
You can take it off a public grid without deleting it from your own camera roll. Those are different asks.

Reply: "I can take it off my profile. I want to keep a copy for me. I'm in it too." If they only care about public, that solves it. If they want every file gone, that is a bigger ask. You can still say no to destroying your only good picture of your face.

I will not invent copyright or platform rules. If they tagged it, they can untag. Untag is often enough. You do not have to litigate.

Are they asking because of a job hunt, a breakup, or just the hair?

### User
They said they started dating someone who "doesn't like old photos of them with other people." I am not an ex. I am a friend in a group shot. There are two other people in the picture. They only messaged me. My grid is small. The photo is from July.

### Assistant
A new partner's preference is not an automatic delete key for your face. You can still be kind.

Offer the profile take-down. Offer to crop if that is possible and you still like your face. If cropping wrecks it, say so. Do not negotiate with the boyfriend through your friend. "I'm not comfortable deleting it from my camera. I'll remove it from the grid this week." Then do the grid this week so it is not a stall.

If they wanted a group consensus, they can message the others. You are not the librarian of the night.

Do not write a paragraph about not being an ex. One clause is enough: "It was a group night." Then the action.

If they escalate to "if you were a real friend," you can say, "I am. I'm also in the picture." Friendship includes both.

### User
If I keep it on my phone, am I being sneaky? They said "everywhere."

### Assistant
Keeping a personal copy of a picture of you is not a covert op. Everywhere is their word. You can define your yes: public gone, private kept. If that is a deal-breaker for them, they can tell you. You may have a sad week. You still get to have a photo of your own face.

Do not hide and lie "it's gone" while it sits in an album. Lying is sneaky. A stated keep is honest.

Reply today. Take it off the grid when you say you will. Keep the file. You are not cruel for existing in a rooftop picture. You are a person who liked how they looked in July. That can stay on a phone. Let the grid go. Let the camera roll stay.

### User
They sent a second text: "if it's still up at midnight I'm going to flip out." It is 8:40. The photo is still on the grid.

### Assistant
You already decided the public part. Take it off the grid now, not at 11:59 as a standoff. Reply: "Taking it off the profile tonight. I'm keeping a copy because I'm in it. I won't lie and say the file is gone." Then do the take-down so midnight is not a test. Their flip is their nervous system. Your honesty is the camera roll. Do not bargain every hour until twelve. Do the grid, send the sentence, mute if they keep moving the line to "everywhere." You can be a friend who heard them and still exist in a rooftop picture. 8:40 is a good time to click remove. Click it. Then go do something that is not a grid.
