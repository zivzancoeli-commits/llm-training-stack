### User
My kid is 14. Piano term is paid through December. Tonight after dinner they said they're done, the recitals are embarrassing, and they want the Tuesday 4:30 slot back for soccer. The teacher already sent the winter piece. I can hear them upstairs not practicing. I don't want to be the parent who bought a hobby as a trap. I also don't want to light the tuition on fire because a Wednesday mood hit.

### Assistant
A Wednesday mood and a paid term can both be real. You don't have to pick a villain tonight.

I'm not going to tell you the teacher must refund you. I don't know the studio policy and I won't invent it. Money already spent is a fact. The Tuesday slot is a fact. Whether piano has to continue as a identity is the actual question, and it's bigger than soccer.

Don't go upstairs with the receipt. Go upstairs with a time limit: ten minutes at the kitchen table, not a trial.

Have they said this before, or is tonight the first hard no?

### User
They grumbled in October. Tonight was the first "I'm quitting." They still like playing when no one is in the room. They hate the recital in the church basement. Soccer tryouts are in two weeks. The 4:30 lesson collides with a pickup scrimmage, not the official practice.

### Assistant
That's useful. They like the instrument. They hate the basement performance. The collision is a pickup scrimmage, not the only path to the team. Those are three different problems. Quitting the term as a bundle is the teenager solution because it's one sentence.

For the next ten minutes: sit down, no phones. You say, "I heard you want to quit. I paid through December. Help me separate recital, soccer, and piano at home." Then you listen. If they only want out of the church basement, that's a conversation with the teacher about skipping the recital, not about selling the bench. I can't promise the teacher will allow that. You can ask.

If the issue is Tuesday 4:30 versus pickup, ask whether another lesson time exists. Studios shuffle. They also sometimes don't. If they don't, you decide whether pickup soccer this term outranks a lesson you already bought. That's a family call. It is not a moral exam.

If they still want zero piano, you can finish the term as paid attendance — show up, no recital optional if the teacher agrees — and not re-enroll in January. That's not a trap. That's using what you bought and then stopping. Lighting tuition on fire is quitting tomorrow and still being mad in March. Finishing Tuesdays until December is boring, which is often the right speed.

Don't bargain with "one more year." Bargain with this calendar.

### User
They'll say I care more about the money than about them. That's the line. I can feel it coming. I do care about the money. The money is why we didn't do both.

### Assistant
You can admit the money without turning into a cashier. "I do care about the money because we chose piano for this term instead of stacking two paid things. That doesn't mean I don't care about you." Then stop. Don't over-explain the budget. Teens hear lectures as winning.

If they cry or slam a bench, you still don't have to solve soccer at 9 p.m. Tryouts in two weeks can wait until morning for a schedule look. Tonight you can agree to email the teacher about the recital and the Tuesday time. Action in writing beats a dramatic quit in the hallway.

I won't diagnose burnout or talent. I can tell you liking private playing and hating a basement recital is common. Treat that as specific. If you skip the recital and keep the lesson, you might keep the thing they still do when the house is empty. If you cancel everything to win the mood, you may get a kid who learns that a hard Wednesday ends a term.

Go upstairs only to invite them down. Kitchen table, ten minutes, three piles: recital, time slot, instrument. You already paid. Use the remaining Tuesdays on purpose, or change what those Tuesdays are for, with the teacher, in January at the latest. Don't make the receipt the villain. Don't make the kid the villain. Make the calendar visible. That's the adult job tonight.
