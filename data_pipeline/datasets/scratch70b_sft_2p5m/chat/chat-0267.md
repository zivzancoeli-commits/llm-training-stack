### User
I went to the stairwell to eat a granola bar at 12:10 because the break room is loud. There's a coworker from two teams over sitting on the landing, crying. We have said hi in the kitchen. I don't know their name for sure. I think it's Riley. They saw me. I have granola in my hand like a prop. I don't want to trap them in a talk. I also don't want to step over them like a fire extinguisher.

### Assistant
You already made eye contact. Stepping over is colder than the granola requires. You don't need their name spelled right to be a person.

Short is kind. "Hey. Do you want company or should I go to another floor?" Then you follow the answer. If they say go, you go. That's not rejection. That's them using the stairwell as a closed room that you accidentally opened.

Don't sit automatically. Sitting turns this into a session. Don't ask what happened. That's a pull. I'm not a therapist. I won't guess why they're crying. Work, phone, nothing you need to solve with a bar.

Is the landing empty besides you two, or are people using the stairs as a thoroughfare?

### User
Empty right now. Someone could come. They shrugged and didn't pick company or go. They're wiping their face with a sleeve. I offered the granola bar without thinking. They shook their head. I still have it. I feel huge in the doorway.

### Assistant
A shrug is not a yes to a meeting. Take it as "don't interrogate." You can sit two steps above or below, not hip to hip, and be quiet for a minute. Or you can say "I'll give you the landing. I'm around the kitchen if you want a tissue." Then you leave. Both are decent. Standing huge in the doorway is the worst of both: audience and blockage.

For the next ten minutes: pick leave-with-an-offer or sit-in-silence. If you sit, eat your granola like a normal lunch so this isn't a vigil. If someone comes, you both get a pass to stand and move. Stairwells are not offices.

Don't report this to their manager. A cry on a landing is not your HR ticket unless they ask you to walk them somewhere. Don't name them in Slack. Don't tell the loud break room.

Tissues: if the restroom is a flight down, you can fetch some without making a ceremony. Put them on a step. Don't hover.

If they start talking, listen. Don't one-up. Don't recruit them into a friendship they didn't ask for. After, you can say "hope the rest of the day is easier" and go back to work. That's enough poetry.

### User
If I leave I replay it as heartless. If I stay I replay it as nosy. They just said "sorry" to me which made it worse.

### Assistant
They said sorry because people apologize for occupying space with a face. You can answer "nothing to sorry for" and then still leave if that's cleaner. You're not heartless for giving the landing back. You're not nosy for asking once.

The replay will happen either way. Choose the version that lets them control the room: they got an option, they didn't pick company, you stop filling the door. Go to another floor with the granola. If they want you, they know you exist now. That's a thread if they use it. It isn't your job to sit until 12:40 proving you care.

Two teams over, maybe Riley. First name optional. Stairwell optional. You did the human thing: you stopped, you asked, you offered a bar. That's the whole decent script. Eat lunch. Let them have the concrete. If you see them later at the kettle, a normal hi is better than a meaningful look. Normal is the kindness after. Move your feet so the doorway is a doorway again.
