### User
Our roommate's cousin arrived with a duffel for "a week while they figure things out." That was six weeks ago. They sleep on the couch, use the shower at 7 when I need it, and now get mail here. My roommate says family is family. I say the lease has two names. I have been polite. I am done being polite in a vague way. I need a conversation that sets an end date without a blowup tonight.

### Assistant
A week that becomes month two is a third roommate you did not agree to. Family is a reason they asked. It is not a reason the couch is permanent.

Talk to your actual roommate first, not the cousin, unless the cousin is the one making plans. The roommate made the invitation. They own the end date.

Use numbers. "The week ended five weeks ago. I need the couch back by Sunday the 14th." A calendar date beats "soon." If they need more time, they propose a date you can accept or refuse. Open-ended figuring-it-out is how mail starts.

I will not invent occupancy law or whether mail creates tenancy. If you are worried about that, you can say you do not want more mail here, and you can look up local advice later. For tonight, the household ask is the date.

Are you two-on-a-lease, and is the cousin paying anything toward the couch?

### User
Two on the lease. Cousin bought pizza twice. Roommate thinks that counts. I work early. The shower is the daily fight. Tonight the cousin is at a movie. Roommate is home.

### Assistant
Tonight is the right night. Pizza is dinner, not rent.

Sit down, not in passing. "We need an end date. I'm asking for the 14th. The shower at 7 has to stop this week even before they leave — that's my work time." If you can live with a later date, name the latest: "Not past the 21st." Then be quiet.

If your roommate calls you heartless, you can say, "They've had six weeks. I'm not doing open-ended." If they ask the cousin to stay in your room somehow, no. Your room is not the overflow.

After you have an agreement, put it in a text so it is not a fog: "Confirming cousin is out by the 14th, and showers before 7:30 are mine on workdays." If the cousin should hear it too, your roommate can tell them. If your roommate won't, you can tell the cousin once, calmly, with the same date. You do not need a tribunal.

Do not wait for them to "figure things out." Figuring can happen from another couch.

### User
If they just don't leave on the 14th, what do I do that isn't me dragging a duffel to the curb like a villain?

### Assistant
You do not have to do theater with a bag. On the 15th you repeat the date to both of them. You ask the roommate what the next housing plan is. If there is none, that is the roommate's problem to solve that day, not yours to extend by default.

I will not script evictions. If it continues, you use whatever tools you already have: a written notice between roommates, a conversation with a landlord if that is appropriate in your building, or advice from a local tenant resource. Those are later. Tonight is a date.

Talk now, while the movie is on. Get the 14th in a text. Take your 7 a.m. shower tomorrow as if the rule already started. The week already ended. Name the new ending.

### User
Roommate said "can we do the end of the month." That is another two weeks on top of six. The cousin is still at the movie.

### Assistant
End of the month is a new ask. You can refuse it. "The 14th, not the 31st. Six weeks was already extra." If you can live with one extra week, name the 21st as the last date you will say out loud, then write it. Do not let month-end become month-three. When the cousin gets home, your roommate tells them, or you do, with the same calendar. The shower rule starts tomorrow morning either way. Pizza does not buy February. Hold a date that fits on a fridge calendar, not a vibe. Text the date before you sleep so the movie does not reset the talk.
