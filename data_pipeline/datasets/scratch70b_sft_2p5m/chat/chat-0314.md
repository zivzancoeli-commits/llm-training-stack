### User
The person next to me takes personal and vendor calls on speaker. I hear hold music, a dentist office, and yesterday a voicemail from their mother. I wear headphones. I can still hear it. I have tried the pointed look. They smiled. Our manager sits far away. I need to write today. I feel like asking makes me the office hall monitor.

### Assistant
Speaker at a desk is not a personality quirk when you cannot think. A pointed look already failed. Looks are not a policy.

You ask once, privately, like a person who needs a brain, not like a monitor: "When you take calls on speaker I hear the whole thing, including personal stuff. Could you use the handset or a meeting room?" Then you stop. If they say they need speaker because of a wrist or a habit, they can still use a room or earbuds. You do not need to diagnose their wrist.

I will not quote OSHA or a handbook I have not seen. If your office has booked rooms and a quiet policy, you can use those facts after you try the human sentence.

Are these all-day or a few times, and is there a room they already know how to book?

### User
A few times a day, 10–20 minutes. There are two small rooms that are often empty. They say speaker helps them "multitask" and they type while the dentist talks. I have a deadline at 5.

### Assistant
Multitask is them choosing their typing over your deadline. You can be blunt about today: "I have a 5 o'clock. Could you take the next one in a room?" Today is a concrete ask. The general habit can be the next sentence if they agree, or Thursday with the manager if they do not.

Do not reach over and hit speaker off. Do not blast white noise as a war. Do not cc the manager on a Slack that says "FYI Jordan's mom." If you escalate, you escalate with "I asked, it continued, I cannot concentrate," plus times, not the content of the voicemail. Repeating the mother's voicemail makes you the second speakerphone.

If they get embarrassed, good. Embarrassment is how people learn the mother voicemail traveled. You do not need to pile on.

Headphones plus a library playlist is still allowed while you wait to see if the ask works. Move to a room yourself if you can, for the deadline block. You should not have to flee forever. For a 5 o'clock, fleeing for two hours is allowed.

If the vendor calls are actually work, they can still not be on speaker. Work does not require a PA system.

### User
If I go to a room I lose the two monitors at my desk. That's why I'm stuck. And I don't want them to mimic me in a baby voice later. This office does that.

### Assistant
Baby-voice offices are real and small. You still get to ask. If they mimic, that is a manager conversation about respect, not about speakerphones only. One thing at a time if you can: speaker today, mimic if it happens.

Two monitors are a reason to fix their behavior rather than exile yourself every time. For this afternoon, if they have a call in the next hour, the room ask is still the cheapest path. If they refuse, you take a laptop to a room for the hardest hour and eat the monitor loss once to hit 5. That is triage, not a new lifestyle.

For the next ten minutes: say the one-sentence ask, book a room for a 90-minute focus block if the calendar allows, put on headphones, and start the hardest paragraph of the deadline. If a call starts on speaker after you asked, you can repeat, "Room?" once. Then you move or you message the manager a dry note.

You are not a hall monitor. You are a person who heard someone's mother on voicemail. That should not be a workplace genre. Ask, focus, hit 5. The smile after the look was the answer that looks do not work. Use words.
