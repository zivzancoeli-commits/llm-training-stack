### User
My sister wants the car Thursday after school. That's the second time this week. I have a paid dog-walking block at four that I actually need. Gas is already ugly. I don't hate her. I just can't keep treating the tank like a wish. Talk to me like I can handle a real plan, not a speech about family.

### Assistant
The car is a budget object. Borrowing it is not free even when nobody hands over cash, because fuel and your Thursday money are both real.

Before we write the rule, I need one fact I shouldn't guess: is the car in your name, a parent car you usually drive, or something you both chip into — and is Thursday a job for her, a ride for friends, or something else? The plan has to match the use, not the guilt.

A tight-budget plan is three pieces, not a novel: who gets it when, how the tank comes back, and what happens if she blows it. Don't invent a "fair" hourly rate. Talk in fuel and clock time.

### User
Parent car, but I'm the one who fills it because I have the walking money. She has a job interview at 3:30 across town. She'd be back maybe 5:30. My walks start at 4. If I cancel, I lose that day's cash, which is how I buy gas in the first place. She said she can "get me back," which is not a system.

### Assistant
You're not the villain for noticing the tank. She's not a villain for needing a ride to an interview. The mess is two jobs on one car and a vague IOU.

Here's a plan that fits a tight week. She does not get Thursday at 3:30 if that cancels your 4:00 pay. That's the money that puts fuel in the thing she wants. She takes the bus, a parent ride, or she moves the interview if the office will move it. I won't invent whether they will. She can ask.

If she can shift the interview to Friday after your walks, she can take the car then. In exchange, she returns it with at least the same fuel you handed her, measured the cheap way: photo of the gauge before and after. If the needle dropped, she puts fuel in before the keys come back, even if it's a small amount. You don't need an app. You need a photo and a habit.

If she truly has no cash this week, don't pretend fuel appears. Then the car stays with the person who filled it, or she swaps a chore that actually saves money — not "I'll get you back." One swap. Groceries with you. Not five promises.

Say it straight: "Thursday at 3:30 no. That's my walk money, which is the gas. Friday after four yes, photos both ways, same fuel. If you can't do fuel, we need a different ride. I can't float another empty tank."

### User
What if she says I'm choosing dogs over her future. That's the line that makes me cave.

### Assistant
That's a guilt hook, not a schedule. You can answer the feeling without handing over 3:30. "The interview matters. The car still has to have gas. Thursday I need the paid block. Friday is the offer." You don't do a speech about her future. You don't do a speech about dogs. Clock and tank.

If she caves you anyway and takes it Thursday, you lose the day's cash and you buy the next tank out of food money. That's not generosity. That's a hole. Sharp version: you already know the hole. Don't step in it because a sentence was loud.

If a parent can drive her Thursday, that's the other path. You're not required to be the entire transportation department.

If she feels treated like a kid, stay flat: "I'm treating the car like a bill. I'm not doing the maybe." A scheduled Friday with photos is a plan. "I owe you" is not.

### User
Thursday no, Friday yes with gauge photos and same fuel, or she buses. I can say that once in the kitchen, not in a paragraph I'll regret.

### Assistant
Say it once. Stick to it even when it's slightly inconvenient. The plan only works if you don't undo it with another maybe. You already knew the rule. You just needed it out loud so it counts as a decision instead of a mood. Tight budget means the paid hour wins the car on Thursday. That's not drama. That's math you can see.
