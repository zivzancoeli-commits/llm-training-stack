### User
There's a guy on the bench next to the squat rack. He left three plates on each side of the bar I need. He's on his phone on a different bench now, ten feet away, earbuds in. The sign says rerack. I've never asked a stranger to rerack. I usually just do it myself and seethe. The plates are 45s. I can move them. I don't want to. This is the first time I want to use my mouth. He's bigger than me. I don't want a scene. I also don't want to be the unpaid intern of the gym.

### Assistant
You ask once, close enough that he can hear through an earbud, not a shout across the room.

Walk over. Don't stand over him. "Hey — are you still on that squat bar? If not, can you rerack those 45s? I need the rack." Point at the bar. That's it. If he didn't hear, a repeat, same words, slightly louder, not a speech about the sign.

If he says "in a minute," you can wait a real minute or you can say "I need it now, I can slide them if you're done." If you slide them, you're back to intern, but you asked. That's still a first time. If he gets rude, you don't debate manners. You either wait for a staff person or you move the plates and you don't make it your personality.

I won't invent gym rules as law. The sign is already there. You can mention it if he shrugs: "Sign says rerack." Once.

Don't film him. Don't tap an earbud with a finger. That's a fight starter.

Is staff visible, and do you actually need that rack or is there another free one?

### User
Other rack is taken. Staff is at the desk behind glass. I walked over. He pulled one earbud out. I haven't spoken. My hands are on my own water bottle like it's a microphone.

### Assistant
Use the mouth. Water bottle down.

The sentence. Then silence. Don't pad with "sorry" and "when you get a chance" and a laugh. Last year's you would have already been moving 45s. Let him answer.

If he doesn't look up, "I need the squat rack" is the second line. Then you wait two seconds. If he waves you off, you can go to the glass: "The squat bar is loaded and the person isn't reracking. Can you ask?" That's using staff, not a scene. You're not a cop. You're a member who needs a bar.

If you're scared of size, staff is the right path after one ask, not a moral failure. One ask is the first time. You don't have to win a dominance game.

Don't rerack while staring at him to teach a lesson. That's a sitcom. Either he does it, you do it to get your set, or staff does the ask. Pick based on his face in the next five seconds, not on a speech you rehearsed in the locker room.

### User
He said "go ahead, you can take them off." That's him assigning me the work. I asked him to rerack. Do I do it anyway so I can squat, or stand there?

### Assistant
You can say, "I'd like you to take them off, that's the rerack." If he doesn't move, you decide: time or principle. If you need the set before your bus, you strip the bar and you don't write a novel. You already asked. If you have time, you wait with your water and you look at the desk. You don't stand over him.

Doing the plates after a no isn't you failing the first time. The first time was the sentence. The intern feeling is real. You can still squat.

Next thirty seconds: the ask is already out. Either he stands up or you strip the 45s or you get the glass involved. Then you squat. You don't owe the gym a trial. You owe yourself a bar. Get the words out — you did — then get the work done. That's a completed rerack conversation even if your hands still move iron.
