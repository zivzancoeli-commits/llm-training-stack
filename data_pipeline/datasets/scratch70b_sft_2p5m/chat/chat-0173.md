### User
I'm supposed to get a photo in front of this mural for my sister's graduation collage. I'm alone. There are people around. I always freeze and then I either don't ask or I ask in this tiny voice and they take a blurry shot of my elbow. I start next week at a job where I will have to talk to humans who don't already like me, so I would like to not treat a photograph like a hostage negotiation. Tell me what to actually say and do. I have like eight minutes before the light goes weird.

### Assistant
Hold on — before I give you a script. Are you asking for a solo shot of you, or do you need the mural and your face both sharp, like a poster, not a selfie-arm crop?

### User
Solo. Me in front of the mural. My sister wants it to look like I meant to be there, not like I photobombed a wall. Phone camera, not a fancy camera. I don't know these people. There's a couple taking their own pictures and a guy on a bench with headphones.

### Assistant
Okay. That's enough to be useful. You're not directing a campaign. You're asking for thirty seconds of a phone.

Don't start with the headphones guy unless he's the only option. Headphones are a "busy" sign. The couple already has their phones out, which means they understand the assignment. Walk to a pause, not into their kiss.

Words, intern-plain, out loud once in your head then once at them: "Hi — sorry to interrupt — would you mind taking a photo of me in front of this wall? I'll show you the frame on the screen. It should take thirty seconds." Then you shut up. The extra joke is how people talk themselves out of the ask.

If they say no, you say "thanks anyway" and you pivot to the next body. A no is not a review of your personality. It's a person who doesn't want to hold a phone.

When someone says yes, you don't hand them the unlocked life of your photos. Open the camera, set it on photo, not video, not portrait mode if you don't know what that does to a mural. Hold the phone how you want it held: horizontal if the mural is wide, which it probably is. Point at the spot you'll stand. "If you can get the yellow bird and my head, that's the shot." One instruction. Not five. Then you walk to the mark, you look at the lens, you don't look at their face.

Take three. "One more, then I'm good." People rush the first one. You check the screen in front of them, not after they've walked off. If your eyes are closed, you say so: "Eyes closed on that one — one more?" That's allowed. You're not being precious. You're doing the errand.

If the photo is dark or the mural is a smear, don't lecture them on cameras. Thank them, take the phone, try one more stranger or do a timer on a ledge if there's a ledge that isn't going to eat your phone. I don't know this sidewalk. I'm not going to invent a safe ledge.

### User
What if I sound like I'm giving orders. Also what do I do with my hands. I always look like I'm being booked.

### Assistant
You're not giving orders. You gave a frame and a time. That's how you keep a stranger from photographing your elbow. Soften the first sentence, keep the second sentence clear. "Would you mind" is the door. "Yellow bird and my head" is the map. Interns get in trouble when they skip the map and then apologize for the result.

Hands: hold something real or put them in your pockets like a person waiting for a bus, not like you're hiding contraband. If you have a bag, let it hang. Chin a little away from your chest. Think "I'm meeting someone in two minutes," not "smile for the collage." Your sister can crop. She cannot invent a neck.

Light going weird means you do this now, not after you workshop your vibe. Pick the couple, ask, three shots, thank you, check, leave. If both of them look busy in a way that would make you feel like a pest, wait until they lower the phones, then ask. If they never lower the phones, the person walking toward you with nothing in their ears is next.

You do not owe them your sister's graduation speech. You do not explain the job starting Monday. Thirty seconds, yellow bird, thank you. That's the whole human interaction. Go before the mural turns brown.
