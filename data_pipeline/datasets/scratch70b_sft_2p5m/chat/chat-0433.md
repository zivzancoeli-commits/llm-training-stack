### User
I'm at my uncle's for a birthday cake. He asked who I'm voting for. I gave a short answer. He's been on me for forty minutes. He wants me to "think again" before Tuesday. He keeps sliding printouts across the table like they're homework. My aunt is doing dishes louder than necessary. I already said I'm not discussing it. He heard that as a warm-up. I have to drive home with him still in my head. How do I end this without a scene that becomes the story of the party?

### Assistant
You already said you're not discussing it. The next move is to stop providing new material, not to write a better essay.

Don't debate the printouts. Don't fact-check at a cake. That's how forty minutes happened. "I'm not changing my vote at this table. I'm done talking about it." Then you physically leave the topic: stand, offer to take plates, go to the bathroom, step outside, ask someone else about the cake. Movement plus silence on the subject is more effective than a clever last word.

If he follows with "just one article," you don't open it. "I'm not reading that tonight." If he says you're uninformed, you don't prove your syllabus. "You can think that. I'm still done."

I won't tell you how to vote. I won't invent election law, deadlines, or what your ballot "must" look like. I also won't diagnose your uncle. He's pushing. You need an exit, not a TED talk.

Are you staying overnight, or can you leave the house tonight?

### User
I can leave in twenty minutes without it looking like I stormed out if I'm careful. Car is around the corner. He already told my cousin I'm "being difficult." The printouts are still next to the forks. I feel like if I go now I'm proving him right.

### Assistant
Leaving on time is not a confession. Staying to win is how you drive angry.

Use the twenty minutes on logistics, not conversion. Thank your aunt. Hug whoever you came to see. If he starts again, you use the same sentence, then you pick up your keys. "I'm heading out. Thanks for having me." If he wants a political goodbye, he can have a short one: "I'm not talking about ballots. I'll text about the game this week." Then you walk.

You don't owe a rebuttal to "difficult." He can tell the cousin whatever he wants. Your job is not to sit still until he feels heard. He can feel unheard. That's allowed.

If he follows you to the door with one more question, you don't answer the content. "I'm going. Love you. Not doing this." Then the door. You can love someone and still not hold office hours in their dining room.

Don't text the group chat a manifesto from the car. That's the scene, just delayed. Drive. If you need to dump the adrenaline, call a friend who didn't hand you paper.

### User
What do I do when he texts a thread of links at 11 p.m.? He will. He did this in August. I muted him last time and then unmuted because my mom said it was rude.

### Assistant
Mute is not rude. It's how phones work. You can leave him muted through Tuesday. You can also reply once: "I'm not discussing votes over text. I'll see you at the next thing that isn't this." Then mute. If your mom calls that rude, she can take the links. You don't have to be the family's public inbox.

If you unmute later, you still don't have to answer the stack. A birthday thank-you can exist without a policy debate attached.

I won't tell you to hide your vote or to announce it. That's yours. What I will tell you is that "think again" at a cake is not a civic duty you failed. It's a relative who wants a conversion. You already declined. Keep declining with fewer words each time.

Next twenty minutes: keys, aunt, "not doing this," car. Leave the printouts by the forks. They're his homework, not yours. Get home with your vote the same as it was when you arrived. That's a successful party exit, even if he tells a story. You don't have to star in it.
