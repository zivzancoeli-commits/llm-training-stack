### User
A friend just texted me in the break room. They want to borrow money. Not a coffee amount. Enough that I would notice it missing from my account. They said "I'll pay you back Friday" which is what they said last time, and last time Friday became three Fridays. I am tired, I have twelve minutes left on this break, and I do not want to be the villain or the ATM. Walk me through the next ten minutes like you are sitting in this chair.

### Assistant
Alright. Next ten minutes, not a life philosophy.

Minute one: do not reply yet. Put the phone face down. Drink whatever is in the cup. Your first draft will be either a panic yes or a mean no, and both of those are leftovers from being tired, not a decision.

Minute two: open the banking app and look at the actual number. If paying them means skipping a bill, groceries, or parking you already owe, that is a no. You need the math, not a speech.

Minute three: remember last time in one sentence, not a courtroom. They were late. You were annoyed. You still like them. All three can be true.

Minute four: decide the category. Gift, loan with a date, or no. A loan you do not believe will come back is a gift you have not admitted. If you cannot afford a gift of that size, you are not loaning it.

I am not going to invent what you should spare. I do not know your rent or theirs. Anyone quoting a fair amount from the internet is guessing.

### User
The number would make rent tight, not impossible. They know I get paid Thursday. That is probably why they said Friday. I feel like they timed it. Also they are not a bad person. Their car died. I get it. I just do not want this to become the way we talk.

### Assistant
Minute five: write the text in notes, not in the thread.

If it is a no because rent gets tight: "I can't float that this week. I hope the car thing gets sorted. I can help look at bus times or a shop after work if you want, but I can't lend the money." That is a full answer, not a character review. You can think they timed the paycheck and still leave that out. Accusations turn a no into a fight, and you still have to go back to your desk.

If you want a smaller yes you can actually lose: name the number, call it a gift, skip Friday. "I can send you X as a gift. I can't do a loan right now."

Minute six: do not add "maybe next week" unless you mean it. A soft no trains both of you to reopen this every payday.

Minute seven: send it. Sitting on the draft until the break ends is how people type "sure" from their chair just to stop the ping.

### User
What if they reply immediately with "please" and a longer story? I have four minutes after I send this. I am bad at second texts. I start explaining my whole budget like I owe them a spreadsheet.

### Assistant
Minute eight: if they reply with a longer story, read it. Do not match the length. One line: "I hear you. I still can't do it this week." Do not narrate your rent or grocery list. Over-explaining invites a debate with each line. A short no is harder to pick apart, and kinder, because they do not sit through your finances on a break.

If they get sharp, you still do not owe a debate. "I'm going back to work. I hope it works out." Phone in a pocket, not face up on the desk.

Minute nine: if you offered a bus look-up or a shop name, do that later. Real help is a calendar block. A paragraph is just guilt with line breaks.

Minute ten: stand up, rinse the cup, go back. You are not a villain for protecting rent, and not an ATM for a Thursday deposit. You handled a money ask in the time the break actually gave you. Tonight you can think about a longer talk. Not in the remaining two minutes with a vending machine humming.
