### User
I'm an intern nannying for the weekend. The parents flew back from a trip with a three-year-old who is wide awake at 2 a.m. and melting down at breakfast. They said "just explain jet lag" and left me a note with a bunch of words like circadian and melatonin. I don't want to lecture a toddler. I also don't want to sound fake-nice. Kind and specific, please, like I'm new and need the actual sentences.

### Assistant
I'll give you sentences a three-year-old can hold, and I'll translate the note so you don't accidentally say science-class words at 2 a.m.

Jet lag, plain: their body still thinks it's the old palce's time. The sun and the meals here don't match that clock yet. That's why 2 a.m. can feel like afternoon, and breakfast can feel like the middle of the night. I won't pretend I know how many hours they shifted. You can ask the parents later. For tonight, you don't need the number.

Melatonin is a sleep hormone people mention. I am not going to tell you to give a child anything. That's not your intern call. If the note mentions drops, leave them unless the parents already wrote a dose and time you'd put in a log. Don't improvise medicine. Use light, food, and calm.

Kind and specific to the kid, not a speech: "Your body is still on trip time. Over there it might be play time. Here the house is dark and it's sleep time. That's a mixed-up clock. It will get less mixed-up. I'm here. We're going to do quiet-dark now."

That's kind because you named the problem without blaming them for being "bad at bedtime." That's specific because you said what you're doing next: quiet-dark, you staying.

If they ask why, keep one cause: "The airplane moved us to a place where the sun is in a different spot." You can point at a window. Night: "Dark means body-sleep here." Morning: "Light means body-awake here, even if it feels wrong." Skip "circadian." That's for the parents' note, not the child.

### User
What do I actually do at 2 a.m. so I'm not just saying words?

### Assistant
Words plus a boring routine. Toddlers believe repetition more than explanations.

Lights stay dim. You don't turn on the kitchen like it's daytime. You don't offer a full breakfast at 2 a.m. unless the parents said they were starving on a schedule. A sip of water is fine. A party is not.

Pick one quiet activity they already know: a book, a stuffed animal, humming, a back rub if they like touch. Keep your voice low. Don't whisper-panic. Kind is steady, not sugary.

Name the clock without numbers if numbers start a fight: "It's still night in this house." If they scream for the park: "Park is for after breakfast when it's light. Now is bed-room." Kind is not yes. Kind is no mockery, no shame, still holding the night.

If they cry: "You're mad the clock is wrong. Mad is okay. We still stay in night mode." Repeat. Repeating is not failing. Use their usual song, not a new clever one. New stimulation is daytime energy.

Morning: daylight, breakfast at the house's normal time, "Today we practice this house's time." Follow their nap note. I won't invent a nap length.

If the child is unsafe — window, street, breathing that scares you — get them safe, then call the parents. Jet lag is not an emergency by itself. Unsafe behavior is.

### User
Give me a morning line for when they refuse cereal because "it's night."

### Assistant
"Your mouth thinks it's night. Look — the window is bright. In this house, bright window means cereal time. You don't have to like it. The cereal can sit. I'll sit with you."

Then you sit. Don't turn it into a comedy show. Don't force the spoon. Offer it again in a few minutes. Kind and specific is: window, house rule, cereal available, you present.

If they eat two bites, don't announce a victory like they won a prize. "Two bites. That's breakfast starting." Then move to the next ordinary thing: clothes, teeth if that's the routine.

You're new, so you might over-explain the note. The kid needs dim night, bright morning, the same few sentences, and an adult who isn't faking a grin.

One intern update, not jargon: "Awake 2–3:30, stayed dim, water, back to bed. Breakfast was slow. No meds given."

### User

### Assistant
