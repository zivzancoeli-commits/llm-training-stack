### User
I borrowed a library laptop for a week. Last night I knocked a glass of water onto the keyboard. I turned it upside down, dried it with a shirt, and left it closed. This morning it smells warm and the power light is weird. It's due back in two hours. I didn't buy the optional damage thing because I thought I was careful. I feel like if I tell the truth they'll ban me, and if I lie they'll plug it in and know. What do I actually say at the desk?

### Assistant
Tell the truth at the desk. Short, chronological, no autobiography.

"I spilled water on the keyboard last night. I dried it and didn't keep using it. It smelled warm this morning and the light looks off. I don't know if it works." Put it on the counter. Do not power it up as a demo unless they ask. More power after a spill is how you turn a maybe into a definitely.

Do not say "it's probably fine." Do not say "my roommate did it." Do not cry as a strategy. Staff have seen spills. They have a form.

I will not invent the library's fine schedule or whether they ban people. Ask them what happens next. Write down what they say. If they tell you to talk to a supervisor, talk to the supervisor. Same story. Same length.

Bring your ID and the charger that came with it. Missing chargers become a second problem.

Did any liquid hit the ports, or was it mostly the keys? And is it still due in two hours, or can you call ahead?

### User
Mostly keys. A little ran toward the trackpad. I didn't call. I thought calling would make it official. I can get there in forty minutes. I used it for a take-home exam that I already submitted.

### Assistant
Calling makes it official in a useful way. Official is coming anyway when you hand over a warm laptop.

Call now, from the sidewalk or the bus: "I'm returning a loaner with a water spill to the keyboard. Who should I check in with?" Then go. Two hours of silence does not unspill water.

At the desk, repeat the same four facts: spill, dried, warm smell, odd light. If they ask you to fill a form, fill it. If they ask whether you want a replacement loaner, only say yes if you still need a machine. You already submitted the exam. Do not invent a second emergency.

Do not offer a random amount of money at the counter. Let them tell you the process. I will not guess a replacement cost. If they quote something, you can ask how payment works and whether there is a review. Asking is not a fight.

If you are scared of a ban, you can ask, "Does this affect my borrowing?" You may get an honest "we'll see" or a policy sentence. Either is better than a lie that collapses when they open the lid.

The take-home is done. That part is safe. This part is property. Treat it like property.

### User
There's a line. The person at the desk is a student worker. I don't want to dump this on them.

### Assistant
Student workers are the desk. You still tell them. They will fetch a supervisor if they are supposed to. That is the job. You are not dumping a trauma. You are returning equipment with a known problem.

If you whisper "can I speak to someone about a damaged loaner," they can pull you aside. Fine. Same facts.

Do not wait for a quieter hour and miss the due time. Late plus wet is two issues.

Go now. Call if you have twenty minutes on the way. Hand it over. Tell the spill. Let them unplug the mystery. You were careful until you weren't. That is most of the laptop stories in that building. Honesty is the only version that does not get worse when they press the power button after you leave.
