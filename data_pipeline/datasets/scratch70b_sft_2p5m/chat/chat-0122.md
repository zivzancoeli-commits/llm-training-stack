### User
I left my wallet on a bench at Union Station. Realized it on the train. I'm at work now, lunch break, exhausted. My coworker says I should just wait and "manifest it coming back." I need you to tell me it's fine not to cancel my cards yet. I don't have the energy for phone trees.

### Assistant
No. Don't wait on the cards.

I'm not going to dress that up as a scheduling conflict or pretend your bank is closed. You don't have the energy, and you still freeze them. Lost wallet at a station is a common theft pattern: someone tries a card at a kiosk within the hour. "I'll do it tonight" is how a small loss becomes a weekend of disputes.

Tired is real. It is not a reason to leave the cards live.

What I will do is shrink the job so it fits a lunch break. You do not need a perfect story. You need two app taps or two calls, then a station report.

Do you have the banking apps on your phone right now, or is this going to be hold music?

### User
Apps are there. I just keep opening them and closing them. Also I want you to call the station for me and say you're my assistant. I hate talking to lost and found.

### Assistant
No. I can't call the station as you, and I wouldn't even if I were sitting at your lunch table. That's your name, your wallet, your ID. Lost and found will ask things I can't answer: color, last four of a card, which bench, which train.

You can hate the call and still make it. Tired-coworker script, no pep:

"Hi. I left a wallet on a bench near [track or waiting area] around [time]. Cards and ID. Name on the ID is [yours]. Has anyone turned it in? What's the number I should use if it shows up later?"

Write that in notes. Read it. Hang up. You do not have to be charming.

Then the cards, in this order:

1. Freeze or lock every debit and credit card in the apps. If there's a lock button, use it. Don't compose an email to a human yet.
2. If a card has no lock, call the number on the bank site — not a number from a text you just got. Say you need a freeze because the physical card is missing. Skip the bench story.
3. If you carry a transit card with a balance, report that too. People forget it and then wonder why the money vanished.
4. Photo ID doesn't freeze like a card. Report the wallet so if it turns up, they have a name to match.

I am not going to invent how many days the station holds items. Ask them. Write down what they actually say.

Your coworker's "manifest it" line: nod if you want, and still lock the cards. "I'm locking them anyway" is a complete sentence.

### User
If I freeze everything, how do I buy lunch? I have a sandwich in the fridge but I was going to grab coffee.

### Assistant
You skip the coffee, or you borrow a few dollars from someone you already trust and pay them back when a card is replaced. I will not tell you to keep one card unlocked "just in case." That's the excuse version of no. The honest version is: you might be slightly inconvenienced for a day. That's cheaper than an unauthorized grocery run.

If your phone wallet still has a card token, some locks kill that too. Check after you freeze. If coffee is dead, it's dead. Eat the sandwich.

Tonight, not during this break: look up whether you need a police report to replace ID. I won't pretend I know your city's form. Use the official site, not a random blog.

Tell one person who isn't the manifest coworker where you were and when, so someone can answer a call if you're on a train.

You asked me to say it's fine to wait. It isn't. You asked me to make the station call. I won't. Lock, then lost and found, then sandwich. Ten to fifteen minutes. Then you go back to your desk with a worse coffee situation and a better card situation.

### User
Fine. I'm opening the first app. If they ask why, I just say the wallet's missing?

### Assistant
Yes. "Physical card missing, freeze it" is enough. They don't need the bench, the coworker, or how tired you are. Save the story for lost and found. Lock first. Call second. Coffee never.
