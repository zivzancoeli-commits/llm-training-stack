### User
We've been together two years. My uncle calls my partner "your friend," or "the guy," or last week a name that belongs to my ex. He laughs. My partner smiled the first few times and now goes quiet in the car after. Dinner is Saturday. I have been pretending not to notice because I didn't want to "make it political." It is not political. It is a name. How do I correct this without a blowup, and what do I owe my partner before we walk in?

### Assistant
You owe your partner a plan, not another silent car.

Before Saturday, say: "If he skips your name, I will say it. You don't have to laugh. We can leave if it keeps going." Then do that. Your job in the room is the correction, not their performance of being unbothered.

In the moment: "His name is Marin." Or whatever the name is. Immediate. Calm. Every time. Do not add "you know that." Do not add a psychology lecture. The name is the correction.

If uncle says "I can't keep it straight," you can say, "It's Marin. Same as last time." Then talk about the potatoes. You are not required to process his memory on dessert.

I will not diagnose him. Forgetting can be age, can be hostility, can be sloppiness. The intervention is the same: you supply the name, you stop laughing along, you do not outsource the work to your partner.

Does your partner want you to handle it, or do they want to say their own name?

### User
They want me to handle it because when they say it, uncle looks at me like it's a joke we're sharing. The ex's name was close — one syllable off — which makes it worse. Mom says "he does that to everyone." He does not do that to my cousin's wife.

### Assistant
Then you handle it, and you stop sharing the joke with your face. No smirk. No "oh uncle." Deadpan name, every time.

Mom's "he does that to everyone" is a peace treaty that costs Marin. You can tell Mom on the phone before Saturday: "Please use Marin's name. I'm going to correct Uncle. Don't laugh it off." She may not help. She has now been asked. That matters later in the driveway.

If uncle uses the ex's name, correct faster, not funnier. "It's Marin. Not [ex]." Then you change the topic. Do not explain the relationship history. He knows enough to be sloppy. You do not give him a map.

If it happens a third time at the same meal, you can add the exit: "We're going to head out." You already told Marin that was on the table. Follow through once so it is real. Leaving is not a tantrum. It is the end of a loop.

You can still be pleasant about rolls and traffic. Warmth and a name are compatible. Warmth and a missing name are the thing that is currently failing.

### User
Marin asked if we should skip Saturday. I kind of want to go because it's my aunt's birthday. I also saw Marin's face last time.

### Assistant
Aunt's birthday is real. Marin's face is also real. You can go for cake and not stay for the third "friend." Tell Marin: "I'd like to be there for Aunt for an hour. If he does the name thing twice, we leave. You can wait in the car anytime and I'll follow." Give them an out that does not require a scene. An hour with a planned exit is different from an open-ended roast.

If Marin would rather skip, skip. A birthday is not a hostage situation. You can send food, call your aunt, and not spend the evening teaching a grown man a two-syllable word.

If you go, you are the name-sayer. Practice it in the driveway so it is not your first time using your voice. "His name is Marin." That is the whole speech. Use it until it sticks or until you are in the car. You do not need uncle to like the lesson. You need your partner to not go quiet all the way home.
