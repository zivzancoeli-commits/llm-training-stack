### User
Sunday lunch at my aunt's. I sat down, picked up a roll, and my uncle said, "Watching the carbs now, or still doing whatever that was last year?" Two cousins laughed. My mom looked at her plate. I smiled like an idiot and ate the roll anyway, which felt like both defiance and a trap. I have to go back next month for a birthday. I don't want a scene. I also don't want to sit there being narrated. What do I say in the moment, and what do I say to my mom after, because she always says "that's just how he is"?

### Assistant
You do not have to win lunch. You have to stop playing along.

In the moment, a short line works better than a speech, because speeches feed him. Try: "Don't comment on my body." Or: "I'm eating lunch." Then you pick up the roll, or you don't, for you, not as a performance. The smile is optional. The smile is what tells the table the joke landed.

If you want a slightly softer version that still shuts the door: "That one's not funny to me. Pass the butter." Then you change the object. Butter is boring. Boring is the goal.

Do not explain your year, your health, or your plate. Explanations are invitations to coach. I will not diagnose you or him. I will say that body talk at a table is optional, and you can decline it.

Are you staying overnight at their house next month, or can you leave when the cake is done?

### User
We can leave. We drive. Mom will want to stay for coffee. Uncle does this every few months, not only to me — he did it to my cousin when she was pregnant. Mom's line is always "don't make it a thing."

### Assistant
It is already a thing. He made it a thing. "Don't make it a thing" means "please absorb it so I don't have to hear a conflict." You can love your mom and still not take that job.

Before the birthday, tell her in the car, not in the doorway: "If he comments on my body, I'm going to say don't. I'm not going to debate. If it continues, I'm going outside." She can want coffee. You can still go outside. You are not taking her hostage. You are describing your own feet.

At the table, one sentence, then stop. If he says "sensitive," you do not put on a seminar. You can repeat once: "I asked you not to." Repeating is not a scene. A scene is him chasing a reaction. Starve the chase.

If cousins laugh, you do not have to police them. You can look at your plate. You can also say, to the air, "I'm good without the commentary." Then ask someone a concrete question — the birthday person's work, the dog, the parking. Move the camera.

Afterward, if Mom says "that's just how he is," you can say, "I know how he is. I'm still not sitting through it." You do not need her to agree in the driveway. You need her to hear that you will not play along next time either.

### User
What if I freeze again and the smile happens before I can think?

### Assistant
Then you repair thirty seconds later, which still counts. "Actually — don't comment on my body." Late is allowed. You are not a courtroom with a shot clock.

If the smile already happened, do not punish yourself by eating or not eating to correct the record. Eat if you want the roll. Next month, decide the sentence in the car so it is pre-loaded. You can even text it to yourself. Reading a line off your phone at a table is slightly awkward and completely fine.

You can skip coffee. You can leave after cake. You can take a walk around the block when he starts. None of that requires a family trial. It requires you treating your body as not-public at lunch. "That's just how he is" can stay his problem. Your plate does not have to be the entertainment.
