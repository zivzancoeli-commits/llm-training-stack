### User
There's a jackhammer in the lot next door. It's 7:10 p.m. The paper in our lobby last month said work was 8 to 4. Nothing on the door this week. The building group chat is conspiracy theories. My baby just fell asleep and then didn't. Partner wants to "go over there." I want to not get into a fight with a crew. I also want the hammer to stop or a real explanation. What do we do tonight that isn't a mob and isn't me pretending the lobby flyer is still true?

### Assistant
Tonight you document and you use a phone, not a confrontation with a jackhammer.

Note the time, the address of the lot, that you had a lobby notice for 8–4, that nothing was posted this week. Video with a clock if you want the file. Then you call whoever you'd call for a non-emergency noise complaint in your city, or the number on the construction fence if there is one. I will not invent whether they have a variance, what hours are legal, or what a variance "must" look like. I won't quote a decibel. You can still say: "Night jackhammer, we were told 8 to 4, baby, no posted notice." Let the person on the line tell you if there's a permit path.

Don't send the partner "over there" to argue with a crew. Crews don't reverse a city paper because a parent is angry. A site number or a city number is the adult path. If the fence has a permit box, photograph it from the sidewalk. Don't hop a fence.

Group chat: one factual message, not a theory. "Hammer at 7:10, no new notice in our lobby. Calling the number on the fence." Then mute the conspiracy for the night.

Is there a number on the fence, and is the work on your building or the empty lot?

### User
Empty lot. There's a plastic sleeve with a faded permit. I can't read the hours. Partner is putting on shoes. Baby is up. I can go to the sidewalk with a phone camera. I don't want partner at the machines.

### Assistant
Partner stays on the baby if possible. You take the sidewalk photo. That's a split that doesn't need a speech about bravery.

Call from the sidewalk or from your kitchen after the photo. If you can't read the permit, say that on the call. Ask them to read the hours if they can see a record. If they say a variance allows evenings, you still don't have to like it. You can ask how long tonight's work is scheduled. I won't promise they'll stop. You might get a time. You might get a shrug. You still tried a number instead of a fight.

If partner insists on going, they can stand with you on the public sidewalk, not at the bit. No yelling into the lot. One of you holds the baby at home. A baby plus a jackhammer plus a crew is how a bad video happens.

If the city line is closed at 7:10, you leave a message if that's a thing, and you email the building manager: flyer said 8–4, hammer now, please post whatever they know. Building may not control the lot. They can still post. Group chat gets the photo of the sleeve, not a novel.

### User
I'll photograph and call. If they say it's allowed until 8, do we just live with 50 minutes of hammer?

### Assistant
If they say it's allowed, you still live with it for tonight unless they choose to stop. You don't get to repeal a permit from a stoop. You can close windows, run a fan, take the baby to the back room, split the remaining minutes. Tomorrow you can ask the building or the city office, in daylight, for the actual hours in writing. That's the boring follow-up. Not a variance lecture you invented.

Tonight: photo, call, no lot fight, baby in the quietest room. The flyer was last month. Tonight is a hammer. Treat the 50 minutes as weather you documented. Then you sleep in shifts if you have to. You don't need a mob. You need a picture of a faded sleeve and a number that isn't your partner's voice at a machine. Get the shoes off the partner if you can. Get the file. That's the whole unposted evening, without a conspiracy.
