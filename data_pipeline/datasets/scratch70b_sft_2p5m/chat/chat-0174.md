### User
I hung a shelf and it's drunk. Left side is high. A marble would roll off and sue me. I used a cheap level, two anchors, a stud on the right I think, and drywall on the left I definitely hit because the bit went in like cake. I have ten minutes before I have to leave for work. I don't want to take the whole wall apart tonight. Tell me what you would actually do if this was last-year you, standing here with a screwdriver, staring at it.

### Assistant
Last-year me would not "live with it" and would not start a renovation. Ten minutes is: make it safe, then make it less drunk, then go to work.

First I'd take everything off. A crooked shelf with weight on it is how anchors walk out of drywall while you're on the bus.

Second I'd put the cheap level back on the middle of the shelf and look at the bubble. Then I'd check by eye against a door frame. Cheap levels lie. Walls lie too.

Third I'd get under the left side — your high side — and see if the bracket is flush. If a screw isn't snug, snug it. Don't crank until the drywall crumbles.

If the left is an anchor in cake drywall and the right is a stud, it will see-saw. I would not pretend I can make drywall as strong as a stud in ten minutes. Load off, snug what's loose, decide if this is an after-work job. Leaving a half-anchored shelf full of books is how last-year me learned about holes. Ten minutes is not "finish the wall." It is "don't let it fall while you're gone."

Do you have a stud finder, or just a knock and a prayer? And is anything still sitting on it?

### User
Nothing on it. I took the books off when the plant slid. No stud finder. I have a screwdriver, the leftover anchors, a pencil, and the level that came in the same blister pack as the shelf. There's a third hole I drilled in the wrong place and it's staring at me.

### Assistant
I'd ignore the staring hole for ten minutes. Ugly, not urgent. Tape later.

Knock along the right bracket. Dull sound and a screw that bit hard: probably the stud. Leave that side. The left is the liar.

Level on the shelf. If the bubble says left-high, loosen the left screw a quarter turn, tap the bracket down a hair with the screwdriver handle, snug, recheck. Tiny moves.

If it doesn't drop, the anchor sleeve may be sitting proud. Back the screw out, push the flange to the drywall, reseat, drive until the bracket doesn't spin. If the anchor spins, it's done. Take the shelf off the left so it isn't hanging on a lie, or take the whole thing down if it wants to pivot, and go to work. Crooked is annoying. A shelf falling on a chair is a day.

I would not add a third guessed hole before work. Tonight: knock, or a magnet for screws in the stud. I won't invent your wall's insides. The wrong hole gets spackle after work, or it waits. Don't hang from it.

### User
If I leave it slightly crooked for a week, will people notice, or am I the only one who will stare at it.

### Assistant
People who sit on your couch will notice a plant that leans. You will notice every time you walk by. Last-year me tried "it's fine" and then straightened it at 11 p.m. anyway, worse, tired, with worse holes.

For the next ten minutes: empty (already done), level on the shelf, snug the proud side, tiny tap, recheck, and if the left anchor spins, shelf comes off the left and you leave. Don't load it "just for today." Set a reminder for tonight that says "shelf, not vibes." That's the whole shift. Go to work. The wall will still be there. The marble can wait.
