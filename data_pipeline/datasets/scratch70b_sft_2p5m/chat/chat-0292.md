### User
The person upstairs started doing something with weights at 6:03. It sounds like a bowling ball on my ceiling. This is the third weekday. I go to bed late because of work. I haven't knocked on the ceiling because that feels feral. I also haven't left a note because notes turn into feuds. I have to be functional at 9.

### Assistant
Three weekdays is a pattern, not a one-off. You do not have to knock like a sitcom neighbor. You also do not have to swallow it until you snap at 6:40.

A daylight conversation or a short polite note that is actually a request is still allowed. Feuds start when notes are sarcastic or anonymous. "Hi, I'm downstairs, the 6 a.m. weights are shaking my ceiling, can we move the heavy stuff later or put a mat down?" is a note, not a war.

I will not quote your building's quiet hours as if I have the lease. I do not. If you have a house rules PDF, read it. If you do not, the first tool is still the person, not a myth about 8 a.m.

Are you in a building with a manager, or is this a house with two units and no middle person? And have you ever spoken to them at the mailbox, or is it a ghost upstairs?

### User
Apartment, there is a manager who answers emails in two days. I've seen the neighbor once. They had headphones on and smiled. They look like they would be reasonable, which makes me feel worse about complaining.

### Assistant
Reasonable people still drop weights. Smiling at the mailbox does not mean they can hear what your ceiling hears. You are not accusing them of being a villain. You are reporting a physical fact: impact, 6:03, weekdays.

Catch them at a human hour. After work, in the hallway, thirty seconds. Same script as the note. Offer a concrete ask: a rubber mat, moving the routine to 7:30, or doing floor work later and the jumping later. You cannot design their fitness plan. You can name the impact.

If you cannot catch them, the note under the door with your unit number and a way to text is cleaner than the manager as a first move. Manager emails have a way of arriving as a formal complaint even when you meant "hey." Use the manager if they ignore you or if the noise includes extras — dragging furniture at midnight, that kind of thing.

Meanwhile, protect 9 a.m. Earplugs, a fan, a white noise app, moving the bed a foot if the worst thuds are over the pillow. That is not you giving up. That is you getting through the week while the conversation happens.

Do not record them through the ceiling for fun. If you later need a log for the manager, dates and times in a note on your phone are enough.

### User
If I ask and they say "this is when I can exercise," I will feel stuck. Also I work from home some mornings and the thuds hit my calls.

### Assistant
If that is their only hour, you still get to say the calls matter. "I get that it's your window. The jumping is landing on my meetings. Can the heavy stuff move to after 9, or onto a mat?" Compromise might be ugly: they keep 6 a.m. for quieter work and save drops for later. Or they cannot, and then you email the manager with dates, not adjectives.

Stuck is a feeling. A paper trail is a tool. I cannot promise the manager will care. Two-day email people sometimes care about patterns.

For the next ten minutes: write the thirty-second script, put earplugs where you can grab them tomorrow, start a log with today's 6:03, and if you hear them in the hall tonight, use the script. If you do not, write the note and wait until evening to slide it, not at 6:10 when everyone is a worse version of themselves.

You can like a smiling neighbor and still need a quieter ceiling. Those are not opposites. Ask once, clearly, without the bowling-ball metaphor. The metaphor is for me. For them, it's "weights at 6, ceiling shakes, please change X."
