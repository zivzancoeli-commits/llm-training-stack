### User
Intercom has been dead for a month. Deliveries wait on the street. Guests text me like I'm a bouncer. Landlord came by, pressed the dead button, laughed, said "old building charm." I laughed too, which I hate. I need it fixed. I also need to undo the laugh so they don't think I agreed that broken is aesthetic. No fake housing statutes. Practical next steps.

### Assistant
Undo the laugh in writing. Laughs don't archive. Email does.

"Following up on the intercom. It doesn't work. I need it repaired, not treated as charm. Deliveries and guests can't reach me. Please send a time." Dry. You can say you know it's an old building. Old is not a repair plan.

I will not invent how many days they have. Your lease may mention entry systems. Read that clause if you have it. If it doesn't, you still ask for a working way to buzz people in. That's a building function.

Photo of the dead panel, date, a note of the visit when they laughed. If they texted "charm," screenshot. If it was oral, your email is the record of what you understood.

Don't keep laughing in person to be likable. Likable got you a month of street deliveries.

Do you have a workaround they blessed — a gate code, a lockbox — or is it only your phone?

### User
Only my phone. They said "you're young, you text." I do text. That's not an intercom. There's a tradesperson who comes for other units and never for this. I pay rent on time and I feel like that means I should be easy.

### Assistant
On-time rent is not a personality that owes them easy. Easy is how dead buttons retire.

"Texting me is not a replacement. Please repair the intercom." If they repeat young, ignore the age comment. Age isn't a schematic.

Ask for the tradesperson by name if you know it: "Same tech who was here for 2A can look at the panel." Specific requests get scheduled more often than vibes.

Until it's fixed, you can put delivery instructions in the apps you use: call on arrival, meet at the door, whatever is true. That's you coping. Coping isn't consent.

If they offer a rent-unrelated joke again, don't laugh as a period on the sentence. Nod, repeat repair. Awkward is fine.

### User
If I put it in email will they think I'm the difficult tenant and stall something else.

### Assistant
They might. I won't predict this person. A charm laugh already stalled this thing. Silence didn't make you easy enough to get a working button.

Keep the email about one system. Don't attach a manifesto of every annoyance. One button, one month, one ask. Difficult in their mouth may mean documented. You can live with that word.

If you have a city 311-style housing line, that's a later fork if they never schedule. I will not tell you it will work. I will tell you the email still comes first so you aren't only a hallway laugh.

Next: email today, no smiley, photo, request a date, stop calling it charm back to them. You can like old buildings and still want a buzzer. That's not a personality clash. That's a broken circuit.

### User
I sent the email, no smiley, photo of the dead button, ask for a time. They replied "charm aside we'll look." That's not a date. Do I reply immediately or wait so I don't look hungry.

### Assistant
Reply once, still hungry for a date. "Thanks. What day should I expect the tech?" Looking hungry for a working buzzer is accurate. Waiting for manners is how a month became a month.

If they don't answer the day question, you send a weekly ping with the same photo, not a new essay. Copy a manager if one exists. Young-so-you-text can stay in their mouth. Your email stays about a circuit.

Laugh in the hallway is undone enough. Don't laugh next visit. Repeat repair. Deliveries can keep calling your phone until the button works, and you can still want the button. Charm is paint and old brick, maybe. Charm is not a dead speaker. Keep the thread boring and dated. That's how look becomes a schedule.
