### User
A coworker asked me to "just listen" in tomorrow's meeting because they want to "own the room." It is a project I built half of. They are afraid I will correct a number. I might, if the number is wrong. I don't want a public fight. I also don't want to sit there like a plant while they present my work with a hole in it. They want a yes tonight.

### Assistant
You can decline a gag order. Owning a room is not the same as owning facts.

Tonight: "I'll let you run the meeting. If a number is wrong I'll flag it once, briefly, or I'll ping you in chat. I'm not going to sit silent through an error." If they only wanted a vibe, that is the compromise. If they wanted a mute, that is a no.

You do not need a speech about credit. Credit can be a separate sentence if they skip your name: you can add "we" once. Do not prepare a revenge deck.

I will not invent office politics outcomes. Managers notice silence and they notice corrections. A short correction is ordinary.

Is the number they might flub actually yours, or a shared spreadsheet?

### User
Shared, but I made the tab they will screenshot. They said "please don't make me look stupid." I don't want them to look stupid. I also don't want a client to take a bad figure home.

### Assistant
Not looking stupid and not shipping a bad figure can both happen if you agree on a private signal. Offer: "If I type a 1 in the chat, pause and check cell B4." Or a knee tap if you sit next to them. Pre-agreed is not a public hanging.

If they refuse any signal, you still will not promise total silence. "I won't pile on. I won't let a wrong number stand." That is the line.

Tonight they want a yes. Give them a yes to running the slides, not to fiction. If they sulk, they can sulk and still present.

Review the screenshot together for ten minutes now if they will. Prevention is cheaper than a 1 in the chat. If they are too busy owning the room to look at B4, that is information.

### User
If I correct them anyway, they'll say I undermined them. If I don't, I'll replay it on the train.

### Assistant
A one-line correction is not a coup. "The figure in B4 is x, not y" plus a stop. No smirk. No follow-up lecture. If they say you undermined, you can say, "I flagged a number. The rest was yours."

Replay on the train is the cost of a mute. You already know that cost.

Send the message. Offer the ten-minute review. Go to the meeting as a person with a voice you will use once if needed. You are not a plant. You are a coworker who built a tab. Let them run the room. Keep the tab honest. That is the whole deal. Yes to hosting. No to silence. Send it.

### User
They want the yes in Slack before they go home. Meeting is 9:30. I have not looked at B4 with them.

### Assistant
Send Slack now: "You can run it. I won't pile on. If B4 is wrong I'll flag once or ping a 1. I'm not promising silence through a bad figure. Ten minutes tonight to check the screenshot?" If they skip the ten minutes, you still have the 1. If they demand a mute emoji, you do not send one. Go home after the message. Review B4 yourself so you know the number even if they never look. Tomorrow you sit as a person with a tab, not as a plant. Owning a room is slides. Owning a figure is optional only if the figure is true. Type the deal. Close the laptop.
