### User
We're at a four-top. Dana is in the middle of a story about their boss. The plates have been gone for twenty minutes. I have a train. The waiter keeps doing laps and making eye contact with me because I think I look like the anxious one. I've never been the person who asks for the check while someone is mid-sentence. I always wait and then I miss the train. Dana doesn't do trains. I have nine minutes of story left if the pace holds, which it won't. First time: how do I cut in without becoming the villain of the anecdote?

### Assistant
You don't cut the anecdote with a speech. You catch the waiter and you tell the table the train.

When the waiter is close: a small hand, "Could we have the check, please?" You don't need to wait for a comma in Dana's story if the waiter is actually there. You can mouth it. Then, to the table, at the next breath: "I have a train. I'm going to pay my part so I can go." If you're paying all of it, say that. If you're splitting, say that. Don't fake that you're not in a hurry. Last year's you missed the train to be polite. This year's you can have a timetable.

If Dana says "one more minute," you can listen for one minute while the check is printing. You don't have to listen for the whole boss saga. The check is already in motion.

I won't invent whether you should tip a certain percent. Pay how you already pay. Don't use the train as a reason to stiff anyone. That's a different problem.

Are you paying together on one card, or can you do yours and leave?

### User
One card, Dana's, "they have points." I always Venmo after. If I leave before the card comes back I look like I dine-and-dashed my own friend. The waiter nodded and disappeared. Dana is still in the story. I have the app open to the train like a pulse.

### Assistant
Then you stay until the card comes back, but you still named the train. That's the first-time part. You asked. You're not trapped in the story; you're trapped in a points card, which is shorter.

When the waiter returns, you can be the person who says "we're good, thank you" so Dana doesn't start a second story at the machine. If Dana wants to keep talking after the receipt, you stand. "I'm walking to the station. Venmo tonight." If they look hurt, "I wasn't bored. I have a train." Those can both be true.

Don't snatch the card. Don't narrate the boss. Don't apologize five times. One apology is plenty: "Sorry to stand up in the middle." Then you stand.

If the waiter disappeared too long, you can flag a different server: "We asked for the check — train." That's not a villain. That's information.

### User
Dana just said "don't be rude, the story's the point of dinner." I can see the waiter with the folder two tables away. My throat is doing the grin. What do I do with my hands?

### Assistant
Don't grin. Catch the waiter's eye. Hands can raise slightly, then you take the folder if they hand it to you, then you give it to Dana if it's their card. "I need to go after this. I want to hear the rest another time." If they insist the story is the point, they can finish it while Dana signs. You can put on your coat during the signature. Coats are a clock.

You asked for the check during talk. That's allowed. Dinner can have a point and also an end. The train doesn't care about the boss.

Next minutes: folder, Venmo later, walk. You don't have to win the philosophy of dinner. You have to be on the platform. Get up when the receipt is signed. That's the first time, completed, even if Dana tells it later as rude. You can live with that better than a missed train and a grin.
