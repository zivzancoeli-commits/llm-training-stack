### Assistant

If you have a relationship with a non-emergency city line or a local non-emergency police number, that's the boring call: racing, crowd, 2 a.m., your block, you're in your apartment. I will not tell you what they will do. I won't invent whether this is a crime on your street. You describe engines and a crowd at 2 a.m. Let the dispatcher sort the rest. If you don't know the number, that's a search for "non-emergency [your city]" in the morning — unless you actually need emergency services because someone is hurt or you're in danger. Don't go outside. Don't film them from the sill in a way that makes you the show. Don't honk. Don't start a group chat pile-on at 2 a.m. that names your unit.

Partner: dark room, water, door shut, you handle the call if a call is happening. Split the jobs.

### User

### Assistant
Silly is cheaper than a 7 a.m. with no sleep and no record.

Call. Short: location, burnout, crowd, second Saturday, you're a resident inside. You don't need to diagnose "street racing" as a legal category. You can say "cars racing / doing burnouts, loud, 2 a.m." If they say they'll send someone when they can, you say thanks and you go back to the dark room. You're not booking a result. You're logging a night.

If it ends before they come, you still called. That's how patterns exist when you call next time. Last time's late car is not a reason to never use the number. It's a reason to keep the report boring and dated.

Don't open the window to yell "people are sleeping." That's a clip. The ring light wants a character. Don't audition.

If it moves to your stoop, you stay inside and you call again. Distance changing is a new fact. You still don't go out in socks.

### Assistant

If the call-taker asks you to go look at license plates, you can say no. You're in your apartment. That's the whole location. You don't owe a stakeout.

Tonight: window, call, earplugs, partner's door. You are not a coward for skipping the asphalt. You're a person with a 7 a.m. who used a phone instead of a speech. Get off the sill.
