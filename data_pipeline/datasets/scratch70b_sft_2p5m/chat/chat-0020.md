### User
It is 1:07 a.m. The person above me is doing something that sounds like rearranging bowling balls. I have work at 7. I've already done the broom-on-the-ceiling tap, which felt medieval. I'm on my break at 1 a.m. eating cereal in the dark like a raccoon. I don't know if I want a script, a legal lecture, or permission to go bang on the door. I also don't know if this is a one-night party or a new lifestyle. I'm tired enough to make this worse.

### Assistant
Before I hand you a plan that turns into a hallway incident, I need to know if you still want help that is about sleep tonight, or if you are mostly venting and you want me to shut up so you can finish the cereal.

Do you still want help dealing with upstairs, or do you want company while you wait it out?

### User
I still want help. I just don't want to become the building villain. There's a chance it's a kid who won't sleep. There's a chance it's a gym session. I can't tell. My lease is a PDF I haven't opened since I signed it.

### Assistant
Good. Help, not a speech about becoming a folklore character.

I am not going to invent what your lease says, and I am not going to invent a noise ordinance for your city. Those depend on where you live and what you signed. If you want that later, you read the PDF or you ask the landlord or a local resource in the morning, not at 1:07 with cereal.

Tonight, scale to what you can still reverse.

First: earplugs or headphones with something boring if you have them. That is not surrender. That is you protecting a 7 a.m. shift while your brain wants a confrontation snack. If you have none, a rolled hoodie over the ears is pathetic and sometimes enough for bass-less thumping.

Second: one written ping if you already have a neighborly channel — building app, text from when they borrowed a pan, a note you can slide. Short: "Hey, it's 1 a.m. below you, the banging is keeping me up, I have an early morning. Please wrap it up." Time-stamp, no novel, no threat. If you do not have a channel, do not start a relationship by pounding like the police. A door knock at 1 a.m. can go fine and can also go sideways with a stranger in socks. If you knock, one knock, step back, "Hi, I live below, the noise is a lot, I need sleep." Then leave. Do not stand there for a debate in a towel.

Third: if it does not change in twenty minutes and you cannot sleep, decide whether this is a log-for-tomorrow problem. Note the time, the sound, how long. That is for a landlord email in daylight, not for a 2 a.m. essay. One night might be a guest, a dropped dresser, a person who does not know sound travels. A pattern is a different conversation. You said you cannot tell. So you should not accuse them of a lifestyle from one hour.

You already tapped the ceiling. Do not do it again in a rhythm that says war. Twice is a signal. Three times is a drum solo.

If you think it might be a kid, that does not mean you have to eat the noise forever. It means you do not open with "what is wrong with you." Kids exist. So does your 7 a.m. Both can be true. Morning you can still ask, kindly, if they can move heavy stuff before 10 p.m. Night you just needs quieter.

### User
If I email the landlord at 1 a.m. I will sound unhinged. If I do nothing I will go to work on three hours. Still want help. The cereal is gone.

### Assistant
Do not email the landlord at 1 a.m. Save the draft if you must. Send after 8. Night-sent emails read like a flare.

Right now: headphones or plugs, one calm message or one knock if you can stay civil, lights down, bed. Set two alarms so the 7 a.m. is not riding on this. If the noise is still a bowling alley at 1:40, you have the note of times and you leave it for morning-you.

You asked me to check if you still wanted help. You did. The help is sleep-first, paper-trail-second, no invented laws, no villain origin story. Go to bed as soon as the last sentence is sent. The hallway can wait until people are wearing real clothes.
