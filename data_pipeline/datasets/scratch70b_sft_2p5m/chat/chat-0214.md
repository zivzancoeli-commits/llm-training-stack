### User
We are on a trail I have done before. My friend wants to turn around. We are maybe a third of the way to the lookout. He says his knee feels weird, not a fall, just weird. I am the one who picked the route. I do not want to bully him to the top and I do not want to mock him. Uncle-skeptic energy is fine. Tell me what you would actually do in the next ten minutes, not a wilderness manifesto.

### Assistant
Next ten minutes, in order.

Minute one: stop walking. Get off the narrow bit if you are on one. Packs stay on until you know he is stable. You do not debate the lookout while still moving.

Minute two: two questions, not twelve. Can you put weight on it. Did you twist it or did it just start. You listen. You do not say it is probably nothing. I am not a doctor and neither are you. Weird knee plus a trail is enough to take seriously.

Minute three: sit him on a rock or a pack. Water. If he has a wrap in his bag, he can put it on. Do not improvise a tourniquet for a maybe.

Minute four: look at the options from here. Back the way you came is a known third. Forward is two thirds plus the return. If he wants to turn around, the job is to start the return clean, not to hold a meeting about grit.

Minute five: weather and daylight, one glance. If the sky is turning or you are already near turnaround time, that ends the debate.

I would turn around with him. I would not send him down alone while I summit. The mountain will be there. You picked the route. You can pick the exit.

Where are you — woods or ridge — and do you have cell service?

### User
Woods, some roots, not a ridge. Spotty service. I have a map screenshot. He keeps saying he does not want to ruin it for me. That sentence is the one that makes me stubborn. We drove two hours. Snacks are for the top.

### Assistant
Minutes six and seven: kill the "ruin it for me" line. You say, out loud, you are not ruining it. We are turning around. I am not hiking ahead. The two-hour drive is already spent. It does not buy a lookout. Uncle math: you do not throw a knee after a car.

Snacks are not a summit prize. Eat something now, especially if he is shaky. Then walk down at his pace, not yours. Put him in front so you can see if the knee buckles. Do not chatter him into going faster.

Minute eight: check the screenshot for the last junction. Confirm you are not about to take a spur that lengthens the return. Pride is how people add miles.

Minute nine: send a text if a bar appears — turning back, same trail, ETA guess if you have one. If no bar, do not wander holding the phone in the air. Walk. Try again at the next clearing.

Minute ten: start downhill. First hundred yards are the test. If he winces every step, slow further and do not reopen the lookout. If he suddenly says he is fine and wants to continue, finish a few minutes of downhill assessment before you reverse again. Flip-flopping burns daylight. I would keep the downward decision unless the weirdness clearly vanishes and he asks with a straight face, not guilt.

You do not owe the trail a badge. You owe him a ride home that does not include a rescue.

### User
What if I am annoyed and it shows. I planned this as a good day.

### Assistant
Annoyance can show as quiet, not as a lecture. You can say I am disappointed and we are still going down. Both can be true. Do not make him manage your mood on a bad knee. Save the recap for the car once he is sitting.

From right now if you already stopped: water, snack, you lead the sentence about turning back, he walks in front, screenshot at the junction, text if a bar, downhill. The lookout is cancelled. The day can still be a woods walk that ended early. That is a smaller story than the one where you talked him uphill. Go.
