### User
I'm next in line. My name is Siobhan. People hear "Sharon" or they just write "Savannah" and then they shout something that isn't me. Last month a guy wrote "Shiv" and thought it was funny. I laughed because the line was watching. I don't want a bit. I want a cup that says a name I can hear from the pickup counter. This is the first time I'm going to ask without turning it into a joke myself. They're already looking at me. What do I say at the register that isn't a TED talk about identity?

### Assistant
You give them a spelling and a pickup word. You don't give them your childhood.

"Siobhan — S-I-O-B-H-A-N. For the cup, you can put Sio, S-I-O. I'll listen for that." That's it. If they start to grin at the letters, you don't grin first. You can say, "Not a joke name, just that." Then you pay and you move.

If they offer to "just put Sarah," you can decline. "Siobhan or Sio is fine." You don't have to explain Gaelic. You don't have to say "it's Irish." That's optional color, not a ticket to the drink.

Last month you laughed so the line would exhale. You don't have to do that shift today. If they write Shiv again, you can say at pickup, "That's not my name. It's Sio." You don't have to perform delight.

Are they already holding the marker, or are you still at the order screen?

### User
Marker. They asked "what's the name for the order?" in the voice people use when they're about to be creative. There's a Sharpie. My card is in my hand. A person behind me sighed. I can feel the laugh in my throat.

### Assistant
Swallow the laugh if you can. Point at the cup or the screen. "S-I-O is enough. Please don't invent a nickname." Then you look at the card, not at their face, so this stays a transaction.

If they still write a bit, you have a second chance at the bar, not a duty to educate the whole shop. Pickup: "Which one is Sio?" If they hold up Shiv, "I asked for Sio. I'll take it anyway, please don't do that next time." That's a correction, not a scene. You don't need an audience vote.

The sigh behind you is not your manager. They can wait ten seconds for spelling. You are not holding up the store by having a name.

If the register person is new and panicked, you can slow down without becoming a teacher: letters, then the short form. One time. If they still mess it up, you drink the coffee. You don't have to die on a Sharpie hill if you're late. The win today is that you asked. The cup can still be wrong. You still didn't volunteer the bit.

### User
They wrote "Chevon" and they're about to yell it. I can leave it. I can also say something. I have thirty seconds before they call it. I don't want to be the customer they talk about in the back.

### Assistant
You can take Chevon and still not become the back-room story. A quiet "that's me" plus you grabbing the cup is a valid exit if you want to leave. If you have the thirty seconds and the energy, you can say, "It's Sio — I'll remember that next time, no worries," which corrects without a trial. You don't need them to reprint the cup. I won't invent whether reprints are free. You don't need a reprint to have asked.

If they yell Chevon, you can walk up without raising your voice. The back will talk about customers anyway. Your job is not to be unnoticeable. It's to stop laughing at your own name so they'll keep doing bits.

Next time, same line at the marker, shorter. Today: take the cup, drink it, leave. You already did the first-time part. The Sharpie is not a referendum. Get the coffee, get out of the sighing line, keep the letters you actually said.
