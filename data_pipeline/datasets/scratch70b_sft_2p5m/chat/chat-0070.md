### User
I am in the waiting room. My dad went back for tests a couple of hours ago. Nobody has told me anything useful. My sister is on a plane. My uncle keeps texting "well??" every twenty minutes like I am hiding a score. I started drafting a group message that sounded like I knew what I was talking about. I do not. I also do not want to be the family news station if I am about to get it wrong. You always have a plan. Do I even want a plan right now or do I want someone to sit here in the sentence I already have: I don't know yet.

### Assistant
Do you still want help with the messages, or do you want me to stop at "you do not have to be the news station"? I will not guess your dad's results, I will not name a diagnosis, and I will not pretend a waiting room clock means anything medical. If you want me to back off and just leave you with the true sentence, say so. If you want a way to handle your uncle without inventing an update, I can do that next. Your call.

### User
Stay. I want help with the uncle texts, not a medical briefing. I keep feeling like if I do not answer I am failing some family job. Also there is a volunteer who asked if I need anything and I said I was fine, which is a lie, but I did not know what to ask for.

### Assistant
Still your call, every few minutes: if this stops being useful, tell me to drop it.

For your uncle: you are not hiding a score. There is no score. A useful text is a status of information, not a status of health.

"I don't have results. He is still in tests. I will text when a nurse or doctor tells me something I can repeat. Please don't ping every twenty minutes — I will send the next thing when it exists."

That is not cold. That is you refusing to manufacture news so his phone can feel busy. If he keeps asking "well??" you can repeat the same two lines. You do not owe a warmer version that secretly contains a guess.

For the group thread with your sister: same rule, shorter. "No news I can stand behind. Plane-safe: I will update when I have a person in scrubs saying a sentence, not before." She is in the air. She does not need your anxiety dressed up as a briefing.

The volunteer: "fine" is a lock people use when they cannot think. If you want to unlock it next time they pass, ask for something concrete that is not medical advice. Water. The wifi password. Where the bathroom is. Whether there is a quieter corner. Those are waiting-room problems. They are allowed.

What I would not do: narrate "it is probably just X" to calm your uncle. You do not know. Filling the gap with a probably is how families remember the wrong story later. I also would not live-blog every door that opens. Doors open for other people's names all day.

If a clinician does come out, write down the words they used, not the vibe you felt. Then send those words. If they said they are still waiting on a test, that is the update. If they said they will come back in a while, that is the update. Vague optimism from you is not a translation. It is a new document.

You already had the honest sentence. Keep using it. I can sit in it with you: you do not know yet, and that is the only accurate report. Want me to keep going on the uncle script, or is that enough for now?

### User
Enough on the script. One more thing and then I should look at the door. If a nurse comes out and says something I only half catch, do I ask them to repeat it, or do I look like I was not listening.

### Assistant
Ask them to repeat it. Waiting rooms scramble hearing. "Can you say that again so I can write it for my sister" is a normal sentence. If you would rather I stop, say so; otherwise write the words you actually heard.

Do not turn half a sentence into a confident update. That is how "waiting on one more test" becomes "everything is fine" in an uncle's head. Send the words. Look at the door if you need to. You do not know yet. That remains the honest report until someone in scrubs gives you a sentence you can repeat.
