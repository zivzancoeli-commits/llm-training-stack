### User
I have to ask my teacher for more time on a paper and I keep writing "sorry I've been so busy" which even I can tell is junk. I don't want to sound like I forgot it existed. Walk me through this like I'm smart, not like I need a pep talk.

### Assistant
You're smart enough to know "busy" is fog. Fog is what reads as flaky. A real ask is: what's done, what's stuck, what date you want, asked before the original date dies.

I shouldn't guess the rest. Is this due tomorrow, already late, or still a few days out — and is the blocker something outside you, like a source you can't get, or did the week just eat you?

### User
Due Thursday. Today is Tuesday. Not late yet. I have the outline and two pages. The last section needs a book that's checked out until Wednesday afternoon. I could turn in a thin version Thursday or ask for Monday. I didn't request the book until last week, which is the part that makes me feel like a flake.

### Assistant
A flake hides until Thursday night. You're asking Tuesday with pages already written. That's a different animal. Sharp version: name the book, name what you have, name the two options, stop.

Don't lead with sorry. Don't list other homework. Don't confess your whole character. One true delay: the book arrives Wednesday, the paper is due Thursday, the last section depends on it.

Something like: "I have an outline and two pages. The last section needs [title], which I can't pick up until Wednesday afternoon. I can submit a shorter paper Thursday, or a complete one Monday if you'll move the deadline. Which do you prefer?"

That last question is the adult part. You're not begging. You're offering a fork so they can choose. People trust forks more than fog.

If you already know this teacher hates incomplete work, skip the thin-Thursday option and ask for Monday, still with the book named. One reason. The "I should have reserved it earlier" thought stays in your head unless they ask. Preemptive guilt reads as "please punish me."

### User
They're going to think I planned badly. I kind of did. Also I always add "no pressure!" which I know wrecks it.

### Assistant
You reserved late. That's a planning miss, not a personality. You don't have to advertise it. If they ask, one line is enough: "I should have requested it sooner. I have the rest drafted." Then you shut up. A speech about how you usually never do this is what a flake writes.

Cut "no pressure." You do need an answer. "No pressure" tells them the deadline was decorative. End on the choice. If they ignore email, you still submit whatever you have Thursday unless they wrote back yes. Asking is not a pause button you invented.

Send it today, during hours a teacher might actually read mail. Tuesday afternoon beats Wednesday at 11 p.m. Late asks are what turn a reasonable delay into a scramble story.

Keep it short enough to answer on a phone. No emoji. No "I feel terrible even asking." The paper isn't complete. That's the reason. Your feelings are not the reason.

If they say no, you turn in the best Thursday version and label the hole: "Section 3 pending [book]; I'll add it if allowed after." That's transparent. Flaky is silence, then a surprise attachment Friday.

If they say Monday, you put Monday on a calendar and you don't spend the extra days adding a fourth argument to look devoted. Extra scope is how an extension becomes a second problem.

### User
Okay. Tuesday email, book named, two pages mentioned, Thursday-thin or Monday-complete, no sorry stack, no "no pressure." If they say no, I still turn in Thursday with the hole labeled.

### Assistant
That's the whole difference: you almost finished, you named the missing object, you asked while Thursday still existed. Send it. Then close the draft and don't keep "improving" the apology. The ask is the work now.
