### User
A coworker slid a catalog onto my desk and said I should join "as a joke" so we can "make lunch money." It is one of those companies with ranks and a launch party. They laughed like if I say no I'm no fun. We have to sit in the same pod. I do not want product in my house and I do not want a downline conversation every Thursday.

### Assistant
"As a joke" is how people get you to a launch party. You can laugh once and still say no. Fun is not a business model you owe the pod.

A clean desk sentence: "I'm not joining, even as a joke. I don't want the catalog." Then hand it back. If they leave it, put it in their chair, not in your drawer where it becomes your problem.

Do not debate compensation plans. Do not ask "is this a pyramid" as a gotcha in an open office. You will not win a definition war, and you will become the Wednesday entertainment. You already know you do not want it.

I will not legally classify the company. I do not need to. You do not want the product or the Thursdays. That is enough.

Is this the first time, and did they already add you to a chat?

### User
First time in person. There is a "team vibes" group chat I haven't joined. They said the kickoff is Friday after work. Our manager likes this person. I don't want to be the sticky one.

### Assistant
Skip the chat. Do not RSVP to Friday. After-work kickoff is not a team meeting. If they @ you at the desk, "I can't Friday" is enough. You do not owe a sermon.

Manager-likes-them is social weather. It is not a requirement that you buy oil or wraps or whatever is in the catalog. If the manager ever asks why you are not "supporting," you can say you keep work and selling separate. If the manager is in the downline, that is messier, and you still get to say no. I would not accuse the manager of anything. I would keep your no boring.

If they keep sliding catalogs, one email or Slack DM: "Please don't leave sales stuff on my desk." Paper trail, calm. If it continues, that is a different conversation with the manager about not selling on the pod. First catalog is a no. A campaign is a pattern.

Do not join as a joke to "quit later." Later has a kit and a login and a relative who thinks you are in.

### User
What if they tell other people I'm not a team player. That phrase is already in my head.

### Assistant
Team player at work means the actual work. It does not mean a Friday rank party. If they smear you in the pod, you keep doing your job and you do not run a counter-campaign about MLMs. If it gets specific and false, you can ask the manager for a private word about being looped into selling. Until then, the phrase in your head is just a phrase.

For the next ten minutes: hand the catalog back, decline Friday in one clause, ignore the chat invite, and start a task on your screen so the next pitch has to interrupt work. If they hover, repeat "not joining." Two words.

You can sit in a pod and still not join a downline. Joke was the wrapper. Under it is a yes they wanted. Give them a no, then go back to lunch money you already earn from the job that put you in the chair.

### User
They just put a sample on my keyboard. Tiny bottle. I didn't ask.

### Assistant
Put the bottle back on their desk, not in the trash where it becomes a mystery. "I'm not taking samples either." Then lock your screen and keep typing. If a third object appears, the Slack line about not leaving sales stuff is due today, not after Friday. A sample is still a pitch with a lid on it. You already answered. Repeat the no with your hands, then with the message if they cannot see hands.
