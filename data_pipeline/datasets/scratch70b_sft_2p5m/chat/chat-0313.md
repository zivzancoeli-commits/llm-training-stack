### User
Every appointment, my hairdresser starts the purple shampoo speech. I have tried it. It turns me gray-lavender and I hate it. I said "I'm okay" last time and they still put a bottle in the bag. I'm in the chair in ten minutes for a cut, not color. I don't want to be rude to someone who holds scissors near my ear. I also do not want another bottle.

### Assistant
You can decline a product without insulting their training. Purple shampoo is a tool, not a personality test. "I don't use purple shampoo — it goes lavender on me. Please don't add a bottle today" is a complete briefing. Say it before they start, not as they bag you out.

Scissors near your ear is a feeling. It is still a service you are paying for. You are allowed to steer.

Do not do a TED talk about undertones unless they ask. Do not fake that you will try it again. They put a bottle in the bag because "I'm okay" sounded like maybe.

Is this a person you otherwise like, and do they commission on product, as far as you know?

### User
I like them. I don't know about commission. They always say my brass is "coming through." I think they think I am ignoring professional advice. The last bottle is under my sink unopened.

### Assistant
Brass talk can be true and still not equal a bottle you will not use. You can acknowledge the observation without taking the product: "I see the brass. I'm living with it. No purple shampoo, no toner surprise." If they offer a toner in the sink, that is a different ask. A cut appointment can stay a cut. If they want to add a service, they should say the name and the time. You can say no.

Commission is common and not a crime. It explains the bag, not your duty. If the bottle appears again, you can hand it back at the desk: "I'm going to leave this." Awkward for ten seconds. Cheaper than a graveyard under the sink.

If they look offended, let them have a face. Then ask a cut question — length, bangs — so the appointment returns to the thing you booked. People who are good with hair are not always good with no. That can still be a good cutter.

I am not going to prescribe a different at-home product. I do not see your hair. If you want brass help later, you can ask "what else besides purple shampoo" and then decide. Today is a boundary, not a makeover.

### User
What if they start the speech while my head is in the bowl and I can't make a normal face.

### Assistant
Say it to the ceiling: "Skip the purple shampoo talk — I'm a no." Short. They can hear you over water. If you wait until you are upright, the bottle is already in the bag.

For the next ten minutes: say the no as you sit down, confirm it's a cut only, and if a bottle appears at checkout, leave it on the counter. Do not apologize for the unopened one at home. Do not buy a travel size as a compromise.

You are not rude for refusing a product that makes you look like a lilac. You are a person in a chair with a preference. Tell them once, early, and then let them cut hair. That is the job you booked.

### User
They already have the cape on me. I can still say it, right, or did I miss the window?

### Assistant
The cape is not a contract. Say it now, before the bowl. "Cut only today. No purple shampoo, no extra bottle." If they have already started the speech, interrupt once. After the cut, watch the bag at checkout like you watch the change. If the bottle is in there, take it out in front of them. That is cleaner than another unopened monument under the sink. You can still like this person and still be a no. The scissors do not get a vote on your cabinet.
