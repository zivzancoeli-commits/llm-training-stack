### User
My neighbor in 2C asked me to tell their landlord, if anyone knocks, that only one person lives there. I have seen two people every morning with two coffee mugs and a second bike in the stairwell. They said it's "just until the lease paper catches up." They brought cookies. I don't want to be the building snitch. I also don't want to lie to a stranger with a clipboard. They want an answer tonight. It's 8:40.

### Assistant
Don't lie. Cookies are not a contract. "Just until the paper catches up" is a story you cannot verify and should not narrate for someone else.

You also don't have to volunteer a headcount to the landlord. Not snitching and not lying can both be true. The sentence is: "I don't talk about other people's apartments." If they push, "I'm not going to say there's one person if I'm asked." That's a refusal, not a campaign.

I won't invent occupancy rules where you live. I don't know the lease, the city, or what the landlord is allowed to ask a neighbor. I can tell you that rehearsing a false number is something you can decline.

Did they say a landlord visit is already scheduled, or is this a hypothetical knock?

### User
They said maybe this week. A guy with a clipboard was in the lobby Tuesday. I don't know if that was the landlord. The second person is quiet. I like them more than I like 2C who asked. The cookies are on my counter. I haven't eaten one.

### Assistant
You can eat a cookie or throw it away. Neither one buys a script. If you keep the cookies, keep them as cookies, not as a down payment.

For the next ten minutes: text 2C if you don't want a hallway scene: "I'm not going to lie if someone asks. I also won't go looking for anyone. Please don't coach me." If they're at your door tonight, say it out loud and close the conversation. You don't need a debate about fairness of rents. You are not their lease lawyer.

If a clipboard person knocks, you can say you don't discuss other units. If they ask who lives in 2C, "I don't know the details" is true enough if you refuse to play census. If they ask you to confirm "only one adult," you don't confirm it. Silence or "I can't help with that" is allowed. You are not on their payroll.

Don't warn the whole floor. Don't start a group chat. That's how you become the snitch you didn't want to be. Handle 2C and handle a knock if it happens. That's the whole radius.

### User
If I say no, they'll act weird at the mailboxes. We share a recycling closet. I don't want a cold war over cardboard.

### Assistant
Mailbox chill is uncomfortable. It is cheaper than a lie that can follow you if this blows up. If they go cold, you can still say good morning and sort cardboard on your own schedule. You don't have to win them back with a fake headcount.

If they escalate — more cookies, more "just cover for us," a story about a sick relative — the answer stays the same. New reasons are still a request that you speak fiction. You can be kind to the quiet second person in the stairwell without becoming their legal fiction.

I can't predict what the landlord will do. I won't guess fines or evictions. That's not my lane and it's not yours unless you own the building.

Tonight: send the text, put the cookies in a tin or the trash, and don't rehearse a line about one coffee mug. If the clipboard comes, you know your sentence. Recycling can stay boring. Boring is the goal. 8:40 is late enough to end this. You're allowed to protect your mouth. You were asked to rent it out. Decline.
