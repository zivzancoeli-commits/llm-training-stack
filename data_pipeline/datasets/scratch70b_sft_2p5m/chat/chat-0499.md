### User
The cashier already bagged my stuff in plastic. I meant to say paper. There's a line behind me. The bags are sitting there looking done. I feel like asking now is wasting everyone's time and also like walking out with plastic is me failing a tiny test I set for myself. I don't need a speech about oceans. I need the next thirty seconds.

### Assistant
Thirty seconds: "Could I get paper instead?" while you keep your card in your hand so the line sees you're still finishing, not starting a seminar. Point at the bags if that is faster than words. You are allowed to correct a packing choice at the counter. The transaction is not sealed because polyethylene touched grapes.

If they say yes, let them move items. Don't snatch and rebuild in a panic; that's how eggs die. If they look annoyed, you still asked. Annoyance is not a veto you have to obey.

If they say they don't have paper, you accept that and you leave. You don't lecture. You don't fake a forgotten bag in the car unless the bag is actually in the car.

Don't apologize five times. One "thanks" is enough if they switch. Over-apology makes a small request feel like a crime, which teaches you not to ask next time.

Are the bags already double-tied, or still open on the counter?

### User
Still open, sitting in the well. I paid. The next person is already putting stuff on the belt. I always freeze after I pay, like the social door closed.

### Assistant
Payment closed the money. It did not close your mouth. The bags are still in the well. That's the easiest version of this ask.

Turn back to the cashier, not to the line: "Sorry — paper bags if you have them?" Then you stop talking. If they start transferring, you can help by holding the empty plastic open so they can dump, or you wait. Match their pace. If they toss you paper bags and look at you to repack, you repack to the side of the well so the next person's bananas can land.

The person on the belt is not a jury. They want their turn. A bag swap of groceries is shorter than your freeze already was.

If you freeze anyway, you can still do it at the end of the counter, out of the well, by asking any staff member with bags: "Do you have paper? They already packed me." Sometimes that's easier because the original cashier is in the next scan. Same request, slightly different body.

Next time, the sentence belongs before the first item lands: "Paper, please." You can say it while they greet you. You don't have to be a person who remembers every time. You have to be a person who can still ask when they forget.

I will not tell you plastic is morally required or forbidden. You wanted paper. Ask for paper.

### User
What if they already tied them and I have to hold up the line while they untie?

### Assistant
Then you choose. If the tie is thirty seconds of untie, ask. If the line is long and you don't care that much, take the plastic and remember earlier next visit. Both are adult. Don't perform a stand for an audience. Don't suffer in silence if you do care.

Tonight the bags are open. Use that. One question, paper if they have it, thanks, walk. The tiny test was never a test. It was a bag. Get the bag or don't. Either way, leave the ocean speech at home. The counter has a line. If your mouth dries, point at the well and say "paper." Two words beat a freeze. You can still be the person who meant to ask earlier. Meaning to ask is not the same as asking. Ask now, while the bags are still undone. Then go. The grapes don't need a speech.
