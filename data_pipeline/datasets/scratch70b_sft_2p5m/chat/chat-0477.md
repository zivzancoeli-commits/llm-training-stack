### User
Jules set up a mic and a foam thing in the living room. They record Tuesday and Thursday 8–9:30 p.m. That's when I used to watch a show and also when I call my sister on the West Coast. They put a "recording" sign on the coffee table. I have to cross the room to get to the kitchen. Last Tuesday they shushed me for a fridge door. I pay half the rent. I don't want to kill their hobby. I also don't want to tiptoe in my own apartment. They're plugging in headphones. Talk tonight starts in twelve minutes. What do I say before the light goes red?

### Assistant
You talk now, before the red light, not during.

"I need the living room to be a shared room. I can't do silent Tuesdays and Thursdays 8 to 9:30 every week. Can you record in your bedroom, or we pick one night, or you do a shorter block when I'm out?" That's a menu. If none of those work, "Then we need a different setup. I'm not going to whisper at the fridge in a room I pay for." Don't mock the foam. Don't call it a fake studio. Last year's you would have worn socks extra quietly. This year's you can have a kitchen.

I won't invent whether a podcast "needs" a living room. I won't tell you their show will fail in a bedroom. Lots of people record in a smaller room. That's their problem to solve.

If they say the acoustics are better in the big room, you can say, "I believe you. I still need the room." Acoustics aren't a lease.

Twelve minutes: this conversation, not a snack run through the set.

Are they a peer roommate on the same lease, and is this new this month?

### User
Same lease. Started three weeks ago. They said they already booked guests for 8 p.m. I don't want to be the person who ruins a guest. I also don't want to stand in the hallway eating crackers. Sister calls at 8:15 my time because that's when her kid is down.

### Assistant
Guests they booked without asking you are not your emergency. You can still be decent: "Tonight, I'll stay in my room after 8. Starting next week, I need the living room back on at least one of those nights, and I need kitchen access without a shush. Move the guest to your room or a library booth or a different hour." Tonight as a one-time courtesy is a gift. Don't make it a precedent in the same sentence. "Tonight I'll work around it. Next Tuesday we need a new plan."

Sister: you can call from your room tonight with a headphone. That's a compromise for twelve minutes from now, not a forever. If your room is too close and bleeds, that's more evidence the living room show is a shared-wall problem, not just a fridge door.

Don't eat crackers in the hall as a personality. That's how resentment gets crumbs.

If they already have a guest on the line in twelve minutes, you do tonight's quiet as the last freebie you intend, and you put the plan in a text after: dates, kitchen, sister, bedroom. Paper after so the red light isn't the only witness.

### User
I'll give them tonight and send the text after. They just asked me to "fill water glasses for the guest." That's me as intern. No, right?

### Assistant
No. "I'm not crew. I'll be in my room. Good luck." Water is their set. You're not the podcast. You're the roommate.

After: text the plan. Next week, walk to the fridge like a person who lives there, on the night you reclaimed, even if the foam is still out. If they shush, you repeat: "Kitchen is shared. Record in your room."

Twelve minutes: sentence, room, door. You're not killing a hobby. You're stopping a living-room takeover. Get the water out of your job description. Get the sister call from a room that's yours. That's the whole red light, without tiptoe rent. Say it before they put the headphones on. Then you don't have to mime the fridge.
