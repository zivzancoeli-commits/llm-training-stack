### User
The smoke alarm in the apartment next door has been going off for like twenty minutes. It's the chirp-scream, not a little battery beep. I can hear it through the wall. Nobody's answering when I knock. I don't smell smoke on my side. My mom isn't home and I'm not trying to be the kid who calls 911 for a low battery. What do I do that isn't dumb?

### Assistant
Before I tell you to do anything: when you knocked, was it the kind of quiet where you could hear the alarm and nothing else — no TV, no footsteps, no one saying "one second" — or did it sound like someone might be in there and ignoring you?

### User
Quiet except the alarm. I knocked twice. I also texted the building group chat and two people reacted with the eyes emoji and nobody volunteered. Hallway doesn't smell like toast or anything. The sound is definitely next door, not the hallway detector.

### Assistant
Okay. Quiet unit plus a loud alarm for twenty minutes is not automatically a fire, and it's also not automatically "ignore it, it's a battery." I can't tell from here. I'm not going to pretend I know what's burning, if anything.

Don't go in. You don't have a key, and even if a spare is under a mat, that's not your door.

Do this in order. First, step into the hallway with your own door locked behind you and check for actual smoke or heat at *their* door seam. If you see smoke, smell a sharp chemical burn, or the door is hot, that's 911, not a group chat. You're not "the kid who overreacted." You're reporting what you observed.

If the hallway is still clear — no smoke, door not hot, just the noise — then it's a nuisance until it isn't. Call the non-emergency number for your city or the building emergency line if you have one posted in the lobby. Say: apartment next to yours, alarm going continuously, no answer at the door, no smoke visible from the hall. Let them decide whether to send someone. I don't know your building's after-hours process and I won't invent a superhero super.

If you don't have a non-emergency number handy, that's what a search on your phone is for. Don't crowd-source it from the eyes-emoji people.

### User
Door isn't hot. Hall smells like hallway. I found a sticker by the mailboxes with a management after-hours number. I feel weird calling because what if they're just in the shower. Twenty minutes is a long shower with an alarm though.

### Assistant
A shower can set one off. People also leave the house with a dying alarm screaming. You can't know which. That's why you call management instead of guessing.

Use the sticker. One minute of awkward is cheaper than thirty more minutes of "maybe it's fine." When they pick up, skip the essay about being fourteen and not wanting to bother anyone. Give the unit number if you know it, or "the unit sharing a wall with [yours]," the duration, and that you knocked with no answer.

Stay in your apartment after you call unless they tell you to wait in the hall. Put on headphones only if you can still hear someone knocking on *your* door. If the sound changes from chirp to a different pattern, or you start smelling something, you call emergency services. Pattern change matters. I won't diagnose the chirp for you; alarms are not all the same.

You're allowed to be annoyed. Annoyance isn't a plan. The plan is: hallway check (you did), management call (do that), then wait.

### User
Calling now. If they say it's probably a battery I'll still want it to stop. Can I bang on the wall?

### Assistant
Don't bang on the wall. It doesn't reset their detector and it makes you the extra noise. Call, report, wait. If management doesn't answer, that's when you use non-emergency dispatch, not a broom handle. Come back if the smell changes. I'll stay on the boring side of this with you.
