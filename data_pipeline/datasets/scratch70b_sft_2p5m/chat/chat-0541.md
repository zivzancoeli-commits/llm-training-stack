### User
Christmas at my cousin's. I sat down with a plate. Before I had a bite, an uncle put a laptop in my lap that "just needs a look." Printer, wifi, a photo folder that vanished, a phone that won't take a charge. I am the youngest person who ever installed a printer. I wanted to eat and watch the movie. I have done this every holiday for years. First year I want to say I'm off the clock without becoming the villain who wouldn't help Grandma.

### Assistant
You can help Grandma and still not be an open ticket queue with gravy on it.

Eat first. Say it: "I'm going to eat. After dinner I can look at one thing." One thing is the whole policy. Printer-plus-wifi-plus-photos-plus-charging is four tickets. People will pick the loudest if you don't make them pick.

You are not required to accept a laptop onto a plate. Put it on a side table. Food and a hot machine is how you spill and still get blamed for the printer.

I will not invent a fix for a vanishing photo folder from here. Vanished photos need a careful look, not a holiday performance. If it's actually important, that's a daytime job with a backup, not a demo between pie and a movie.

Who is "Grandma" in this — is there an actual older relative who only trusts you, or is uncle using her as the reason?

### User
Grandma is real. She wants the photos. Uncle wants the printer for gift tags. Cousin wants the wifi password written on a card because guests keep asking. The charging phone is uncle's. Grandma is the only one I'd feel bad saying no to. The movie starts in an hour.

### Assistant
Sequence it like a person, not a help desk.

Dinner now. Then Grandma's photos, because that's the one with feelings. Sit with her, not with the uncle hovering. Ask what she last saw, which app, which device. If the photos are on her phone and she just can't find the album, that's a find, not a recovery. If they're actually gone, you tell her the truth: you may not get them back tonight, and you won't rummage in a way that makes it worse. I will not diagnose the folder from a dining room.

Uncle's printer and phone wait. Gift tags can be handwritten. A movie is allowed to start without a printer driver. If he puts the laptop back in your lap, "After the movie, and it's one item." You can show him where the paper lives.

Wifi card: cousin can write the password. That's host work. You can say "You have the password. Put it on a sticky note by the router." You didn't become cruel. You declined to be signage.

If uncle says Grandma needs all of it to be happy, that's bundling. Grandma asked for photos. Keep the bundle split.

### User
If I only do Grandma, uncle will sulk through the movie and tell people I "changed." I kind of have changed. I used to just take the laptop.

### Assistant
You did change. You got older and you noticed the plate. Sulking through a movie is his extra. You don't have to pre-pay it with four tickets.

"I can look at Grandma's photos after we eat. I'm not doing a full IT night." If he announces you changed, you can let that be true in public. Changed is accurate. Villain is his upgrade.

After photos, you watch the movie. If there's leftover energy, one uncle item, timed: twenty minutes, then you're done. Timers feel clinical and they work. Without a timer, Christmas becomes a bench.

Eat. One queue. Grandma first. No laptop on the plate. You're off the clock when the movie starts, unless you freely pick up a charger cable for two minutes. Freely is the word. Years of default yes is how the laptop found your lap before the fork did.
