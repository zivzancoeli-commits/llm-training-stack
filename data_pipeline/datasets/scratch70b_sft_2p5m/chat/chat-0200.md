### User
A cat that I am pretty sure belongs to the neighbor two doors down has been on my fire escape for four days. Then it came in when I cracked the window. Now it sleeps on my radiator cover and looks at me like I signed a lease. I am exhausted. I work early. I did not adopt a cat. I also keep feeding it because I am not a monster. I texted you because I do not know if I even want advice or if I just want to complain for the length of a break. Check that before you build me a whole plan.

### Assistant
Do you still want help figuring out the next step, or do you want me to sit with the complaint and not turn it into a project?

I can do either. I will not assume "texted you" means "fix my life." You said you are tired. Tired people get volunteered into errands they did not agree to.

If you want to vent: the cat moved the boundary, you fed it, now you have a radiator roommate you did not request. That is a real mess. You can be kind and still be resentful. Both.

If you want help: say so, and tell me whether you have actually spoken to the neighbor yet. That split changes everything.

### User
I want help, I think. Keep it small. I have not knocked. I have not put up a sign. I bought a can of food because the meowing got in my head. I do not want to become the building cat lady by accident. I also do not want to dump the animal in a hallway. Still want help. If I start sounding like I want you to drop it, drop it.

### Assistant
Small, then. And I will check again if this gets bigger than a break-room plan.

Tonight or tomorrow before your early shift, do one contact, not five. Knock two doors down with the cat inside your place or with a photo on your phone. "I think this is yours. It's been coming in my window. Do you want me to bring it over?" That is a question. It lets them claim the cat. If they say yes, you hand it over and you stop buying cans.

If they say it is not theirs, or they are gone, or they get weird, you stop expanding the job until you decide you still want it. You do not owe a missing-pet flyer empire on a work night.

While the cat is in your space: water, a box with torn newspaper if you have no litter, and the window less inviting if you do not want a revolving door. I am not a vet. I will not diagnose why it chose you. If it looks hurt — limping, eye gunk, not eating — that is a different call, to a clinic or a local rescue, and only if you still want that call.

I am not quoting ordinances about stray animals. I do not know your city and I will not fake a fine.

### User
The neighbor might be the kind of person who "lets the cat be outdoor." Then I look like I stole their pet. Also I am at work in my head already. If this becomes three phone calls I want out.

### Assistant
If they say it is an outdoor cat, you still get to say your window is not a second home. "I need it not coming into my apartment. Can you keep it in, or I'll stop leaving the window open." That is your boundary, not a kidnapping. You did not steal a cat by letting it in once. You also do not have to keep hosting.

If the conversation wants to become three phone calls — rescue, landlord, group chat — pause. Ask yourself the same check: do I still want help, or did I want one knock? You can knock, return the cat if they claim it, shut the window, and be done. That is a complete job.

If they do not claim it and you do not want a cat, a photo on a building board or a local lost-and-found page is one action, not a campaign. One post. Then you go to sleep.

You fed it because you are not a monster. That does not automatically make you its person. You asked me to check whether you still wanted help. You said yes, small. The small version is: one knock, one honest sentence, window policy after that. If you text later and you want me to stop planning, say stop. I will. The radiator cover can wait. Your break cannot.
