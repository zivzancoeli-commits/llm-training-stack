### User
Okay so I need a picture of me and my cousin in front of this mural and I keep waiting for someone who looks "safe" and then they walk past and I freeze. I don't want to sound like a tourist who is about to hand over a phone and never see it again. Also I don't want to be that person who dumps a whole photoshoot on a stranger. How do I even start the sentence without sounding weird?

### Assistant
Before I script the sentence: are you trying to get one quick shot of the two of you, or do you want a few angles and you are hoping they will stick around for that? Those are two different asks, and the second one is a lot more to put on someone who is just walking by.

### User
One good one. Maybe a backup if the first is blurry. Not a whole album. We're standing in front of a wall with a huge bird painted on it, downtown, lots of people, afternoon. My cousin is shy so I would be the one talking. Phone is just a regular phone, not some giant camera.

### Assistant
Good. One shot plus a backup is a normal sidewalk request. Treat it like asking someone to hold a door, not like hiring a photographer.

Walk up to a person who is already stopped or moving slowly — not someone mid-crosswalk, not someone with both hands full of groceries, not someone wearing headphones and looking at the ground. Face them, leave a little space, and keep the phone in your own hand until they say yes.

The sentence can be short: "Hey, sorry to bother you — would you mind taking one photo of us in front of this wall? It should take like twenty seconds." That last part is the kindness. You are telling them the job has an end.

If they say yes, do the setup before you hand the phone over. Show them the shutter button with your thumb. Tell them the frame you want in one line: "The bird's head above us, and both of our faces." Then you and your cousin get in place, look at the lens, and wait. After the first click, say "one more, same spot?" and stay put. Two clicks. Then you walk back, take the phone, and check the screen while they are still there so you are not chasing them down the block if it is a thumb over the lens.

If they say no, that is not a verdict on you. Smile, say "no worries, thanks anyway," and pick someone else. Do not argue or add a speech about how it would only take a second. They already heard you.

Keep it from feeling like a heist: stay close enough to see the screen. Unlock the phone, open the camera, and hand it over in camera mode. Do not make them hold your bag, your drink, and the phone. One object.

If nobody says yes after two or three tries, use a timer, prop the phone on a ledge, or ask someone who already has a camera out. People taking pictures of the mural are already in photo mode.

After they hand the phone back, say "thank you" and check the screen while they are still nearby. If it is actually unusable — both of you blinking — you can ask once for one more. Once. Then you are done.

### User
What if they start directing us like "move left, smile bigger" and it gets awkward, or they take like eight photos?

### Assistant
You can still run the clock. After the second shot, step forward, hand out, "that's great, thank you." You do not have to wait for them to finish a photoshoot you did not ask for. If they are being nice and overdoing it, you can laugh and say "two is plenty, we don't want to keep you." That is not rude. That is you ending a favor at the size you named.

If they start posing you and you hate it, you do not have to obey. A simple "this is good right here" is enough. You asked for a button press, not art direction.

As soon as you have a usable shot, lock the phone. Say thanks, walk ten steps, then look at it properly. Short ask, short job, short thank you. Go get the bird in the frame.
