### User
School emailed at 4:50. My kid can't come tomorrow without a vaccination form they say they don't have. We did the shots. I thought the clinic sent it. Clinic is closed. School office voicemail is full. Kid is six, already anxious about missing the class hamster. I am not a person who fights schools. I also cannot invent a form tonight. Walk me through tonight without fake medical advice and without me promising the kid a definite tomorrow.

### Assistant
Tonight you gather proof and you tell the truth in two directions: school and kid. You do not diagnose anything. You do not guess which vaccine is missing. You do not promise the hamster.

Look at what you actually have: shot card, patient portal PDF, email from the clinic, a photo of a paper in a drawer. Forward or upload whatever the school's last email asked for, even after hours. Many offices process queues in the morning. A timestamped send is better than a speech.

Leave voicemails anyway. Clinic's after-hours line if they have one, school nurse or attendance line, not only the full main box. Short: child's name, that records were done at clinic X on date Y if you know it, that you'll bring paper at the door, that you need to know whether tomorrow is a keep-home.

I will not invent the school's exclusion rule or the state's shot list. Those are on the email and on public school pages. Read the email twice. Sometimes they will take a portal screenshot at the door. Sometimes they won't. You find out by asking at drop-off if nobody answers tonight, and by keeping the kid's plan for home as a real backup.

How sure are you the clinic was supposed to send — did you sign a release, or did you assume?

### User
I assumed. There was a clipboard. I signed several things. I have the paper card in a kitchen drawer under takeout menus. Portal shows dates if I can get the password to work. Password isn't working. Kid is asking if the hamster will forget them.

### Assistant
Card in the drawer is tonight's document. Photograph it now, all sides, good light, no thumb over dates. Email that photo to the address on the school message. Save it to your camera roll twice. Paper goes in the backpack by the door, plus a folder so it doesn't get folded into a permission slip.

Portal password: use forgot-password now. Don't fight a login for an hour while the kid spirals. The card is older technology and it still counts as a record you hold. Clinic can confirm when they open. You are not waiting for the portal to be a good website.

To the kid, no fake guarantee. "The school needs a paper we have in the kitchen. I am sending a picture tonight. Tomorrow we might go to school, or we might have a home morning and try again. The hamster will still be there." If they want a hamster plan, a drawing for the class is a thing they can control. Missing a day is not being forgotten. Don't oversell. Six-year-olds smell propaganda.

You can also text another parent in the class: "Did they send a shot-form cutoff? We're sorting records." Information, not a campaign.

### User
If the door person says no in the morning, do I argue with a six-year-old listening.

### Assistant
You don't argue the policy at the door with an audience. You ask one question: "What exact document lets them in, and can I bring it later today?" If the answer is no for this morning, you say to the kid, "Home morning. We're still working on the paper." Then you leave before it becomes a debate. Door staff are not the legislature. Humiliating them doesn't open the classroom.

Then clinic at open, school office at open, physical card in hand. Ask the clinic to send records to the school while you wait. Ask the school how they want it — fax, email, upload, nurse mailbox. I will not invent their method.

Tonight: photos, emails, voicemail, honest hamster sentence, backpack folder, home-morning backup. No fake shots talk. No promise of tomorrow. You're a parent with a card under menus, which is a very normal kind of urgent.
