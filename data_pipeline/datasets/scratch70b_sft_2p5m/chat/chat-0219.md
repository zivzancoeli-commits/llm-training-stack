### User
My friend's birthday is Saturday. I want to do something that feels like a birthday, not like we forgot. I also have almost no money this month. I keep thinking if I say "let's just hang out" they'll hear "your birthday is a burden." How do I plan this so it doesn't feel cheap and sad?

### Assistant
Cheap and sad are not the same thing. Sad is no plan, last-minute pizza in silence, and you apologizing the whole time. Cheap with a plan can still feel like a day that belongs to them.

First, pick a shape: a few hours, not a whole production. Birthdays feel real when there's a start, a middle, and a small marker that this is their day. The marker can be a song, a candle in a grocery cupcake, a walk to a place they like. It does not have to be a restaurant.

I won't invent prices. I don't know what a bakery cupcake costs on your block or whether the park has a fee. You know your actual cash. Name a number you can spend without resenting Saturday, then build inside that number.

What's the number, roughly, and do they like being the center of attention or do they hate a fuss?

### User
I can do like twenty, maybe twenty-five if I skip lunch two days. They hate a fuss. They like being outside and they like specific snacks, not balloons. Last year someone did a big dinner and they looked trapped.

### Assistant
Then don't copy last year. You're already ahead because you noticed they looked trapped.

Twenty to twenty-five with an outdoor, low-fuss person is enough if you spend it on their snacks and a little time, not on decorations. Make a short list of what they actually eat. Buy those. Skip the generic "party mix" that nobody asked for. If they have a favorite chips-and-soda combo, that's the cake. If they like a particular fruit, buy that. Put it in a bag so it looks like you showed up with something, not like you raided your own cupboard at the last second.

Invite one or two people they already like, not a crowd. Text them first if you can: "I was thinking a walk and snacks Saturday, just us, no restaurant. That okay?" Let them say yes. A birthday they didn't agree to is the sad version, even if you spent more.

Pick a free place with a view or a path they already use. A reservoir loop, a neighborhood hill, a bookstore with a bench outside. Meet there. Don't make them host. Hosting costs energy they shouldn't spend on their own birthday.

Bring one cheap marker that says "this is the day." A card you wrote, a playlist queued on your phone, a cupcake with one candle if they like that. One marker. Not five. Fuss is a pile of markers.

If weather is bad, same shape indoors: snacks, a walk around a mall or a library, sit somewhere with windows. Still no restaurant unless they ask.

### User
I keep worrying they'll think I didn't try. Also I don't want the other friend to show up with a huge gift and make my snacks look pathetic.

### Assistant
Trying is the plan plus the snacks plus showing up on time. That's visible. You don't need to narrate how broke you are. Don't open with "sorry this is cheap." That sentence makes the day sad before the walk starts. If they thank you, say "happy birthday" and keep moving.

On the other friend: text them privately. "I'm doing a low-key outdoor thing, snacks, no big gifts. Can you match that?" Most people will, if you say it early. If they already bought something large, you still don't have to compete. You can say at the meetup, "We're keeping presents small today." Then you stick to your snacks. Comparison only wins if you join it.

If you skip lunches to hit twenty-five, that's your choice, but don't spend the extra five on something they didn't ask for. Spend it on the snack they named. Specific is what reads as care.

Saturday, in order: confirm time and place Friday night, buy the snacks that morning, write the card on the bus, meet them, walk, sit, do the one marker, go home before anyone gets tired. That's a birthday. It fits a tight budget because you spent the money on them and the time on a shape they can actually enjoy. No apology tour. No balloons. Just a day that has their name on it.
