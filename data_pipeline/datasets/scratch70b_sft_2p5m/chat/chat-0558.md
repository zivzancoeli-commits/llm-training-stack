### User
Shower drain is slow. I pulled a clump. It's not all mine. Roommate said "my hair just does that, it's genetic, you're making it a thing." I am making it a thing because I don't want to flood the pan. I bought a drain catcher. They take it out because it "feels gross on their ankle." I need a bathroom policy that isn't a science class.

### Assistant
Genetics can be real. The clump is still a chore.

"Hair in the drain is on both of us. Catcher stays. If you hate the ankle thing, we clean it every couple of days." Policy, not a biology quiz. You don't need to litigate follicles.

I will not diagnose their hair. I will not pretend a catcher is optional if the pan is backing up. Slow drain is a building problem if it keeps going after you clean. First you clean.

Put the catcher back. If they remove it, you talk once: "If the catcher is out, you snake or you pay for the next clog-clearing with me." I will not invent a plumber's price. Shared cost is the point.

Is this the only bathroom?

### User
Only bathroom. They shower at night. I shower in the morning and meet the clump. They said I can just grab it, it's two seconds. It is two seconds I resent.

### Assistant
Two seconds every morning is a job. Jobs rotate.

"I'll grab morning hair if you grab a night pass with the catcher in. Catcher stays." If they want you to be the designated grabber, that's unpaid janitorial. You can refuse the designation.

Resentment is information. Script doesn't need the resentment word. It needs the rotation.

If they keep dumping the catcher, you take a photo of the clump and the removed catcher once, not as blackmail, as a "this is the pattern" for a later house meeting. Once. A gallery is a war.

Flooded pan is worse than an ankle tickle. You can say that without a TED talk: "I don't want standing water. Catcher stays."

### User
They offered to "use a bag" on their head. They will not use a bag.

### Assistant
Don't build the policy on a bag they won't wear. Catcher plus a weekly clean is the policy. Bag is a fantasy that lets the clump win.

Tonight: catcher in, one conversation, rotation, no genetics seminar. If the drain is still slow after a real clean, then it's a maintenance ticket to the landlord, both names if you can. Clogs that survive hair-pulling are not a personality. They're a pipe.

You can respect their hair and still not want it in a wet hill in the morning. That's a bathroom, not a referendum on DNA.

### User
Catcher is back. They already flicked it onto the ledge "for tonight." Night shower hasn't happened. I can put it in again. I can also feel the genetics line loading in my mouth.

### Assistant
Put it in. Don't load the genetics line. "Catcher stays in. I'll clean it tomorrow morning if you do a night pass." If they flick it again while you're in the apartment, you say the standing-water sentence once more and you stop debating DNA.

If the drain is still slow after a real pull and a catcher week, that's the maintenance ticket, both names, photo of the standing water if it happens. You're not the science class. You're the person who doesn't want a pan to flood.

Only bathroom means you share the pipe. Sharing the pipe means a tool that stays installed, not a bag they won't wear. Ledge is not a compromise. Ledge is off. You already bought the catcher. Leave it where it works. Hair can be genetic. Clumps in the morning are still a chore with a rotation. That's the policy. Small on purpose. Put the catcher in before their shower so the ledge doesn't win by default.
