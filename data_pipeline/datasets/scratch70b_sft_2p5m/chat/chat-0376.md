### User
I'm on a first date. We finished the main course. I feel suddenly awful — sweaty, stomach, not drunk, I had one beer. I thought it would pass. It is not passing. I need to leave before dessert. I don't want them to think I'm fleeing the date. I also don't want to throw up on a white tablecloth. They just asked if I want to split a cake. I haven't answered. What do I say that is true and not a dramatic medical scene?

### Assistant
Leave. True and short.

"I feel sick — I need to go. It's not you. I'm going to get home." Put money for your share on the table or ask to split the check now. Then go. Bathroom first if you need it. Then night air.

You do not owe a symptom list. You do not owe a promise of a second date in the same breath. If you want one later, you can text from home: "Sorry I left. I was actually sick. I'd like to reschedule if you do." Later is cleaner than bargaining while green.

I will not diagnose you. Sudden sweat and stomach can be many things. If it gets scary — chest, faint, can't keep water — you do not drive as a hero. You get the help you would get if you were not on a date. Tonight you have described leaving a restaurant. So leave the restaurant.

Did you come in their car, yours, or transit?

### User
Mine. Parked two blocks away. They look concerned and started to stand up. I don't want a walk to the car with an audience. Check isn't here yet.

### Assistant
You can let them stand and still not take a procession.

"I'm okay to get to the car. Stay — I'll message when I'm home." If they insist on walking, one block of company is not a crime. Two blocks of narration is. You can say, "I'd rather go alone. Thank you for dinner." Then you go.

Flag the server: "I need the check now, I'm not well." Restaurants understand this. They will not make you wait for cake as a moral lesson.

Pay your part. If splitting takes too long, you can pay the whole thing and settle later. Money is cheaper than a tablecloth.

Do not drive if you feel like you might pass out. Sit on a bench. Water. Then decide. I am not your clinician. You know if this is "get home" or "don't get behind a wheel." If it is the second, a ride app from the curb, car stays, you retrieve it tomorrow.

### User
I made it to the sidewalk. They texted "was it something I said?" I haven't started the car. I still feel bad. I feel guilty too.

### Assistant
Text before you drive, one line: "You didn't say anything wrong. I felt sick. I'll text when I'm home." Then if you can drive, drive. Guilt is a passenger. It does not get the wheel.

When you are home, water, bed, whatever you do when your stomach goes. Tomorrow, if you want to see them, a human message without overselling romance: "Thanks for being decent when I bailed. I'm better. Coffee another night?" If you do not want to see them, you still send the "it wasn't you" so they are not writing a story. You do not have to book the coffee.

You left. That was the assignment. Cake can happen on a day you are not sweating through a shirt. First dates survive exits. Tablecloths survive too, because you stood up. Start the car if you are safe to. If not, sit and get a ride. Either way, the date is over. Let it be over for a medical-feeling reason, which is what it is. You do not need to perform okay-ness in a parked car. Go.
