### User
Doors were 7. Opener at 8. Headliner at 9:15. At 8:40 they announced a delay, no new time. I was going to buy a shirt in the merch line during the opener because that is the only way I get a size that fits. The merch line is now a second concert of people with the same plan. If I stay in merch I might miss the first songs. If I skip merch I go home without the shirt I promised my sibling. Phone is at 18%. I need a decision, not a vibe.

### Assistant
Pick a primary: the show or the shirt. Delays punish people who try to win both.

If the sibling promise is a specific item, ask whether a later restock, a booth after the headliner, or a next-day pickup exists. Ask merch one question: "Will you still have [item/size] after the set?" If they say no, you decide with that. If they say probably, treat probably as a maybe and look at the stage.

Phone at 18%: drop to low power, dim, text your sibling the situation in one line, then pocket it. A dead phone in a delay is a worse night than a missing shirt.

I will not invent whether the band will play a full set. Announcements will lie or not. You control the line versus the rail.

Is your sibling here, or is the shirt a souvenir you deliver later?

### User
Later. They're twelve and they wanted the blue one. I can already see the table from here. The blue stack looks thin. Security is not letting people save spots at the rail. I came alone. Delay just got a new text: "shortly."

### Assistant
Shortly is not a time. Thin blue is information.

If the blue stack is the promise, buy now if you can check out in a few minutes, then go in. A twelve-year-old wanted a color, not a perfect view of a first song that may not exist yet. If checkout is a 40-minute snake, skip it. A shirt after the show, or a different color, or a poster, is a better sibling story than you missing the band you paid to see.

You came alone. No one is holding a rail. Accept a worse spot if you leave merch late. That is the trade.

If you stay in merch, pick a cutoff: if you are not paying in ten minutes, you drop out of line. Cutoffs need a watch. Use the phone's clock, not the venue's shortly.

Do not try to bribe a worker to hide a shirt. That gets messy. Ask once about holding a size if that is a thing they do. If they don't, they don't.

### User
What do I tell my sibling if the blue is gone and I watched the show instead? I already hyped the shirt in a text. Also if I buy and then the show is cancelled, I have a shirt and a refund maze.

### Assistant
Tell the sibling the truth without a saga: "The line was huge because of a delay. I couldn't get blue without missing the band. I'll look after, or we'll pick something else." Twelve can handle a change if you do not over-hype a rescue. If you still want to try after the encore, try. If the table is stripped, you stop.

I will not invent refund rules for a cancelled show. If it cancels, you follow whatever the ticket place emails. A shirt you bought is a shirt you bought. That may be fine. That may feel like a tax. You still should not skip a working show for a maybe-blue in a 40-minute line.

Ten-minute cutoff in merch, or walk to the room now. Text sibling after you know. Save 18% for the ride home. You are not a bad sibling for hearing the band. You are a person in a delay. Choose. Then stop refreshing shortly.
