### User
I went to bed at 10:30. At 11:15 the dishwasher started. It's an old one that sounds like gravel in a drum. I share a wall with the kitchen. I don't know if it was my partner or a roommate — we have both, it's a crowded place. I am awake and angry in a small way that will get bigger if I storm out there in socks. I need tonight and I need a house rule that isn't a laminated chart.

### Assistant
Tonight: if you can sleep with a pillow and a closed door, do that. Storming in socks turns gravel into a fight about tone. If you cannot sleep and the kitchen is two meters away, you can walk out once, calm, and say, "I was asleep. Please don't start this after 10:30. Can we pause it until morning?" Some machines have a cancel. Some don't. If cancel dumps a lake, you don't cancel. You suffer the cycle and you talk in the morning. I won't guess your model's buttons.

Don't unplug a running dishwasher unless you know what that does to the cycle. I won't walk you through forcing it.

The house rule can be one sentence at breakfast: "No dishwasher after 10 on work nights." That's a clock, not a chart. If someone needs dishes done, they run it at 8, or they wash a pan by hand, or they live with a sink until morning. Three options. None of them require a poster.

Do you already have any quiet-hours habits, or is this the first late machine?

### User
First late machine this month. Partner sometimes starts it because they hate waking up to a sink. Roommate sometimes starts it because they cook at 10. Everyone thinks they're the reasonable one. I am the one with the wall.

### Assistant
Then breakfast is a three-person sentence, not a hunt for the culprit of 11:15. Culprit hunts make people lie. A clock doesn't care who pressed start.

You can say, "Whoever starts it, not after 10 on nights before work. The kitchen wall is my bedroom wall." If they need a sink in the morning, they can rinse tonight and run the machine at dinner. Hating a morning sink is a preference. Sleep is also a preference. In a crowded place, the louder appliance loses the late slot.

If someone says "it isn't that loud," you don't hold a listening party at 11. You say it is loud in the bedroom and you are asking for a time. They can visit the bedroom during a cycle tomorrow if they truly don't believe you. Daylight demos beat midnight trials.

A delay start, if the machine has one, is a tool. I won't claim yours has it. If it does, they set it for 7 a.m., not 11 p.m. If it doesn't, the rule is earlier hands, not smarter plastic.

Don't stack this onto unrelated chores. One appliance, one clock. That's how you avoid the hour meeting.

### User
If I go out there now I'll sound snappish.

### Assistant
Then don't go unless you cannot sleep. Snappish at 11:20 becomes the topic instead of the gravel. Put the sentence in your mouth for morning, when you sound like a person. If you must go now, one line, no history of who cooks at 10, no character analysis. Then back to bed.

Morning: the 10 p.m. rule, delay start if it exists, dishes at dinner. You can hate a sink at 7 and still not run a drum of gravel through a shared wall. That's a house, not a chart. If someone "forgets" next week, you repeat the clock, not the biography of who cooked. One appliance, one hour. You can say it while pouring coffee, which is a better soundtrack than socks at 11:20. Put the rule in the shared space once. Then sleep like a person who has a wall.
