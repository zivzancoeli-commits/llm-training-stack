### User
Someone in my building parks a motorcycle under my bedroom window. They start it at 6:50, rev, then leave. At night they come back and it idles. I sleep with the window cracked because the room gets stale. I don't know which unit owns it. There is no assigned parking posted that I could see. I work early. I am considering a note on the seat and also considering that a note on a seat is how movies start.

### Assistant
A note on a motorcycle seat is a bad movie. Paper blows, rain happens, the wrong person reads it, or it feels like you touched their thing. Do not do that.

A note on a building board, an email to the manager, or a conversation if you ever see the rider with the helmet off is better. You need the behavior: 6:50 start under a bedroom window, idle at night. You do not need a speech about their personality.

I will not invent whether that parking spot is legal, whether idle is banned, or what quiet hours your city uses. If you have a lease attachment about parking, read it. If you do not, the manager still might care about a pattern.

Do you have a manager or a landlord you already email, and can you see a plate from your window without hanging off the sill?

### User
There is a manager office that is open three days a week. I can see a plate if I stand at the side of the window like a raccoon. I already feel like a racoon. I don't want to be the plate person.

### Assistant
You do not have to lead with a plate. Lead with time and location: "Motorcycle under the north windows, starts around 6:50, idles at night, it shakes my room." If they ask for a plate later, you can decide then. Collecting plates as a hobby is how you get weird. A manager asking for one to find a tenant is a different request.

Close the window at night even if the room gets stale. Crack it when you leave in the morning. Stale is a fan and a ten-minute air-out. A 6:50 rev is sleep. You can also move the bed a few feet if the room allows. Geometry is not surrender.

If you catch the rider in a civil moment — helmet off, not late, not in rain — one sentence: "Hey, I sleep above that spot. The morning start is loud in my room. Any chance of rolling it out before you start it?" Some people did not know. Some people did and will shrug. A shrug is data for the manager email.

Do not record them in secret for the internet. A dated log of times is enough. If you later need to show a pattern, a notebook beats a creepshot.

Earplugs plus a fan are still allowed while this crawls through a three-day office.

### User
If I email the manager they'll tell the rider it was me. We share a laundry room. I don't want laundry tension.

### Assistant
They might. Buildings are small. You can still ask the manager not to name you, and you cannot control whether they do. If laundry tension is unbearable to you, try the in-person sentence first, then email if nothing changes. Order matters: human, then paper.

If you never want to be known, you are limited to your own room: window, fan, bed, earplugs. That may be the trade. I will not pretend a secret campaign is cleaner. Secret campaigns leak.

For the next ten minutes: close the window, start a two-line log for this morning, draft the manager email without a plate, and decide whether you will send it after one in-person try. If you see them tonight with the helmet off, use the sentence. If not, send on the next office day.

A motorcycle under a bedroom is a design problem. You are not a raccoon for wanting sleep. Skip the seat note. Ask a person, then a manager, and fix the stale air without paying for it in revs.
