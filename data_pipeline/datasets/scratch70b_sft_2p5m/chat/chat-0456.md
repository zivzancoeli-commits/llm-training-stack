### User
Priya asked me to "just dummy the dashboard" for a client demo at 10 a.m. She wants realistic names and a revenue chart that "looks like our good month." We don't have that month. The real numbers are messy. She said nobody will export the CSV. I own the warehouse queries. If I fake it, it's my query history. If I don't, she'll say I'm not a team player. Demo is tomorrow. Slack is open. What do I actually offer?

### Assistant
You can help a demo without minting a fake company.

Offer labeled sample data, or a subset that's real, or a clearly synthetic set with fake names that couldn't be customers. "I can load a demo environment with fake names and made-up metrics, labeled DEMO, or I can show last week's real messy numbers. I won't dress our actual warehouse up as a good month." That's the fork.

Don't invent a revenue figure. I won't either. "Looks like our good month" is a request to lie with a chart. If the client asks "is this current," someone will look at you. Your query history is a reason, not a personality.

If she needs pretty, pretty can be fake and stamped. If she needs true, messy is the product. Those are different meetings. Ask which meeting it is.

Is this an external client, and is your name on the live dashboard they'll click?

### User
External. My name is in the footer because I built the thing. She said "they'll never click footer." That's not a plan. I can spin a demo schema in an hour. I don't want to be the blocker. I also don't want a screenshot floating around as if we print money.

### Assistant
Then spin the demo schema. Label it. Don't point the client at prod.

Slack: "I can have a DEMO schema by 9 with fake accounts and a note on the page that it's sample data. I won't put inflated revenue on prod. If you need prod, we show real numbers." If she hates the banner, that's the cost of not lying. A banner is team play. A quiet fake in prod is you.

If she says the client will "feel it," they can feel a labeled demo. If they wanted a sales fiction, that's a deck, not your warehouse. You can even help the deck with hypotheticals that don't live in SQL. Hypotheticals in slides are ordinary. Fake prod is sticky.

You are not a blocker for needing a stamp. You're the person whose name is in the footer. Footers get screenshots even when people "never click."

Don't stay late inventing a perfect fake economy. An hour of obvious dummy is enough. Realistic names can be obviously fake: "Example Hospital," "Test City Clinic." Don't use real patient-shaped names if that's your domain. I don't know your data. If there's any chance of real people, dummy means dummy.

### User
She replied "fine, banner, but make the trend up and to the right." Still a lie, just in DEMO. Do I?

### Assistant
A demo can show a capability with invented trend. It should not be presented as last quarter. If the banner says sample, and you say out loud at 10 a.m. "this is not live revenue," an up-and-to-the-right toy chart is a product screenshot, not a filing. If she wants you to imply it's us, you don't. Agree on the sentence you'll say in the room: "Sample data to show the layout." If she won't say it, you say it when they share your screen.

If even that makes your skin wrong, offer a flat or messy demo. You can still say no to a specific fake story. "I'll dummy the layout. I won't build a fake success quarter." That's still help.

Tomorrow: DEMO, banner, your line in the room, no prod. Send the Slack now. Then close the warehouse. You can be a team player with a label. That's the whole dummy. Get the schema, get the stamp, don't print money in SQL.
