### User
The 9:40 never showed. It's 10:06, it's dumping rain, and I'm standing under this glass bus shelter that leaks right onto the bench. My phone says the next one is "delayed" with no number. I told my mom I'd be home by 10:30. I don't want to walk because it's like forty minutes and the sidewalk floods near the overpass. What do I actually do right now without looking like I'm panicking?

### Assistant
Stay under the driest corner of the shelter first. Don't start walking yet. Open the transit app, screenshot the delay screen, and send that picture to your mom with one sentence: "Bus is late, still at the stop, I'll text when it comes." That buys you time and keeps her from assuming you vanished.

While you're there, check whether the app lists a vehicle number or just the word delayed. A number means the bus exists somewhere on the map. Delayed with no number might mean it was dropped.

Can you see any other people waiting, or are you alone at the stop?

### User
There's one guy further down with a backpack. He's not talking to me. The app just says delayed, no bus number. The shelter light is buzzing. I already texted my mom "running late" but not the screenshot. Walking still feels stupid. There's a gas station across the street that's open.

### Assistant
Good. You're not stranded; you're waiting with a backup building in sight. Send the screenshot now, not another vague "late." Vague texts make parents invent worse stories.

Stay at the stop for one more scheduled slot if the timetable shows anything after 9:40 — even a 10:15 or 10:20. If the board or the app has gone blank, treat the gas station as your dry room, not as a place to wander. Cross at the light, not mid-block. The street is shiny and drivers will not see you as well as they think they can.

Inside the gas station, stand near the window facing the stop so you can still flag the bus if it ghosts in. Ask the clerk, "Is this stop usually dead after ten?" Clerks often know which buses skip the rain. You do not owe them your life story.

Do not start the forty-minute walk unless the app shows the rest of the night cancelled. Flooded overpasses turn a late bus into a soaked mess. Your mom would rather you be twenty minutes later and dry.

If the bus still hasn't appeared by the time you'd miss a 10:30 arrival anyway, call her. Voice is better than a third text. Say the stop name, the time, and that you're at the gas station. Ask if she can pick you up or if she wants you to wait for the last listed run. Let her choose.

The backpack guy can stay a background extra. You don't need a stranger's plan.

### User
If I call her she's going to do the voice. You know the one. And I still feel like I should have left the house earlier. The 9:40 is the one I always take after late practice. I didn't check the weather.

### Assistant
The voice happens because she cannot see you. A call with a location attached is shorter than the voice. Lead with facts: "I'm at Maple and 12th, gas station, bus didn't come, I'm dry." Then stop talking. Let her answer. If she offers a ride, take it. Pride about a late bus is a terrible raincoat.

You did not cause the rain. Checking weather would have told you to bring a jacket, not that a scheduled bus would vanish. Next time, before practice ends, glance at the app for the actual vehicle, not just the printed 9:40. If the map shows a gap, you leave five minutes earlier or you pre-text "might be delayed." That's the only habit worth keeping from tonight.

For the next ten minutes, do this in order: screenshot, send it, cross to the gas station, stand where you can see the stop, set a timer for twelve minutes. When the timer hits, call. If a bus arrives before that, get on, sit near the driver if the bus is empty, and text "on the 10:something, home soon."

You are allowed to be annoyed. Wet shoes and a missed curfew are a weeknight, not a character test. Get dry, stay visible, keep your mom in the loop with pictures and a stop name. That's the whole job until the wheels show up or a car does.
