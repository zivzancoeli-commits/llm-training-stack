### User
I had bloodwork on Monday. The portal showed "result ready" at 6:02 p.m. I opened it. Every line is a gray lock. At the top it says "see comment." The comment is "provider will review." No numbers. No call. It's Wednesday 8:40 p.m. I have been refreshing like it's a skill. I don't want you to tell me what I have. I want to know what a person does when a portal is a locked cabinet. My clinic's line is closed. I'm spiraling at a kitchen table.

### Assistant
You don't diagnose from a lock. You also don't have to sit in a refresh loop until morning.

I will not interpret your labs. I will not guess what "see comment" means medically. I won't tell you it's cancer or it's nothing. I don't have your numbers and I shouldn't.

Tonight: stop refreshing. Write down the time it posted and the exact comment. If the portal has a message button that's actually staffed after hours, send one message: "Portal says results ready / see comment / provider will review. I can't see numbers. When will someone contact me, and is there an after-hours number if this is urgent?" If that button is a black hole, don't send five. One.

If you have symptoms that feel like an emergency — I won't list a fake checklist as if I'm triaging you — you use the emergency path you'd use without a portal. A locked PDF is not a reason to wait through chest pain or whatever would have already sent you in. If you're "just" scared because of a gray lock, that's miserable and it's usually a morning-clinic problem, not a 2 a.m. ER for curiosity. I can't sort those for you from here. You know if this is fear or a body that's failing right now.

Sleep hygiene is unsexy and it's the actual job: phone in another room, paper book, the note already sent. Refreshing is not care.

Do you have a nurse line on the after-visit summary, or only the main clinic number that's closed?

### User
There's a nurse line on the printout I forgot. It's 24-hour for the health system. I thought that was for chest pain, not for a comment. I feel stupid. I also feel like if I call I'll be the person who wasted a nurse over a website.

### Assistant
The nurse line is for "I don't know if this is urgent," which is you. You're not required to have chest pain to use a number they printed.

Call. Script: "I had labs Monday. Portal says results ready, I can't see them, comment says provider will review. It's Wednesday night. I'm scared because I have no numbers. Can you see whether anything was flagged as urgent, and when the provider is expected to contact me?" Then you let them look. If they say nothing is flagged and morning is fine, you can ask what you should do if you feel X — you name your actual symptom if you have one, not a WebMD tour. If you have no symptom except fear, say that. "I'm not in acute pain. I'm scared of the lock." That's honest. They can still tell you whether anyone marked it urgent.

If they can't see the result either, you haven't failed. You've confirmed the cabinet is locked on their side too. Morning clinic it is.

You're not stupid for forgetting a line on a printout. Portals train people to stare at pixels. A nurse is a person.

Don't ask me, after they talk, to second-guess them. I still won't diagnose.

### User
I'll call. If they say "it's fine, wait for the doctor," how do I actually stop the spiral tonight without numbers?

### Assistant
You take their "nothing urgent flagged" as the only medical sentence you get tonight, and you treat the spiral as a separate object: walk, shower, write the morning plan (call the clinic at open, ask for a same-day explanation), phone charger in the kitchen, not in the bed. If you have a person you can text "portal locked, I used the nurse line, waiting until morning," do that. Don't text a group a panic theory.

If the nurse says it isn't fine, you follow their instructions, not mine.

You asked for what a person does. A person uses the printed number, stops refreshing, doesn't crowd-source a diagnosis, sleeps if the nurse said wait. The comment is not a secret you're failing to decode. It's a queue. Get on the nurse line. Then get off the portal. That's the whole locked cabinet, without a guess about your blood.
