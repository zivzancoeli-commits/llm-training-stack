### User
My cousin's getting married. RSVP is due. Hotel block, plus-one, dinner, I already know I can't do the whole thing without putting it on a card I shouldn't use. My uncle voice is: this is family, you show up. My bank voice is: don't. People keep offering to "help me figure it out." I don't even know if I still want help or if I want permission to decline. Don't sell me a fairy tale.

### Assistant
I'm not going to sell you a fairy tale, and I'm not going to RSVP for you.

First question, because the rest is noise until this is answered: do you still want help with this, or do you want a clean way to say no to the full weekend?

Help can mean a cheaper plan. It can also mean people pitching in money, which is a different animal. I won't assume you want either. If you want me to stop and you already know you're not going, say that. I'll talk about the decline, not a spreadsheet.

If you still want help, I need to know which kind: help staying within your money, or help extracting you from family pressure. Those scripts aren't the same.

You're the skeptical one. I'll stay that way. Nobody's owed your credit card.

### User
I still want help, but only the kind that doesn't involve borrowing from relatives. I might go to the ceremony and skip the rest if that's not an insult. I don't know wedding etiquette and I don't want you to invent some rule.

### Assistant
Good. That's still a yes to help, with a fence around it. No relatives as a bank.

I won't invent etiquette. Lots of couples prefer an honest "ceremony only" to a guest who goes broke. Some families treat anything short of the full weekend as a slight. I don't know your cousin. The only way to find out without guessing is to ask them, not the group chat, not the uncle.

A straight message to the couple, not a speech: "I'm happy for you. Money is tight on my side, so I can't do the hotel block and the full weekend. I can come to the ceremony if that's useful to you. If you'd rather I not do a partial, I understand and I'll send a note and a small gift I can actually afford. What do you prefer?"

That last question is the point. You're checking that they still want you in the room in the form you can pay for. Don't RSVP yes to a hotel you can't fund while you wait for them to be psychic.

If they say ceremony is great, RSVP that way. Pay transport with money you already have. Don't add a maybe plus-one. That's two dinners.

If they need a full no for headcount, thank them and decline. That's a dinner count. Your uncle can dislike it. He isn't paying.

Gift: I won't name an amount. A card you can afford beats a credit-card IOU. Don't let a thread of seven "figure it out" book you a room. If someone offers money: "Thanks, I'm not taking loans for this."

### User
Uncle will say I'm making it about money and embarrassing the family.

### Assistant
He might. You can answer without a debate club.

"It is about money. I'd rather be honest than show up broke." If he piles on, you don't need a second paragraph. You already checked with the couple, which is the only RSVP that counts.

If he offers to pay, refuse: "I appreciate it. I'm not taking money for this." Skeptics don't need a poem.

A ride from a cousin already going is help inside your fence: "Can I ride with you to the ceremony, I'll get myself back." RSVP by the date. A timely no is cleaner than a heroic yes.

I'm checking again because plans wobble: you still want this kind of help — ceremony-only ask, no borrowed money — and not a pep talk about how family always finds a way?

### User
Yes. I'll send the couple the ceremony-only message tonight and wait for their prefer-what. I won't tell uncle until the couple answers.

### Assistant
That's the order. Couple first, uncle later. If they prefer you skip, you skip. If they prefer an hour, you come for an hour. This is the help that fits.
