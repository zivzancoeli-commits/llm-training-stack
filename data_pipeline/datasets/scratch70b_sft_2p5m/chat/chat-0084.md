### User
There is a drip under my kitchen sink. I opened the cabinet and everything in there is slightly sticky and I kind of want to close the door and move. I do not know if this is "put a bowl down" or "the apartment is flooding." I have ten minutes before I have to leave for work. Talk me through those ten minutes like I am last-year me, the one who would have thrown a towel at it and hoped.

### Assistant
Last-year you would have thrown the towel, shut the door, and thought about it on the bus. This-year you can do a short, unromantic sequence and still catch the bus.

Minute one: do not yank every bottle out in a panic. Pull the cleaners from the puddle so you are not mixing mystery liquids with your sleeves. Put them in the dish rack.

Minute two: look at where the drip is actually coming from. Phone flashlight. Common cheap-apartment picture: a U-shaped pipe under the drain, two slip nuts, sometimes a dishwasher hose. If the drip is at a joint, that is different from a drip on the shutoff valves on the wall.

Minute three: stop more water from arriving. Under the sink there are usually two oval handles or two small valves on the wall — one hot, one cold. Turn them clockwise. Clockwise is "off" on most of these. If they are stuck, do not prove you are strong; you can snap a cheap valve. If they turn, run the kitchen faucet for a second. If it slows to nothing, you just bought yourself a dry cabinet until tonight. If it does not slow, the leak may be on the drain side, which only drips when the sink is used. Then do not run the sink.

Minute four: bowl. A real bowl, not a sponge you will forget. Particle board under a sink turns to mush. Ugly is the point.

Minute five: dry what you can reach. If a slip nut on the U-pipe is wet, try snugging it by hand, maybe a quarter turn. Righty-tighty. If it takes a wrench and you do not have one, stop. I would rather you leave a controlled drip into a bowl than crack a nut on the way out the door.

Minute six: if there is a garbage disposal, do not switch it on to "test." If there is a dishwasher, do not run it.

Minute seven: take a picture of the wet joint. Future you will not remember which nut.

Minute eight: if water is coming from the valves on the wall, not the drain, that is a supply leak. If shutting the valves did not help and water is still arriving, leave a note for whoever owns the building, or call the number on the lease, before you get on the bus. I am not going to invent your building's rules.

Minute nine: put a dry towel in front of the cabinet so you do not skate when you come home.

Minute ten: wash your hands. Then leave. You are allowed to go to work with a bowl on duty. That is triage, not neglect.

Tonight you can look up whether you need a new washer or a plumber. I will not quote a price. I do not know your city or whether you rent. Renters: a photo and a message to the landlord is often the next step. Owners: a hardware-store washer sometimes is. Either way, the ten-minute version is the same: stop extra water, catch the rest, don't crack plastic, document, go.

Last-year you hoped. This-year you can have a bowl and a photo and still be on time. Hope is not a shutoff valve.

### User
The valves will not turn. They feel painted over.

### Assistant
Stop. Painted valves are how people break a pipe and then really miss the bus. Bowl under the drip, do not run the faucet, do not run the dishwasher, photo, message to whoever is responsible for the unit, then leave. Tonight you can ask for help with valves that may need the paint cracked by someone who is not late for work. Ten minutes is for stopping the situation from getting dumber, not for winning a fight with old paint.
