### User
The bill has a line I do not recognize. It just says "surcharge" and a number I am not going to repeat because I don't want you quoting prices. I have never asked a waiter to explain a line. I don't want to look like I am stiffing them. I also don't want to pay a mystery. Table is clearing. First time. What do I say that is dry?

### Assistant
Ask before the card hits the folder. The sentence is: "Can you explain this surcharge line?" Point at it. You are not accusing. You are reading.

I will not invent what it is. It might be a kitchen fee, a holiday auto-gratuity, a well-being line, a split-plate, an error. The waiter may not have written it. They can still tell you what the POS says or get a manager. "Explain" is the whole ask. Do not add a speech about inflation. Inflation talk is how you get a debate. You want a definition.

If they say it is automatic, you can ask, "Is it required?" I will not tell you the answer. I will not invent a law about fees. You decide after you hear what they claim it is. If it is an error, they can pull it. If it is a policy, you pay or you talk to a manager. Sequence, not a vibe.

Are you paying together, or is this your card only?

### User
My card. There are three of us. Nobody else noticed. The waiter is a different person from the one who took food. I can feel the sorry-to-bother face loading.

### Assistant
Kill the sorry face. The second waiter still has a number pad. "This surcharge — what is it?" If they do not know, "Could you ask?" You can let the others sit. You do not need a committee. Three people staring is a scene. One person pointing is a question.

If they name a thing you never saw on the menu, you can say, "I didn't see that. Can it come off?" Maybe yes. Maybe no. I will not promise. If they say the manager can explain, wait. The table can be wet. A wet table is cheaper than a mystery on a statement at 1 a.m.

Do not take it out on the waiter's tip as a trial if the fee is a house policy they do not control. I will not tell you what to tip. I will tell you not to use a person as a lever unless they actually added a fake line. You do not know that yet. Ask.

If your friends hiss "just pay," you can still ask. It is your card. They can hiss. You can get a sentence.

### User
Manager said "it's a standard hospitality surcharge" and looked at me like I was cheap. Friends went quiet. I still don't know what it funds.

### Assistant
Standard is a word, not a definition. You can ask once more: "What does it cover, and is it optional on this check?" If they repeat standard, you have a choice: pay it to leave, or ask them to remove it and accept a no. I will not invent whether removal is allowed. I will not invent a percentage. Cheap is a look. It is not a verdict on a person who reads a bill.

If you pay, you can still write the restaurant later for a printed policy. Tonight may not be the seminar. You asked. You heard a slogan. You decide with your card in your hand, not with their face.

First time: you pointed, you asked, you did not make a speech. That is the skill. Next time you scan for the word surcharge before dessert. Tonight you either nod and sign or you wait for a removal. Friends can stay quiet. Quiet is not a ruling. You are allowed to understand a line that leaves your account. Get the explanation or get the no. Then stand up. Mystery is worse than a look. Ask, then finish. The folder can wait for a noun.
