### User
There's a clerk at the pharmacy counter who calls me honey every time I pick up. I'm an adult. It's not a date. I freeze, smile, and leave. Tomorrow I have to go back for a refill and I want to say something once, calmly, without a TED talk. I've never corrected a stranger about a nickname. I don't want to get a manager involved. I also don't want to hear honey again while I'm holding a paper bag.

### Assistant
You can correct a nickname without a meeting. One sentence is enough.

At the counter, when it happens, look at their face, not the bag. Say: "Please don't call me honey. My name is on the bag if you need one." Then you stop. You do not explain gender, age, or your day. You do not apologize for having a preference.

If they say they call everyone honey, you can repeat once: "I know. Please don't use it with me." Repeating is not a fight. Adding a speech is how you start shaking.

You do not need a manager for a first request. Managers are for a second problem. Tomorrow is the first request.

Do you want them to use your actual name, or is "you" enough as long as honey stops?

### User
My first name is on the order. I'd rather they just skip the pet name. If I give my name I feel like I'm asking to be friends. There's a line behind me. I always worry I'll hold people up and then I'll look like the difficult customer.

### Assistant
A nickname correction is shorter than the smile-and-freeze you already do. The line will survive two seconds.

You do not have to offer your first name. "Please don't call me honey" is complete. If they ask what to call you, "Just skip that part" works. If they glance at the bag and use the printed name, that is their choice, not a friendship.

Stand in your spot. Hold the bag. Do not laugh after you say it. Laughing is how people hear "just kidding." If your voice comes out quieter than you meant, that is still a real sentence. You do not have to redo it louder unless they literally did not hear you.

If they look wounded, let them look wounded. You did not insult their family. You declined a pet name at a register. That is ordinary.

If they double down with "relax, honey," you then have a second problem. Still no TED talk. "I asked you not to." If it continues, that is when you can say, "I'd like a different cashier," or step aside and ask whoever is free. You do not have to pre-write the manager plot. Tomorrow may end at the first sentence.

### User
What if I say it and then they get icy about my refill and I have to come here every month?

### Assistant
Icy is uncomfortable. Honey every month is also uncomfortable. You already live in the second one.

A refill is a transaction. You can stay polite about the pickup even if the pet name stops. "Thanks" at the end is enough warmth. You do not need to soothe them for following a basic request.

I will not invent store policy or whether they can withhold a filled prescription over a nickname. If something truly blocks the pickup, that is a different conversation with the pharmacy, not a reason to swallow honey forever. For tomorrow, assume they can still hand you a bag.

Practice once tonight out loud in the car: "Please don't call me honey." Flat. Then tomorrow you say it at the counter, take the bag, leave. If your hands shake, that is allowed. You still said it. First times are allowed to be clumsy. They are not required to be speeches. Go get the refill.

### User
I'm going at lunch. If I rehearse too much I'll sound scripted and then I'll lose the sentence. Is one practice in the car enough?

### Assistant
One practice is enough. Twice if your mouth still goes blank. You are not auditioning. You are declining a pet name at a register. Tomorrow: bag, sentence, thanks, door. If honey happens before you can speak, say it on the next breath. Do not wait for a perfect opening. The refill is the opening. Go.
