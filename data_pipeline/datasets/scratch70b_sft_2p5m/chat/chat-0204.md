### User
I washed a shirt and it came out smaller. Not "vintage fitted." Genuinely shorter in the sleeves and tight across the chest. I still have the receipt in the email. I have about ten minutes before I need to leave the house. Walk me through those ten minutes like you are last-year me, the one who would have stuffed the shirt in a drawer and sulked. I want to actually start the return, not just think about it.

### Assistant
Minute one: do not sulk in the drawer. Put the shirt on the table. Find the receipt email. Search the store name plus "order" if you have to. Screenshot it or leave the email open. You need the date and the order number, not a feeling of injustice.

Minute two: read the care label on the shirt. If you washed it hotter or drier than the label, the store may still take it, or they may not. I will not invent their policy. You still go in or start the return flow with the truth if they ask. Lying about the wash is how you get stuck in a story. If the label said tumble low and you used high, say that when asked. If you followed the label, say that.

Minute three: look up the store's return page or the packing slip. Some places want it unworn, tags on. Yours was worn and washed. That may mean store credit, a no, or a yes anyway. I will not promise which. Your ten minutes are for starting, not for winning a fake guarantee.

Minute four: fold the shirt. Put it in a bag with the receipt printout if you can print, or with a note of the order number on a sticky. Keys, wallet, phone.

Minute five: decide channel. If the store is on your way, you drop it after the thing you are leaving for, not as a separate quest that dies at 9 p.m. If it is online-only, open the return portal now and start the form even if you submit later tonight. Starting the form is the opposite of the drawer.

### User
It is a chain store I pass on the bus. I dried it on high even though the label said low. I already know that part is on me. Last year I would have decided I "deserved" to lose the money. I have six minutes. What do I say at the counter so I do not freeze?

### Assistant
Minutes six and seven: write the sentence in your notes app.

"Hi. I bought this shirt on [date], order [number]. I dried it on high and it shrank. I'd like to see what you can do for a return or exchange."

That is the whole speech. You named the mistake. You did not grovel and you did not demand a law. Counter people hear this all day. They will look at a screen. They may offer store credit, a different size, or a no. You can accept or walk out with the shirt. You do not need a closing argument.

If they say policy is no on washed items, you say "thanks" and you leave. Then you still did not sulk in a drawer for a month. You got an answer.

Minute eight: put the bag by the door so you cannot "forget."

Minutes nine and ten: leave the house. On the bus, do not rehearse a sadder version. You already have the sentence.

Last-year you skipped the counter because shame felt like a complete process. Shame is not a process. A sentence plus a bag is.

### User
What if they are rude? And what do I do with the shirt if they say no? I do not want it in the rotation looking like it belongs to a cousin.

### Assistant
If they are rude, you stay on the sentence. You do not match it. "Okay. Thanks." Then you go. You can email customer service later with the order number if you still care. I will not write you a viral script.

If they say no, the shirt does not have to live in your rotation. Donate it if it is in decent shape, give it to someone it actually fits, or cut it for rags if it is ruined for people. Drawer exile is how it stays a little monument to the dryer. Get it out of the bedroom tonight either way.

You asked for ten minutes. You used them to find the receipt, tell the truth about the heat, pack the bag, write one counter line, and put it by the door. That is the return starting. The drawer does not get a vote this time.
