### User
There's a drip in the wall behind the bathroom. I hear it at night. I reported it in the portal in March, April, and last week. Someone came once, said they couldn't find it, and left. It is louder. I have videos. I don't want to sound unhinged in the fourth ticket. I also don't want to sleep next to a drip until the wall fails. What's the fourth message, and what do I do tonight that isn't opening the drywall myself?

### Assistant
Do not open the drywall. That turns a drip into your hole.

Tonight: towels if anything is wet, a bowl if it is reaching a visible pipe, the fan if it helps you sleep, and the videos backed up to two places. You are documenting, not remodeling.

Fourth ticket: short, dated, numbered. "Fourth report. Previous tickets: [IDs/dates]. Sound is louder at night. Video attached, recorded [date/time]. Please schedule a return visit that includes checking the line in the wall, not only the visible trap. I can be home [two windows]." Ask for a work order number in the reply.

I will not diagnose a leak or a mold risk. I will not invent a building code. If you see actual water on the floor, that is a same-day call, not a portal essay.

Is there any visible wet spot, or only sound?

### User
Only sound, plus a faint cold patch on the paint. No puddle. The portal auto-closes tickets when they "complete" a visit. That is how three became three. They like to say "monitor it."

### Assistant
Then your fourth message should say the last visit closed without a find, and monitoring has not reduced the sound. Ask them not to close the ticket until a source is identified or a follow-up date is set. They may still close it. You still write it.

Call if the portal eats things. Get a person, get a name, get a time. Follow the call with a one-line email: "As spoken with [name] at [time], requesting a return visit for the drip reported [dates]." Paper after voice.

Cold patch: photograph it next to a coin or a note with the date. Same lighting if you can. A patch is more than a vibe.

Sleep in the other room if you have one. A drip can make a brain hunt. Hunting all night does not fix a pipe.

If they say monitor again, ask what they are monitoring and when they will return. "Monitor" without a date is a shrug.

### User
Should I tell other tenants on the stack in case it's their tub? I don't want to knock on strangers. I also don't want to be the only one they can ignore.

### Assistant
You can put a polite note in the hallway or ask the office to check units above if that is how your building works. Knocking on strangers is optional. "Hi, I hear a drip at night in 3B — if you have a slow tub, could you look?" is enough if you do knock. No accusations.

The office is still the owner of the wall. Other tenants can add tickets. They cannot be your only strategy.

Tonight: photo of the patch, backup the videos, sleep elsewhere if you can, write the numbered ticket with two time windows. Do not take a knife to the paint. Fourth time is allowed to be firm and still short. Send it. Then put on a podcast that is louder than a drip, on purpose, so your ears get a job besides waiting.

### User
I started the fourth ticket and the portal already suggested "issue resolved — reopen?" which feels like a trap.

### Assistant
Reopen is the correct button if that is how their system works. Use it. In the box, paste the numbered history, the video date, the cold-patch photo, and two time windows. Ask them not to close it until a source is named or a follow-up is dated. If the form is tiny, put the rest in the first reply email after you submit. Tonight you still do not cut the wall. Sleep in the other room. Tomorrow you call if the portal auto-completes again. A suggested "resolved" is their template, not your ears. Your ears still hear a drip. Reopen. Attach. Ask for a work order number. Then stop refreshing until morning.
