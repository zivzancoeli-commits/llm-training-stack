### User
I have been in this library for twenty minutes pretending to look at the new-books shelf. I need a bathroom. I can see the desk. The person there looks busy with a line of people holding holds. I feel stupid asking for a bathroom like I need a guided tour of the building. There are no obvious signs from where I'm standing. I don't want to wander into staff-only and I don't want to hover. What do I actually say, and do I wait for the line?

### Assistant
You ask for the bathroom. You do not request a tour. Those are different sentences, and librarians hear the first one all day.

If there is a line of people with books in their hands, you can wait for a gap, not for the line to die. A bathroom question is short. You are allowed to be the short question. If the desk person is mid-scan, stand to the side, not in the checkout path, until they glance up.

The sentence is: "Hi — where is the restroom?" That is the whole script. You do not need "I'm so sorry to bother you" or a story about how you usually know buildings. Extra apology makes it feel like a tour request. It isn't.

If they point and you still don't see it, you say, "I don't see it from here. Is it past the stairs or the other way?" One clarifying question is fine. Hovering after they pointed is how you get a volunteer who walks you there while you die inside. You can decline an escort: "Thanks, I can find it." Then you walk in the direction they pointed with purpose even if you are 60 percent sure.

Are you carrying a bag that needs a stall, or do you just need the room?

### User
Just the room. I have a backpack. I keep thinking they'll assume I don't have a library card and I'm loitering. Also there's a second desk deeper in that looks less busy. Maybe I should go there so I don't interrupt checkout.

### Assistant
A bathroom is not a membership test. You do not flash a card to pee. If they ask whether you have a card, you can say yes or no truthfully. Neither answer is a crime. I will not invent this library's visitor rules. If a sign at the door already said the public can come in, you are in.

The quieter second desk is a reasonable choice if you can reach it without getting lost. A less busy person can point without a queue staring at your bladder. If the second desk is reference, they still know where the toilets are. People who work in a building know the toilets.

Do not wander in a widening spiral hoping a restroom appears like a plot twist. That is how you end up in workrooms. If you see a "staff" sign, you turn around. Ask a human.

Backpack: wear it. Don't leave it on a chair "for a second" if you don't know the layout. Bring it to the stall or put it where you can see it, depending on the room. I won't pretend I know this building's stall size. If the first restroom is a single occupancy and your pack won't fit comfortably, you set it down inside and lock the door. Ordinary.

If both desks look slammed, you can ask anyone with a staff lanyard who is walking, same one-liner. Moving staff would rather give a point than watch you orbit the new books for another twenty minutes.

### User
What if they start explaining the whole floor and I just need to go?

### Assistant
You interrupt politely because your body has a deadline. "Thanks — I just need the restroom for now." Smile is optional. Clarity is not. If they keep talking about maps and programs, you take one step in the direction they already indicated and say "got it." Movement ends tours.

You waited twenty minutes because you treated a building question like a social exam. It isn't. Walk to whichever desk you can reach first. Say the short sentence. Follow the point. If you miss it, ask the next staff person the same short sentence. You do not owe a story. You need a door with a lock. Go get it.
