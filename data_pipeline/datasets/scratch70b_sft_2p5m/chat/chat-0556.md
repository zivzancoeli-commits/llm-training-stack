### User
Hail on a tin roof. It's 1 a.m. It sounds like someone pouring bolts. I cannot think, let alone sleep. I'm in a rental with this roof. No basement. Dog is under the bed. I keep wanting to do something, like that's a task. There is no task. Talk me through the next hour so I don't start pacing like the hail is a problem I can solve with a personality.

### Assistant
There isn't a personality that stops hail. There is an hour you can make less stupid.

Stay off the porch. Hail can hurt. Dog under the bed is a reasonable dog. You can sit on the floor near the bed so you're a pack, not a pacer. Pacing adds your own noise.

Ear protection if you have it: earplugs, headphones with something brown-noisy, a pillow cave. Tin roofs win against cheap foam sometimes. Layer anyway. You are aiming for less, not silence.

Move to the room with the least roof in your ear if there is one — interior bathroom, closet with blankets, a car only if it's covered and you're safe, which many people aren't. I will not invent your layout. Pick the smallest, most insulated box you have.

I will not diagnose the dog. If they're shaking, you stay, you don't scold, you don't film it for a story. Water in a bowl where they can see it.

Is the hail mixed with lightning close, or is it mostly ice on metal?

### User
Lightning a few miles out, I think. Thunder after. Hail is the main event. Phone says it should pass but phones have lied. I already texted my sister "this roof" which was useless. I want to check the car. The car is in the driveway, not under anything.

### Assistant
Car stays. You don't go stand in hail to inventory a dent. Dents wait for daylight. Driveway heroics are how people slip.

Sister can get "we're okay, it's loud, talking in the morning." Useless texts happen at 1 a.m. You can send the better one now or not. You don't need a conversation.

If thunder is delayed, you're not in the worst of the cells, maybe. Still not a porch night. Chargers unplugged if that's your habit in storms. I will not lecture you into a prepper kit. One surge strip unplugged is enough of a task if you need a task. Then you sit down again.

Phone forecast is a maybe. Set your mind on "it ends" without a minute. Hail cells often dump and move. Some stall. You don't control stalling.

### User
If I can't sleep at all, work is wrecked. I keep doing the math of how ruined tomorrow is.

### Assistant
Stop the math. You cannot bank sleep that isn't happening. You can reduce harm: lie down even if you're awake, eyes shut, dog nearby, plugs in. Rest is not only sleep. Tomorrow you tell work you had a loud storm night if you need a slower morning. You don't owe them a weather essay.

At first light, then the car, then the roof from the ground, then a note to the landlord if you see damage. Not now. Now is not a claim.

Hour plan: off porch, off driveway, ear stuff, smallest room or floor by the dog, one honest sister text if you want, no pacing, no dent inspection. Hail on tin is supposed to sound like bolts. You're not failing a test. You're waiting out ice. That's the whole job until it thins.

Stay on the floor if the bed feels like a drum. Count breaths if you need a task that isn't pacing. Don't refresh the radar every thirty seconds; it makes the cell feel personal. When the hits slow, that's the pass starting, not a trick you have to verify on the porch.

Sister gets the okay text or silence. Car gets morning. Landlord gets daylight if there's a dent in the tin you can see from the ground. Tonight you are a person and a dog waiting out ice. Personality doesn't enter it. Bolts on a roof are just weather being metal. You can hate the sound and still not solve it. That's competence at 1 a.m.
