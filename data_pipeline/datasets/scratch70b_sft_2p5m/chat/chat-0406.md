### User
The family group chat is 14 people. My cousin is posting screenshots of arguments with their spouse and asking "am I crazy??" I have not answered. Aunts are answering. I was close to both people at Thanksgiving. If I side with my cousin I lose the other. If I stay silent I get a direct message that silence is betrayal. I do not know the marriage. I know the chat is a bad courtroom. How do I get out without a statement on the merits?

### Assistant
You can refuse to try a marriage in a 14-person thread. Silence in that thread is not a verdict. It is a venue choice.

Reply once, in the group or in the DM, with a process sentence: "I'm not going to weigh in on your marriage in the chat. I care about you. I'm not a judge." Then you mute the thread if you can. Muting is not a moral failure. It is how you stop being pinged into a trial.

If they DM that silence is betrayal, you can repeat: "I won't score the screenshots. I can talk to you as a cousin, not as a witness." If you do not want that call either, "I can't be in the middle" is complete.

I will not interpret the screenshots or guess who is right. I do not have the marriage. Neither does the aunt who typed first.

Are you closer to staying quiet in the group, or do you need one public line so people stop tagging you?

### User
I need one public line. They @ me. I also don't want to look like I'm siding with the spouse by not piling on. Thanksgiving is in two months. I can already feel the seating chart.

### Assistant
Public line: "Please don't @ me on this. I'm not commenting on your marriage here." Short. No emoji jury.

You do not have to add "I love you both" in the group if that will detonate. Save warmth for private, if you have warmth. Group chat is a stadium.

After the line, mute. If someone says you are siding with the spouse, you can DM: "I'm siding with not doing this in a thread." You will not convince the people who want a team.

Thanksgiving seating is a future problem. You can deal with it in October with whoever hosts: "Please don't sit me as a referee." You do not need to pre-fight September.

Do not send a paragraph about healthy communication. The chat will eat it and ask for a score anyway.

### User
What if I see something in a screenshot that looks scary, not just messy? I don't want a rule that I always ignore.

### Assistant
Messy arguments and scary ones are different. I will not diagnose from a description. If you believe someone is in danger, that is not a group-chat vote. That is a private check-in with the person, or whatever local help you would actually use. You still do not need to litigate it in front of 14 relatives.

For the ordinary pile-on, your line and the mute are enough. Post the sentence. Turn off notifications. Let the aunts have their evening. You can be a cousin at Thanksgiving without being a commentator tonight. Type the one line. Then put the phone in another room for an hour. The thread will continue without a closing argument from you.

### User
They @ me again while I was typing. The aunts have heart emojis. My draft feels late.

### Assistant
Late is fine. The thread will not wait for a perfect tone. Post the line: "Please don't @ me on this. I'm not commenting on your marriage here." Then mute. Do not scroll the heart emojis for a score. Do not add a paragraph under yours to soothe the aunts. If a DM lands in the next minute, paste the same process sentence. You are not late to a trial. You are leaving a stadium. Send. Mute. Put the phone face down. Thanksgiving seating can wait until someone actually sends a chart. Tonight you only owe one public sentence and a closed notification shade.
