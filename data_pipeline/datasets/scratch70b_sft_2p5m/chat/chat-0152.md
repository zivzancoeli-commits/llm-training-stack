### User
Friend keeps "just dropping by." No text. Last time I was in sweatpants eating cereal at 8 p.m. and they stayed two hours. I'm on my break, already tired of being the house with an unlocked vibe. I need to say no without inventing a yoga class or a contagious flu. How do I do that without a fake calendar.

### Assistant
You say no to the drop-in. You don't rent an excuse.

Next time they appear, at the door, before they fully enter: "I can't hang out tonight. Text me before you come next time." That's the whole no. Sweatpants are not a moral failure. Unannounced company is the problem.

Don't add a story about a headache, a Zoom, a cousin in town. Tired-coworker version is the meeting is over: short, repeatable, slightly bored.

I don't know your lease, your roommate situation, or whether this person has a key. Do they have a key, or are they knocking?

### User
Knocking, but I always open and then I feel rude shutting it. They say "I was in the neighborhood." Neighborhood is a twenty-minute bus. I like them. I don't like the surprise. If I say text first, they'll ask why I'm being weird.

### Assistant
"I was in the neighborhood" is their line. Yours is still "text first." If they ask why, you can tell the true why without a costume: "I don't do surprise visits. I want a heads-up." That's not weird. That's a preference. Weird would be lying about yoga.

If they push "but it's just me": "It's still a visit. I need a text." Just-me is not a free pass through the door.

You can like them and still not open for a two-hour cereal interruption. Liking is not a 24-hour lobby.

Practical door mechanics: open enough to talk, not enough to let the weather and the friend fully in. "Can't tonight. Text next time." Close. If that feels brutal, it's because you've been using open-door as the only polite setting. Polite has a second setting: words.

If they're already inside from habit, you don't have to fake a sudden appointment. "I need the apartment to myself tonight, so I'm going to ask you to head out." Give a few minutes for shoes. Don't fill those minutes with a consolation episode of a show.

I'm not going to script a fake emergency. If you want a softer version for the first time you change the rule, you can add "I should have said this earlier" — that's accountability, still not an excuse.

### User
What about a standing plan so they don't feel banned. I don't want to lose the friend. I also don't want Sunday 11 a.m. knocks.

### Assistant
Standing plan is good if it's real. "Thursday after work, text me by 5 if you're coming" is a plan. "Come whenever, just not randomly" is how you got cereal-o'clock.

Offer one window you can actually stand, not a fake busy week. If Thursdays are bad, pick a weekend hour. Then the drop-by is not banned; the surprise is.

If they sulk: you don't soothe by unlocking the old rule the same night. "I still want to see you. I still need a text." Two sentences. Tired people over-explain until the no becomes a maybe.

Texts from the bus: if they send "on my way" when they're already two stops away, that's not a heads-up, that's a countdown. You can reply "tonight doesn't work, let's pick a day." You are not required to win the race to the door.

If they show up after you've said no by text, you don't have to open. That's allowed. If you feel unsafe — different category than annoyed — don't treat it as etiquette. Get another person involved. Annoyed is this script.

You're on a break. Breaks are for not hosting. The recommendation is: no excuse, door-line tonight if they appear, text rule going forward, one standing window if you want the friendship fed on purpose.
