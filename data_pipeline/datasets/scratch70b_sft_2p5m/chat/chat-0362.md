### User
There's a brown map on my bedroom ceiling. It grew. I emailed photos two weeks ago. The landlord came, looked up, and said, "Old buildings have character. Keep an eye on it." A bucket is not on the floor yet. The paint is bubbling. I work under that ceiling. I don't know if this is a "wait and see" or a "the upstairs shower is emptying into my light fixture." They smiled when they said character. I did not smile. What do I send, and how soon do I send it?

### Assistant
Send today. "Character" is not a repair.

Write facts: date you first emailed, date they visited, photo of the growth, that the stain is larger, paint is bubbling, location (bedroom ceiling). Ask what they will do and when. Ask whether they are checking the unit above. You do not need a theory about whose shower. You need an action.

Tone: flat. No sarcasm about character. Sarcasm gives them a feeling to argue with. Size of stain is harder to argue with. Include a coin or a ruler in one photo if you can, so "grew" is not vibes.

I will not invent your housing code or how many days they have. Those depend on where you live. I will say that water plus electricity is a reason to mention the fixture if the stain is near a light. If the fixture is wet, you say that plainly and you do not keep using a dripping light. Flip the breaker for that room if you know how and if that is the safe move you already understand. If you do not, you ask them, and you can also ask a licensed person. I am not walking you through wiring.

Is the stain near a light or a fan, and have you been in the upstairs neighbors' good graces enough to ask if they have a leak?

### User
It's about a foot from the ceiling light, not on it. I don't know the upstairs people. The bubbling is new since the visit. Landlord texts more than they email.

### Assistant
Text and email. Text for speed: "Stain has grown and the paint is bubbling since you were here. Please send a plumber / roofer and tell me the day. Photos in email." Then email the photos so there is a record with attachments. Texts vanish in people's heads. Email sits.

Do not accept "keep an eye on it" as the plan. Eyes do not dry a ceiling. You can say, "I need a repair date, not monitoring." If they repeat character, you repeat bubbling. Same word, second time.

If they offer to paint over it, paint is not a leak fix. You can say, "Paint after the source is fixed." You are not a difficult tenant for wanting the water to stop.

A bucket under a dry stain is optional. A bucket under a drip is required. If it starts dripping, you photograph the drip, you move the bed, you text immediately.

### User
They replied "we'll look after the first of the month." That's eleven days. The bubbling is today.

### Assistant
Reply: "It's active now — bubbling since your visit. Please come sooner. If it drips or the light gets wet I will need someone the same day." You can ask who to call after hours. Get a number. I will not promise they must come tomorrow. I will say eleven days is a long time to watch a ceiling map.

If you have renters insurance, read whether it wants a report. I will not guess your policy. Reading it is useful.

Document every day with a photo. Same angle. Date in the filename. That is the opposite of character. That is a timeline.

You work under it. Move the desk if you can. Not because I think the ceiling will fall tomorrow — I don't know that — but because you will stare less and the paper pile won't be the first casualty of a drip.

Character can stay in the brick. Water belongs in pipes. Keep saying bubbling until someone who owns the building treats it like water. That is the job of the next message, not a smile you didn't give.
