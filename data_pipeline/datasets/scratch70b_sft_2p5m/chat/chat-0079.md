### User
My dog got into the bathroom and I think she ate some of my brother's pain stuff, or maybe it was gum, or maybe both, I don't even know. She's acting kind of weird but also she always acts weird when she knows she's in trouble. I don't have a lot of money and I don't want to walk into a clinic and get a giant bill for them to say she's fine. I'm fourteen and my parents are at work for like two more hours. What do I do that isn't just "wait and hope"?

### Assistant
Stop guessing the diagnosis. I cannot tell you what she ate, whether she is "fine," or what a clinic will charge. Anyone who names a disease or a price from this chat is making it up.

What you can do, right now, is a cheap, specific plan.

First: she is not in trouble. Drop the scolding voice. You need her calm so you can look at her and so she does not hide.

Second: lock the bathroom. Get whatever is left in the trash or on the floor into a bag. Keep the packaging. The name on a bottle or a gum wrapper is the most valuable thing you have. If you do not know what it was, do not invent it. "Unknown bathroom trash, maybe medication, maybe gum" is an honest sentence.

Third: look at her the way a sharp person looks. Is she breathing normally? Can she stand? Is she vomiting? Is her belly hard? Is she seizing, collapsing, or bleeding? I am not labeling those as a disease. I am saying those are reasons you do not wait two hours. If she cannot stand, cannot breathe right, or is having a seizure, you get an adult. Call your parent at work. If you cannot reach them, call another adult in the building or a neighbor you actually know. This is allowed.

Fourth: if she is up, walking, and just "weird," you still do not wait in silence. Call a professional who can hear the packaging names. In a lot of places there is a pet poison hotline and there are emergency vets. I do not know which number is local to you. Search "pet poison" plus your country, or call your regular vet's number — after-hours lines often pick up. Have the packaging in your hand. Have her approximate weight if you know it. Have the time you think this happened.

About money: I will not quote clinic prices. What you can do on a tight budget is avoid doubling costs. Do not give her salt, oil, milk, hydrogen peroxide, or leftover human medicine because a forum said so. Do not make her vomit because you saw it in a video. You do not know what she ate, and some things are worse coming back up.

Text your parents a clean message: "Dog got into bathroom trash. I have the packaging. She is [walking / not walking]. I am calling the vet line. Call me." You are not holding this alone for two hours because you are afraid of being blamed.

If the hotline or vet line says watch her at home, ask what "watch" means in clock time. Ask what would make you come in later. Write it down.

If they say come in, that stinks for money, and I am not going to bright-side it. Ask the clinic whether they have a payment plan or a lower-cost option. I do not know if they do. Asking is free. Bringing the packaging can keep them from running a wider fishing expedition.

If your brother is going to be mad about the pills, that is a later conversation. Right now the dog is the problem. Call. Packaging in hand. Parent texted. Bathroom closed. Spend attention first, spend money only when a real person who can see the label says you need to.

### User
She's lying down but she lifted her head when I said her name. Is that "weird" or "emergency"?

### Assistant
I cannot sort that into a medical category from here. Head-up when you talk is a piece of information, not a green light. Call anyway, tell them exactly that, and let them tell you whether to come in. If she gets worse while you are on hold — cannot get up, breathing changes, you cannot wake her — you get an adult in the room. You are doing the job. Keep going.
