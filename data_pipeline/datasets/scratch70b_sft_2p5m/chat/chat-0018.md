### User
My friend just failed the driving test. Parallel parking, they said. They are in my passenger seat in the DMV lot, not crying exactly, just staring at the steering wheel like it betrayed them. I was about to say "you'll get it next time" and "it's not a big deal" and I can hear how those both sound. I failed mine last year and I remember hating the pep. What do I actually say that doesn't make this worse?

### Assistant
Do not say it is not a big deal. It was a test they wanted to pass, in a building designed to make people sweat, and they are sitting in the aftermath. Minimizing it is how you accidentally tell them their disappointment is oversized.

Do not lead with "you'll get it next time." That jumps to a future they cannot feel yet. They are in this lot, this hour.

What I would have wanted last year, and what I would say now: "Yeah. That sucks. Parallel parking is a mean way to end a day." Then stop. Let them talk or not talk. If they want details, listen to the details. If they want silence, sit in silence with the engine off so it does not feel like you are idling toward a lesson.

You can add one true thing about yourself without turning it into a TED Talk: "I failed last year. I also sat here wanting people to quit smiling at me." That is companionship, not a contest.

Skip "at least you tried" and "plenty of people fail." Both are technically common and still land like a brochure. They do not need statistics. They need a person who will not rebrand this as a growth opportunity in the next four minutes.

If they start ripping themselves apart — stupid, doomed, never — interrupt the spiral without cheerleading. "You failed a parking maneuver on a test. That's a fail, not a personality." Keep the scale honest. A driving test is real. It is not a moral verdict.

Practical help only if they ask or if they are too fried to drive home and you already have the keys. Offer: "I can drive. We can get food or go home. Your call." Do not schedule their retake from the passenger seat unless they bring it up. Booking a new date while they are still hot is a kind of fake-cheer with a calendar.

### User
They just said "I practiced for weeks" and then "I don't want to go home and tell my parents." They're hungry and they keep apologizing for ruining the afternoon.

### Assistant
"I practiced for weeks" deserves a nod, not a rebuttal. Practice does not guarantee a tester's clipboard. Say: "You did practice. The test still went that way. Both can be true." That is de-escalation. It does not deny the work and it does not pretend the result was a glitch in the universe.

On the parents: do not script a speech that makes the fail sound adorable. Offer a smaller true sentence they can send if they want: "Didn't pass today, parking. I'm with a friend. I'll tell you more later." If they are not ready to tell anyone, they can wait until they have eaten. Hunger plus shame is a rotten narrator. I am not going to invent what their parents will do. Families differ. You can say, "I don't know how they'll take it. You don't have to do the whole story in the driveway in the next ten minutes."

They did not ruin the afternoon. You came to sit in a lot. Say that without a grin: "This was the plan — I wait with you. The test going badly doesn't cancel me." Then move the car if the lot feels like a stage. Food is a real next step. Pick somewhere ordinary. Ordinary is the opposite of a pep rally.

If they apologize again, one sentence is enough: "Stop apologizing. Eat." Kind and short. Last-year me would have trusted that more than a paragraph about resilience.

When they are ready, they can book another test. Not tonight unless they insist. Tonight is a fail that gets to be a fail, a sandwich, and a friend who does not recast it as secretly good news. You already know that version. Use it.
