### User
There's a third toothbrush in the cup. Purple. It showed up Tuesday. My roommate's partner stays three or four nights. We never talked about a third adult in the lease. I brush at 6:40 a.m. and they're in the doorway with a towel. I don't hate them. I hate the surprise furniture of a toothbrush. I pay half the rent. It's Thursday 8 p.m. Roommate is on a call in their room. Partner is on my couch watching a show I didn't pick.

### Assistant
A toothbrush is a flag. It isn't a lease by itself, but it's how people move in without a meeting. You pay half. You get a conversation, not a new cup arrangement by stealth.

Don't start with the lease law. I will not invent occupancy rules. Start with the bathroom clock and the couch. Those are daily.

Tonight the partner is on your couch. That's a now problem. The purple brush is the symbol. Talk to the roommate, not a speech to the partner during a show.

Have you and the roommate ever said overnight guests are fine, or has it just drifted?

### User
Drifted. I said "of course stay over" in January when it was one night. It's a pattern now. I haven't timed it. The towel in the doorway makes me late. I eat breakfast standing because the table is their laptop. I feel petty about plastic bristles.

### Assistant
January's of course was one night. Patterns rewrite of course. You're not petty about bristles. You're tracking a third schedule in a two-person rent.

For the next ten minutes: don't grab the purple brush and throw it. That's a war crime in a cup. Wait until the roommate's call ends. Then: "Can we talk about overnight stays and the bathroom in the morning? The extra toothbrush surprised me." If you need it shorter: "We need a guest plan. I'm getting boxed out at 6:40."

The couch show can end without a blowup: "I need the living room after 9" if that's true. If you don't need it tonight, save the energy for the roommate.

Ask for a cap: two nights, closed bathroom door, table clear at breakfast, toothbrush if you agree. If you don't agree to a third brush, they can bring it in a kit. That's not cruel. That's storage.

Partner doesn't have to be the villain. The missing meeting is.

### User
If I bring it up they'll say I should have spoken in February. That's true. I didn't. I smiled at the first toothbrush joke.

### Assistant
You smiled. That was a delay, not a forever contract. You can say "I should have said this sooner. I'm saying it now." Then the plan. Don't spend the whole talk on your delayed courage. Spend it on 6:40, the table, and how many nights.

If they want the partner to move in, that's a new roommate conversation with numbers and the actual lease, which I will not interpret. If they want flexibility, you still get mornings.

Thursday 8 p.m. is a good time to put a calendar placeholder: talk tonight when the call ends, not a passive-aggressive rearrangement of the cup. The purple brush can stay until you speak, then it can live in a kit. The couch can be theirs some evenings if you get the table at 6:40. Trade in the open.

You're not crazy. You're late to a sentence. Say the sentence. Half the rent includes half the sink. The doorway towel needs a door that closes. Ask for that. Then go brush your own teeth without an audience if you can still catch the roommate between the call and the next episode. That's the window. Use it.

If the partner is still on the couch when you talk, you can ask the roommate to step into the kitchen. This is a household meeting, not a three-person trial about who is nice. Nice is how the purple brush arrived without a date. You can still be civil and still want the sink at 6:40. Say 6:40 out loud. Numbers survive smiles. The call will end. The next episode can wait ten minutes. Ten minutes is the plan. Stand in the kitchen with a closed bathroom behind you and make the guest pattern visible. That is the whole Thursday job.
