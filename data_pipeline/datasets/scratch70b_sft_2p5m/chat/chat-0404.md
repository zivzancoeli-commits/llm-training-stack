### User
Family party on Saturday. Aunt always comes in with both arms. I do not hug her. I never have liked it. I usually freeze and let it happen. This year I want to decline. I am an adult. I still feel twelve when she says "come here." I don't want a scene in the doorway. I don't want a group discussion about how I'm cold. I need a body plan and a sentence that isn't a manifesto about touch.

### Assistant
You can decline a hug without a philosophy seminar. The body does most of the work.

In the doorway, keep a little distance as she approaches. A step back, a hand up at chest height, palm out, friendly not shoving: "I'm not hugging, but hi." Then you offer what you will do: a wave, a verbal hi, a side pat on your own terms if you actually want that. If you do not want a pat, skip it. Wave is complete.

Say it early, before her arms close. Mid-hug refusal is a wrestle. Pre-hug is a greeting.

If she says "don't be like that," you can repeat: "I don't hug. Good to see you." Then you move toward the coats or the kitchen so the doorway does not become a stage.

I will not invent family law. You do not need a medical excuse. "I don't hug" is enough.

Is there one ally in the room who already knows, or will this be news to everyone?

### User
My sibling knows and will probably joke to smooth it. Jokes make it worse. Aunt will ask if I'm angry at her. I am not angry. I just do not want her chest in my face. There will be a photo moment later. That is when she grabs people.

### Assistant
Tell your sibling beforehand: "Please don't joke if I skip the hug. A joke turns it into a bit." If they still joke, you do not have to laugh. You can look at the chips.

When she asks if you are angry: "No. I just don't hug." Do not over-prove warmth with extra stories. Over-proving is how you end up in the hug anyway.

For photos, stand at the edge, or hold a plate, or put a glass in your hand. Occupied hands are less grabable. If she pulls you in, you can step out and say, "I'm good here." The photographer can live with a gap. If they cannot, that is their album, not your body.

You do not have to announce it to the whole room. The people who hug you will notice once. Once is cheaper than years of freeze.

Practice the hand and the sentence tonight in a mirror if that helps you not go blank. It will feel silly. Saturday will feel fast.

### User
What if she tells my parent I humiliated her, and I get a lecture in the car? Also I might cry, which will look like I did something cruel.

### Assistant
Crying is allowed. It is not evidence you were cruel. Freeze-and-cry was the old pattern. Speak-and-cry is still you having a nervous system.

If a parent lectures in the car, you can say, "I greeted her. I didn't hug. I'm not discussing my body in the car." Then you can be quiet. You may not win the car. You can still have done the doorway.

If you need an exit, a bathroom trip after the greeting is a legitimate reset. Water on the wrists. Then you return as a guest, not as a defendant.

Saturday: sibling warning, doorway distance, sentence, move to the kitchen, occupied hands for photos. You are not required to make her proud of the new rule. You are required to get through the door as a person who does not hug. That is the whole assignment. Go eat a pretzel after. Pretzels help.
