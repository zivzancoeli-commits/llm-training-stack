### User
I ordered running shoes. Size 9. They sent 10.5. The listing said "please confirm size" like I failed a quiz. I've been on break for twelve minutes writing messages that sound like a lawsuit or a whimper. I might not even want the shoes anymore. I might just be mad and tired.

### Assistant
Then we shouldn't jump into a script as if the goal is already "get a 9." You might want a refund, an exchange, or to vent until the heat drops. Those are different jobs.

Do you still want help fixing this, or do you want someone to agree the seller is sloppy and then you'll decide later? Both are allowed. I just don't want to coach a return you don't actually want to do.

I'm not going to invent what the platform "has to" do. Policies differ. Spare-room sellers are not big stores. Stick to what you can see: you paid for a 9, a 10.5 arrived, a photo of the tongue label helps.

### User
I think I still want the 9 if they can swap without me paying shipping twice. If that's a hassle, refund and I'm done. I'm not collecting a saga. I hate looking like I don't know my own feet.

### Assistant
You don't look foolish for opening a box. They packed the wrong pair, or their shelf did. That's ordinary. The foolish feeling is optional.

If you still want help, keep the first message boring. "I ordered size 9, order [number]. The pair that arrived is labeled 10.5. I still want a 9 if you can exchange it. If that's not straightforward, I'll return these for a refund. Which can you do, and who pays return shipping?" Photo of the label. No feelings, no apology, no review threat in sentence one.

If they reply with "the chart is right, maybe try them," stay dry: "I ordered a 9. These are 10.5. I'm not debating the chart. Exchange or refund, please." That's not rude. That's refusing a side quest.

Don't wear the 10.5 "just to see" if that complicates a return. Don't cut tags if you still might send them back.

### User
I messaged last night. Quiet since. I keep refreshing like that's work. Also I tried one shoe on in the kitchen and now I feel like I ruined the case.

### Assistant
Refreshing is not work. Give it a normal amount of time for a small shop — often a business day, not an hour. I won't pretend I know their clock. If the platform has an order page with a dispute path, that's after a clear first message, not instead of it.

Trying one shoe on in the kitchen is not a crime. Don't do a five-mile test. Don't get them dirty. If you write again, make it additive: photo, order number, the two options. Don't send "???" tonight. That reads as heat.

And I want to check again, because silent hours change people: do you still want to keep this process going, or are you at the point where you'd rather eat the annoyance and donate the 10.5? I need the real answer, not the proud one. A tired read of your first message is that half of this is pride. That's fine. Pride is a bad shipping coordinator.

If you're done wanting help, say so. We can stop. If you still want it handled, we stay on exchange-if-easy, refund-if-not, then phone in another room.

### User
I still want it handled. Exchange if it's easy, refund if it isn't. I'll send the photo paragraph. I won't sit here writing a manifesto. I'm still on break for like four minutes.

### Assistant
Good. Send it in the four minutes. Then put the phone down. If they offer a prepaid return and a 9, take it. If they offer store credit you don't want, say no: "Refund to the original payment, please." If they go quiet past a reasonable wait, open the platform's order help with the same facts, same photo, no essay.

You already knew the shoes weren't the whole problem. The whole problem was feeling like you had to perform either rage or apology on a break. You don't. Wrong size, two options, wait, next step. That's the adult version, and it doesn't require a second personality. If you change your mind and you'd rather drop it, you can say that later. For now you said you still want help, so that's the job.
