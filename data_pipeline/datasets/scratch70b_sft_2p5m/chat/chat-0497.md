### User
My mail-order refill was supposed to land Thursday. Tracking still says in transit. It is Saturday. The nearest pharmacy I know is closed until Monday, and I don't have a car. I have a couple of days of pills left if I keep my usual schedule. I am trying not to panic-search forums. I don't want you to tell me to skip, split, or double anything. I want a practical weekend plan that doesn't pretend I'm a doctor.

### Assistant
Good. I will not tell you to skip, split, double, or substitute. I am not your clinician and I don't know the medication.

Weekend plan is logistics and people who are allowed to advise you.

First, read the bottle you still have: remaining count, pharmacy phone, prescriber name. Call the mail-order number on the package or site and ask where the package is and whether they can do an emergency local fill. Stay on hold if you can. Ask them to say out loud what they can do when a delivery misses. Write down the time and the person's name.

Second, call or message the prescriber's office after-hours line if there is one. You are asking how to not run out, not for a new diagnosis. Some offices can send a short fill to a pharmacy that is actually open. I will not promise yours will. I don't know their rules.

Third, find any pharmacy that is actually open this weekend, even if it is farther. Chain stores sometimes have Sunday hours. Use the store's own locator, not a rumor. If you have no car, ask a human you trust for a ride or a delivery app that the pharmacy itself uses. I will not invent a price.

Do not start a leftover from an old bottle unless the prescriber or pharmacist tells you that specific leftover is appropriate. Old bottles lie.

How many full days do you have at your usual schedule, and is this a medicine you were told not to miss?

### User
I think I have enough through Monday morning if I don't miss today's dose. I was told not to stop suddenly. The mail-order chat bot keeps offering me "tips for tracking." The clinic line is a recording that says Monday. I feel stupid for not picking it up locally last month.

### Assistant
You are not stupid. Mail-order fails sometimes. Shame doesn't open a pharmacy.

If you have enough through Monday morning, the job is to protect those remaining doses and to get a human on Monday as early as they exist. Set an alarm for when the clinic opens. Call mail-order again anyway; bots are not the last layer. Ask for a pharmacist, not tips.

If a neighbor or friend can drive you to any open pharmacy today, you can ask that pharmacy whether they can fill an emergency supply if the prescriber authorizes it. The pharmacist may say no without a new script. That is an answer, not a personal rejection. You still ask. You still don't change your dose on a stranger's forum.

I will not name a medication class or withdrawal timeline. "Don't stop suddenly" is a reason to treat Monday as a deadline, not a reason to experiment this afternoon.

Keep the remaining pills in the bottle, not in a dish, so you don't lose count. Take today's dose at the usual time. Don't "save" it as a strategy. Saving is skipping, and you asked me not to tell you to skip. I'm not going to.

If symptoms change in a way that scares you, that is urgent-care or emergency territory in the real world, not a chat. I won't diagnose the difference from here. Use the emergency resources you would use for any sudden bad turn.

### User
What do I tell work if I'm a wreck Monday waiting on hold?

### Assistant
Tell a true small sentence: "I'm dealing with a delayed prescription; I may be on the phone with the clinic at open." You don't have to name the drug. You don't have to perform panic. Block the first hour if you can.

This weekend: no dose experiments, no forum protocols, remaining pills counted, mail-order and any open pharmacy asked, clinic at open Monday. You wanted a plan that doesn't make you a doctor. That's the plan. Humans who can fill bottles, not me, finish it.
