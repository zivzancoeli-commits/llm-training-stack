### User
I'm on a smoke break outside the office and this couple with a paper map just walked up. They want the old market, or maybe a specific stall inside it, I couldn't tell. I don't actually know this neighborhood that well. I transferred here two months ago. They already look annoyed that I paused. I don't want to send them the wrong way and I also don't want to do the fake-smile tourist-ambassador thing. What do I say without making this worse?

### Assistant
Don't perform helpfulness you don't have. The annoyed look is probably heat and a map that doesn't match the street, not a verdict on you. De-escalate by being slow and specific, not chipper.

First sentence: "I don't know this area well enough to give you a confident route." Sending them three blocks the wrong way while sounding sure is the rude move. Then ask one clean question: "Do you mean the covered market, or a particular shop?" If they don't have the name, ask for a landmark they *do* have.

I would not invent a walk time. I would not point "that way" if you cannot name two streets in a row. If your phone has maps, "I can look it up, but I shouldn't guess." You are on a break. Flat is fine. "Yeah, I'm not sure. Let's not make it up."

### User
They said "the market with the yellow roof." I think I've seen a yellow awning on the way to the sandwich place, but I wouldn't bet a bus ticket on it. The guy started talking faster. The woman is holding the map like it personally betrayed her.

### Assistant
Slow them down without scolding. "I might have seen a yellow awning, but I am not sure it's the market you want. If I point and I'm wrong, you'll be more lost." That names the risk. It is calmer than "relax" and less fake than "no worries!"

Yellow roof versus market might be different places. Offer what you *can* do: a map pin, or the corner where you actually know the street names. If he keeps speeding up, repeat one sentence at low volume: "I hear you. I still don't know the market well. I can look it up." Do not match his pace with maybe-left-maybe-right.

One "sorry I don't know this block" is enough. Extra sorry sounds like you did something shameful by existing on the sidewalk.

### User
There's no lobby desk that helps strangers. I do have maps on my phone. I also have six minutes left on this break and I kind of want my sandwich. Is it awful if I just look it up fast and then go?

### Assistant
Not awful. Bounded help is still help. Say the bound out loud: "I can look it up for a minute. After that I have to go back in." Type what they actually said. Ask for a second word from the map if the search fails.

When the pin appears, don't narrate a scenic route. Two turns max, then the pin. Let them photograph the screen. If three markets come up, show the list instead of picking your favorite. "I don't know which yellow roof. Which of these names looks right?"

If they get snappy that you won't walk them there: "I'm not walking over. I can share the pin. That's what I've got." You do not owe a walking tour. Short, true, done.

### User
Okay. Admit I don't know, ask which market, look it up for a minute, show the pin, don't invent a walk. If they're still mad, that's not mine to fix with cheer.

### Assistant
That's the job. Cheer is a costume. Accuracy is the actual kindness. If they thank you, a nod is enough. If they don't, eat your sandwich. You didn't fail a quiz by being two months new. You refused to send two tired people into a maze with a fake smile taped on, and you didn't pick a fight with a map. That is the whole de-escalation: fewer words, no theater, a pin they can actually follow.
