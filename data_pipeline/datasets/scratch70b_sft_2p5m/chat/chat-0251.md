### User
I'm in a rec center lobby with a gift bag. I thought I was staying. The invite said 2–4, pizza, gym. Other parents handed kids to a teenager with a clipboard and left. My kid is 7. They are looking at me like I might vanish. Nobody emailed "drop-off." I have a work call at 2:30 I was going to take in the car, which now feels like a different universe. The clipboard teen asked if I was staying "as a helper." I said I didn't know.

### Assistant
You can stay. Drop-off that wasn't named is not a trap you have to walk into. A 7-year-old who thinks you might vanish is data. The clipboard is a staffing system, not a court.

I'm not going to invent what this rec center requires for adults in the gym. Some places want a helper ratio. Some want parents gone. Ask the teen, or whoever is wearing a staff shirt: "I didn't see drop-off on the invite. Can I stay in the gym or the lobby?" Get a yes or a no. Don't accept a shrug as policy.

The 2:30 call is a separate problem. If you stay, the call waits or you step into a hallway for ten minutes if staff says that's allowed.

Is your kid the kind who warms up in five minutes, or do they still check the door at other parties?

### User
Door-checker. Last bounce house they cried when I went to the bathroom. The birthday kid is in their class. I don't want to be the only parent on the bleachers looking like a helicopter. The gift bag has a roll of stickers I said I'd help pass out, which is why I thought I was staying.

### Assistant
Stickers are a perfect cover if you need one. "I'm here to help with stickers" is a job. Bleachers plus a job looks less like a helicopter and more like an extra pair of hands. If staff wants parents out, the stickers go to the clipboard and you negotiate the door-check.

For the next ten minutes: find the person in charge, not only the teen. State that the invite didn't say drop-off, your kid is 7, you'll stay in the lobby if the gym is a problem. If they want you in the gym as helper, say yes. Put the gift on the table. Sit where your kid can see you without you being in the game.

Move the 2:30 call. Text: "Need to push 20 minutes, at a kid party that was drop-off by surprise." If you cannot push, step out for the call only after your kid is in a structured activity — pizza still in boxes doesn't count — and tell them you'll be in the hall and back. Door-checkers need a location, not "I'm around."

Don't apologize to other parents for staying. They're gone. Their kids may be older or fine. Yours is looking at you. That's the assignment.

### User
If I stay the whole two hours I'll miss the call and look like I don't trust the rec center. If I leave and they melt down I will hear about it in the car until dinner.

### Assistant
Trust isn't the only variable. Age 7 plus a door-check history plus an unlabeled drop-off is a reasonable stay. Missing one work call is a trade. I don't know your job. If the call is truly immovable, you stay for the first stretch, take the call, come back. Two hours does not have to be a moral identity. It can be 40 minutes in sight, 15 in the hall, 40 in sight.

If staff says parents cannot stay, you tell your kid the truth: you'll be in the lobby the whole time, not the parking lot. Lobby is not vanish. Sit where they can see you through the gym door if there's a window. If there's no window, agree on a check-in at 3:00. A time on a clock is better than "I'll be close."

You don't have to perform helicopter or cool parent. You have a gift bag, stickers, a clipboard teen, and a child who uses their eyes as a tether. Stay until the tether loosens. Then take the call if you must. The car lecture until dinner is what you get if you copy the other parents against the kid you actually brought. Bring the kid you brought. The invite was incomplete. You can complete it by remaining a person in the building.
