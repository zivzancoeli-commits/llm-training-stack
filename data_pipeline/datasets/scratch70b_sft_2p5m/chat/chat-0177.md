### User
There's a person on my floor who heats fish in the shared microwave. The whole bay smells like a harbor for an hour. I've almost said something twice and then I rehearse it into a TED talk about "community norms." I don't want to be the smell police. I also don't want to eat lunch at my desk with a sweater over my nose. I think the move is a kind, specific request, not a poster. I'm just not sure what's actually allowed, or if I'm imagining that offices have rules about this.

### Assistant
You're not imagining that fish in a closed kitchen is a lot. You're also right that you don't know the rule. I don't either. I won't invent a policy, a fine, or a "standard corporate guideline." Bluffing "you're not allowed" is how this turns into a fight about authority you don't have.

The missing piece is probably not a better speech. It's who you say it to.

Kind and specific, without an audience: "Hey, when fish hits the microwave the smell sits in our bay for a long time. Could you do that at a different time, or use the other kitchen, or bring something else on the days you're here? I know it's a pain. The smell is a pain too." Then stop. No ocean joke. No poll.

If you don't know their name, that's the first tiny job. "The fish person" is how you stay mad. I don't know if your building has a second kitchen. Assume competence, not villainy, until they sneer.

Have you confirmed it's one person and one microwave, or is it a rotating fish?

### User
It's one person. Same blue container. They do it around 12:10, which is when I heat leftover rice. There's another microwave two floors down I think, but I've never used it. I don't want to tell them to walk two floors if that's a made-up solution. Also I don't know if I'm allowed to ask them to change food.

### Assistant
You're allowed to ask. Asking is not a law. They can say no. I will not pretend I know whether your handbook mentions salmon. If you want the official version, you look at whatever kitchen email or wiki you already have, or you ask facilities one sentence: "Is there a shared-kitchen smell guideline?" If they say they don't have one, that's the truth. Then you're in neighbor territory, which is where you already were.

Don't send them two floors unless you've seen the other microwave. "I think" is how you sound sure and then they're in a locked break room. You can say, "I'm going to check if there's another microwave," and then check. If it exists and it's open, you can offer it as an option, not an order. If it doesn't, you don't mention it.

You almost had the request. Cut "community norms." Cut the poster. Blue container, 12:10, your bay, the lingering smell, one ask. If they get defensive, you don't escalate into a science lecture about oils. You can say, "I'm not trying to ban your lunch. I'm asking about the microwave in this kitchen." That's uncertainty-honest: you don't know their constraints, they don't have to know yours beyond the smell.

If they say they have to use this one, you have choices you also shouldn't bluff about: eat earlier, eat later, eat farther away, or — if it stays daily and they blow you off — then you involve a manager with the same facts, not a morality play. I don't know how your manager handles lunch. Some will care. Some will tell you to buy a candle.

### User
What if I freeze and they say "it's just food."

### Assistant
Then you agree it's food, and you repeat the part that isn't a debate: "Yeah. The leftover smell in our bay is the issue. Can we work around the microwave at 12:10?" If your brain empties, that's the line. You don't need a ruling from HR in your pocket.

If you freeze too hard to talk, a short chat message with the same sentences is still a request, not a dossier. Don't CC the floor.

You're close. Don't invent a ban. Don't pretend you know the policy. Ask about the 12:10 fish in this microwave, offer a real other kitchen only after you've walked there, and let them be a person with a blue container. That's the whole conversation. The sweater can come off your nose after you've tried the human version first.
