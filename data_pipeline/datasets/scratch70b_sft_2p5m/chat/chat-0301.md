### User
The school sent home a catalog and a pep talk. My kid is supposed to sell 40 rolls of wrapping paper to "win a pizza party for the class." We do not have 40 neighbors. I work from home. I am not putting a pitch in the work Slack. My kid already thinks we are failing the class. The form is due Friday. I feel like a bad parent and also like this is a part-time job I did not take.

### Assistant
You are not a bad parent because a catalog assumed a sales force. Forty rolls is a fundraising design, not a measure of your kid's worth or your street.

You can opt out. You can sell a number that is real. You can donate a small amount if that is how this school actually lets people skip the catalog — I do not know if they do, and I will not invent a buyout. Read the packet for a "family donation" line. If it exists, that is a door. If it does not, you still do not have to run a store.

Do not dump this on your kid as "we don't care about pizza." They hear social exile. Do tell them the true size: "We're not selling 40. We'll do what fits our people, and pizza is not a test."

Does the packet say the pizza is all-or-nothing for the class, or is it a personal prize chart? And is there a rule about selling at your job?

### User
It's a class pizza if they hit a group goal, plus individual prizes. Work is silent about this kind of thing, which I take as a no. My parents live far. I have maybe six people I could text without hating myself.

### Assistant
Six people is a list, not a failure. Text them a plain ask, not a guilt story: "School fundraiser, wrapping paper, no pressure, here's the link, ignore if not useful." Then stop. One follow-up at most. Do not send it to work.

If the class goal is a group number, your six orders will not be the thing that "saves" or "dooms" pizza. Those goals are built on a fantasy of everybody selling 40. If pizza fails, that is a school choice about incentives, not your child's betrayal. You can say that out loud at dinner.

If you have money and no appetite for sales, ask the teacher or the parent group, in writing, whether a direct donation counts. If they say no, believe them and do not hide cash in a catalog order as theater unless you actually want paper.

Your kid may still feel the classroom chatter. Prepare one sentence for them: "Some families sell a lot, we're selling a little, you're still in the class." If another adult uses your kid as a messenger — "tell your parents to try harder" — you email that adult, not a note in a backpack for a child to manage.

I am not sure how intense this school is. Some are gentle. Some track kids on a wall. If there is a public tracker, that is a separate conversation with the teacher about not shaming kids who do not sell. You can ask for that without volunteering as catalog captain.

### User
My kid asked if we can just buy 40 ourselves so they don't have to talk. That is a lot of paper I do not need.

### Assistant
Buying 40 to avoid talking is how you get a closet of paper and a lesson that money erases social discomfort. If you want to buy a few because you actually wrap gifts, fine. Forty is a gag gift to yourself.

You can buy zero and still love them. You can buy two. You can let them practice asking one grandparent on speakerphone if that is a skill you want, and stop there.

For the next ten minutes: read the packet for a donation option, write the six names, send one group text or six singles with the link, tell your kid the pizza line tonight, and put the catalog on top of the fridge so it is not a homework stack on the table. Friday you turn in what you have.

The school assumed a sales team. You have a household. Those are different sizes. Fill the household size and let the wrapping paper company live with the rest.
