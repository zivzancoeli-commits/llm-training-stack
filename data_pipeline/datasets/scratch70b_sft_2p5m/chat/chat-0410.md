### User
There's a drone outside my third-floor balcony. It showed up at dusk, hung there, then left. Tonight it is back. I was in a t-shirt. I don't know if it has a camera. I assume it does. I don't know which unit it belongs to. I don't want to stand on the balcony and yell at the sky. I also don't want to be filmed in my house. I need a first night plan that isn't me pretending this is cute.

### Assistant
A hover at balcony height is not cute. You can treat it as a privacy problem without becoming a detective in the dark.

First night: go inside, close the door, drop the blinds or a sheet if you have them. Do not perform for the lens. Do not throw objects. A falling thing from a third floor is a worse night.

If you can see a person on a nearby balcony or in the lot with a controller, that is your talk, in daylight tomorrow, not a shout tonight. If you cannot see a person, do not roam the building with a flashlight accusing doors.

I will not invent drone statutes or whether a camera is legal there. Those rules exist in some places and not others. Your first tools are curtains, a note to the building, and a calm ask when you identify a person.

Did it face into the room, or was it more in the airshaft / over the parking lot?

### User
It faced the sliding door. I have sheers, not blackout. I felt frozen, then angry. There's a teenager two floors down who got a drone for a birthday. I don't know if it's them. If I guess wrong I look paranoid.

### Assistant
You do not have to knock on a teenager tonight with a theory. You can ask the building or the parent in daylight: "Has anyone been flying a drone near the upper balconies at dusk? It hovered at my door." That invites a yes without a prosecution.

Hang a thicker layer tonight: blanket over the rod, tape if you must. Ugly is fine. You want a visual block.

If it comes back, you can record the time and a description from inside: color, how long, which direction it left. Notes help if you talk to management. I will not tell you to film the operator in the lot. That can escalate. Your interior is the priority.

If you see the teen flying it in the lot in daytime, you can walk over and say, "Please don't hover at third-floor doors. It comes into our rooms." Parents should hear it too if they are there. If they laugh, you still said it, and then you take it to the office.

Paranoid is a word people use about privacy. Hovering at a slider is a reason.

### User
What if management says "kids will be kids" and it happens again tomorrow while I'm changing? I don't want the police as my personality, but I also don't want to undress in a fishbowl.

### Assistant
Change away from the glass. That is not you accepting the drone. That is you not giving it a show while the process is slow. Keep using the blanket.

If management shrugs, you send a dated email anyway: times, balcony, request that drones not be flown at windows. A shrug in the hallway is weaker than a written trail. If it continues, you can ask what their next step is. I will not promise a ban. I will not tell you to call a police line; that is your judgment if you feel threatened. Feeling watched is already a reason to block the glass and talk to adults.

Tonight: cover the door, stay inside, write the times. Tomorrow: office, and a daylight ask if you see a controller. You are not a fishbowl. Make the glass boring. Then make the complaint boring too. Boring is how this gets treated as a building issue instead of a vibe.
