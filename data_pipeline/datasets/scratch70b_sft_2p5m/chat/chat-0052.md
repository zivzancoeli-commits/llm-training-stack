### User
My friend Marcus has started showing up at my apartment without texting. Three times in two weeks. Last time I was in sweatpants eating leftovers and he walked in because I had told him the door code in January when I had a package emergency. He acts like it is charming. It is not charming. I do not want to invent a fake roommate or a fake night class. I want to say no. Talk to me like you do not buy my polite stories. I already know I sound like I am making this a bigger deal than a drop-in.

### Assistant
It is not bigger than a drop-in. It is a door code and an unlocked habit. Those are different. You do not need a night class. You need a closed door and a sentence he cannot hear as maybe later.

Say it on a call or in person, not in a cute text he can laugh-react: Marcus, I do not want unannounced visits. Text me first. If I do not answer, do not come over. I am also changing the door code. That is not because I am angry. The code was for packages, not for you walking in.

Do not tack on I love hanging out unless you actually want to schedule something. Extra warmth after a no is how Marcus hears a puzzle. If you want to hang out, offer one time: Saturday at two, or not this week. Not whenever.

Charm is his story. Yours is you were eating in your kitchen and someone used a leftover code. You are not dramatic. You are late.

Change the code today, before the talk if you can. Management can have a code. Marcus gets a knock and a wait.

If he says I thought we were close enough: we can be close and you still text. If he jokes, do not joke back. Repeat once. End it. You are not on trial. Stop shopping for a costume for the no.

### User
He is going to say but I was in the neighborhood. That is his whole personality. Also I feel mean changing the code like he stole something. He did not steal. He assumed.

### Assistant
In the neighborhood is a weather report, not a reason. Say: I believe you were nearby. Nearby is not an invitation. If I say not now, you keep going. He can feel insulted. Insulted is not you being wrong.

Changing the code is not accusing him of theft. Say that out loud so you stop accusing yourself. The code was a temporary tool. Tools expire. You can own the delay: I should have changed it after the package week. That is on me. I am changing it now.

Mean would be humiliating him in a group chat, or letting him walk in and then icing him. A rule plus a new code is boring. Boring lasts. Dramatic becomes his story about how sensitive you got.

Plain version: I like you. I do not like surprise company. Do not explain the sweatpants. That invites I do not care how you look, which misses the point. The point is access.

Short version until it sounds like you: I do not want drop-ins. Text first. I changed the code. If you add unless it is an emergency, define it. Emergency is blood or a lockout, not I had a feeling. Three times in two weeks is a pattern. Patterns need a stop, not an excuse.

### User
What if he shows up anyway after I say this. I am not calling the cops on a friend. I also do not want to open the door to be polite.

### Assistant
Do not open the door. If he knocks after you said text first, text from inside: I am not answering the door today. Go home. I will talk later if you text. Do not narrate through the wood. Opening to be polite teaches him the rule was theater.

You are right not to jump to police for a friend who is annoying, not threatening. If he is only knocking, wait him out. If he yells in the hall or works the handle hard, use building security or neighbors, not a debate. I will not pretend I know your building.

After he leaves, no long processing call the same night. Short message: when you came by after I said no, that made this worse. Text only from now on. If he cannot do that, the friendship shrinks to places that are not your home. Home is not a public park.

No fake class. No fake roommate. Code changed. Sentence said. Door stays shut if he tests it. You already knew that.
