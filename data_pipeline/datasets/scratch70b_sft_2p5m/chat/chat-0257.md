### User
I'm at the desk. Checkout was supposed to be a tap and a receipt. There's a $47 "destination amenity" line I didn't see when I booked. The agent says it's mandatory and "on the website." I booked on my phone at 11 p.m. three weeks ago. I have the confirmation in email. It lists room and tax. No $47. There's a person behind me with a suitcase hitting my heel. I need a shuttle at 10:15. It's 10:02.

### Assistant
Don't turn this into a lobby play. Step slightly aside so the suitcase can stop using you as furniture, and keep the conversation to the screen.

$47 you didn't agree to is worth a question. "Mandatory" is their word. Your confirmation is a document. Ask them to show where it appeared in your booking path. Stay calm. I will not invent hotel law or what a "destination amenity" must include. I don't know this brand's fine print. I can tell you that a line you didn't see is a reason to ask, not a reason to explode at 10:02.

If they won't remove it, you can pay under protest and dispute later, or you can refuse and miss a shuttle. That's the ugly fork. Shuttle at 10:15 is a clock. The fee is money. Rank them.

Does the email PDF show a total that matches what you were charged except this $47, or is the whole bill a surprise?

### User
Room and tax match. The $47 is extra today. They offered to print the "terms." The print is tiny. I don't have time to read a booklet. Shuttle is hotel-operated and they said it won't wait. My card is already on file from check-in.

### Assistant
If the card is on file they can post the $47 without your speech. That's why this feels like a mugging. You still ask, once: "Please remove the amenity fee. It wasn't on my confirmation." If they say no, say "I'm disputing this after I travel. Please note that on the folio." Then you go. Note on the folio is sometimes real, sometimes theater. I can't promise. A timestamped email to yourself with the confirmation attached is something you control: send it in the next ten minutes from the sidewalk if you have to.

For those ten minutes: don't read a tiny booklet at the cost of a 10:15 shuttle unless $47 is the hill you will die on today. If it is, say so and skip the shuttle on purpose. If it isn't, take the printout, take the shuttle, fight in writing from home.

Don't sign a paper that says you agree the fee was disclosed unless you agree. If they slide a slip, read the one sentence above the line. Signing in a hurry is how "on the website" becomes your signature.

The person behind you can check out. You don't need an audience.

### User
I'll be furious on the shuttle. Also I used the "gym" which was a treadmill in a closet. If I used it, did I agree?

### Assistant
Using a treadmill does not automatically mean you agreed to a $47 line you weren't shown. I still won't invent the contract. Some fees are junk. Some are disclosed in a click you missed at 11 p.m. Your confirmation without the line is your strongest artifact. Keep it.

Furious on the shuttle is allowed. Don't take it out on the driver. They're not the folio. Write the dispute email while you're mad and the details are sharp: booking number, confirmation total, $47 at checkout, request for removal. Send it to the hotel and the card if you go that route. I won't promise a win.

10:02. Ask once, get the folio noted, take the printout, walk to the shuttle. You can be a person who questions a fee without becoming the delay in the lobby. The closet gym can live in the email as a sentence if you want. It doesn't have to be a speech at the desk. Go. The van does not care about amenities. Your card might, later, in writing. That's the correct venue. This desk at 10:02 is a speed bump. Treat it like one.
