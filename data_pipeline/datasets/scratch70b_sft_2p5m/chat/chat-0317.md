### User
There are five of us. Two people always "forget" cards at the end. I am tired of doing math on a phone while the waiter waits. We just ordered. Water is on the table. I want to ask to split it five ways now, or at least two cards. I have never done it this early. I don't want to look cheap in front of people I work with.

### Assistant
Asking before food is the kind version, for the waiter and for you. End-of-meal splits on a slammed Saturday are how errors happen. Cheap is a story. A five-way split is a method.

Flag the waiter on the next pass, not by snapping. "Could we split the check five ways from the start, or two and three if that's easier?" Let them tell you what the system can do. I will not pretend every POS is the same. Some can itemize, some can only even split, some hate it. If they say even split only, you can still take that and settle the fries later in a payment app, which is still better than one hero card.

Do not announce a speech about the two forgetful people. This is logistics, not a character study.

Are the two forgetters in earshot, and is this expensed or personal?

### User
Personal. They're in earshot. One of them already said "I got you last time" which is not true. The waiter is coming with bread. I feel my face doing a thing.

### Assistant
Let the face rest. When bread lands: "Quick thing — can we split five ways when you ring it?" If a forgetter jumps in with last time, you do not litigate history at the bread. "Tonight let's just split" is enough. If they insist on one card, you can say, "I'm not putting it on mine." That sentence is the whole boundary. You are not required to be the bank because you asked first.

If the waiter says they will split at the end, ask them to note it now. A note in the system is why you spoke early.

If someone orders a second bottle on the assumption that the table shares, you can still split even. Even is imperfect and faster. Itemized is nicer if the system allows. You do not need to police appetizers unless the gap is ugly. Ugly can be "I'll send you $12 in the app" after, not a fight about calamari.

Work people remember tone more than they remember who asked for split checks. Calm and early looks like competence. Late and sweaty looks like drama.

### User
What if they joke "wow okay, accountant." I will laugh and then I will pick up the bill anyway. That is my pattern.

### Assistant
If they joke, do not laugh yourself into the bill. A flat "yep" and a drink of water is enough. Accountant jokes are how tables keep a banker. You already named the pattern. The bread moment is where you break it.

For the next ten minutes: tell the waiter on this pass, refuse your card as the default, eat bread, and if a forgetter "lost" a card at dessert, they can walk to an ATM or send the app request before anyone stands up. Do not stay last as the closer.

You are not cheap. You are five adults who ordered food. Split it while the system is still friendly. Last time is a story. Tonight is five ways, asked once, before the entrees exist.

### User
The waiter said they can only do even split, not by item. One person is only eating a salad. I don't want to nickel them, and I also don't want the two forgetters hiding in the even math.

### Assistant
Even split is still better than your card. Tell the salad person quietly that you will send them a few dollars back in the app after, if you mean it. Do not open a table hearing about fairness. The forgetters do not get a discount because the POS is blunt. If anyone objects to even, they can pay the whole thing. You already declined to be the bank.

When the check comes, put your card in with the others or pay your fifth on the machine. Do not wait to "settle later" with the two who forget. Later is how last time was born. Ask, split, walk. The accountant joke can ride home without your card.
