### User
I'm at my friend's house. They made soup from a family recipe and they're watching my face. It is very salty. I already drank a lot of water. They asked "is it good?" and I said "mm." There is a whole bowl. I do not want to insult their grandmother and I also do not want to eat a salt block. We're not a couple. Just friends. I have to say something or keep swallowing.

### Assistant
"Mm" already told them you are managing your face. You can still be kind and specific. You do not have to mention the grandmother.

Skip "this is inedible." Skip fake raves. A usable middle: "The flavor is there, it's saltier than I can do a whole bowl. Do you mind if I add water or a potato if you have one, or could I have a smaller cup?" You are asking for a fix, not a verdict on their identity.

I will not tell you a chemistry guarantee. Dilution and starch are home tricks people use. They might work. They might make a sadder soup. The cook gets a vote on whether you alter it.

Are there other people at the table performing enjoyment, or is it just you two?

### User
Just us. They already said "I didn't even add much, it's the stock." There's bread. I ate bread. They look hopeful. I think they cooked this because I said I was tired.

### Assistant
They cooked because you were tired. That is care. Care can still miss the salt. You can thank the care out loud so the salt comment is not the only sentence: "This was really kind, I was wrecked, and I need to be honest that the salt is hitting me hard." Then the ask about water or a smaller portion.

If they get defensive about the stock, do not argue about milligrams. You are not a lab. "My mouth is sensitive tonight" is a true-enough shield if you need one. If you want to be more direct, "too salty for me" is allowed among friends.

Bread was a reasonable stall. Do not keep using bread as a plan; you will only be thirstier.

If they want to taste it again and then dump your bowl in the sink in a huff, let them have the huff without chasing. You can eat the bread, a leftover if there is one, or suggest eggs if the kitchen is still open. You are not required to finish a bowl as proof of friendship.

Do not later text the group chat about the soup. This is a two-person kitchen.

### User
What if they push "just a few more spoons." I am a completer. I will do it and feel awful.

### Assistant
Completers get to put the spoon down. "I'm going to stop so I don't feel wrecked" is a complete sentence. If they push, repeat once, then stand and offer to help with dishes. Motion breaks the completion spell.

For the next ten minutes: say the thank-you plus salt line, ask for dilution or a smaller cup, drink water, and stop at the point your mouth already told you. If they take it personally, stay on kindness plus limit. Do not add a joke about high blood pressure or doctors. You are not their clinician.

A proud cook can survive one honest bowl. A friend who swallows a salt block to protect a recipe will remember the stomach more than the pride. Speak once, eat what you can, and let the rest be soup.

### User
There's leftover rice on the stove. If I ask for that instead of the soup, is that ruder than watering it down in front of them?

### Assistant
Rice is not ruder. It is a plate that still uses their cooking. "Could I have rice and a little of the soup on the side?" keeps them in the meal without forcing a full bowl. If they want to water the pot, that is their call. You do not have to perform the science experiment if your mouth already voted.

Thank them again for feeding you on a tired night. Then wash a dish. Help is a better closer than another fake "mm." You can like the person, like the effort, and still not drink the stock. That is a normal friendship move, not a review of their grandmother.
