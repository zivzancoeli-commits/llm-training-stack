### User
Gabe wants me to be "the fun cop" in Slack. He means I should reply-guy the jokes that go too far and also post the Friday poll. He said I have "the right energy." I do not want to moderate a channel I do not own. I also do not want to be the office mom. How do I say no without becoming the unfun cop instead?

### Assistant
Fun cop is two jobs glued together: clown and hall monitor. Decline the glue. You can like jokes and still refuse a nickname that turns you into after-hours staff.

Say: "I'm not going to be the fun cop. I won't run the poll and I won't be the person who taps people for jokes." If you like the actual Friday poll, you can run a poll without the nickname. Nicknames are how unpaid roles stick. If you do not like the poll, decline both. GIF wars do not need a host. They need someone with too much time. That does not have to be you.

I will not invent your company's social-channel policy. If there is a real moderator, that is a named person or an admin, not a vibe Gabe assigned at 4 p.m. If jokes "go too far," the path is whoever already owns HR or the channel settings, not you as a personality.

Do not accept "just until we find someone." You become the someone.

Did he ask in public Slack, or in a DM?

### User
Public, in #random, with an @ me. Three people added cop emojis. I reacted with a smile because I panicked. The poll is a GIF war. I hate GIF wars.

### Assistant
Public needs a public no or the emojis will keep voting you in. Reply in the thread, short: "Appreciate it, but I'm not volunteering as fun cop. Someone else can run GIFs. I'll just be in the channel like a person." Then remove the smile if you still can. If you cannot, the sentence overwrites it.

Do not post a joke to prove you are still fun. Proving is how you get nominated again. You can be dry in #random. Dry is allowed.

If Gabe DMs "c'mon," repeat once: "Not doing that role." Then stop. If he wants a poll, he can post one. The buttons are free. Your energy is not a shared battery.

If the jokes that "go too far" are actually a problem, you can flag a specific message to the right person later, as yourself, not as a mascot. A mascot has to be on call. Yourself can pick a real incident. I will not tell you what counts as too far from here. I will tell you not to take a job titled cop in a GIF channel.

### User
There's a manager in the channel who hearted Gabe's message. I don't want it to look like I refused a duty.

### Assistant
A heart is not a work assignment. If the manager wanted a moderator, they could name it in a sentence with "please" and a job. They hearted a bit. You can still decline a bit.

If you want a belt-and-suspenders line, DM the manager: "I'm not taking a fun-cop role in #random. If you need an actual moderator, that's a different ask." Then you have said it once where it counts. I will not promise they care. I will promise you are not on duty because of an emoji.

If someone later pastes "fun cop?" on a joke that made you wince, you still do not have to put on the badge. You can ignore it, or you can talk to the person who actually has the admin gear. Reacting with a silent grimace in your chair is not a dereliction. It is you staying off a volunteer roster.

Stay out of the GIF war on Friday. Lurk. React to a photo of a dog if you want to look alive. Do not host. Hosting is the trap. If Gabe posts the poll anyway with your name in the question, reply once: "Not me. Unrelated." Then you are done.

Reply in the thread today. No cop, no poll, no proof-of-fun set. You can be a coworker in Slack without a badge. Gabe can find another energy. Yours stays yours.
