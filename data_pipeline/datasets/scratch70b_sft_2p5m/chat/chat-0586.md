### User
Aunt Dee wants me to call in sick Friday for Uncle's "surprise" 60th. Uncle already asked me what time the cake arrives. Dee knows he knows. She still wants the full theater, including me missing a shift I actually need. I don't want to lie to my supervisor. I also don't want to be the relative who "doesn't care about family." How do I skip the fake sick day without ruining the cake?

### Assistant
You can go to a party without faking a fever. Those are different favors. Dee wants bodies in chairs at 6. Your supervisor wants you at work. Uncle already punctured the surprise. The remaining event is cake, not espionage.

Say no to the sick day in a sentence: "I won't call in. I can come after my shift." If the party starts before you are off, "I'll be late" is a complete plan. Cake does not require you to open the door at minute one.

I will not invent your attendance policy or what "points" your job uses. I will not invent a wage. If you need the shift, you keep the shift. Family theater is not a doctor's note.

Do not offer to "see how you feel Thursday." That is a maybe, and Dee will hear yes. Do not ask me to write you a symptom list. I will not. There is no diagnosis because you are not sick.

Does the party start while you are still on the clock, or is it an evening thing you could make honestly?

### User
It starts at 5:30. I get off at 6, twenty minutes away. Dee said if I'm not there for the fake entrance at 5:30 the whole surprise dies. Uncle texted me "see you at the thing, I already know." Dee doesn't know he texted me.

### Assistant
Then the surprise is already dead in a group chat. You are being asked to miss work for a play whose audience already read the script. You can tell Dee a true smaller truth: "I get off at 6. I'll be there around 6:25. I'm not calling in. He'll still have a party." You do not have to forward Uncle's text. That is optional. If you think Dee will melt down at 5:30 without you in the entryway, you can add, "Don't hold the fake entrance for me." That frees her to start without your body as a prop.

If she says family first, you can say, "I am coming. I'm not lying to work." Showing up late with a present is family. A fake flu is a second job.

Travel: twenty minutes is twenty minutes. Do not speed to save a ritual. Text when you leave the parking lot. That is the only live update she needs.

If she tries to rebook you as the person who distracts Uncle at 5:15, decline that too. Distraction is another unpaid role. He already knows. Let him walk into his own cake.

### User
She's going to tell my mom I chose a shift over blood. I can already hear it. Also I don't know if I should tell Dee that he knows.

### Assistant
Mom can hear a true sentence from you first if you want to cut off the rumor: "I'm going after work. I'm not calling in sick. It's not a surprise anyway." Whether you mention Uncle's text is your call. Telling Dee he knows may save her a 5:30 performance. It may also create a new fight about who spoiled it. If you tell her, keep it flat: "He already knows. You can drop the entrance." If you do not tell her, you still arrive at 6:25 and refuse the sick day. Both are honest about your shift.

"Blood versus shift" is a slogan. You are not choosing against Uncle. You are choosing not to lie at work for a secret that leaked. Bring whatever you were already going to bring. Eat cake. Do not perform shock.

Friday: work the shift, drive normal, walk in late, hug people, skip the fake gasp. Dee can have her feelings in the kitchen. You can have a job on Monday. The party survives a 6:25 arrival. The surprise is already gone. You do not have to die with it.
