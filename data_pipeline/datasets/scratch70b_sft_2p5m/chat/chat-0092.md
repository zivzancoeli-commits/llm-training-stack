### User
I was at the market this morning. I had a bag of tomatoes and a bunch of greens in my hands. The vendor's card reader failed twice. They asked if I had cash. I didn't. They asked if I could Venmo. I don't use Venmo, and I don't want to download it in a parking lot because a stranger is holding my vegetables. They got tight in the face and said "so you don't want them?" I put the stuff back. I felt rude. I also felt like I was being pushed into an app I didn't ask for. I still need produce. There's another stand. I have a debit card that works other places. How do I handle this without a speech, and without inventing a fake reason?

### Assistant
You can say no without a novella.

The true sentence is: "I don't have cash, and I'm not going to pay by Venmo. I'll leave these." Then you put the food back. You do not owe a medical story, a broken-phone story, or "my bank is weird today." Those excuses are how you end up arguing about your life in front of a crate of tomatoes.

"I don't use Venmo" is a complete reason. "I don't want to" is also complete. You are not required to onboard a payment app for a Saturday stall. The vendor is not required to give you food for free. Both of those can be true at the same time. The transaction failed. You walk.

If you want one extra clause that stays factual: "If the reader works, I'll buy. If it doesn't, I'll go to another stall." That is a plan, not a personality.

Don't apologize four times. One "sorry for the hassle" is plenty if you mean it. Don't criticize their equipment in a crowd. Don't announce that cash-only is backward. You are tired, they are tired, the line is listening.

### User
They muttered something about "people like you wasting my time" while I was putting the tomatoes back. I almost turned around. I didn't. Now I'm on a bench being angry at a person who still has to stand there in the sun. Do I go back and say something, or do I just go buy carrots from the other guy?

### Assistant
Buy the carrots. Don't go back for a closing argument.

You already said no. Returning to correct their mutter turns a failed swipe into a scene. You will not get a clean apology in a market aisle, and you might get a louder version of the same line. Let them have the mutter. You don't have to swallow it as true. You also don't have to litigate it.

If you still feel the hook later, that is for a friend on a break, not for round two at the stall. The useful leftover, if you even want one, is a note to yourself: next week, ask "card working today?" before they pile food in your hands. That is not you accepting blame. That is you not holding two pounds of tomatoes during a tech failure.

If another vendor's reader fails, same script. No fake cash-in-the-car routine. No "let me just set up this app real quick" unless you actually want the app for you. I will not tell you Venmo is unsafe or safe. I don't know your setup. I know you already decided you don't want to install it in a parking lot. Keep that decision. You don't have to spice it.

### User
There's a part of me that wants to say I'm a regular and I always pay. That feels like an excuse even if it's true.

### Assistant
It's a resume. You don't need a resume to decline an app. Regulars still get to say no. First-timers still get to say no. If you say "I always pay" while putting food back, it can sound like you are asking them to trust you and hand you tomatoes on credit. Don't do that unless you two already have that relationship, and even then, I wouldn't invent it on a failed reader.

"No cash, no Venmo, I'll skip this stall" is the whole thing. Then go get the carrots. Eat something. You were trying to buy vegetables, not win a debate. If you want a sentence for the ride home, use: they asked for a payment method I don't use, I refused, I left. That is not rude. That is a completed no.
