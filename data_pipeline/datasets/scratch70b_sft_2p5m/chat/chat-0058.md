### User
We are going camping Saturday. Four adults, one tent pad left so we are sharing a site. Priya hates bugs. Not a cute dislike. She will spiral if a moth hits the lantern. Last trip someone told her to just enjoy nature and she went silent for an hour. I am the one who planned the site. I am not going to pep-talk her. I also cannot cancel because the reservation is paid and the other two will mutiny. Talk like you do not believe in inspirational wilderness posters.

### Assistant
Good. Do not tell her to enjoy nature. That sentence is how you lose an hour of silence. Bugs are not a mindset. Your job is logistics and a flat tone.

De-escalate: believe her, change what you can, do not smile like this is a sitcom. Say: bugs will be there. We can make the site less interesting to them. We cannot empty a forest. If that is a dealbreaker, say so now, not at 11 p.m. at the lantern.

What you can change: lantern, food, sleep setup. Put the lantern away from the tent door, not in the vestibule like a moth club. Pack food, wipe the table. No open sweet drinks at dusk. If she wants the car for twenty minutes while you cook, that is allowed. A car is a screen with a door, not a failure.

Sleep: farthest spot from the door. A head net is equipment, same as a rain fly, not a joke gift. I will not invent a drugstore price. A long-sleeve she already owns is enough. No gear montage.

Pregame the other two tonight: no just enjoy nature, no she will get used to it, no grasshopper bits. If teasing is their idea of bonding, they can bond with a tree. You planned the site, so you set that rule.

If she spirals, give a task with an end: help me hang the food bag, then you can sit in the car. Do not trap her with we already paid. Paid is a sunk cost. I do not know your park's cancellation rules. If she can get a ride, she can leave. A smaller trip is not a moral collapse.

I do not buy that everyone has to love camping to be a good friend. Some people hate bugs. That is a preference with teeth. Plan around teeth. Do not sand them down with a poster.

Are you sharing one tent or two. That changes whether moth-in-the-lantern becomes moth-in-the-bedroom.

### User
Two tents. She is with me. The other pair is the lantern people. They think I am overmanaging. Priya already bought citronella and I think citronella is mostly theater.

### Assistant
Citronella can be theater. I will not pretend I have a lab result in my pocket. Some people find the smell reassuring. Reassurance is part of the kit even when the chemistry is modest. Let her burn it if the site allows open flame in that form. If the site forbids it, you follow the posted rule, not the candle. I do not know your park's fire rules. Read the board at the entrance. Do not bluff.

Lantern people: they can have the lantern at their tent, not in the middle of the picnic table like a stadium. You are not overmanaging. You are separating light from sleep. Say it boring: lantern stays on your pad after dinner so we are not running a moth airport over our door. If they call you mom, you can say yep, and you still move the lantern. Mockery is not an argument.

In your tent: zipper discipline. Go in, brush off, zip. Do not hold the door open for a story. If a moth gets in, do not flap a towel at her head. Light off, door cracked, wait. Flat tone: there is a moth, light off, it will leave. Then stop narrating it.

If she goes silent, do not ask are you okay every four minutes. Sit. Offer water. Offer the car. No resilience speech. That is the poster again.

You planned the site. Planning includes the unromantic parts: light, food, zipper, an exit that is not humiliation. That is enough. Nature will still have bugs. You already knew that. You wanted someone who would not sell you a miracle.
