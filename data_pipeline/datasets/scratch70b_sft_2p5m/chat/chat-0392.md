### User
I live on the second floor. The neighbor across the hall, Len, knocks whenever he hears my keys. Not emergencies. Chat. Weather. His knee. Fifteen to forty minutes. I have started waiting in the stairwell until I think he's away from the peephole. That is a stupid way to enter my own apartment. I like him enough to say hi at the mailbox. I do not like him enough to be his evening show. How do I stop the knock without making the hall a cold war?

### Assistant
You can greet a person and still not be on-call for their boredom. Keys are not an invitation.

The next time it happens, keep the door mostly closed. Body in the gap. "I can't talk tonight. I'm just getting in." Then you close the door. You do not open the conversation topics he brings. Topics are how fifteen minutes happens.

A longer talk belongs to a time you choose. If you want to stay neighborly, offer a defined slot: "I'm not free when I walk in. If you want to catch up, Saturdays at the mailbox work." If you do not want a slot, skip the offer. "I need to go in" is complete.

I will not invent building harassment rules. If the knocks continue after a clear no, you can talk to the super about a pattern. First you give Len a chance to hear a boundary that is not a vibe.

Have you ever told him no, or have you only smiled and listened?

### User
I have said "I have to put groceries down" and then he waits in the hall until I come back to the door. Once I didn't come back and he knocked again ten minutes later. I felt watched. I don't think he's dangerous. I think he's lonely. I still want my entryway back.

### Assistant
Lonely is real. Your entryway is still yours. Waiting in the hall after "groceries" means he heard a delay, not a no. Switch to a no that does not include a sequel.

"I can't talk when I get home. Please don't knock unless it's an emergency." If he starts a story anyway, you repeat, "I have to go," and you shut the door. Repeat is not cruelty. Open-ended kindness is how he learned the schedule.

If he knocks ten minutes later, you can ignore it. You are allowed to be home and not available. If ignoring feels unsafe, you can speak through the door: "Not now." Then you stop answering.

Do not wait in the stairwell. That teaches you to sneak into your life. If you need a buffer, put headphones on before you leave the stairs and keep them on until the door is locked. Headphones are a prop. The sentence is the actual tool.

A note on his door is backup if talking fails: same words, apartment number, no essay about his knee.

### User
What if he tells other neighbors I'm rude, and the building is small? Also I feel guilty because his spouse died last year. I brought a lasagna. I didn't sign up for nightly visiting hours.

### Assistant
A lasagna is not a subscription. Grief can last. Your evenings can still belong to you. Other neighbors may talk. You cannot run a PR campaign in a four-unit hall. You can be consistently civil at the mailbox and consistently unavailable at the knock.

If someone repeats that you are rude, you do not need a defense speech. "I say hi. I don't do door chats after work." That is accurate.

If you want to offer something that is not nightly, a monthly check-in you initiate is cleaner than reacting to keys. You control the start. If even that is too much, you are allowed to only do hallway hellos.

Tonight, when the keys come out: door, gap, sentence, close. No groceries-as-excuse. No lasagna memory in the doorway. If he looks hurt, the hurt can exist. You still get to put your bag down in peace. That is the whole change. Practice the sentence on the stairs so it is ready when the knock hits.
