### User
I keep finding the empty cardboard tube. I replace it. I buy the packs. My roommate Jules uses the bathroom the same amount I do, as far as I can tell. They always have a story: they were going to, they thought there was a spare, they don't like my brand. Last night I sat down to nothing. I had to waddle to the closet. I'm done being funny about it. We don't have a chore chart. The lease is both our names.

### Assistant
Empty tube plus a waddle is not a branding debate. It is a last-square problem. Funny was the version of you trying not to be "that roommate." You can stop being funny without becoming a landlord.

A chore chart is optional. A rule about the last roll is not fancy: if you finish it, you put a new one on, and if you used the last spare, you add it to a list or you buy. You already buy. The missing piece is Jules doing the swap, not you stocking a warehouse.

Do not hide the paper as a lesson. Weaponizing toilet paper is how you get a worse story and a possible mess. Do not send a meme in the house chat as the first real conversation.

I will not guess what the lease says about "common supplies." Most leases do not referee toilet paper. This is a household norm.

Do you share a bathroom 100 percent, and have you ever said the direct sentence, or only jokes?

### User
One bathroom. I said "we're out again" twice in a light voice. They said "oh no" and then didn't buy. There are two packs under the sink that I bought on Sunday.

### Assistant
"Oh no" is a sound, not a plan. The packs under the sink mean this is not poverty of paper. This is who believes restocking is your job.

Talk in the kitchen, dressed, not through the bathroom door. "When the roll is done, I need you to put a new one on. If you take the last pack, you buy the next one. I'm not doing the empty-tube thing anymore." Then stop. If they go to brand, you can offer a compromise: they buy their preferred pack and it lives under the sink too. Two brands is cheaper than a war. If they refuse to touch your brand, they still hang a roll.

If they laugh, do not laugh with them. Repeat the last-roll rule. If they call you intense, you can say, "It's toilet paper. It's also weekly." Intensity is a word people use when they liked the old unpaid labor.

Put one pack in a less magical place if they "can't find" it — a visible basket. Not a lockbox.

If this is part of a larger they-do-not-see-mess pattern, you still start with this one behavior. Winning a full personality makeover tonight is not the assignment.

### User
I'm scared they'll start a scoreboard of things I forget. I leave dishes sometimes.

### Assistant
They may. If they do, you can agree that dishes are a separate, real item, and still not let dishes cancel toilet paper. Two things can both be true. Scoreboards are what you get when nobody wanted a five-minute talk months ago. You can accept a two-item list: rolls and dishes, with actual times, not a courtroom.

If they only scoreboard as a dodge, name that: "We can talk dishes tomorrow. Tonight I'm asking about the roll." Do not let the conversation become your entire character.

For the next ten minutes: move one pack to a visible spot, send or say the three-sentence rule, and do not buy a third brand as a peace offering. If they are home, say it. If not, a text is fine: same words. Then go do something else.

You both signed a lease. You did not sign up to be facilities. Hang the roll, split the packs, drop the jokes. The cardboard tube can go in the trash instead of on the holder as a little flag for you.
