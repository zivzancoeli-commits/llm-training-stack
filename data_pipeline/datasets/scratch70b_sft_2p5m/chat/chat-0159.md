### User
Friday is my friend's birthday dinner. Everyone picked a place with pizza and shared apps. I cannot do gluten. I am not trying to be the person who lectures the table. Money is tight this month so a second restaurant after, or a whole separate meal, is not happening. I almost have a script and then I freeze because I do not want to sound high-maintenance. Help me make a plan that is cheap and quiet.

### Assistant
You are not high-maintenance for needing food that will not wreck your night. You are also not required to give a TED talk at the table.

I will not invent menu prices or claim I know this kitchen's protocols. Restaurants vary. Some have a marked pizza. Some will tell you the fryer is shared. Some will shrug. Your job is to get a true answer early, not to perform politeness while you gamble.

Tight budget means one meal, not a backup restaurant. So the plan is: confirm a plate you can eat before you sit down, and bring a small, boring fallback that does not look like a picnic protest.

Have you already called the place, or have you only looked at the online menu?

### User
I scrolled the menu. It says "cauliflower crust +3" and a salad. I did not call. I was going to just order salad and hope. There will be eight people. I usually eat beforehand when I am nervous, but I cannot really afford two dinners.

### Assistant
Do not eat a full extra dinner. That is how a tight month becomes two receipts. Eat a cheap snack at home so you are not starving if the kitchen is slow, not a second meal. Toast is out if toast is the problem. Fruit, yogurt, leftovers you already have. I will not price a cauliflower crust. That "+3" is their number, not a promise it is safe or that you should pay it.

Call today. Off-peak. You are not making a scene on the phone. You are doing the quiet work so Friday is quiet.

Script, short: "Hi, I am coming Friday with a group. I cannot eat gluten. What can you actually make that does not touch regular dough? Is the salad dressing and toppings okay, and is there a pizza option that is separate?" Then stop. Let them talk. If they say the crust is prepared on the same peel, believe them. If they say they cannot, believe them. Do not argue them into a yes you will pay for with your evening.

If they have one solid plate — salad with protein, a marked item, a simple meat-and-veg they already cook — that is your order. You decide it on the phone, not in front of eight friends while the waiter is standing there. Friday you say, "I called ahead, I'll do the [thing]," and you pass the menu. That is the opposite of a scene.

If they cannot feed you safely, eat a cheap plate at home first and still go for the birthday. Some places hate outside food. I will not invent their policy. If cake is wheat, skip the slice without a speech. "I'm good" is a complete sentence.

### User
The group chat is already "we should get the big sampler." I have not said anything. I feel like if I speak up I ruin the vibe, and if I don't I sit there moving lettuce.

### Assistant
Sampler is the trap. Shared fried things are where gluten hides and where you either announce yourself or go hungry. You do not have to veto their sampler. You veto putting your fork in it.

One message, not a manifesto: "I need a gluten-free plate so I called the restaurant. You all do the sampler. I'll order my own thing so I'm not picking through it." That is a plan. It costs you one entree you were going to buy anyway, not a second restaurant.

If someone teases you, you do not joke-apologize for existing. You also do not lecture the table about cross-contact. Smile-flat: "Yeah, it's a food thing, I already handled it." Then you talk about the birthday person.

You almost had it: call, one plate, no double dinner, no sampler fork, no speech. Make the call before Friday so the only thing you do at the table is order what you already know they can make.
