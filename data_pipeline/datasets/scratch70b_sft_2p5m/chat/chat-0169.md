### User
My cousin is thirteen and used to ride when she was little. She wiped out two summers ago and now she says the bike "feels fake," like her feet don't belong on the pedals. Her mom asked me to help this weekend. We have almost no money for gear, lessons, or a new bike. I don't want to buy a bunch of junk she won't use. Also I don't want to talk to her like she's five. Walk me through a plan that actually fits a cheap weekend.

### Assistant
We're not buying a comeback. We're using what you already have.

If the bike rolls, brakes, and fits — she can stand over the top tube with a little space, and sit with feet on the ground when the saddle is low — that's the bike. I will not quote a shop price. If the brakes don't stop her, you don't teach on it. A helmet that sits level and straps under the chin is worth more than cute lights. Borrow one if you have to. Don't skip it to save five dollars.

Use a parking lot, not a street. Empty school lot, church lot, quiet path. Grass looks soft and then the tires bog. You stand where she can see you without hovering on the seat.

What does she actually have: a bike, a helmet that fits, and how many free hours this weekend?

### User
Helmet from when she was ten. It's tight. Bike is her brother's old one, a bit big. We have Sunday morning, like two hours before her mom needs the car. There's a school lot three blocks away. I have maybe the cost of a pizza if I skip lunch, that's my whole teaching budget.

### Assistant
Skip the pizza-budget shopping trip unless the helmet will not go on. Try it. If it sits on the back of her skull, ask her mom, a neighbor, or a rec center before you spend. I don't know what yours charges. Borrow first.

The brother's bike being big is the real problem, not her "feeling fake." Lower the saddle until both feet plant. If the post is slammed and she still tiptoes, the weekend is a no. Wait for a smaller bike. That's not wasting two hours on fear.

If she can plant her feet: walk the three blocks. Pump the tires with whatever is in the garage, or ask a gas station. I'm not going to invent whether that's free.

At the lot, take the pedals off if you have a wrench and she's still scared. She scoots like a balance bike. If the pedals stay on, feet on the ground, push, glide, feet down. No "hands off" show. No filming unless she asks.

Talk to her like a person who already knows falling sucks. "You're not learning from zero. We're proving the ground is still there." Don't narrate her courage. She's thirteen. She can smell a speech.

### User
What if she refuses to go at all. Her mom already told her I would "fix it." I didn't agree to be the fix.

### Assistant
Then you are not the fix. Text her, not the mom: "Sunday morning, school lot, two hours, we can stop whenever. If you don't want to, we don't." If she says no, you cancel. You don't drag a scared person to a lot because an adult promised a miracle.

If she says yes, your job is one hill-free loop: glide, one pedal stroke, stop with her feet, go home while it's still okay. Ending early is the plan.

Budget recap: no new bike, no paid lesson, helmet that fits even if borrowed, saddle low, empty lot, water from the tap. That's the spend.

Ask her one question when you get there: "Do you want me next to you or farther back?" Then do what she said. Sharp kid, cheap morning, no performance. If the bike is too big even slammed, tell her mom the truth and spend the pizza money on fries instead of pretending.
