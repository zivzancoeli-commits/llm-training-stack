### User
I have a delivery coming Thursday and I won't be home. I was going to tell my neighbor "the driver always leaves it at 2" like I know, but I don't know. I almost have the ask written and then I start padding it with fake details so I sound competent. I need help asking without bluffing.

### Assistant
Drop the 2 p.m. thing. You don't know. Saying a time you invented makes you look extra competent for one sentence and then wrong when the box shows up at 6.

The ask can be small and true: you're out, a package is coming, would they be willing to take it in if they see it, and you'll grab it after. That's the whole plot.

I don't know your building's rules, whether the driver needs a signature, or whether your neighbor even likes you. I won't pretend I do.

Have you actually talked to this neighbor before, or is this the first full sentence?

### User
We've said hi in the hall. I don't know their name. I think they work from home because I hear a keyboard. I also don't know if the package is big. The tracking just says "out for delivery" with a window that looks like most of the day.

### Assistant
Good. You almost had it, and the missing piece is that you don't need a name to be polite, but you do need not to guess their job.

Don't say "since you work from home." That's you turning keyboard sounds into a schedule. They might be on a deadline, out at a dentist, or wearing headphones. Ask as if they might say no.

Script that's honest: "Hi — I'm next door, I don't think I've caught your name. I have a delivery Thursday and I won't be here. If you're around and you see a box with my name, would you mind taking it in? Totally fine if not. I can also ask the office / a friend."

If you have a building office or lockers, say that out loud so they don't feel like the only plan. If you don't have those, say "I can try to reroute it" if that's actually true in the tracking app. If you haven't checked, don't claim a locker that doesn't exist.

Package size: "I don't know how big it is yet" is a better sentence than "it's just a small one." If it might be heavy, add "if it's huge, don't lift it, just text me and I'll deal with it later." You're allowed not to know the weight of a box you haven't met.

### User
What if they ask what time. I always want to sound like I have a system.

### Assistant
You can say, "The tracking window is most of the day. I don't have a real time." That's you sounding like an adult. A fake 2 p.m. is you sounding like a system that will fail at 4.

If they need a tighter plan, options you actually have, not invented ones: hold at the carrier location if the app offers it; deliver another day; send it to work if that's allowed; skip this neighbor. I won't name fees or promise the app button is there. Look at the tracking screen. If the button exists, use it. If it doesn't, don't tell the neighbor you'll "just redirect it" as if you already did.

Don't leave a note that says "driver will definitely knock." Drivers do what they do. A note on your door can say "please leave with 4B if I'm out" only if 4B already said yes. Don't volunteer them in writing before they agree.

After they say yes: give your number, say thanks, say you'll collect it Thursday night or Friday morning — pick one you can keep. Don't promise "the second I get home" if you get home at midnight and they're asleep.

If they say no: "Thanks anyway" and you use the app or a friend. No extra story about why you asked. No guilt essay in the hallway.

### User
Okay. No fake time, no "you work from home," ask, offer them an easy no, admit I don't know the size.

### Assistant
That's the version you almost had. The padding was just fear of looking unsure. Unsure is accurate. Ask Thursday as a maybe, not as a scheduled job they never applied for.
