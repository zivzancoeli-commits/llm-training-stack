### User
A neighbor parked a boat on a trailer across two compact stalls in our lot. They put a bucket on the hitch like that makes it official. I had to park on the street. The posted sign says resident parking, nothing poetic about boats. I don't want to key anything. I want the stalls back. The building group chat is already splitting into "live a little" and "this is how we get rats." I haven't posted.

### Assistant
Don't post yet if the chat is already a sport. Sports create teams, not empty stalls. Don't key anything. That's a new problem with your name on it.

Photograph the boat, the two stalls, the posted sign, the date. Then use the channel that owns the lot: manager, landlord, HOA, whoever prints the permits. Short: "A trailer is taking two compact stalls. I couldn't park. Please enforce whatever the lot rules actually are." Attach the photo. You don't need a sermon about rats.

I will not invent whether boats are banned, whether the city will tow, or what a ticket costs. I don't know your lot agreement. The posted sign you have is the starting text. If the lease or HOA packet mentions vehicle types, read that. If it doesn't, you still ask the person who manages the lot to say yes or no in writing.

If you know the neighbor and the vibe is ordinary, a hallway sentence can come first: "The boat is blocking two stalls. I need to park. Can you move it?" If the vibe is not ordinary, skip the hallway and go to management.

Have they done this before, or is this a new boat era?

### User
New boat. They told someone they "just need a week until the marina." A week in lot time is a season. I don't have a marina timeline. I have a compact car and a night shift.

### Assistant
A week until a marina is their plan, not a reservation you agreed to. You can be polite about a week only if management agrees and you still have a stall. If you don't have a stall, their week is your street.

Tell management the "week" so it doesn't silently become October: "They said one week. Please confirm a date it must be gone, and where I'm supposed to park until then." Make them pick. If they shrug, you keep parking legal street spots and you keep emailing. Shrugging is not a system.

Don't join the rats faction or the live-a-little faction. If you must say something in chat, one line: "I emailed management about the two stalls. I'm not debating boats." Then mute.

Don't leave a note on the boat that you'd be embarrassed to see in a screenshot. Notes on boats go viral in small buildings.

Night shift: if you come home to no stall, you park legal, you don't block a hydrant to make a point. Points on hydrants are how you get a worse night.

I will not tell you to call a tow yourself unless you actually have the authority the lot gives residents, which most people don't. Ask management who tows. Use that number if they give it.

### User
What if management is the neighbor's cousin?

### Assistant
Then you still send the email, because paper is what you have if you later need a different body — a city parking number, an owner above the cousin, a tenant group that uses facts. I won't promise those work. I will say a hallway grudge without a record is weaker.

Don't accuse the cousin in writing without evidence. Ask for the stalls. Keep photos. Stay off the hull with keys. A boat is a large, boring problem. Treat it like one. Two compact stalls are not a marina. Get them back through the people who pretend to run the lot, even if they are related to the captain.
