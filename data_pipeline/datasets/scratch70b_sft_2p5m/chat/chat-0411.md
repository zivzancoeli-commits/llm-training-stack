### User
All-hands is in forty minutes. A coworker just pinged: can I skip it and finish their deck because their laptop died. It is their presentation. I have a section that is already done. They want me to rebuild their half from a messy email thread. I am not their designer. I also don't want to look unhelpful. My manager notices who is in the all-hands. Help me say no without a manifesto about boundaries.

### Assistant
You can help in a smaller way and still go to the all-hands. Their dead laptop is their fire. Your calendar is yours.

Reply now: "I can't skip all-hands. I can send you my section file and a bullet list of what I know from the thread. You'll have to rebuild your half." Attach what you actually have. Do not open a forty-minute rescue.

If they push "it'll only take you," you can repeat: "I have to be in the meeting. Here's the file." Then you walk.

I will not invent whether all-hands is mandatory. If your manager tracks faces, that is a reason you already named. Use it without accusing them of surveillance: you are going.

Is their name on the agenda as the presenter, or are you listed too?

### User
They present. I am a helper on the project, not on the agenda. They said if I don't help, they'll tell our manager the deck slipped because I wouldn't jump in. That part made me mad.

### Assistant
That is a threat dressed as a scramble. You still go to the meeting.

Send the file and the bullets. CC nobody yet. If they actually tell the manager you refused to skip all-hands, you can say, "I sent them my section and went to the required meeting. I didn't rebuild their slides in forty minutes." That is a boring true sentence.

Do not write a manifesto in Slack. Do not joke-threaten back. If you have two minutes, add: "Next time, let's freeze a deck the day before." Process is for later. Now is no.

Put your laptop in bag-on-shoulder mode so you are physically leaving for the room or the link. Sitting at the desk invites one more ping.

### User
If I go and their slides are ugly, I will still feel it in the room. People know I work with them.

### Assistant
Ugly slides are their name on the agenda. You can sit in the all-hands as an audience member. You do not have to look stricken. If someone asks you later, "I sent my section; they owned the rest" is enough.

Feeling it is not the same as owing a skip. Go. Send the file in the next five minutes. Do not rebuild. If they need a designer, they needed one yesterday. Your job in forty minutes is a seat in the meeting. Take it.

### User
They pinged again: "so that's a yes?" All-hands is thirty-five minutes out. The messy thread is still a mess. I have not sent the file.

### Assistant
That is a no. Reply: "I can't skip all-hands. File attached. I won't rebuild your half." Attach the section you already finished and three bullets from the thread, even if the bullets are ugly. Ugly bullets in five minutes are more honest than a silent rebuild. Then close Slack on the way to the room. If they ping "please," you do not owe a fourth message. You already answered. Put the laptop in the bag. Walk. Their agenda slot is still theirs. Your face in the all-hands is still yours. Thirty-five minutes is enough to send the file, close the thread, and stand up. Do not open the deck editor. Do not let "so that's a yes" sit unanswered while you hover. Send the no. Walk to the all-hands with the section you actually own. Their laptop dying is not your missing meeting.
