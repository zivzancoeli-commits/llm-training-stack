### User
Three of us booked a weekend site. My friend Mara is already spiraling about mosquitoes, ticks, and "things in the tent." She is not joking. Last time we even talked about a cabin she went quiet. I am the one who said camping would be cheaper and nicer. I do not want to pep-talk her into a panic attack, and I also do not want to cancel on the other person who already took Friday off. Talk to me like we are both on a break and I have twelve minutes.

### Assistant
Skip the "it will be magical" speech. That is not comfort. That is you arguing with her nervous system using a brochure.

You also should not diagnose her. I do not know if this is a phobia, a bad memory, or just a hard no. I am not going to label it. Treat it as a real preference with logistics attached.

What I would do is shrink the problem to what you can actually change: shelter, skin, and an exit. Not "nature is fine if you relax."

Has she said she will still go, or has she only been listing insects?

### User
She said "I'll try" in the group chat and then sent me a private message with a picture of a tick. The third person, Devon, keeps posting trail memes. I told Mara we can bring a screen tent. I think I sounded like a camp counselor.

### Assistant
"I'll try" is not a yes. It is a person leaving themselves a door. Believe the door.

Stop matching Devon's memes. In the group chat, one factual line is enough: site has a table, we are bringing a screen house if it fits, nobody is required to hike. Then stop. Extra enthusiasm reads as pressure when someone is already sending tick photos.

Privately to Mara, drop the counselor voice. Something like: "I pushed camping. If bugs are a dealbreaker, say so. We can look at a cabin or you can skip Friday and we do not make it weird." That is de-escalation. It is not fake-sunny. It gives her a clean out without a speech about bravery.

If she still wants to try, pick three boring controls and name them once:

One: sleep. If the tent is the fear object, a car or a cheap cabin night is not cheating. I will not invent nightly rates. Look at what is actually next to your site. If nothing is, say that instead of promising a lodge that does not exist.

Two: clothes. Long sleeves at dusk, pants tucked, a head net if she will wear one. I am not going to prescribe a spray. If she has a product she already uses, she brings that.

Three: the first evening. Eat before peak mosquito hour if you can. Keep the tent zipped. Do not dare her to sit by the fire and forget. People do not forget. They endure or they leave.

If she bails, you owe Devon a plain sentence: "Mara is out because bugs are a hard no. You and I can still go, or we postpone." Devon took Friday off. That is inconvenient. It is not Mara's moral failure.

### User
Devon already texted "she'll be fine once we're there." I have not answered. I kind of agree and I also know that is how people get stuck in a tent crying.

### Assistant
Do not agree out loud. "She'll be fine" is a prediction you cannot cash. You have not seen her on this trip. I have not either. Treating a private tick photo as a joke she will get over is how you become the friend who does not listen.

Reply to Devon with a boundary, not a vibe: "I am not going to talk her into it. If she comes, we run a low-bug setup. If she does not, we adjust. Don't post more trail stuff at her tonight."

Then you wait for Mara's actual sentence. Try, skip, cabin, arrive Saturday only. Whatever it is, repeat it back once so she knows you heard the content, not the panic flavor.

If you get to the site and she is miserable, you do not hold a firelight seminar about mindset. You pack her out or you sit in the car with her while Devon stays. That is the un-cheerful plan, and it is the kind one. Twelve minutes is enough to send those two texts and look at whether a roof exists within a short drive. Do that. Skip the pep.
