### User
I'm the neighbor. I can see Maya from 2C standing at the corner stop. She's eight. No umbrella. No phone. The 7:50 bus is not here and it's 8:14 and it's pouring. Her mom already left for the early shift. Maya waved at me once and then looked at the ground. I don't want to be the stranger who "helps" and makes this worse. I also don't want her standing in a gutter. I have a porch, a car, and a brain that's inventing every headline. What do I actually do in the next few minutes?

### Assistant
You make her visible, dry, and connected to a known adult. You don't improvise a new custody plan.

Stay where she can see you and other people can see both of you. Porch is better than a back room. Call out from a normal distance: "Maya — bus late. You okay? Want to wait on the porch out of the rain?" If she doesn't want to move, bring the umbrella to the stop and stand there, not huddle in a way that hides her.

Don't drive her to school unless a parent or the school has asked you to. I don't know your district's rules and I will not invent them. Porch plus a phone call is the boring path.

You have a phone. She doesn't. Call the mom if you have the number. If you don't, knock 2C. If nobody answers, call the school: late bus, child at stop, rain, you're the neighbor on the porch. Ask what they want you to do.

Is she willing to come to the porch, and do you have the mom's number?

### User
She'll come to the porch. She said "Mom said wait for the bus." I have the mom's number from the building holiday list. I already texted. No reply. Maya's lips look purple. I have towels. I feel like if I call the school I'll sound like I kidnapped a kid by offering a porch.

### Assistant
Porch plus towels is not a kidnapping. It's weather. Wrap the towel. Don't make a production of her body. "Sit here, bus might still come, I'm calling grown-ups." Keep the door open or stay in view of the street so this looks like what it is.

Call the mom. Voice, not a second text. If it goes to voicemail, leave the facts: your name, unit, Maya is on your porch out of the rain, bus didn't come, call me. Then call the school. Same facts. Ask if the bus is running and whether they want Maya to wait, come in, or they will send someone. I won't invent the school's policy. That's why you ask.

If Maya says she isn't allowed to leave the stop, wait with her under the umbrella, still on the street, and keep calling. "Wait for the bus" was for a bus that exists.

Don't put her in your car "just to warm up" unless a parent or school told you to. I am not going to diagnose purple lips from here. Cold rain does that. If you're truly worried she's not okay — can't talk, or you're scared — you call emergency services and you stay with her in view.

### User
Mom just called back. She's twenty minutes away. She asked if I can keep Maya until she gets here and not put her on a random late bus if it shows. Maya wants to know if she's in trouble. The bus just appeared two blocks down. I can see the yellow.

### Assistant
Listen to the mom you actually have on the phone. She said keep Maya, skip the mystery bus. Tell Maya, out loud, so the instruction is shared: "Your mom said stay here. You're not in trouble. The rain isn't your fault." Then you stay on the porch. If the bus stops and the driver looks confused, you can say, "Parent asked her to wait, thanks," without a speech. You don't put Maya on because the vehicle finally exists. The parent already overrode that.

When mom arrives, hand off in the open: kid, towel, times. Don't editorialize. Don't accept a grand debt. "Glad she wasn't on the corner. I have to get to work." If she wants to pay you, I won't invent whether you should take cash. You can say no.

Maya isn't in trouble for waving. You aren't in trouble for a porch. Next minutes: stay, skip the bus, mom's car, then you go. That's the neighbor script, and it never needed a secret chauffeur.
