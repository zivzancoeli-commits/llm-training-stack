### User
I'm at a packed bar. I don't want alcohol. I want water. I have been at the rail twice with a bill visible. They served two people who arrived after me. I am not angry in a throwing way. I am thirsty and I feel invisible. I have never flagged a bartender with words. I usually wait. Waiting is failing. I need a sentence that is not a tantrum and not a whisper they can skip.

### Assistant
You are allowed to want water. Packed does not mean you are furniture.

The third time, get a little taller in your space, make eye contact, and when they are in your zone say, clearly: "Hey — could I get a water when you can? I've been up twice." The last clause is information, not a trial. Then you stop. If they nod, you wait for that round. If they look through you, you repeat once: "Water, please."

A bill on the bar is not as loud as a voice. Use the voice.

I will not invent whether water is free. If they name a cost, you can accept or leave. Do not argue a menu you cannot see.

Are you alone, and is there another bartender on the far side?

### User
I'm alone. Two bartenders. I picked this side because it looked slightly less deep. I don't drink. I feel like they think I'm not worth the tap. I can move. I don't want to lose this two-foot gap I already fought for.

### Assistant
You can keep the gap and still speak. If this side keeps skipping you, the gap is a trap. A worse spot that serves water is a better night.

Try this side with the sentence. If three more minutes pass with no water after eye contact, you move. Pride about a two-foot hole is how people stay thirsty.

If you do not drink, you still occupy a stool's worth of air. You do not have to order a decoy beer to be real. If they only respond to a paid drink, you can order a soda if you want a labeled item. That is optional. Start with water.

Do not start with "you ignored me." Start with the water. Add the twice only if they are in front of you. Accusations from mid-pack get lost. Requests get filled.

### User
What if they finally look at me and I freeze, or I sound sharp because I waited too long? I don't want to be the story at the well.

### Assistant
If you freeze, point at a glass and say "water." One word works. Sharp is likely in your head more than in the room. Bartenders hear worse.

If it comes out sharp, you can add "please" and a thanks when the glass lands. Repair is a second of tone, not a speech.

You are not a story for asking. You are a person at a rail. Say it on the next pass. If they skip you after that, move down two bodies or go to the other bartender. Drink the water. You did not come to win a waiting contest. You came to swallow something. Flag them. Then swallow.

### User
They're pouring a row of shots for a group that arrived after me. I have a two-foot gap. My throat is dry. Next pass is coming.

### Assistant
The next pass is the one. Eye contact, "Hey — water, please. I've been up twice." If the shots have their hands, you still exist at the rail. If they skip you after that sentence, you move. The gap is not a prize. A farther tap with a glass is a better night. Do not buy a decoy drink to become real unless you want that drink. You came for water. Ask on this pass. Then swallow. If freeze hits, point and say the one word. Packed bars still have taps. You are not furniture. Flag them. The group with shots already got served. Your turn can be a glass. Take it.
