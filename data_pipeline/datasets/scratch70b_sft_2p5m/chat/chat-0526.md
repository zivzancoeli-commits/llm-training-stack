### User
The waiter keeps bringing bread. I said "we're good" once in a small voice. Another basket arrived. I am full in a bread way and we still have mains coming. I don't want to be rude to someone whose job is to be generous. I also don't want a third basket. They're walking over. I need one sentence that stops the bread without a speech about fullness.

### Assistant
Look at them, not at the table shame: "No more bread, thanks. We're set." Then you can smile if you mean it. The sentence is a stop, not a review of their hospitality.

"We're good" is easy to hear as "we're good for now," which in restaurant English can mean "yes, in a minute." "No more" is a closed door. "We're set" tells them not to check again in five minutes.

You are not insulting the kitchen. Bread is a refill habit. Habits need a clear off switch.

If they already have the basket in hand, you can hold a palm up, polite, the same words. Don't take it "just so they don't have to walk back" unless you want it. Taking it is how third baskets happen.

I will not tell you bread is morally required or that you must finish it. You asked to stop the offer. Stop the offer.

Are you alone, or is someone at the table still hunting crusts?

### User
Two friends. One of them likes the bread and will say "maybe just a few" which is how we got basket two. I don't want to police their carbs. I don't want the basket in front of me either.

### Assistant
Then split the request. You can say, "I'm done with bread. If they want more, they can say so." Put it on your friends for one second. If the friend says a few, the waiter has a yes from someone. You still don't have to eat it. You can move the basket toward them and keep your plate for the main.

If you want the table to be done, you need a three-person yes. You can ask the friends, quietly, before the waiter arrives: "Do we actually want more?" If they say no, you use the table voice: "We're done with bread for the table." If they say yes, you use the personal voice: "None for me, thanks." Personal voice doesn't require a speech about fullness. It requires "none for me."

Don't glare at the friend who likes crusts. That's a different dinner. You came here to stop an automatic refill, not to run a diet committee.

If the waiter jokes "saving room," you don't have to joke back. "Yes — no more bread" is enough. Joking loops the offer.

### User
They're here with the basket. My friend's mouth is opening.

### Assistant
You first, one clause, then your friend can add. "No more for me." If the friend wants some, they can take the basket. If you want the table off, add "we're set on bread" only if you actually are. Don't drown your friend. Don't freeze.

If the basket lands anyway, you don't have to eat from it to be polite. Move it down the table. When mains arrive, you can say to the waiter, if they hover with more, the same two words again. Repeating is not rude. Refill systems are built to ignore soft nos. You already learned that with "we're good."

You asked for a stop. "No more" is the stop. Use it. Then look at the water like a person who has mains coming. Bread is not a personality test. It's a basket. Park it or pass it. The generous waiter can still be good at their job when you close the refill. Generosity is not a contract you have to keep swallowing. One clear sentence, palm if needed, then the rest of the meal. That's the whole thirty seconds you came here for.
