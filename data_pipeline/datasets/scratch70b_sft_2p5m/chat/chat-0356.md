### User
I'm in a park. A parent I don't know walked up with a phone already unlocked, two kids on a bench, and said "could you take a picture of us?" I usually say yes. Today I don't want to. I don't want their kids' faces on a camera in my hands, and I don't want to be the person who makes a nice afternoon weird. They're waiting. I haven't answered. What do I say that isn't a speech about the internet?

### Assistant
You can say no without a TED talk.

"I'm not comfortable taking photos. Sorry." Then you can offer a real alternative if you want: "There's a bench arm you can prop it on" or "I can watch the path while you use the timer." Watching the path is not holding their camera. Propping is not your fingerprint on their gallery.

You do not owe them your hands. You do not owe an essay about strangers and images of minors. "Not comfortable" is enough. If they look confused, you can repeat once. Repeating is allowed. Debating is not required.

If you freeze, you can also say "I have to go" and go. Leaving is a no. It is a slightly ruder no. It still works.

Are the kids close enough that saying no will feel like you rejected them personally, or is this really just the parent and a phone?

### User
It's the parent. The kids aren't paying attention. The parent laughed and said "I don't bite." The phone is still out. I already took a half step back.

### Assistant
Keep the half step. "I don't bite" is a pressure joke. You can not laugh. "Still going to skip the photo. Hope you get a good one with the timer." Then turn back to whatever you were doing — your bag, your own kid, the path.

If they push a third time, you do not need a new reason. "No thanks" is the whole reason. New reasons invite them to solve your reason. There is nothing to solve.

Do not take the phone "just once" to end the social pain. That is how you do the thing you already decided against. Awkward thirty seconds is cheaper than handling a stranger's children in a viewfinder you did not want.

You are not required to police their photography. You are required to not become the photographer. Those are different jobs.

If you feel you must be useful, point at the timer button if you know that phone. Then your hands stay off it. If you do not know the phone, do not grab it to teach. They can learn a timer. Adults have been learning timers.

### User
They walked off muttering "some people." I feel like I made the park colder. I also feel relieved. Which one do I trust?

### Assistant
Trust the relief. "Some people" is the sound of a request bouncing. It is not a verdict on your character. Parks contain people who take photos and people who do not. Both live here.

You did not make the park colder. You declined a task. The muttering is theirs to carry out of range.

If this happens again, you already have the line. Use it earlier so you do not half-step through a whole paragraph in your head. Early no, optional timer, walk away. You do not need to like being talked about by a stranger you will not see at dinner. You need your hands free and your afternoon. That was the point. Keep it.

### User
I keep replaying whether I should have taken the photo to be decent. Decent is eating at me.

### Assistant
Decent is not holding a stranger's children in a viewfinder you did not want. Decent is a clear no and a timer suggestion. You already offered the adult tool. They wanted your hands. Those are different.

Replay once, then stop. The park will have another parent tomorrow. You will say the line at the start. You will not owe a warmer park to a person who muttered "some people." Eat your own picnic. Let the muttering finish walking away. Relief was the accurate read. Trust it when the replay starts. That is how you keep the afternoon you already protected.
