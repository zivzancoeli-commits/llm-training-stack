### User
We just ate with five people. Two of us got the cheaper plates. One person ordered an extra appetizer, a cocktail, and dessert, and now they're saying "just split it five ways, it's easier." The total is on the table. I don't want to be the intern who makes it weird, but I also can't really eat the extra. How do I say something without sounding like I'm auditing them?

### Assistant
You don't need to audit anyone. You need a split that matches what people actually ordered, said once, kindly, with numbers instead of a speech about fairness.

I'm not going to invent the prices. Look at the paper. You know which items weren't shared. "Split five ways" is easier for the person who ordered more. Easier is not the same as even.

Before you talk, do one quiet count: your plate, tax share, tip share. If you paid in cash last time and they Venmo, don't mix those stories tonight. Tonight is this check.

A useful line is specific and short: "I want to pay for what I ordered plus my share of the shared stuff. Can we do it that way?" Then you name your number if you already have it. People argue less with a number than with a vibe.

Are you the one holding the card, or is someone else about to tap and make you settle up later?

### User
Someone else already put down a card. They're going to Venmo request us. The extra-order person is the one who offered to "just split it." Two of us looked at each other. Nobody spoke. I have like twelve dollars of food. They probably have closer to forty. I don't know the exact total.

### Assistant
Okay. The card is down, so the fight, if there is one, happens in the Venmo, not at the terminal. That's actually gentler. You can still keep it kind.

When the request hits, you don't have to match it. Reply in the group or in a side text to the payer, not as a call-out to the whole table: "Hey, I had the cheaper plate and none of the extra apps or drinks. Can you request me for my items plus tax and tip instead of an even fifth?" If you can see the itemized receipt, list them. "Salad, water, my share of the bread if we did bread." Specific is kinder than "that's not fair," which makes people defend their character.

If you don't have the receipt, ask for a photo of it. That's normal. It is not an accusation.

Don't estimate forty versus twelve as if those were facts I can bless. I don't know the menu. Speak in "my dishes" and "the extra that wasn't shared." Let the payer do the arithmetic. Your job is to refuse the even split without a lecture.

If the extra-order person repeats "it's easier," you can agree with the feeling and still decline the method: "Yeah, even split is simpler, I just can't do that this time. I'll take my own items." You are not calling them greedy. You are stating a constraint. Interns get to have constraints. People who eat out a lot forget that.

### User
What if they say I ruined the vibe, or the person with the card gets annoyed because now they have to math?

### Assistant
Then you stay kind and you stay specific. "Sorry for the extra math. I can screenshot what I ordered if that helps." Offer labor, not a personality defense. If they still want even fifths, you can pay a little more than your food to cover tax rounding and still not eat a cocktail you didn't drink. Rounding is goodwill. Subsidizing someone else's dessert as a policy is not.

If the vibe comment comes, don't debate vibe. "I had a good time. I'm splitting by order so I can keep saying yes to dinners." That sentence is the long game. Even splits quietly push the cheaper eaters out of the group.

I won't tell you a percentage tip. I don't know the city or the service. Follow what the table already agreed, applied to your portion.

Next time, catch it before the card: "Want to do items, or even split?" asked while menus are still out. Tonight you missed that window. That's okay. The window now is the Venmo. Answer with your list, your yes to tax and tip, and your no to the extra appetizer. One message. Then put the phone down. You don't need a closing argument. You need a request that matches your plate.
