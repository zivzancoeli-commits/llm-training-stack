### User
My aunt just called from the highway. She and my uncle are "forty minutes out" and they thought it would be fun to surprise me. My apartment is a wreck. I have a roommate who is on a work call until 5. I am a new-intern kind of panicked. I do not know if I am allowed to say no, and I do not know the family rules. Talk me through this without pretending you know my relatives.

### Assistant
I do not know your relatives. I will not invent a family constitution. Some aunts treat a surprise as love. Some treat a closed door as an insult. I cannot see which. Anyone who tells you "family always means yes" is bluffing with your afternoon.

You are allowed to say you cannot host. That is not a legal opinion. That is a person in a small apartment with a roommate on a call.

Forty minutes is not zero, but it is not a dinner party. Decide the next two hours, not the relationship.

Have they stayed overnight before, or is this a drop-in they think is a weekend?

### User
They live three hours away. They have a hotel miles from here, I think. Aunt said "we won't be any trouble" which is how trouble starts. Roommate does not know them. Kitchen is dirty. I have leftover rice and not much else. I keep wanting to say it's fine because that is the intern move.

### Assistant
"We won't be any trouble" is a hope, not a plan. Believe the hotel if they named one. If they did not name one, I would not assume they have a bed. Ask. "Are you staying at the hotel tonight or were you hoping to sleep here?" That is a fact question. Guessing they have a reservation is how you end up making a couch at midnight.

Intern-fine is how you get a silent roommate and a sink full of their coffee cups. You can be warm and still put up a rail.

Call her back now. Do not text a novel. Voice is faster when a car is moving.

Script you can actually say: "I'm glad you wanted to see me. Today is not a good drop-in. My roommate is on a work call until 5 and the place is a mess. If you want to meet for a short visit after 5, we can do coffee near you, or I can come to the lobby of your hotel. I cannot host this afternoon."

If you do not know whether they even have a hotel, say that: "I don't know your plan for tonight. I don't have a spare setup here." Honesty about the spare bed is not rudeness. Bluffing that you have space is how surprise visits become sleepovers.

I will not tell you they "have to" accept this. They might get chilly. If they are already close and refusing alternatives, pick the least bad: a short hallway visit with a warning to the roommate, or meet them outside. You do not owe a tour of the wreck.

### User
If I say after 5, uncle will joke that I am ashamed of the apartment. He always does that. I do not know if I should pre-explain the dishes.

### Assistant
I do not know uncle. I will not predict his joke or coach you on a personality I have not heard. If he usually jabs about mess, a pre-explanation of dishes is you handing him the punchline. Skip the inventory. "Apartment's a mess, we're meeting out" is enough. You do not narrate the rice.

Warn the roommate in one sentence on paper or a chat they can glance at: "Aunt and uncle might be in the building after 5. I am steering them to coffee. Sorry." Then you honor that. If you sneak them in during the call, you are not a good intern. You are a person who dumped family into someone else's job.

If they arrive anyway and knock, you can open, step into the hall, and repeat the plan. You do not have to let them past the threshold. I am not claiming a law about guests. I am saying another adult on the lease did not consent to an audience.

I do not know if they will take offense. I do not know if this is a test. What I know is you have forty minutes, a call until 5, and a kitchen that is not a hosting argument. Call, offer a time and a place that is not the wreck, ask where they are sleeping, and stop filling the silence with "it's fine." Fine is the bluff. The specific alternative is the kindness.
