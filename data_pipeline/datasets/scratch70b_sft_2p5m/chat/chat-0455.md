### User
I signed a lease on a tablet in the basement office three weeks ago. I asked for a PDF. The landlord said "I'll email it." Nothing. I asked again Friday. They said "you have the keys, that's the lease" and laughed. I don't have a copy. I don't remember the pet line or the notice days. I need to know if I can paint a wall. I don't want to threaten. I also don't want to be the tenant with no paper. What do I send that's boring and doesn't invent a law?

### Assistant
You ask for the document you signed. You don't accept a joke as a file.

Email, not only a text, so you have a thread: "I signed the lease on [date] in the office. Please send a complete copy (PDF is fine) by [a real weekday, a few days out]. I need it for my records, including the pet and notice sections. Keys aren't a copy." If you have the tablet screenshot, a confirmation text, a money order receipt, attach that. You're not building a case file for TV. You're showing you were there.

I will not tell you they "have to" by some statute I invent. Places differ. I won't quote a number of days. You can still ask, twice, in writing. If they keep laughing, the next boring step is whoever actually owns the building if that's a different person, or a tenants' hotline in your city that you look up yourself, or a legal clinic. I won't pretend I know which one applies. Don't paste a fake "according to law" sentence. That's how you sound like a template and they stop reading.

Don't paint until you know. That's the practical part. A wall is not an emergency. A missing lease is a paper emergency. Treat them separately.

Do you have any photo of the signature page, or truly nothing?

### User
I have a photo of the last page with my signature and theirs, blurry, no clauses. Rent was paid through the portal. They still joke. Super says "that's between you and him." I want to paint a dusty blue. I also want the PDF more.

### Assistant
Paint waits. PDF first.

Second email, same facts, plus: "Attached is a photo of the signature page. Please send the full agreement that goes with it." CC yourself. If there's a portal that might store documents, look once, calmly, before you assume they have a vault they're withholding. If the portal is empty, say that: "Nothing in the portal. I still need the full lease."

If they reply with "come to the office," go, take your phone, ask them to print or email while you stand there. Don't leave with another promise. If they won't print, you can take photos of each page if they put paper in your hands. That's not sneaky. That's a copy. If they refuse to let you see it at all, that's a different temperature, and you write down the date they refused. Still no fake statute in the email.

Super: you don't need them to take a side. You need the owner. "Thanks, I'll email him" is enough.

Dusty blue: "I'd like to paint X wall dusty blue. Please confirm in writing if that's allowed under the lease." If they say yes in email, that's something even without the PDF. If they say no, you don't paint. If they say "sure" verbally, get it in writing anyway. Verbal plus no lease is how walls become a deposit fight. I won't invent what they can deduct. You already know deposits get weird.

### User
I'll go to the office Saturday. If they still won't show pages, do I withhold rent? People on the internet said that.

### Assistant
Don't withhold rent because a comment section told you to. I won't bless that. It can go badly in ways I won't predict. Keep paying how you've been paying, keep asking for the copy, keep the portal receipts.

Saturday: office, full copy or photos, no laugh as the answer. If you get the PDF, you read the paint line before you buy tape. If you don't, you send the third email with the date of the visit and you look up a real local tenant resource, not a threat paragraph.

Next week: records, not a mural. You signed something. You're allowed to read it. That's the whole request. Get the pages. The dusty blue can wait until the paper exists. Keys were never the lease. You already knew. Make them send the file.
