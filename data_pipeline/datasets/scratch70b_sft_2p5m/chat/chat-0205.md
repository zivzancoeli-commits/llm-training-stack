### User
I have to call a dentist. Last visit was three years ago, maybe more. Mom said I am a "new patient" even though I went there in middle school, and the website is full of words I do not actually know. Prophylaxis. Recare. Bitewings. I am fourteen and I can handle a phone call if someone translates the junk first. I do not want to sound like I am diagnosing myself. Walk me through the call in plain speech.

### Assistant
Plain speech first. "New patient" here just means they do not have a current file on you. Three years is long enough that they treat you like a first visit even if you sat in that waiting room at eleven.

Do not use prophylaxis on the phone unless they say it. Say cleaning. Recare means a regular check, not an emergency. Bitewings are the small side X-rays with the cardboard tab you bite. You do not order those. Ask if they usually take pictures after a gap, and let them say yes or no.

You are booking, not diagnosing. Do not say I think I have a cavity. If something hurts, say "upper left, hurts when I drink cold," and stop. Pain is a location and a trigger, not a label.

Script: Hi, I need to schedule a visit. I have not been in about three years. I think that makes me a new patient on your side. I want a checkup and a cleaning if that is what you do after a gap. Then let them ask insurance or a guardian.

Who is paying, and is this the same office you used in middle school?

### User
Same office. Mom is still on the insurance through work. She said I should mention "eligibility" which I hate because it sounds like I am applying for a contest. Also I have one tooth in the back that twinges with ice water. I was going to hide that so they do not freak out.

### Assistant
Eligibility here is not a contest. It means: is this plan still allowed to pay here, this month. Skip the word. Say my mom's insurance is still through her job; can you check if you take it. If you do not have the card, do not guess the group number. Say I will have her text a photo, or I can call back with it.

Do not hide the twinge. Translate it without drama: I have one back tooth that hurts for a second with cold water. It is not constant. I am not asking you to name it on the phone.

If they say comprehensive exam, that is a long look plus notes. Perio screening is a gum check with a little ruler, not a verdict. Full series means more pictures than the small side ones. Ask, is that everything, or just the sides. You do not agree to extra treatment on the call. The call is a chair and a time.

Mom should be nearby. If they ask you to consent, that is usually a parent signature at your age. I am not going to invent your state's rule. Say I am fourteen; do you need a parent on the line.

### User
They are going to ask why I waited three years. Honest answer is we moved, then we forgot, then I got scared of the lecture. I do not want a speech about neglect. Also I do not know if cleanings cost extra if I am "new."

### Assistant
I will not invent a price. Ask: for a first visit after three years, what do you usually do, and can you tell my mom the estimate before we sit. Estimate means a guess in writing, not a promise. If they will not talk money with a fourteen-year-old, hand the phone to Mom.

On the three years: one sentence. We moved and it slipped. If they start a lecture, say I am here to book the visit. Booking is the fix.

If the first opening is months out, take it, then ask if they keep a cancellation list. That means leftover time when someone else bails.

Sticky note before you dial: name, age, last visit about three years, same office, mom's insurance, cold twinge on one back tooth, checkup and cleaning, parent on hand. Do not improvise extra symptoms.

If they pile on recare and bitewings, translate back: so that is a regular visit, cleaning, and the small side pictures. If they use a word you do not know, say I do not know that word; can you say it in regular words. Smart people ask. Call while Mom is in the room. Do not diagnose. Do not quote a fee from a forum. Book the chair.
