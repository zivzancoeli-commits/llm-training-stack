### User
My landlord texted that they'd "swing by for ten minutes" to look at the sink. That was forty minutes ago. They're still here, opening cabinets I didn't offer, asking about my job, commenting on the dishes. I don't want to be rude because they control the lease, and I also don't want this to become a hang. I'm standing in my own kitchen like a host. Help.

### Assistant
You don't have to host. They came about a sink. Ten minutes was their number, not a personality test.

Don't fake-cheer this. Don't offer coffee. Don't laugh at the dishes comment like it's bonding. A small, flat, true sentence works: "I need to get back to my afternoon. Are we done with the sink?" That is not a fight. That is a clock.

If the sink is actually unfinished, you can stay on the sink. "What do you still need to see?" Then you stand near the problem, not in a tour of the apartment. If they start a cabinet that isn't related, you can say, "Those aren't part of the leak." You don't have to smile when you say it.

I don't know your lease or what entry rules apply where you live, and I won't invent them. If they showed up with notice for a repair, that's one situation. If this is turning into an inspection they didn't name, that's another, and you can ask, "Is this still the repair visit?" Bring it back to the reason they're in the room.

Are they alone, and is the water actually off, or are they mostly talking?

### User
They're alone. Water is on. They already looked under the sink and said they'd send a plumber next week. Now it's stories. I said "okay, thanks" twice. They didn't move. I can feel myself getting the polite grin I used last year when I didn't know I could end things.

### Assistant
The plumber sentence was the ending. You already have the next step. Everything after that is extra.

Drop the grin if you can. You don't need a new personality. You need a body that walks toward the door. Start moving while you talk. People take movement more seriously than another "okay thanks." Pick up your keys or your bag even if you're not leaving the building. Open the path to the exit. Say, "I've got to jump on a call" only if that's true. If it isn't, don't invent a call. Last year's you would have invented a call. This year's you can say, "I need the apartment back now. Thanks for checking the sink. I'll watch for the plumber."

If they look offended, let the air be awkward for ten seconds. Awkward is cheaper than another half hour of cabinets. You are not responsible for entertaining the person who rents you the kitchen.

If they keep talking from the doorway, you can repeat once: "I need to go." Then you stop adding content. No extra jokes to soften it. Softening is how forty minutes happened.

If they mention other repairs they "might as well look at," you can schedule that: "Text me a time for that. Today's just the sink." Surprise extra inspections are how people feel hunted in their own place. You can decline the add-on without yelling.

### User
What if they take it as me being a difficult tenant and it comes back at renewal?

### Assistant
I can't promise how a landlord will behave at renewal. I won't pretend I know your market. What I can say is that a calm end to a visit is ordinary. Standing silently while they narrate your dishes is also a relationship, just a worse one. You already said thanks. You already agreed to the plumber. That's the cooperative part.

If you're worried, keep it documented the boring way: a text after they leave. "Thanks for looking at the sink today. I'll wait to hear about the plumber next week." Short, factual, no essay about the extra forty minutes unless you want that on paper. Some people prefer the paper. Some don't. Your call.

For the next two minutes, do the un-cheerful version: stop nodding, walk to the door, say you need the apartment back, thank them for the sink, wait. If they still don't move, you can pick up your phone and stand there. You don't have to narrate a fake meeting. You have to stop hosting. That's the whole de-escalation. Get your kitchen back.
