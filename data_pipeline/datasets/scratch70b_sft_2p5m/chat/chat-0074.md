### User
I hung a floating shelf in the hallway and it looks drunk. One side is clearly higher. I used a cheap bubble level, two anchors, and I was already tired when I drilled. I do not want to rip a giant hole in the drywall if I can avoid it. I have like a lunch break's worth of patience and a drill still on the floor. What do I actually do in the next ten minutes?

### Assistant
Alright. Next ten minutes, not a renovation montage.

Minute one: leave the shelf on the wall if it is still holding weight. Do not yank it. Put anything that was on it onto the floor. Stand at the end of the hallway and look. Press the cheap level on the top edge. If the bubble is off, you are not imagining it.

Minute two: figure out which side is high. Drop the high side; do not raise the low side, because raising means a new hole above the old one. If it is only off by a couple of millimeters, you might shim. If it is obviously crooked from across the room, you are redrilling one hole.

Minute three: look at how it mounts. A lot of cheap floating shelves are a hidden metal bar with a wood sleeve. Slide the sleeve off if it will come off without a fight. Two visible brackets are actually easier: two independent mistakes.

Minute four: painter's tape along the underside, mark the current screw holes, draw a new level line. Use a phone level on the tape if the cheap bubble is why you are here.

Minute five: abandon the high-side hole. Back that screw out. Spackle later. Loosen the remaining screw a quarter turn so the bar can rotate; do not remove it yet if the whole thing will fall.

Minute six and seven: rotate down to the new line. If it will not rotate enough, both screws come out and you mark two new holes. Drill on the corrected line. Fresh anchor if you are in drywall. Do not reuse a plastic anchor that already spun.

Minute eight: snug the screws. Do not crank like you are assembling a truck. Over-tightening in drywall cones out the hole and the shelf goes drunk again in a week.

Minute nine: shelf back on. Look down the hallway. If a wall is not flat, a thin cardboard shim behind the low bracket is not cheating.

Minute ten: one book, not the whole row. If it sags immediately, stop. Do not drill bigger holes in the same crumbly spot. I would not spackle on a lunch break. Geometry first.

If the shelf is already pulling away, that is holding, not level. Empty it. I am not going to guess the kit's weight rating. You are allowed to do one careful hole instead of four angry ones.

### User
The hidden bar will not slide off. It feels stuck. I have maybe six minutes left before I have to get back.

### Assistant
Leave it on. Work through the front. Loosen both visible set screws if it has those tiny ones on the underside — that is often what pinches the bar. If there are no set screws, do not pry with a screwdriver against the drywall; you will crater the paint and still have a stuck sleeve. Mark the high side on tape, drop that corner by backing its screw out and reseating lower, and live with the sleeve still on. If the high-side screw is the only one you have time to move, that is still a win. Six minutes is enough to stop the drunk look. It is not enough to invent a new mounting system. Put the drill away on time even if it is only mostly better. A slightly imperfect shelf you can walk past is better than a hole you make because the clock ran out. Fill the old hole tonight with spackle, not during lunch, and do not hang a plant on it until you have watched it hold a book.
