### User
I almost have this. There's a used bike on a neighborhood app, seller wants cash tonight in a parking lot. I have a tight budget — I told myself I wouldn't go over what I already set aside, and I still need a lock and lights if they aren't included. I keep rewriting the message. Help me make a plan that fits the money, not a dream bike.

### Assistant
You're close. The miss is usually letting the listing set the budget instead of the other way around.

Start from the pile you actually have, then subtract the stuff that makes a bike usable at night. I won't invent prices for locks, lights, or the bike. You already know your number. Split it on paper: bike, a lock you would actually use, lights if you'll ride after dusk, a helmet if you don't have one, and a little left so you're not stranded if a tube dies next week.

If lock plus lights eat most of the pile, the bike has to cost less, or you wait. That's the whole plan. A cheap bike with no lock is how the bike disappears on day two.

Don't do parking-lot cash tonight as the default. Public place, daylight if you can, someone who knows where you are. Don't flash the full cash. That's less sloppy, not a guarantee.

Message that fits a tight budget, not a love letter: "Hi — still interested if it's as described. I can do [your bike number], cash, in daylight at [busy place]. I need to sit on it, check brakes, gears, rust, and that the serial number looks intact. If you need more than [number], I'll pass. Do lights or a lock come with it?"

The last question matters. If they say no extras, your number for the bike has to drop, or you walk. Don't steal from the lock fund "just this once."

### User
They already said they can only meet after 8 in a lot behind a closed store. Bike looks fine in photos. I feel dumb walking away from a maybe-good deal.

### Assistant
Walking away is part of a budget, not a personality defect.

After 8 behind a closed store is a worse meeting, not a better discount. Ask to move it: "I can do the grocery lot on Main while it's open, before 7, or weekend daylight. Night behind the store won't work for me." If they refuse every public daylight option, you pass. A used bike is not scarce like a kidney. Another listing will show up. I don't know when. I know this one is allowed to not be yours.

If they accept daylight, inspect: tires, wheels, both brakes, gears, rust, seat and bars that don't twist. Serial number on the frame; if it's scratched out, I wouldn't pay. I'm not a mechanic. A maybe repair is a second bill your budget didn't plan.

Test ride in the lot. Pay after. Count cash. Handwritten bill of sale: date, amount, color, serial, their name, "sold as-is."

If they pressure "other buyer in ten minutes," that's hurry. "Then sell it to them. If it's still there tomorrow in daylight, message me."

You're almost done when you can say the walk-away line without rewriting it.

### User
My number for the bike after lock and lights is lower than they're asking. Should I still message?

### Assistant
Yes, once, with the real number. Don't "start low" as a game if the real number is already the max. State it.

"I can do [lower number] because I still have to buy a lock and lights. If that works, grocery lot, daylight. If not, no worries."

If they ignore or mock it, you're done. If they meet it, you still inspect. A lower price is not a reason to skip the serial number.

If they say "bring extra cash in case," you don't. Extra cash is how tight budgets become stories. Bring the bike number plus bus fare, not a secret second envelope.

Plan in one line: daylight public meet, inspect, pay at most the pre-split bike number, lock and lights from the rest, skip the night lot. Let this listing die if it only exists after 8 behind a store.

### User

### Assistant
That's the budget talking, not the photos. Send it once, then let it go if they won't move.
