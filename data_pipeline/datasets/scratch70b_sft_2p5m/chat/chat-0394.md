### User
I rented this place because it was close to the park and the listing said "charming historic block." The church next door rings on the hour from early morning into the evening. Sundays are a concert. I walked the street at 2 p.m. when I toured. I did not walk it at 7 a.m. I feel stupid. I also cannot sleep. I don't think I can ask a church to stop being a church. I need a plan that isn't just hating myself for signing.

### Assistant
You toured at the wrong hour. That is a real miss. It is not a personality. Bells are still bells at 7 a.m. whether you were clever or not.

You probably cannot negotiate a historic church into silence. I will not invent an ordinance about hours of ringing. Some places have rules. Many do not. Your first tools are in the apartment: sleep timing, sound, and whether the lease has an out you actually have.

For sleep: earplugs you can tolerate, a fan or other steady noise, and a bedtime that does not fight the 7 a.m. ring if that ring is fixed. Weekends may need a different tactic than weekdays. Sundays might be a leave-the-apartment morning until you know the pattern.

Track the hours for a week in a note: first ring, last ring, extra days. A pattern is more useful than "it's a lot." You may find a pocket that is quieter, or you may find it is constant. Either way you stop guessing.

Did you sign a lease with months left, or are you month-to-month?

### User
Nine months left. No early-out that I spotted, but I am not a lawyer and I am not asking you to be one. I like the park. I like the kitchen. I hate the hour chime. Neighbors seem used to it. I feel like the new person who didn't do homework.

### Assistant
Nine months is a season of bells, not a life sentence, and also not nothing. You can dislike a sound and still keep a kitchen.

Do the week of notes. Then decide whether this is a sleep problem you can buy down with plugs and noise, or a move problem you will start in a few months. Those are different budgets of attention. I will not name product prices.

Talking to the church is optional and often goes nowhere. If you do it, go as a neighbor asking whether certain hours are fixed, not as a customer demanding a refund of charm. A sexton might tell you the schedule. A schedule helps you plan. It will not likely delete Sundays.

Neighbors who are used to it can still tell you which rooms are worse. Ask one person you already nod at: "Does the hour bell bother you on this side?" If they say they sleep in the back, you might move your bed. Geography inside the unit is cheaper than shame.

Do not spend the next nine months narrating how stupid you were. That story does not mute bronze. The homework you missed is done. The remaining homework is earplugs, bed placement, and a calendar reminder for when you can choose again.

### User
If I complain to the landlord they will say I knew there was a church. The listing even showed the steeple. I feel trapped in a joke I walked into.

### Assistant
A steeple in a photo is not the same as an hourly night ring. You can still mention sleep to a landlord if you want a window weatherstrip or a different unit later. I will not promise they must fix bells. Many will shrug. Shrugging does not forbid a request for practical help: better seals, a fan leftover from another unit, permission to add thick curtains. Small asks.

You are not trapped in a joke. You are in a lease next to a church. That is a specific inconvenience. Treat it like one. Tonight, set out whatever you use for sound, move the bed if the wall is the problem, and write tomorrow's first ring time. Next week, look at the list and pick one change. Keep the park. Keep the kitchen. Let the self-roast go quiet even if the bells do not.
