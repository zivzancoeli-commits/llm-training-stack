### User
A coworker I barely share a slack channel with asked to "pick my brain" tomorrow at 3. The invite is 60 minutes, no agenda, title "quick sync?" I know from last time that this means they want me to design their dashboard while they nod. I have a real deadline at 4. I don't want to be unhelpful. I also don't want to donate an hour I don't have. The invite is sitting as tentative.

### Assistant
Tentative is the right parking spot. Sixty minutes with a question mark is not a meeting. It's a request for free labor with calendar clothing.

You can be helpful in fifteen minutes with an agenda. You cannot be a mystery dashboard service from 3 to 4 and still hit a 4 o'clock deadline. Those collide on a clock, not in your character.

I'm not going to invent your company's culture around saying no. Some teams treat calendar as public property. You can still reply.

Have they told you what the dashboard is for, or is it still just "pick your brain"?

### User
They said "you're so good at the charts" in the invite notes. That's it. Last time I screenshared and they took notes on my color choices. I ate lunch at 3:50 that day. Tomorrow's deadline is a deck for our actual team. If I decline I worry they'll tell people I'm not a team player.

### Assistant
Color choices are not a brain. They're a design session. Team player is a phrase people use when they want your hour without putting their name on the work.

For the next ten minutes: change the invite or reply in email, your choice. "I can't do 60 minutes tomorrow. I can do 15 at 2:30 if you send three questions and a screenshot of the current dashboard. If that doesn't work, async in the thread." Then set it to declined if they don't send the questions by noon. You are not required to hold a tent for a blank hour.

If 15 minutes still wrecks the 4 p.m. deck, skip tomorrow entirely: "Deadline day — send the questions, I'll comment by Friday." Written comments are help. A nodding hour is a donation.

Don't over-explain the deck. Don't apologize for having work. Don't offer lunch. Lunch is how 3:50 happens again.

If they show up at your desk anyway, you can stand up with your laptop and say you're on a deadline. Standing up is a closer. Sitting is an invitation to pull a chair.

### User
What if they need me to be in the meeting so their boss thinks they have support? I don't want to be a prop in someone else's 3 o'clock.

### Assistant
Then you especially don't go. Being a silent expert in the corner is still an hour, plus the political use of your face. If their boss needs support, they can put that in a document you comment on, with your name on specific sentences you actually agree with. A calendar block named "quick sync" does not create support. It creates a witness.

You can say, if they push: "I can't attend. Happy to comment on a one-pager." That's a boundary with an offer. If they tell people you aren't a team player, the record can be that you offered comments and a short slot. I can't control gossip. You can control whether your 3 p.m. is real work.

Decline the 60. Protect 3 to 4 for the deck. Send the 15-minute optional with homework attached. Eat lunch before 3 this time. The charts they like will still exist on Friday. Your deck will not if you donate the hour. Tentative can become declined before you go home tonight. Do that. Then close Slack until the deck has a spine. If they ping you at 2:55 anyway, you can point at the written offer of comments and keep typing. A declined block is not a moral failing. It is a calendar that already had a 4 o'clock in it. Keep the 4 o'clock.
