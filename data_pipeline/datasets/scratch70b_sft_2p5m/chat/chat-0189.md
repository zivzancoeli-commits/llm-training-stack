### User
I'm at the city museum with my cousin. There's a special exhibit, dim rooms, a guard who already clocked my phone. Signs say no photography, no flash, no video. I wanted photos because the postcards in the shop are like a car payment and I don't have that. Cousin is whispering that I should just shoot from the hip. I don't want a scene. I also don't want to leave with zero memory of the weird clock sculpture. Tight budget. Help me make a plan that isn't "buy the catalog."

### Assistant
Don't shoot from the hip. That's how you get walked out and still have no good picture. The guard already saw the phone. You are not invisible.

Budget plan: use the free stuff the building already gives you. Grab a map and the exhibit brochure at the desk if they're free. Write the work titles and a couple of notes on the back of the map while you're in the room — "clock sculpture, brass, room 3, sounds like a click." That's a memory you can keep without a file. If the museum has a free audio guide or a QR that goes to their own photos, use that. Their photos are allowed. Yours might not be.

Shop: skip the fat catalog. If you want one cheap object, look for a small card pack or a single postcard of the one piece you cared about, not a stack. If even that is too much, skip it. Your notes plus the museum's website later is a complete souvenir.

Ask the guard, politely, whether the lobby pieces or the courtyard are okay. Some places ban only the special exhibit. That's a real question, not a loophole speech. If they say no everywhere, you stop. You don't argue copyright. You don't invent a student exception unless you actually have a written policy that says so.

You almost have this. You already don't want a scene. Now don't let cousin turn that into a dare.

### User
Guard said special exhibit is no photos, permanent collection is fine without flash. Clock is in the special exhibit. Cousin rolled her eyes like I asked permission to breathe. There's a sketch stool in the corner. I can't draw. Is sitting there and staring a plan or just sad.

### Assistant
Sitting there is the plan. Looking is the thing you paid for, or got free day for. A stool is not a consolation prize. It's how you actually see the clock instead of hunting an angle.

If you can't draw, you can still list: materials, size compared to a person, what the clicking does, how the room smells, who else is watching. That's not homework. That's how you tell the story later without a JPEG. Voice-memo in the lobby after you leave the room if talking inside is frowned on. Not in the gallery if it's quiet.

Cousin can roll her eyes. You can say, "I'm not getting us kicked out for a blurry clock." Then you sit. She can roam. You're not her entertainment director.

If you want something to take home that costs nothing, check whether the museum emails a newsletter with exhibit images, or whether the artist has a public page. I wouldn't promise that. Look when you're on wifi. Don't stand in the gallery googling with the brightness up.

### User
What do I say if cousin keeps hissing "one picture, they won't care." I fold when people make me the hall monitor.

### Assistant
You're not the hall monitor. You're the person who asked and got an answer. Repeat the answer: "They care in this room. Permanent collection after this." Then you walk to the next gallery when you're done looking.

If she takes the picture anyway, you don't need to grab her phone. You can step away so you're not in the argument. That's allowed. You're on a budget of money and of stress.

Afterward, go through the permanent rooms and take the photos they already said yes to. That's the treat. Cheap and legal. If the clock is the only thing you came for, you still got the stool and the notes. That's a full visit.

You don't owe the shop. You don't owe a sneaky file. You owe yourself a day that doesn't end at the security desk. Sit, write, then photograph the stuff that's allowed. That's the whole tight-budget plan.
