### User
It's 1:20 a.m. Someone is in the alley dumpster under my bedroom window. Metal lids, bags, a laugh, then a crate hitting concrete. I can hear the conversation. Two people, maybe three. I don't want to start a fight through a screen, and I also don't want to lie here pretending this is normal. Building is three stories. I'm on the second. Street is empty. Help me do the next ten minutes without becoming the neighborhood cop.

### Assistant
Stay in the room first. Don't open the window to lecture. Don't go downstairs in socks to "just say something." Noise at 1:20 is a problem. It is not an invitation to meet strangers at a dumpster.

Close the window if it's open. If the AC needs a crack, close it anyway for ten minutes. Put on actual shoes if you might leave the bedroom later. Phone in your hand. That's the whole prep. You are not suiting up.

I will not invent a city rule about dumpster diving, quiet hours, or who owns the trash. Those change. What you have is noise under a bedroom at night. Treat it as noise, not as a court case.

If you can see them from the window without leaning out, look once. Count people. Notice whether anyone looks injured, or whether this is just loud scavenging. Then step back from the glass so you're not a silhouette.

Do you actually need to sleep in that room tonight, or is there a couch that faces away from the alley?

### User
It's a studio. Couch is the same wall. I looked once. Two people, a cart, flashlights. Nobody looks hurt. They're talking like this is a job. One of them slammed the lid twice on purpose, I think, to get something unstuck. I already hate that I noticed "on purpose."

### Assistant
You noticed because you're awake. That's not a personality flaw. Leave "on purpose" alone. You cannot prove it from a second-floor window, and arguing about intent is how people yell.

Studio means you cannot migrate. So you reduce the sound and you decide whether you will contact anyone.

Earplugs or headphones with something boring, not a revenge podcast. If you have a box fan, turn it on facing you. White noise is not cowardice. It is how you keep from writing a speech.

If you want them to know a human is awake, one option is the light. Kitchen or bathroom light on, then off, is sometimes enough. A voice through the window is the other option, and it should be one sentence if you use it at all: "Hey, it's late, can you keep it down?" Not a lecture about whose trash it is. Not a threat. If you do not want a conversation, do not open with a conversation.

Calling a non-emergency line is allowed when the noise is ongoing and you cannot sleep. I will not invent hold times or what they will do. You can say alley, dumpster, 1:20, two people, cart, no one looks hurt. Stick to that. If you would rather not involve anyone, that is also a complete choice. You do not owe the building a report because you heard metal.

What you should not do: go downstairs alone to "set a boundary" at a dumpster. Boundaries at 1 a.m. in an alley are just you, uninvited, in a dark loading area.

### User
If I text the landlord now they'll see it in the morning and think I'm dramatic. If I don't, this happens again next week. I can already feel the speech forming.

### Assistant
Text in the morning if you want a paper trail. Night texts to landlords often read as panic even when the facts are fine. Write the note now in your drafts, send it after 8. Three lines: time, alley dumpster, noise lasting however many minutes, that you could hear it from bed. No essay about carts as a lifestyle. No demand that they "do something" you cannot name.

If it happens again, the second note is shorter because the first one exists. If it never happens again, you still slept eventually and you didn't pick a fight with two flashlights.

For the next ten minutes: window shut, fan on, light trick if you want it, one sentence only if you speak, no trip downstairs. Count the minutes instead of the lid slams. If it's still going after you would have been asleep anyway, then you decide about a non-emergency call using only what you saw. Two people, a cart, a dumpster, late. That's the whole story. The speech can stay in the drafts.
