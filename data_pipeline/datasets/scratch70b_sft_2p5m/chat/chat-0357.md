### User
There is a pan in the sink. It has been "soaking" since Saturday. It is Tuesday. The soak is a gray lake with a spatula drowned in it. I cook at night. I have been washing around the pan like it is a monument. My roommate says soaking is "how you don't ruin the coating." I think soaking is how you never wash it. We don't have a dishwasher. What do I say that is not a Ted Talk about chores?

### Assistant
You say a time, not a philosophy.

"Please wash the pan today. I need the sink tonight." If you want one concession to the coating story: "Soak is fine for an hour. Three days is occupying the sink." You do not need to win materials science. You need a basin.

Talk when they are in the kitchen, not via a passive-aggressive note on the first try. Notes are for people who never appear. This person appears; they just don't wash.

If they are gone all day, a text with a photo is fair. Photo of the pan, not a novel. "Need the sink — can you finish this tonight?"

I will not invent a roommate-law. Shared sink is shared. A three-day soak is storage.

Do you have a second pan you have been overusing, or is this the only skillet?

### User
It's the only skillet. I bought it. They use it for eggs and leave it. I have been cooking in a saucepan like a raccoon. They did dishes last month when their parents visited.

### Assistant
Your skillet. Their eggs. Three-day lake. That is a clean story. Keep it that clean.

"That's my pan. Wash it the same day, or don't use it. Soak an hour max." Then, if you want a physical cue, after you wash it tonight, put it on your shelf or in your room cabinet if you have one. People respect a pan that is not in the communal pile. That is slightly petty and extremely effective. Petty that returns your only skillet is allowed.

If they object to "your pan," you can say they can buy a second. You are not required to donate cookware to a soak museum.

Parents-visiting dishes do not count as a system. A system is Tuesday night, pan clean, sink empty. Ask for the system. If they agree and fail, you wash it once, you repeat the rule, you relocate the pan. Three strikes is a lot of eggs. Two is enough.

Do not throw the gray water on their bed. Fantasy, not a plan.

### User
They're on the couch. The pan is still in the sink. I have to cook in forty minutes. Do I wash it myself again?

### Assistant
Wash it this once so you can eat. While it is in your hands, say the sentence. "I'm washing this so I can cook. Same-day from now, or I'll keep it in my room." Then cook. Then, after food, put the dry pan somewhere that is yours.

Couch is not a reason to wait three more hours for a moral awakening. Hunger plus a speech with no skillet is how you eat crackers.

If they say "I was going to," you can say, "Great — next time, before Tuesday." Then you stop. You have a rule, a relocated pan, and dinner. That is a complete Tuesday. The coating will survive a wash. The sink might too.

### User
What if they buy a second pan and still leave that one in the lake? I only wanted my skillet back.

### Assistant
Then you still take yours. Their second pan can soak in their own timeline. Your skillet lives in your drawer. If they use yours again, you repeat the rule once and you do not loan it "for eggs." Eggs are how this started.

A house can have two pans and one sink. The sink rule stays: same day, hour of soak max, no three-day monument. You cook tonight. You put the dry pan away. You do not stand over the couch waiting for a conversion experience. Conversion is optional. Access to your only skillet is not. That was the whole ask. Keep it that size when they try to make it about coating science. Coating science can happen on their pan. Yours goes in the room. Then you eat.
