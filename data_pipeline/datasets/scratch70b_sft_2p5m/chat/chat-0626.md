### User
Every evening around 7:40 the neighbor fires a leaf vacuum that sounds like a jet in a pipe. Dusk. My kid is in bed at 8. Leaves are not an emergency. I don't want you to invent a city noise code. I want the machine to wait until Saturday morning. How do I ask without a lawn-care war?

### Assistant
Dusk is a time. Bed is a time. A leaf vacuum is a machine with a clock you can request.

I will not invent a decibel rule. I will not invent a permitted-hour statute. You can still say: "The leaf vacuum around 7:40 is hitting bedtime. Could you run it in daylight, or on weekend morning?" Daylight is the ask. Saturday morning is the offer. You are not banning leaves. You are moving a motor.

A note in their box or a daylight conversation beats a 7:41 porch in pajamas. If you catch them as they spool down, one sentence, then you go inside. Do not stay to debate mulch.

If they say the only time they have is after work, you can say, "After work before 7, or weekend." You can acknowledge their job without donating 7:40. Jobs end. Vacuums can wait.

Is this every evening, or a seasonal spike you can live with for a week?

### User
Seasonal, but it's been twelve days. Kid is waking. I waved once and they waved with the tube still on. There's a pile of bags already at the curb, so I don't even know what is left to vacuum.

### Assistant
Twelve days is a season with a pattern. Bags at the curb are a useful fact: the emergency is over. The rest is cosmetics in the dark.

Daylight note: "Hi — leaf vacuum after 7:30 is waking a kid who sleeps at 8. I see bags at the curb. Can the rest wait until weekend morning or before 6:30?" Before 6:30 may be too early for them; you can pick a window that is not dusk and not dawn-war. I will not pick a legal window. I will pick a neighbor window: Saturday 9, or weekday 5:30 if that exists in their life.

Wave-with-tube was not a conversation. Do not count it. You still have to use words. Words on paper if you freeze in person.

If they ignore the note, one follow-up. Then whatever actual process your block has — manager, city request — with times, not feelings. I will not promise a city comes for leaves. I will promise a log of 7:40s is better than a vibe.

Kid waking: white noise, door closed, the ask. You cannot out-parent a jet pipe forever. You can move the pipe's clock.

### User
They'll say I should close the window. I like a crack of air. Also I don't want to be the new person who polices hobbies.

### Assistant
Closing the window is their favorite solution because it costs them nothing. You can close it tonight as a truce and still ask for daylight. Both. Truce is sleep. Ask is the pattern. A crack of air is a preference, not a sin. You do not have to bake the room to be polite.

Hobbies are fine. Jet-pipe at 7:40 is a hobby with a spillover. You are not banning rakes. You are asking a motor to live in Saturday morning light. New person or old person can ask that. Twelve days of a waking kid is enough seniority.

Note in the box tomorrow, not at dusk tonight unless they start up and you can do one sentence without a fight. Tonight: window, noise, kid. Tomorrow: bags at the curb, bedtime, daylight please. Let them keep the leaves. Let them lose the dusk slot. You can be a neighbor who likes yards and still wants 8 p.m. to sound like a house, not a runway. Ask for the clock. Leave the war in the bag.
