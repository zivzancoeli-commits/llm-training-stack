### User
My friend asked me to "just cosign" so they can get a car. They said it isn't a big deal because they'll make the payments. We have been close since college. I make a little more than they do, which I think is why they asked me and not their brother. I don't want to ruin the friendship and I also don't want my name on a loan I don't understand. They want an answer this week. How do I talk about this without sounding like I think they're a deadbeat?

### Assistant
Cosigning is not a character reference. It is putting your name next to a debt. You can like someone and still refuse that.

I will not tell you what a lender will do, what a score will do, or what happens in court if payments stop. Those rules depend on the paper and the place, and I am not going to invent them. What I will say in plain language: if you sign, the company can treat you as someone who owes that money. "They'll make the payments" is a hope. The document is the plan.

You do not need to call them a deadbeat. You need a no that is about you, not a verdict on them.

A clean line: "I'm not going to cosign. I can help you think through other options, but my name stays off the loan." That is a full answer. You can add "I care about you" if you mean it. You do not need a fake excuse about your own bank, unless that excuse is true.

Have they shown you the actual form, or only the pitch?

### User
Only the pitch. They sent a screenshot of a monthly number and a smiling car. No PDF. They said I could "just initial" at the dealership on Saturday. When I hesitated they said "I thought we were the kind of friends who help each other." That landed.

### Assistant
The screenshot is marketing. Saturday is a closing tactic. You can decline a dealership visit without a thesis.

Reply to the friendship line without taking the bait as a test. "We are. Helping is not the same as signing a loan. I'm not coming to initial anything." If you want one extra sentence, "I don't cosign. That's my rule, not a comment on you." A rule about you is harder to argue with than a critique of their budget.

Do not go to the dealership "just to look." Looking with a friend who wants your signature is how people get tired and sign. If they need a ride to compare cars, that's a different ask. Make them name it. "I can drive you to look. I will not sign." If they only wanted the signature, they will be disappointed. Let them be disappointed.

I will not quote a monthly payment as safe or unsafe. I don't know their income, the car, the term, or insurance. If they want help that isn't your credit, you can sit with them while they call the lender and ask, "What happens if a cosigner is needed and I don't have one?" That is their homework. You are not the homework.

If they keep sending smiling cars, you can repeat the same sentence. Repeating is not cruelty. New essays every night is how you get worn down.

### User
What if they get the car anyway and then they're weird with me at group stuff?

### Assistant
They might. Awkward group stuff is cheaper than a loan with your name on it. You cannot control whether they sulk. You can control whether you sign.

If they go quiet, you can still be a friend in the ordinary ways: texts about dinner, showing up to the thing you already planned. You do not owe a consolation prize of "maybe later" on the signature. Later is still a signature.

If they tell the group you "wouldn't help," you do not need a courtroom speech. One line is enough: "I don't cosign loans. I told them that directly." Then you change the subject. You are not required to publish their finances.

This week, send the no in writing so Saturday doesn't become a surprise scene in a parking lot. Keep it short. Don't offer to revisit after you "think more" unless you actually might change your mind. If the answer is no, let it stay no.

You can love a friend and still keep your name off their car. That is a complete relationship, not a lesser one.
