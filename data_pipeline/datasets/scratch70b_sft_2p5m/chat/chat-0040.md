### User
I ordered a jacket. Medium. They sent a large. The listing said "check the size chart" like that's a personality test I failed. I've already written three drafts of a message and they all sound either pathetic or like I'm about to sue a person who sells coats out of a spare room. I don't even know if I still want the jacket. I might just be mad.

### Assistant
Then we should not jump into a script as if the goal is already "get a medium." You might want a refund, an exchange, or just to complain until the heat drops. Those are different jobs.

I'm going to check the obvious thing first: do you still want help fixing this, or do you want someone to agree the seller is sloppy and then you'll decide later? Both are allowed. I just don't want to coach a return you don't actually want to do.

Also, I'm not going to invent what the platform "has to" do. Policies differ, and sellers in spare rooms are not the same as big stores. Stick to what you can see: you paid for a medium, a large arrived, photos help.

### User
I think I still want the jacket if they can send the right size without me paying shipping twice. If that's a hassle, I'd rather send it back and be done. I'm not trying to collect a saga. I just hate looking foolish.

### Assistant
You don't look foolish for opening a box. They packed the wrong one, or their warehouse did. That's ordinary. The foolish feeling is optional.

If you still want help, keep the first message boring. "I ordered a medium, order [number]. The jacket that arrived is labeled large. I still want a medium if you can exchange it. If that's not straightforward, I'll return this for a refund. Which can you do, and who pays return shipping?" Photo of the tag. No feelings, no apology, no review threat in sentence one.

If they reply with "the chart is right, maybe try it on," you can stay dry: "I ordered a medium. This one is a large. I'm not debating the chart. Exchange or refund, please." That's not rude. That's refusing a side quest.

### User
What if they ignore me? I sent a message last night and it's been quiet. I keep refreshing like that's a skill.

### Assistant
Refreshing is not a skill. Give it a normal amount of time for a small shop — often a business day, not an hour. I won't pretend I know their exact SLA. If the platform has an order page with a clock on disputes, that's the path *after* a clear first message, not instead of it.

Meanwhile, don't wear the large "just to see" if that complicates a return. Don't cut tags if you still might send it back. Don't write a second message tonight that says "???" — that reads as heat, not information. If you write anything, make it additive: photo of the tag, order number, the two options.

And I'll ask again because people change their minds when the silent hour stretches: do you still want me to help you keep this process going, or are you at the point where you'd rather eat the annoyance and donate the large? I need the real answer, not the proud one. A skeptical read of your first message is that half of this is pride. That's fine. Pride is a bad shipping coordinator.

### User
I still want it handled. Exchange if it's easy, refund if it isn't. I'll send the boring paragraph with the photo. I won't sit here writing a manifesto.

### Assistant
Good. Send it. Then put the phone in another room for a bit. If they offer a prepaid return and a medium, take it. If they offer store credit you don't want, say no: "Refund to the original payment, please." If they go quiet past a reasonable wait, open the platform's order help with the same facts, same photo, no essay.

You already knew the jacket wasn't the whole problem. The whole problem was feeling like you had to perform either rage or apology. You don't. Wrong size, two options, wait, next step. That's the whole adult version, and it doesn't require you to become a different personality.
