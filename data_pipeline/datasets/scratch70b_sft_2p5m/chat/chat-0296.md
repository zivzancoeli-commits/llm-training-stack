### User
Every time I answer a simple question in Slack, Devin cc's our manager. "Adding Priya for visibility." The questions are like where a file lives. I now write like a lawyer. Priya has not said anything. I feel like I'm being documented. I don't want to look sensitive and I also don't want this to be my personality now.

### Assistant
"For visibility" on file locations is not visibility. It is a paper trail with an audience. Your writing like a lawyer is the predictable side effect. You can change the channel without a blowup.

Do not clap back in the thread with "why is Priya here." That becomes the thing Priya actually has to read. Do not match him by cc'ing his manager's manager. Escalate sideways and everyone looks twelve.

The first tool is a private, boring question to Devin, not a theory about his motives. I do not know if he is anxious, political, or copying a habit from a last job. Motive is optional. The behavior is the problem.

Is Priya actually your shared manager, and are these threads in a public channel or DMs that he then expands?

### User
Shared manager. Public channel. He @'s her even when she is already in the channel. It's like a tap on the shoulder every time I speak. I have a one-on-one with her Thursday.

### Assistant
If she is already in the channel, the extra @ is theater. Thursday is useful if you go in with examples, not with "I feel documented." Pick two threads, dates, and the pattern: operational questions getting a manager ping. Ask Priya what she wants. Some managers hate the noise and have not said so. Some like being copied and will tell you to live with it. You need her preference, not a guess.

Before Thursday, try the small door: a DM to Devin. "You don't need to add Priya on file-path stuff — she's in the channel. I'll ping her if something actually needs a decision." Short. No psychoanalysis. If he says he is "just being transparent," you can answer, "Transparency for a folder name is the channel. I'll @ her when it's a call." Then go back to writing like a person. Lawyer-voice in Slack is a tell that the dynamic is winning.

Do not stop answering questions. Do not go cold. Do not make a joke in the channel about hall monitors. If a question is genuinely for Priya, @ her yourself and be done.

If Devin is copying her because he does not trust your answers, that is a competence issue, and Thursday is where you ask whether your work is landing. Better to hear that than to keep guessing.

I cannot tell you if this is a PIP in slow motion. Probably it is a habit. Possibly it is politics. Priya's reaction to the examples is the data.

### User
What if Thursday she says she likes being copied. Then I look like I complained about nothing.

### Assistant
Then you learned a preference and you can stop treating the pings as a secret attack. "Got it, I'll assume you want eyes on those" is a clean landing. You did not complain about nothing. You asked how she wants information to move. That is your job.

If she says she does not like it, let her be the one to tell Devin, or ask her if she wants you to. Do not become her messenger with extra spice.

For the next ten minutes: screenshot two bland examples (not to post, to remember), draft the two-sentence DM to Devin, send it if it still reads dry after one reread, and put a Thursday bullet: "Slack @ pattern, what do you want?" Then go do an actual task. Do not write a manifesto in Notes.

You can be easy to work with and still not enjoy a shoulder tap on every sentence. Ask once, privately. Let Priya set the volume. Then match the volume instead of matching your fear.
