### User
It's 9:50 p.m. There's a drip from the ceiling over the dining table. A brown coin-sized stain grew while I was washing the serving platter. Guests come at noon tomorrow. I live on the second floor. I don't know if this is a pipe, the roof, or the upstairs tub. I have a mixing bowl under it. I don't want to wake the people above me with a panic, and I don't want a wet ceiling on a tablecloth.

### Assistant
Move the table. That's first, not a group text. Slide it out of the drip line. Put the bowl or a bucket on a towel on the floor or on a chair you don't care about. A mixing bowl is fine until it isn't. Check it before you sleep.

Turn off any ceiling fixture in that wet area if you can do it from the wall switch. Water and lights are a bad mix. I am not a plumber or an electrician. I won't diagnose the stain. Brown and spreading at night is enough to treat as active.

I also won't invent building rules about who you can knock on after 10. If you have a super or after-hours number, that's the channel. If you only have upstairs neighbors, a knock is reasonable for a possible tub overflow. A panic speech is not.

Can you hear water running above you, or is the apartment quiet except the drip?

### User
Quiet. No footsteps. The drip is maybe every four seconds. I texted the upstairs number I have from a package mix-up. No reply. The stain is bigger than a coin now, more like a saucer. I took a photo. Guests are my sister and two kids. I can cancel. I don't want to.

### Assistant
Don't cancel yet. A saucer stain is ugly. It is not automatically a collapsed ceiling. I can't promise it won't get worse overnight. That's the honest part.

For the next ten minutes: table moved, bucket on a towel, lights in that spot off, photo with a timestamp if your phone adds one, text the super or landlord if you have one: "Second floor, dining ceiling dripping, photo attached, guests tomorrow." Short. If you have no super, the upstairs text already sent is the start. Knock once if the drip speeds up or you hear a tub. One knock, one sentence: "Water coming through my ceiling. Can you check your bathroom?" Then come back down.

Put a pot you can empty at 1 a.m. if you wake. Set an alarm for 2:30 if you're a person who sleeps through drips. Emptying a bowl is less dramatic than a wet floor under a toddler.

Tomorrow morning, look at the stain in daylight. If it's dry, you can host and keep the bucket in the kitchen. If it's worse, you call again and you can move lunch to the park or to your sister's place. Canceling is a tool, not a failure. Using it at 10 p.m. because of fear is different from using it at 8 a.m. because of a bigger stain.

### User
The kids will ask why there's a bucket. My sister will offer to climb in the attic. There is no attic hatch I can see. I don't want her on a chair with a flashlight.

### Assistant
Tell the kids "the ceiling is dripping, don't touch the bucket." Kids handle boring sentences. Mystery makes them poke.

Tell your sister no attic and no chair. You don't have a hatch. A flashlight on a dining chair is how a leak becomes a fall. She can help by bringing a plastic bin if your bowl is small, or by taking the kids to the playground if you need a plumber's window. Help is logistics. Help is not her on your furniture under wet plaster.

I won't guess whether this is a roof or a pipe. Morning will tell you more than a theory tonight. Your job before bed is catch the water, kill the light in that patch, leave a message for whoever maintains the building, and sleep in a room that isn't under the drip. The tablecloth can wait in a drawer. Guests can eat at the coffee table. Noon is still possible if the bowl is doing its job.

Check the bowl once more before you lie down. If it's filling fast, knock upstairs even without a reply. Quiet apartments still have tubs. Then go to bed. You already moved the table. That's the part that saves tomorrow.
