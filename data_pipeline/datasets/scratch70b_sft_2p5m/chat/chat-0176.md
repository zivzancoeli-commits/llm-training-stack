### User
My kid is fifteen and wants midnight on weekends. Right now it's 10:30. They have a group that "everyone else's parents" let stay out. I work early. I am not trying to be the villain in the group chat. I also don't want to invent a city law I can't point to. I'm on my break. I have a sandwich. Please don't give me a TED talk about trust. Give me something I can say tonight without starting a war.

### Assistant
Two options, then what I'd actually pick. I'm not going to quote a legal curfew for your town because I don't know it, and a fake number is how you lose the argument the second they search.

Option A: keep 10:30 as the default, and sell exceptions as a plan, not a mood. Specific night, specific address, specific ride home, you still awake enough to confirm they walked in. That's not "midnight forever." That's "Saturday, Maya's, her parent is there, you're on a bus I can track, I get a photo of the door at 11:30." You can say no to the blank midnight and yes to one later night that has a skeleton.

Option B: move the weekend time to 11:00 for ninety days, with a written if-then. If they miss the time, it goes back. If they check in once without being hunted, it stays. 11:00 is not their ask. It's a step that respects that 10:30 might be from when they were smaller, without handing them a clock you can't supervise because you sleep at 9.

I would not jump to midnight. That's a lot of dark and a lot of other people's cars while you are the person who has to be at work. I also wouldn't hold 10:30 just to win. Frozen times turn into a personality.

I'd recommend A this month, not B, because you work early and you already sound like a person who will not actually be a midnight concierge. Exceptions you can stay conscious for are more honest than a new number you resent.

What's the actual thing they're going to — a house, a downtown nothing, a show — and do you know an adult who will be awake there?

### User
House party, friend they've known since sixth grade. I've met the mom once. Kid says the mom "doesn't care." That's supposed to comfort me. It does the opposite. They also said if I don't go to midnight I'm "treating them like a child."

### Assistant
"Doesn't care" is not a chaperone. Treat it as missing information, not as a vibe.

Tonight's sentence, tired-coworker short: "I'm not doing a standing midnight. I don't know that house after 10:30, and 'doesn't care' isn't a plan. If you want later, I need the address, the mom's number, and a ride that isn't a mystery. One night we can try 11:30 if those show up. Midnight as a lifestyle is a no."

That's A, with a number attached so they hear you moved. You are not debating whether they're a child. Fifteen is both: more brain than twelve, less than they think at 11:47. You can say, "You're not a toddler. You're also not a roommate who pays rent. Curfew is me knowing you're in the door so I can sleep and go to work." Then stop. The "everyone else's parents" line is bait. You don't have those parents' sleep schedules.

If they want to argue law, you still don't invent one. "I don't know what the city posted. I'm not using a cop as my personality. I'm using the fact that I have a shift." That's cleaner than a fake ordinance.

### User
What if the mom texts me "they'll be fine" and nothing else.

### Assistant
"Fine" is a shrug. You can reply, "Thanks. Will you be home? What time are people heading out?" If she doesn't answer like a person, that's data. Your kid can be disappointed. You can still pick A: no standing midnight, one later night only if the skeleton is real.

Put the two options on a sticky note so you don't improvise a third while you're hungry: A, case-by-case later with details; B, 11:00 trial if you actually want a new default. Recommendation stays A until the house is a known house. Eat the sandwich. Tonight you need a sentence, not a new identity as the fun parent. Fun parents still have to get up.
