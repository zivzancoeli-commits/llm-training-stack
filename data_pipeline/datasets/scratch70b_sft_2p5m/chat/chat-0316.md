### User
Chris wants me to "get in early" on an app that is "like a calendar but social." There is no prototype, no company, a logo they made in an afternoon, and a pitch that changes when I ask who pays. They asked for a number that is real money to me. We are friends from college. They said if I believe in them I would do it. I do not want to fund a logo and I do not want to be the friend who killed a dream.

### Assistant
Believing in a person is not a wire transfer to a logo. You can like Chris and still say no to vague.

Do not debate the TAM of calendars. Do not help them workshop the pitch as a substitute for a no. A workshop feels like a maybe. Maybes get followed up on Friday.

A clean no: "I'm not investing. I can be a friend who listens, not a check." If you want to be slightly softer and still clear: "I don't put money into ideas at this stage. If you ever have a build and actual terms, I still might not, but I would at least know what I'm looking at." Do not promise a later yes.

I will not value the app, guess a legal structure, or tell you this is or is not a security. I cannot see papers that do not exist. No papers is already an answer.

Did they name a dollar amount in writing, and are they asking only you or a whole friend group?

### User
They named it in a voice memo. Not writing. I think they asked two other people. One already said "maybe after bonus." I can feel a group chat forming.

### Assistant
Stay out of the group chat if it forms, or say the same no once in it. "Maybe after bonus" is how people get harassed until bonus. Do not be the second maybe.

Ask for nothing you will not read. If they send a deck, you can ignore it. Reading it is unpaid labor that they will treat as warmth.

If they use the believe-in-me line again, you can answer the line: "I believe you can make things. I'm still not putting money in." Split the person from the check. They glued them together on purpose. Unglue.

If you have ever wanted to help in a non-money way — testing a prototype, intro to someone who actually does this work — offer only what you will enjoy. Do not offer intros you will resent. An unpaid intro is still a gift. It is not a consolation equity.

I am uncertain whether Chris will take this personally. Some friends cool off. That is information about the friendship's terms. Better now than after a transfer you replay for years.

### User
What if they say I can just do $200 as a "token of faith" so I'm not left out when it blows up.

### Assistant
$200 is still money, and "left out when it blows up" is a sales line. There is nothing to be left out of yet. If you would not set $200 on fire for a story, do not send it. Token of faith is how people get a thousand tokens.

For the next ten minutes: reply with the one-paragraph no, do not ask to see the logo again, and mute the thread if they start sending competitor screenshots. If the group chat appears, paste the same no or stay silent. Silence is not a yes.

You are not killing a dream. You are refusing to be early-stage financing because you went to the same dining hall. They can build a calendar. They can do it without your rent money. Say no like an adult who can still get coffee. Coffee is cheaper than a logo. If they keep pitching after the no, you end the thread; a third explanation is still a maybe in their ears.
