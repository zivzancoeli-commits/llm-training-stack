### User
I am fourteen and Dad asked me to "put him on that password thing you use" because he wrote the Amazon password on a sticky note again. He is not dumb. He just hates new menus. I do not want to sound like I am scolding him. I also do not want to set it up for him and then be the family IT desk forever. Kind, specific, like you are talking to me, not like a corporate tutorial. He has an Android phone and a laptop.

### Assistant
Kind and specific means you sit next to him, you name buttons, and you do not sigh. He asked. That is already the win.

One sitting of twenty minutes. Pick the same app you already know, if it has Android and a laptop add-on. If not, pick one brand so he is not learning two. I am not going to name a paid plan as required. If yours costs money, say so: this one is free on one device, or this is a subscription, I do not know your exact price. Let him choose spend. You should not put a card down for him.

Plain words. A password manager is a locked list. He remembers one long password, the master, and the list remembers the rest. The sticky note is the old list on the desk. The new list is behind the master.

Phone first. Let him tap install. Let him create the account. If you grab the phone and finish it, you become the desk forever. Master password: he invents a sentence he will not post. You do not choose it. You do not write it in your notes. He can put it on paper once, in the tax folder, not on the monitor.

Which app are you on, and does he already have a Google account on that phone?

### User
I am on a free one with a browser add-on. He has a Google account. He is going to ask me to reuse the email password because he "already remembers that one." Also he wants me to save the banking login first, which feels like a lot of trust.

### Assistant
Do not let him reuse the email password as the master. Say it kindly and once: if someone gets the email password, you do not want that key to open the whole list. The master has to be a new sentence. Look away if he wants.

Banking first is his call, not yours. You can say we start with Amazon, the sticky-note one, and add the bank when you are ready. If he still wants the bank first, you do not have to watch the screen. Teach the save prompt on a low-stakes site, then let him do the bank alone. You should not collect his bank password. Kind is also a boundary.

On the laptop, install the same brand's add-on in the browser he actually uses. Log into Amazon the old way once. When the save box appears, let him click save. Then log out, unlock, and fill. That fill is the moment it clicks. If it fails, say the add-on is off, or this site blocked fill, and try the copy button inside the app. Specific, not mystical.

Agree now: I will help you add three logins tonight. After that, you add new ones when the box pops up. If you get stuck, we sit on Sunday, not at 11 p.m. on a school night. Unlimited night support is how you get mean.

### User
He is going to ask what happens if he forgets the master. I do not want to scare him with "you lose everything." Also my little brother uses the laptop.

### Assistant
Tell the boring version. If he forgets the master and has no recovery, he may have to reset accounts the slow way, email by email. That is a hassle, not the universe gone. That is why the paper in the tax folder exists for the first month. If the app has official recovery, read that screen together. Do not promise it works. I will not invent their policy.

Little brother: the manager should lock. Show Dad the auto-lock timer and set it short, like when the lid closes. A sibling should not get the master. School sites are a different login, not Dad's vault. Do not set up one shared vault for the house. That is a sticky note with extra steps.

Tonight: three saves, Amazon first, master is new, you do not watch bank, Sunday is the help window. You are translating buttons, not raising him. If he jokes that you are the IT kid, you can joke that you are off the clock after three logins. Kind, specific, done.
