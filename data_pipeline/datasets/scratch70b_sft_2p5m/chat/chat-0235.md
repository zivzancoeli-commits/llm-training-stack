### User
It's 6:12 p.m. I'm at the exit gate of the garage under my office. The ticket went in. The screen says "card error" every time I tap. Same debit card I used to get in this morning. There's one car behind me. The barrier is down. My phone is at 8 percent. I don't have cash in the console. I have a meeting across town at 7.

### Assistant
Stay in the car. Don't get out into the exit lane to argue with a box. Put the car in park if you aren't already. Hazard lights if someone is stacking up behind you. That's courtesy, not a confession.

"Card error" can mean a dirty reader, a network blip, or a card the garage doesn't like after hours. I don't know this garage's vendor and I won't invent a fee. I also won't invent whether they take cash. Look at the machine for a red help button, a printed number, or a "call attendant" line. If there is a camera, look at it once so they can see a person, not just a hood.

Don't keep tapping the same way six more times. Twice was data. Six is a ritual.

Is there a paper ticket still in the slot, or did the machine swallow it?

### User
It swallowed it. There's a help button. I pressed it and got hold music. The car behind me honked once. I tried the card chip instead of tap. Same error. I have Apple Pay but the screen only shows a chip picture and a swipe slot that looks greasy.

### Assistant
Hold music means a person exists somewhere. Stay on that call. Put the phone on speaker on your lap so you can still watch the mirror. One honk is noise. Don't get out to apologize to the honk.

For the next ten minutes: stay on the help line, try the chip one more time with the card facing the way the little diagram shows, and if the greasy swipe is the only unused method, wipe it with a tissue and swipe once. If Apple Pay isn't pictured, don't spend the 8 percent guessing. Dead phone in a stuck lane is worse than a delayed meeting.

When a human picks up, lead with location, not feelings: "Exit gate, ticket swallowed, card error, one car behind me." Ask them to raise the barrier or to take a card number by phone. I can't promise which they will do. If they tell you to reverse back into the garage, only do that if the car behind you has space and you can see it. Don't reverse into a honk on faith.

Text the 7 o'clock person a single line: "Stuck in the office garage exit, might be 15 late." Send it now while you still have 8 percent. Don't write an essay.

### User
What if they say I have to go back in and pay at a lobby machine? The car behind me is basically on my bumper. Also I don't know if I'll get billed twice.

### Assistant
I don't know their billing system. I won't guess a double charge as fact. If you do pay twice, that's a later email with timestamps, not something you solve by sitting under a barrier inventing math.

If they want you at a lobby machine, you need the car behind you to reverse or pull around. Put a palm up in the rearview, crack the window, and say "gate's stuck, I have to back up." Short. If they won't move, tell the attendant that on the phone: "I cannot reverse. Bumper is on me. Please lift the arm." That's their problem to dispatch, not yours to perform with a dead battery.

Don't abandon the car in the exit throat to run to a lobby. A driverless car in a gate is how you get a worse night. If an attendant can walk to you, wait for the walk.

Once you are moving, go to the 7 o'clock meeting as late as you are. Don't spend the drive reconstructing the error. Tomorrow, in daylight, you can ask the garage office what the ticket number did. Tonight the job is: human on the speaker, barrier up, phone alive long enough to send the late text. You already did the useful part. Keep the hazards on until the arm lifts.
