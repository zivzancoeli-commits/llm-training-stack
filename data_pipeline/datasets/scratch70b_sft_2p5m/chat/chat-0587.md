### User
I had a temporary in after a crown prep. The lab delayed the permanent. Wedding is Saturday. Today is Thursday. The temporary feels thick on one side and I lisp on s sounds. I'm in the wedding party. Photos are a thing. The office said "we'll see if the lab can rush." I don't want you to diagnose me. I want to know how to talk to the office and how to get through a toast without sounding like I have a mouth full of clay.

### Assistant
I will not diagnose you. I will not invent what the lab can do in two days or a fee for a rush. Your job is a clear ask and a backup for Saturday, not a medical theory.

Call the office today. Sentence: "The permanent is delayed. I have a wedding Saturday and I lisp on the temporary. What is the earliest you can recement, adjust, or seat something before Saturday afternoon?" Write down what they say. If they say the lab might rush, ask when they will call you back. "We'll see" is not a callback.

If they offer an adjust, take the earliest slot. I am not promising a chair can fix the lisp. I am telling you to ask, not to wait in hope.

Photos and toast are Saturday. Today is the phone.

Have they given you a dated lab promise, or only "we'll see if they can rush"?

### User
Only we'll see. Voicemail. I have a fitting for the suit at 4. The temporary has been in nine days. I can eat pasta but I chew on the other side. I'm scared it's going to pop out during the first dance.

### Assistant
Call again. If you get a person, skip the life story. Lab delay, wedding Saturday, thick temporary, lisp, worry it will pop. Ask: same-day adjust, temporary recement if it feels loose, any chance of the permanent before Saturday noon. Three asks. Circle the answers.

If it feels loose, say loose. Loose is a different sentence than thick. "It moves" gets a different chair than "it feels bulky." Do not wait for the dance to run the experiment.

Suit fitting at 4: you can still call in the parking lot. You can also ask the office whether they have a 3 o'clock cancel. Do not skip the fitting to sit in a waiting room with no appointment. That wastes both.

I will not tell you the temporary will or will not pop. I will tell you to avoid taffy, crusty bread, and chewing ice if you already know those yank temps. Pasta on the other side is the conservative version you are already doing. Keep doing that through Saturday if the office cannot see you.

Lisp on the toast: shorten the toast. Cut the s-heavy jokes. You can hold the mic slightly away and speak slower. A short toast is kinder anyway. You are not required to hide in the photos. You are allowed to close your mouth in posed shots. People do that when they are tired, too.

### User
What do I say if they only offer Monday and tell me to "just be careful"? I don't want to be rude to the front desk. They didn't delay the lab on purpose.

### Assistant
You can be precise without being rude. "Monday is after the wedding. Is there any emergency slot Friday for an adjust or recement? If not, please note I asked for something before Saturday." You are making a record, not scolding the receptionist for a lab. If they still only have Monday, take Monday so you are on the book, and ask whether a dentist on call can look at a temp that feels loose. I will not promise an on-call exists.

"Just be careful" is not a plan. Translate it into your own rules: other-side chewing, no sticky candy, backup dental wax if they ever sent some, phone in your pocket at the reception so you can step out if something moves. If it comes out, you do not super-glue it. You put it in a clean container and call the emergency number on the after-hours voicemail. I will not invent that number. It is on their recording or your paperwork.

Thursday: call, ask for Friday, take Monday if that is all, suit at 4. Saturday: short toast, slow s sounds, closed-mouth smiles if you want them, other-side pasta. You asked for a mouth that gets through a wedding, not a diagnosis. Keep the ask dated. Keep the backup boring. You can still stand in the party with a lisp and a live temporary.
