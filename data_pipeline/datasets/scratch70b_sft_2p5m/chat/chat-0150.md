### User
First session tomorrow. I'm in that last-year head where I will talk about the weather for ten minutes so I don't have to say why I booked it. I want help with the small talk without turning it into a performance. Also I keep almost asking you if this means I'm "depressed" like that's a sticker you can put on me from a chat.

### Assistant
I'm not going to diagnose you. A first session is not a label machine, and I'm not a clinician.

Last-year-you used weather because weather is safe. That's allowed for the walk from the waiting room to the chair. It is not the whole hour.

I need to check something before we script chairs: do you still want to go, or are you hoping I'll give you a reason to cancel that sounds mature?

### User
I still want to go. I'm scared I'll freeze and then they'll think I'm wasting the slot. I don't know if I should mention the thing that actually made me book, or wait until session four like a normal person.

### Assistant
If you still want to go, go. Freezing is common. Wasting the slot is a last-year story. Showing up with "I don't know how to start" is still using the slot.

You don't have to wait until session four for the real reason. You also don't have to dump your whole life in twelve minutes. A middle exists: one true sentence about why you booked, then you can go back to smaller talk if you need air.

Example true sentences, pick only if they're true: "Work has been sitting on my chest." "I keep canceling on friends." "I get stuck in my head at night." If none of those are yours, use yours. Don't borrow a dramatic plot.

Small talk that isn't fake: the commute, whether you found parking, that the building was hard to find. That's human. Then, when they ask what brings you in, last-year-you will say "oh, you know, stress" and smile. This-year-you can say the one true sentence and stop. Stopping is allowed. They can ask the next question. That's their job.

If they do small talk first — weekend, job title — you can answer short and then say, "I'm a little nervous about jumping in." That's not rude. That's you steering.

I still want a clear yes: you want help in the room tomorrow, not just a pep talk tonight?

### User
Yes. I want the room. What if they ask about meds or family and I don't want to go there yet. Last year I would have answered everything to be a good patient.

### Assistant
You can say "I don't want to get into family today" or "I'm not talking about meds yet." Good patient is not a grade. Consent is. If a question feels like a hallway you don't want to walk down, you say not yet. A decent therapist can work with not yet. If someone pushes past a clear not-yet in a way that feels wrong, you can leave, or you can say so, or you can not go back. I won't tell you who is "good" from here. That's your body in the chair.

Paperwork: fill it as honestly as you can without writing a novel in the lobby. If a form asks for a diagnosis you don't have, don't invent one. Blank or "I don't know" is fine if that's true.

Crying: possible. Not required. Last-year-you thought crying was the receipt. It isn't.

If you freeze: "I booked this because things have been too much, and now that I'm here my mind is blank." That's still content. They can work with blank.

After: you don't owe friends a recap. You don't owe me a recap. You can text yourself "I went" and eat something. Last-year-you would grade the session in the car. Skip the grade. You attended.

Canceling in the parking lot: if you truly don't want the appointment anymore, call and cancel instead of no-showing if you can. That's respect for the calendar. If you do want it and you're only scared, walk in anyway. You already said you still want help. I'm treating that as the answer.

### User
Still want it. One true sentence, weather is okay for the hallway, I don't have to earn a diagnosis from you or from them on day one.

### Assistant
That's the first session. Go, say the real reason in one line when they ask, keep the door closed on topics you're not ready for, and don't wait for a sticker. Last-year-you survived on weather. This-year-you can use weather as the hallway, not as the hour.
