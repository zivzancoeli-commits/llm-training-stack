### User
Bathroom door latch died. The tongue does not catch. Door swings open if the cat leans. I am on my lunch. There is one hardware store, the kind with a bell and a guy named Dale who will talk for twenty minutes if you let him. I do not have energy for a renovation story. I need to walk in, get what I need, and get back. Also I am not made of money. Give me two paths and tell me which one you would do.

### Assistant
Two options, then I will pick.

Option A: replace just the latch piece in the door edge — the part that moves when you turn the knob — and maybe the little metal plate on the frame if it is chewed up. Same holes, same knob. You unscrew two or three screws, swap the latch, test the door. Fast if the new latch matches the backset, meaning the distance from the edge of the door to the center of the knob. Bring that measurement. A scrap of paper with "about this far" is better than guessing in the aisle.

Option B: replace the whole passage set — knob, latch, and strike plate as a kit. You do this if the knob is loose, the latch body is a weird size, or Dale cannot match the old piece. More parts, more time, usually more money. I will not invent a price. Ask him for both, then look at the tags.

What I would do on a lunch break: Option A if the knob still feels solid and the holes line up. You want the door to click shut tonight, not a new aesthetic. Take the old latch with you in a sandwich bag. Hardware stores think in parts on the counter, not in stories.

### User
The knob is a little wobbly. The strike plate screws are stripped in the wood. Cat is an agent of chaos. Dale is going to ask what happened like it is a mystery novel.

### Assistant
Wobbly knob plus stripped strike screws nudges you toward a hybrid, still not a renovation.

Still two paths, updated.

Path one: latch plus a longer-screw trick for the plate. You keep the knob, swap the latch, and ask for longer strike-plate screws that bite into the frame instead of spinning in the old holes. If the wood is blown out, ask for toothpicks-and-glue or a wood filler stick — Dale will know the cheap version. This is still the small repair. Risk: the wobbly knob stays wobbly and the door feels cheap.

Path two: a complete passage lockset that includes new knobs, latch, and plate, and those longer screws. You pull the old knobs, drop in the new set, use the new plate. This fixes wobble and catch together. Risk: it takes the whole lunch if you have never done it, and you might need a screwdriver you do not have in the car.

Recommendation, tired-coworker version: buy the full passage set if the knob wobbles in your hand. A new latch in a floppy knob still gives you a cat door. Get the set labeled for an interior bathroom, not a keyed lock, unless you actually want a lock. Ask Dale: "Interior passage set, same hole size as this," and put the old latch on the counter. If he starts the novel, you can say, "I've got to get back to work — which of these two will catch on this door tonight?" Point at the latch-only and the full set. Then buy the full set.

### User
I do not know backset. I do not want to look helpless. One sentence to measure at home plus one sentence in the store.

### Assistant
At home: measure from the edge of the door to the center of the knob hole, write the number, bag the old latch.

In the store: "I need this to catch again tonight. Latch-only if it matches, otherwise a full interior passage set. Which of these two should I buy for this?" Then take the recommendation you already decided — the full set — unless he shows you a latch that is clearly the same size and you are willing to live with the wobble.

Pay, leave, eat the other half of lunch after the screws are in. If the door still will not catch, the plate is probably not lining up with the tongue; a small up-or-down shift of the plate is the next five minutes, not a new philosophy of doors. You are not building a house. You are stopping a cat. Buy the kit, skip the memoir.
