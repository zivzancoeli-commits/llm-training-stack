### User
There's a guy on my team, Derek. Every email, even the ones that say "looping you for visibility," he hits reply-all with "Thanks!" or a joke or a question that only one person can answer. My inbox is a landfill. I have twelve minutes before standup. What do I actually do.

### Assistant
Two real options, then I'll tell you which one I'd pick if I were you and half-awake.

Option A: handle it once, in private, about the pattern. Not about his personality. "When you reply-all on those visibility threads, it pings everyone. Can you reply just to the sender unless the whole group needs the answer?" That's a request with a rule they can follow. Short. No speech about professionalism.

Option B: change how the threads work so his habit has less room. Whoever starts the mail can write "please reply to me only" in the first line, or move the actual work to a channel where a thumbs-up isn't a twelve-person notification. You can also mute a noisy thread after you've seen it, which is self-defense, not a fix.

I'd start with A if you have to talk to him anyway and he's not your boss. I'd start with B if you don't have the energy for a conversation and the mail is coming from people above you. You sounded tired. Which is it — peer, or someone who outranks you?

### User
Peer. Same level. We share a manager. I'm not trying to "manage" him. I also don't want to look like the inbox cop.

### Assistant
Then A, done like a coworker, not a cop. Do it after standup, not in standup. Standup is public, and public feedback about email manners turns into a personality trial.

Don't collect screenshots. Don't say "you always." Pick one recent thread if you need an example, then drop it. "That 'thanks' on the budget thread hit twelve people. Next time just hit reply." If he laughs it off, you can add, "I'm asking because my phone buzzes every time." That's a consequence he can picture. It's not a lecture.

If you want a second move that isn't a talk: when you send mail, put the people who must act in To, and the people who only need to know in Cc. Then write "please reply to To-line only." Some folks ignore that. Derek might. But you have given everyone else a script, and if he keeps blasting, it's clearer that he's ignoring a written request, not reading your mind wrong.

Don't reply-all to tell him to stop reply-all. That's the joke version of this problem and it makes you the second landfill.

### User
What if he says he's just being friendly? I don't want to be the guy who hates thanks.

### Assistant
Friendly is fine. Friendly to twelve people who didn't ask is still noise. You can agree with the intent and reject the method. "I like that you're responsive. The all-reply is the part that's landing on people who can't use the thanks." You're not asking him to become cold. You're asking him to aim.

If he needs a rule of thumb: questions for one person go to one person. Jokes about the thread go to chat, not mail. "Thanks" on a FYI goes nowhere, or to the sender if you actually needed to confirm you saw it. That's three buckets. He doesn't need a flowchart.

Recommendation stays A, plus one tiny B: mute the worst repeating thread today so your next twelve minutes aren't spent swiping notifications. Muting is not giving up. It's keeping your brain for standup.

If it keeps happening after one private ask, then you have a pattern you can mention to your manager as a process thing — "this list is getting reply-all chatter, can we move status to the channel" — not as "Derek is annoying." Process language keeps you out of the cop role. Personality language puts you in it.

### User
Standup in five. I still haven't decided if I talk today or wait until I'm less irritated.

### Assistant
Wait until you're less irritated, unless the next mail is about to go out and you can prevent a blast with one line in your own send. Irritated talks sound like character judgments. Calm talks sound like inbox mechanics.

Today: mute, don't perform. After lunch, if you still care, the one private sentence. If by then you don't care, that's also data.

Don't draft a manifesto in the hallway. One sentence is the whole intervention. Finish standup first. This problem does not get a trophy for being urgent.
