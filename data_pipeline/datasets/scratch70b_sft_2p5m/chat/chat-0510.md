### User
A coworker built a deck and then asked me to present it in the all-hands because "you have a better stage presence" and they "hate public speaking." They already put my name on the title slide. I didn't write the work. I don't even agree with two of the charts. The meeting is tomorrow. They said it would be a huge favor and that I could "just read." I do not want to be the face of their slides.

### Assistant
Don't present work you didn't write and don't believe. A title slide with your name is not a compliment. It's a transfer of risk.

Tell them today, in writing: "Please put your name back on the deck. I won't present this. I didn't build it, and I'm not comfortable owning those charts." If you want a smaller kindness, "I can listen to you practice for fifteen minutes" — only if you mean it. You are not required to be their stage fright medication.

"Just read" is how people get asked questions they cannot answer. All-hands audiences ask questions. Your name on the slide tells them you are the author.

I will not invent your company's attribution policy. I don't know it. I know you don't want the face. That's enough.

Have they already sent the file to the organizer with your name on it?

### User
They sent it to the organizer and cc'd me. So now the organizer thinks I made a deck I haven't fully read. I feel ambushed. They said if I back out I'll make them look flaky.

### Assistant
They made themselves look flaky by putting your name on work you didn't accept. You backing out is a correction, not a betrayal.

Email the organizer, short, cc the coworker so it isn't gossip: "This deck is [coworker's]. My name was added by mistake. I won't be presenting it. [Coworker] should present or we should pull the slot." You don't need adjectives. You need the calendar to match reality.

If the coworker panics in Slack, you repeat the same fact. You don't take the slot to soothe them. Soothe is how your name stays on the chart you don't agree with.

If they ask you to "just do the two charts you like," that's still their story with your mouth. No, unless you actually take authorship of a slice you rewrote and can defend. Tomorrow is late for a rewrite. Pulling is cleaner than a half-adoption.

Don't trash their charts in the all-hands. You aren't presenting. You don't need a public review.

The ambush feeling is accurate. Treat it as information about how they use other people's reputations. You can still be civil in the kitchen. You don't have to lend your face.

### User
There's a director who likes me more than them. I think that's why they picked me.

### Assistant
Then your name is being used as a keycard. That's a stronger reason to decline, not a reason to help. Directors asking you questions about work you didn't do is how you get stuck explaining someone else's optimism. You will get the hard question. They will get the credit in the hallway after, or they will get none and you will still own the miss. Neither version is a favor.

If the director already replied "looking forward to your deck," you still correct it. A forward-looking email is not a tattoo. "Quick correction — this is [coworker's] work. They should present." That's housekeeping. It is slightly awkward. Awkward is cheaper than two charts you don't believe.

Don't offer to sit in the audience and jump in if they freeze. Jumping in is how you become the presenter with a chair in the second row. If they freeze, they freeze on their own slides.

Fix the organizer email today. Get the name off. Let them present or let the slot go. Stage presence is not a community resource. Keep your name on things you can stand next to when the lights are up. Tomorrow morning should find you with a calendar that tells the truth, not a title slide that borrowed your face.
