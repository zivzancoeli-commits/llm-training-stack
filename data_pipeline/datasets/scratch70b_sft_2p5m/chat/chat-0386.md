### User
It's a Tuesday night ceramics class. Eight of us. The instructor keeps holding up my mug as the "see how the rim got thick" example. Second week in a row. People glance at me. I paid for this. I am a beginner. Last night I laughed so I wouldn't look brittle, then I went home and hated the mug. Next class is tomorrow. I want them to stop using my work as the prop without me dropping the class or writing a manifesto on craft.

### Assistant
You can stay in the room and still refuse to be the prop. Beginners are allowed to have thick rims. Instructors are allowed to teach. They are not entitled to a volunteer mannequin.

Talk to them off the wheel, not in the circle. Before class or at the break, when the others are wedging clay: "Please don't use my pieces as the example in front of the group. I'm happy to get notes one-on-one." That is a boundary, not a craft theory.

If they say everyone learns from it, you can say, "I learn better without being the demo." Then you go back to your board. You do not need to smile it into a joke.

I will not invent studio policy or a refund rule. If the school has a coordinator, that is a later step. Tomorrow is a private sentence.

Do they do this only with you, or do they rotate victims and you just noticed yourself?

### User
They rotate a little, but my mug is the regular. There's a woman who gets praised for thin walls every week. I know that's not the point. I keep wanting to skip tomorrow and email. I also already bought clay. If I speak up I think my voice will shake and then they'll go gentle in a pity way.

### Assistant
A shaking voice still counts. Pity is uncomfortable and still better than a weekly showcase. Skipping teaches them nothing and leaves you with clay you are not using.

Go tomorrow. Arrive a few minutes early if they are usually at the slab table. If they are not, wait for the first break. Do not open class by announcing it to the eight. This is not a group vote.

If they pick up your mug before you have talked, you can say, in the room, one line: "Could we use a different piece for that?" Then you let the silence sit. That line is allowed even if your ears get hot. After class, still do the longer sentence so they have the full request.

Notes one-on-one can be useful. You can even ask for the technical point: "Show me on a spare cylinder how to thin a rim." That keeps the teaching and drops the theater. You are not asking them to lie that your mug is perfect. You are asking not to be the weekly object.

Do not compare yourself out loud to the thin-wall woman. That turns a boundary into a ranking. Rankings are their hobby. You do not have to join.

### User
If they keep doing it after I ask, do I email the rec center, or do I just take worse notes and leave at week six? I don't want a complaint file. I want to learn how to make a mug.

### Assistant
Ask first. Then watch the next class. If they stop, you stay and you make the mug. If they do it again, you have a second data point, not a personality.

A rec-center email can be short and untheatrical: you asked not to be used as the demo, it continued, you want to learn. I will not predict what the rec center will do. Some coordinators talk to instructors. Some shrug. You can still switch sections if another night exists, or finish the remaining weeks and not re-enroll. Leaving at week six is allowed. It is not a moral failure. It is a class.

What you should not do is sit for four more Tuesdays as the prop because email feels official. Official is optional. Your board is the point.

Tomorrow: early or at break, one sentence, then work. If they go gentle, take the notes anyway. Gentle for a week is not a tragedy. Being the mug on a stick for the term is the thing you are trying to end. Go make a rim. Let it be thick while you learn.
