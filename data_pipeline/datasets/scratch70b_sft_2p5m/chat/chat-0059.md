### User
Team lunch Friday. I need gluten-free, not as a trend, as a I-will-be-sick thing. I am the new intern. I do not want to make a scene at a sit-down place, and I also cannot spend intern money on a second meal if the kitchen shrugs. Help me plan this so I eat and I still look easy to work with. Budget is tight. I brought a sad salad last time and someone joked about it.

### Assistant
Scene is optional. Logistics are not. You handle this before you sit down, in a message to whoever picked the restaurant, not at the table with a raised hand.

Ask the organizer today: can we pick a place with a gluten-free option that is not just lettuce, or can I see the menu ahead. You are asking for a menu, not a parent. If they already booked, look the menu up tonight. If nothing is safe, tell them privately: I cannot eat there safely. I can come for water and company, or we can move, or I can eat first. Three options. You will pick with budget in mind.

Tight budget: eat a real meal at home first, then order the cheapest safe item if it exists, or tap water if you already ate. I will not invent menu prices. If the cheapest safe plate is still a blow, skip it. Showing up fed is not rude. Sitting hungry and performing ease is how the sad-salad joke happens again.

At the table, one sentence to the server, not a speech to the team: I need gluten-free, not cross-contact if that is possible, what would you suggest. Then you take the suggestion or you say I will just do a drink, I ate. You do not narrate illness in detail. You do not debate another intern who says a little bread is fine. A little is not your medical file. I am not diagnosing you. I am saying you already know your body. Other people's food opinions are not data.

If someone jokes about the salad again, you can smile once and say I eat before team lunches sometimes, it is a diet thing. Diet thing is vague on purpose. Vague is kinder than a lecture and safer than oversharing on week three.

If the organizer gets snippy about special, you stay boring: I know, I am trying to make it easy, that is why I asked early. Early is the whole anti-scene move.

Which of the three do you want as the default if the menu is a brick wall: skip the meal and attend, ask to move restaurants, or skip the lunch entirely.

### User
I should attend. Skipping the lunch looks like I am not a team person. Moving the restaurant as the intern feels like I am steering. So eat first, then water, unless there is a cheap safe plate. How do I ask the server without the whole table hearing a TED talk.

### Assistant
Ask when they are at your shoulder, not when they are performing the specials to the group. Lean half a step toward them. Quiet voice: gluten-free, I get sick, what is actually safe. Actually safe is the intern translation of cross-contact without the seminar. If they look unsure, that is your answer. Unsure kitchen plus intern budget equals you do not order the maybe pasta. You say water is fine, thanks. Then you turn back to the table like nothing happened.

If a coworker repeats it louder for fun, you do not correct the volume. You let it die. Correcting the volume is the scene. Your goal was no scene.

Eat first at home: rice, eggs, leftovers you trust. Do not buy a specialty bowl as a third expense. Tight money means food you already own, then a social lunch that is mostly sitting.

Bring a granola bar that you know is safe in your bag only if you can eat it without a performance. Bathroom or a short walk after, not at the table. Table snacks become the new sad salad.

Easy looks like: you asked early, you showed up, you did not make the picker feel stupid, you did not drown the table in explanations. You still ate, just earlier. Interns who handle constraints quietly get to keep them.

Default locked: attend, eat first, water unless a cheap safe plate is clearly marked and the server sounds sure. No TED talk. No second bill. If it is pizza-only, you still go, still drink water, still talk about work. Honor attend without going hungry. Hunger makes people snappy, and snappy is how an intern accidentally makes a scene.
