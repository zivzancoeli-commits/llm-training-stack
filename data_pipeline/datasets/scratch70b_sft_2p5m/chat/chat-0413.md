### User
I have gone to the same stylist for four years. They start with "the usual?" and I say yes because it is easier. I booked tomorrow because I want a shorter cut and bangs. I practiced in the mirror and still think I'll freeze and get the usual. I don't want to hurt their feelings. I also don't want another six months of a shape I am done with. I need words that land before the cape goes on.

### Assistant
Say it before you sit, if you can, at check-in: "Not the usual today. I want it shorter, and bangs." Then you say it again in the chair before the spray bottle. Twice is not rude. The chair has a script. You are interrupting the script on purpose.

Bring a photo of the length, not a celebrity if that stresses you. A photo of your own hair held shorter with a clip is even better. Point at where the bangs should sit on your forehead. Hands on your head are allowed.

If they say "are you sure?" you can say, "Yes. Please don't default to the usual." Sure is a complete answer. You do not need a life story about why you are done.

I will not invent how hair will grow or whether bangs will suit you. That is taste. You can always grow it. You cannot un-sit a freeze.

Do they usually cut while talking, or do they wait for you to confirm in the mirror?

### User
They talk a lot. I go quiet. Last time I wanted an inch and they took less because they "know my face." I paid and tipped and hated the walk home. Tomorrow I already feel like I'm cheating on the usual.

### Assistant
You are not cheating on a haircut. You are hiring a service.

When they start chatting, you can let the chat exist and still hold the plan. If they pick up scissors on the usual path, you stop them with a hand: "Shorter than last time, and bangs first so I can see." Sequence helps. Bangs first is a visible commitment. If they skip to the usual outline, you repeat the photo.

Tipping and paying last time did not buy mind-reading. Tomorrow you pay for the thing you ask for. If they look disappointed, they can look disappointed and still cut.

If you go quiet, use a written note on your phone you can show: three bullets. Usual no. Shorter. Bangs. Showing a note is less freeze-prone than finding a speech.

Do not apologize for changing. Apologies invite them to save you from yourself.

### User
What if I hate the bangs at home and I have to go back in two weeks like a person who doesn't know their own head?

### Assistant
Then you go back in two weeks like a person whose hair grows. That is ordinary. You can even say tomorrow: "If I hate the bangs I'll come back for a tweak. Please still do them." That gives you an exit without canceling the ask.

Hate-at-home is not a reason to freeze-at-chair. The usual is already a known dislike. A new cut is a risk you are choosing.

Tomorrow: check-in sentence, phone bullets, photo, bangs first, "not the usual" if the scissors drift. Sit up. Look in the mirror when they turn you. If it is already the old shape, speak then, not on the walk home. You are allowed to want a different head. Ask while the cape is still dry.

### User
Appointment is 10:15. I have the photo on my phone. I still think I'll say "the usual" because they smile first.

### Assistant
Let them smile. You still speak before the cape: "Not the usual. Shorter, and bangs. I have a photo." If "the usual" comes out of your mouth by reflex, correct it in the next sentence: "Wait — not the usual today." Corrections in the chair are legal. The smile is their greeting, not a spell. Sit up. Show the photo. Ask for bangs first. Look in the mirror when they turn you. If it is already last year's shape, talk then, not in the car. You booked this slot to change a script. Change it while the hair is still dry. 10:15 is the time. The usual can miss one visit.
