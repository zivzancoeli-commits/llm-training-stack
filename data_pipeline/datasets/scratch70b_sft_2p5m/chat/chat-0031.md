### User
We flew. Toddler is wide awake at 2 a.m. and furious that the sun is "wrong." I tried "your body clock is off" and got a blank stare plus a sippy cup to the chest. I need words a three-year-old can use, and I need them to not sound like I'm mocking them. I'm the older kid in this house in my head. Talk to me that way.

### Assistant
"Body clock" is adult shorthand. To a toddler it sounds like a toy they don't own. You want a picture they already know: sleep is a habit tied to light and meals, and the plane moved the day while their sleep didn't catch up.

Try this, kind and specific: "Your body still thinks it's home time. Home is the afternoon. Here it's night. That's why you feel awake in the dark." If they ask why the plane did that, you can say, "The plane went to a place where the sun comes up at a different time. We moved. Your sleepy feeling didn't move yet."

Don't make it a lecture about time zones. Don't say they're being bad. They're not. They're a small person whose cues got scrambled. The anger is the scramble, not a plot.

What time is it there now, and what time would it be at home? I want to match the picture to your actual gap, not a generic one.

### User
It's almost 3 a.m. here. Home would be like... late morning? We went east. They keep asking when Grandma is coming, which is a different clock problem.

### Assistant
Late morning at home and 3 a.m. here is a big mismatch. Of course they're wired. Their stomach and their eyes still voted for snack-and-play.

Grandma arriving is a second clock. Don't stack it onto the jet lag speech or they'll think Grandma controls the sun. Split the topics.

For the night waking: "It's still night here. The sun is down. We stay in bed and do quiet. Morning is when the sun comes back." Then give one allowed quiet thing: a book, a stuffed animal, lying down with you. Not a full breakfast, not a cartoon marathon, unless you are choosing that as survival. I won't pretend I know the perfect sleep rule for every kid. I do know that a 3 a.m. party teaches the body that 3 a.m. is day.

For Grandma: a concrete marker, not "soon." "Grandma comes after two sleeps" or "after this day and one more day," whatever is true. If you don't know the hour, don't invent it. "I don't know the clock time yet. I know the day" is better than a fake appointment.

If they cry that it's unfair, agree with the feeling. "Yeah. It feels wrong. Your body is still on home. It will catch up." Catching up, in kid words: "Each day the sleepy feeling moves a little closer to night here." You can use a window. "When it's dark, we try sleep. When it's light, we play." That's the rule you can point at with a finger.

### User
They want a reason they can repeat to stuffed animals. Also I said "jet lag" at dinner and now it's a monster named Jet.

### Assistant
Let Jet be a silly name if that helps, but make Jet a mix-up, not a villain. "Jet is when your sleepy and the sky don't match, because we rode a plane. Jet isn't chasing you. Jet is just confused." Then the stuffed animals can "help" by doing the same rule: dark equals quiet.

A repeatable script, short enough to memorize:

"We flew. The sky here is on a different time. My body is still on old time. That's why I woke up. The sky will teach my body the new time."

That's five ideas: plane, sky time, old time, waking, learning. No jargon. If they add "Jet did it," you can nod. "Yep. Jet is the mix-up. We're helping Jet learn this bedroom."

Keep your voice ordinary. If you go singsong, some kids think it's a game and get louder. Kind is not the same as performing. Specific is: this room, this dark, this one book, then lying down. Not "be good." Being good isn't the issue.

Tomorrow, in daylight, use the same words so 3 a.m. isn't the only classroom. "See? Sun is up. This is morning here." Point at breakfast. Point at shoes. The body learns from repeats more than from one perfect speech.

If they're still up after the script, you don't have to win a debate. You already explained. Then you do the quiet plan. The explanation is for their story. The dark-and-quiet is for their body. Both can be true at once.
