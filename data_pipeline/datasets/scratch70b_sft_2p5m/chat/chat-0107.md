### User
I got a parking ticket on the car I share with my roommate. I was the one driving. I thought the street was free after 6. The sign was confusing and it was my second week in this city. Ticket is on the fridge. Roommate gets home in an hour and I don't know what to say. I don't even know if this is a real ticket or a warning. The paper looks official but I haven't looked it up. I start this job as an intern on Monday and I already feel like I'm failing basic adult.

### Assistant
You're not failing adult. You're holding a piece of paper you haven't decoded yet. That's different.

I don't know your city's signs, and I won't pretend I can read a ticket I can't see. "Free after 6" is the kind of rule that is true on one block and false one street over. I am not going to bluff a statute.

Before the roommate walks in, do two things you can actually do. Photograph the ticket, both sides. Photograph the sign if you're still near the car, or go back if it's a five-minute walk. Then look at the ticket for a phone number, a website, or a citation number. If there's a site, that's where you find out whether it's payable, protestable, or a warning. I won't tell you it's definitely one of those.

When you talk to your roommate, lead with facts you have, not a theory of the sign. "I drove, there's a ticket, I thought the curb was free after 6, I'm looking up how to pay or contest it." If you don't know whose name the car is registered under, say that too. I don't know your registration. Don't guess it's yours or theirs to sound prepared.

Do you know whose name is on the registration, or is that also on the fridge pile?

### User
Car's in their name. Insurance too. I just pay into a pot. That's why I feel sick. They're going to think I dinged their record. I don't know if parking tickets even go on a driving record. I was going to say they do, to sound like I researched it, but I actually have no idea.

### Assistant
Don't say they do if you don't know. A lot of people mix up parking citations and moving violations. I am not going to declare what your state puts on a driving record. That is exactly the kind of thing interns get wrong with extra confidence. Look it up on the ticket site or your DMV FAQ *after* you talk, or say "I don't know yet, I'm checking."

Sick is a reasonable body feeling. The conversation can still be short. Your roommate is allowed to be annoyed. Annoyed is not the same as you being a bad intern-in-advance.

Script that doesn't bluff: "I parked on [street], ticket's on the fridge, car's in your name so I wanted you to see it before it sat. I thought the sign meant after 6 was fine. I don't know yet if this affects your record. I'll pay it if it's payable in my name, or I'll pay you back if it has to go through you. I'm reading the instructions tonight."

If they ask "is this going to raise insurance," the true answer is "I don't know." Insurance companies are not all the same, and parking tickets are often a different bucket from crashes. I will not invent a premium.

### User
I'll say I don't know about the record. I'll offer to pay. If they want to fight it, I don't even know if I have standing. That's another thing I was going to fake.

### Assistant
Don't fake standing. If the ticket is in the car owner's name, the contest process might need them. I don't know. The ticket instructions will say who can file. Read that together if they're willing. If they're not willing and they just want it paid, pay the pot back on a date you can actually hit, not "soon."

You're allowed to be new in a city. New is not a defense the sign cares about, and it's still a true description of you. Keep the talk to ten minutes. Then look up the number. Come back if the site is written in riddles; we'll parse buttons, not pretend I already know your ordinance.
