### User
A friend asked me to hop on a video call tomorrow and pretend I'm "Sam from their team" so a client thinks they have more staff. They said I'd just nod and say we're on track. I do not work there. I don't want to tank the friendship in one text, and I also don't want to play dress-up as an employee. They framed it as a five-minute favor. It does not feel like five minutes.

### Assistant
I'm not going to help you impersonate a coworker. Nodding along as fake staff is a lie to the client. I won't script Sam, coach you on the product, or help you look official.

You can still be a friend who says no.

The text: "I can't pretend to work at your company. I won't join the call as Sam." That's the whole favor declined. You don't need a joke to make it palatable. Jokes are how this becomes a story they tell later as if you almost did it.

If they say nobody will know, that's the point. The client is supposed to not know. You would know. That's enough.

I will not dress this up as "it depends on the contract." I don't know their job. I know you were asked to be a person you are not.

Have they already sent a meeting link with a fake display name, or is this still the ask?

### User
Still the ask. They offered to pay me in dinner. They also said the last freelancer flaked and the client is "picky about bench strength." I feel like I'm the backup body.

### Assistant
Dinner is not a costume budget that makes this fine. Backup body is an accurate description. You can decline being a prop.

If they pivot to "just sit with camera off," that's still the same ask with worse lighting. No.

If they want actual help that isn't fraud-flavored, they can hire help under a real name, reschedule, or tell the client the truth about staffing. Those are their options. You don't have to design their business. You especially don't have to populate it with ghosts.

When they guilt you — freelancer flaked, picky client — you can acknowledge the bind without entering it: "That's stressful. I still won't pretend to be staff." Empathy and participation are different.

Don't offer to "review their talking points as a friend tonight" if you can hear yourself sliding toward playing Sam in the notes. Helping them prep as themselves is optional and separate. Helping them prep a fictional employee is the same no.

If they already listed you on a slide, tell them to take the name off. You didn't consent to being a headcount.

### User
They'll say I'm overreacting to a white lie.

### Assistant
They can say that. You don't have to adopt their scale. A white lie about being five minutes late is not the same as a fake employee on a client call. You don't need a philosophy paper. You need a closed door.

If they send the link anyway, you don't join. You don't lurk in the waiting room "just in case." You don't change your display name for a laugh. If they introduce you from their end while you're not there, that's their mess. You still don't hop on to save it.

Send the no tonight so tomorrow morning isn't a scramble with a link. Don't leave it as "I'll think about it." Thinking about it is how people end up in a waiting room named Sam.

You can still get dinner as friends another week, as yourselves. You cannot get dinner as a coworker you aren't. Keep your own name. Let their bench be the people who actually work there. Five minutes of nodding is still a person you are not. Decline the minutes. Keep the friendship in your own clothes. Sam can stay fictional without your face.
