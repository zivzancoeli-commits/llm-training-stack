### User
The counter pail is full. Again. Fruit flies. My roommate Jules "forgets" the compost because they say they don't eat as much produce as I do. I cook more, yes. I also take the pail down three nights a week. The building bin is one flight. It is not a hike. I don't want a chore chart with stickers. I want the pail emptied when it's full, including by them. They are home tonight. I can hear a show.

### Assistant
Full pail, fruit flies, one flight. That is a household fact, not a produce contest.

Talk tonight, not via a passive-aggressive lid. Pause in the kitchen when the show is on a break, or stand in the doorway with the pail in hand: "This is full. I need you to take it down sometimes, including tonight. It's not only my job because I cook vegetables." Then you wait. If they go, good. If they negotiate calories, do not debate your salad.

A chart is optional. A rotation in one sentence is enough: "You take Tuesday and Friday. I take the rest. If it's full, whoever sees it goes." Forgetfulness that always lands on you is a pattern. Patterns get named.

I will not invent building rules about who may touch the compost closet. If only you have the fob, share the fob or go together once so that excuse dies.

Are they actually willing, or have they already called this "your project"?

### User
They called it my project last month when I bought the pail. They still throw banana peels in it. They just don't walk. Tonight they yelled "in a minute" from the couch. That was twelve minutes ago.

### Assistant
"In a minute" is not a plan. Walk the pail to the couch, set it on the floor in their sightline, and say, "It's full now. Please take it before the show's over." Then leave the pail there. Do not stand over them. Do not empty it yourself in the next five minutes unless you like training the delay.

If they get annoyed that a bucket is in the living room, that annoyance is useful. The bucket belongs in the bin, not on your conscience.

If they refuse outright, empty it this once so you can sleep without flies, and the next morning you have the real talk: "I bought the pail. We both use it. I need two nights a week from you or we stop using it and you keep peels in your own trash." Stopping the pail is a real option. It is uglier. It is still a boundary. You are not required to run a municipal service for a roommate.

Do not add stickers. Do not add a speech about the climate. This is a flight of stairs.

### User
If I put the pail in front of the couch I feel like I'm parenting them. We split rent evenly. I don't want this to become the personality of the apartment.

### Assistant
Even rent does not mean you donate labor. A pail on the rug for twenty minutes is a reminder, not a personality. Parenting would be a lecture. You are asking for a walk.

If you hate the couch move, skip it and use a calendar text: "Your compost nights are Tue/Fri. Tonight is leftover-full — please take it." Then you watch. Two missed nights and you retire the pail or you bill the labor in whatever way you already split chores. I will not name a dollar amount.

Tonight: one clear ask, then stop doing their minutes. If they take it, thank them once, not with a parade. If they don't, you decide in the morning whether the pail stays. Fruit flies are not a roommate's aesthetic. They are a full bucket. Get it off the counter.

### User
They're still on the couch. I can take the pail down in ninety seconds and keep the peace. That is the old loop.

### Assistant
Ninety seconds is how the loop survives. If you take it tonight, say out loud as you go, "This is the last time I do your walk." Then tomorrow you do not. The pail in their sightline is still available. Peace that smells like fruit flies is not peace. Let the show pause. Let the bucket be their problem for one episode. That is the new loop.
