### User
Utilities come out of my account. My roommate is supposed to send their half when I text the amount. They "forget" until I ask a second time, sometimes a third. It's been six months. They always pay eventually, with a joke about being bad at adulting. I am tired of being the collections department in my own kitchen. The bill isn't huge. The chase is huge. How do I change this without a roommate meeting that lasts an hour?

### Assistant
Stop treating it as a memory problem if the pattern is six months long. Forgetting that often is a system that works for them. You are the reminder service.

You don't need an hour meeting. You need a payment path that doesn't depend on your mood. Pick one: they put a recurring transfer on the calendar for a typical amount, then you settle the leftover when the real bill lands; or they get on the utility account if the company allows a second payer; or they send their half the same day you forward the bill, every time, no joke.

Say it once, standing up, not as a speech over dinner: "I need your half within a day of my text, or we set up an automatic transfer. I'm not going to ask three times." Then you wait. If they laugh, you don't laugh with them. The joke is the delay.

I will not invent late fees or what a utility will do. I don't know your provider. I also won't tell you they "owe" a legal penalty. This is a house rule about cashflow.

Do they pay other shared things on time, or is it only utilities that slip?

### User
Rent is on time because it's a portal with a deadline that emails them. Utilities are "whenever [me] nags." They offered to venmo me extra "for my trouble" once and then didn't.

### Assistant
Believe the portal. Deadlines with machines work. Deadlines with your face don't. So stop being the machine's substitute.

Forward the bill the day it arrives with a single line: "Half is [amount], please send today." If they don't, you ask once the next day: "Still need the utility half." If they don't after that, you stop adding jokes and you move to structure: "This isn't working. Set a monthly auto-send for [rough number] on the first, and we'll true-up." If they refuse structure and refuse timely payment, then you have a roommate problem bigger than adulting, and you decide whether you can live with chasing or you start looking at options you actually have — a different roommate, a different split of whose account the bill sits on — without me inventing lease law.

Don't accept "extra for your trouble" as a concept unless the extra actually arrives. IOUs for emotional labor are how this continues.

Put the amounts in writing in the same thread every month so it isn't folklore. Screenshots of the bill, not vibes.

If you can move the account into both names or into theirs, ask the utility what they allow. I won't guess. If they only allow one name, you remain the payer of record and you need the auto-send even more.

### User
They'll say I'm being intense about a small bill.

### Assistant
Small bills with a long chase are not small. You can say, "It's small when you pay it. It's not small when I have to extract it." Then you return to the auto-send. Intensity is their word for your Thursday evening. Let them have the word. Get the transfer.

This month: one clear deadline, one follow-up, then a standing transfer. You can still be friendly about dishes. You don't have to be friendly about being the bank. Adulting is sending the money. The joke can retire. If they miss the new deadline once, you don't restart the six-month comedy. You point at the auto-send and you wait for the machine to do what your face was doing. Your kitchen can go back to being a kitchen, not a collections office with a fridge.
