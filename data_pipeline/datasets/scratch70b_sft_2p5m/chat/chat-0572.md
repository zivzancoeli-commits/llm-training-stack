### User
Neighbor's hose is draped over the fence, trickling into my yard, making a mud fan near the foundation. It's been on long enough that the soil is shiny. I don't think they're home. I can reach the faucet on their side if I lean. I can also not trespass in my head. I like them. They forget things. First hard conversation if they're home later. Right now the water is still moving.

### Assistant
If you can shut it off from your side of the fence without going onto their property, do that. Trickle into a foundation is a today problem. Like-them is a later conversation.

If shutting it off requires you leaning onto their yard, don't do gymnastics that make you the person in their garden. Knock, ring, call, text. If nobody answers, a note on their door: time, hose into your yard, please turn it off, mud near my foundation. Photo from your yard of the hose and the mud.

I will not invent water-waste law or fence law. I will not quote a fine. You can still not want a swamp against your house.

Don't yank their hose back so hard you break a connector. Don't cut anything. Off and a note.

Is the faucet visible from your yard, or only the hose-over-fence?

### User
Faucet is on their wall. I can see it. I would have to step around the fence post onto their dirt to reach it. Their car is gone. Mud is maybe a foot from my siding. I am thinking about mold like a horror movie.

### Assistant
Don't step around the post. Gone car means the note plus a call/text plus, if you have a building manager or a landlord for a rental next door, that number. If they're homeowners and you have no number, door note is the main tool.

Mold horror: you don't diagnose the siding from a shiny patch. You can still move your own stuff out of the mud, put a towel or a board as a silly dam on your side if it helps, and point a fan later if the wall is wet. I will not tell you the wall is ruined. Daylight after the water stops is when you look.

If the trickle continues and you have a non-emergency city utilities line for running water, that's a maybe when nobody can be reached and a foundation is getting a bath. I will not promise they will come. Describe hose, empty house, water crossing the property line. Stick to that.

Like-them survives a note. Like-them does not require you to trespass a faucet.

### User
Tonight if they're back, I will want to joke it off so they don't feel accused.

### Assistant
Joke-off is how the hose returns next month. You can be kind and specific.

"The hose ran into my yard today and sat against the foundation. Please keep it on your side and off when you leave. I left a note because the car was gone." If they get embarrassed, let them. Embarrassed is useful. You can add "I know it was a forget" without adding "it's fine." Fine invites a sequel.

If they offer to look at your siding, you can say you'll look in the morning and tell them if you need a towel brigade. Don't invent damage. Don't hide the mud.

Right now: no fence-post ninja, photo, note, text if you have it, dam on your side if you want a task. Later: one non-joke paragraph. You can like a neighbor and still not want their river. That's a fence, not a friendship test.

When they're back, skip the joke. Foundation plus a forget still gets the paragraph. If they turn it into a tour of their tomatoes, you can like tomatoes and still want the hose on their side. Photo from today lives on your phone until the mud dries. If the siding looks worse in the morning, you tell them what you see, not a horror movie. You didn't trespass the faucet. You used a note. That's the competent version of liking a neighbor.
