### User
I washed a shirt I bought last week and it came out looking like it belongs to a middle schooler. Tags still on the receipt in my email. I have a break in ten minutes and I was going to start the return from my car. I am too tired to do this the dramatic way. Just tell me what you would actually do in the next ten minutes so I don't sit here staring at the steering wheel.

### Assistant
I'd use the ten minutes like a checklist, not a feeling.

Minute one: open the email receipt. Screenshot it or download the PDF so you're not hunting for signal in a store later. Confirm the store name, the last four of the card if it's there, and whether it says final sale. I can't see your receipt. If it says final sale, the next nine minutes are "call and ask," not "drive over convinced."

Minutes two and three: look at the shirt. Don't stretch it in a panic; that can warp the neck. Check the care label. If you washed it hot or dried it on high and the label said cold hang-dry, say that honestly if they ask. Lying about the wash doesn't help, and I won't script a lie.

Minutes four and five: check the store's return page on your phone for time window and whether they want the tags on. Don't treat a blog post as law. Use their site. I won't invent their day limit.

Minutes six and seven: put the shirt in a bag, receipt screenshot on the phone lock screen, wallet. If the store is on your way home, that's tonight. If it's across town, the ten minutes are for booking the trip, not for performing the return.

Minutes eight through ten: if you're still in the car, call the store while you can still think. "I bought a shirt last week, it shrank after one wash, can I return it in person with the receipt?" Write down the person's first name if they give one. Then start the car.

Where is the store relative to this break — five minutes away, or a weekend errand?

### User
It's like twelve minutes from here, so I can't do it on this break. I dried it on medium. Label said tumble dry low. I know, I know. Receipt is not final sale. There's a QR code. I'm embarrassed more than angry. I just wanted a shirt that fit.

### Assistant
Medium isn't low. That's the whole story, and it's a common one. Embarrassment is allowed. It doesn't change the next moves.

You can't finish the return in this break. You can finish the *prep*. Screenshot the receipt. Put the shirt in the bag when you get home so it doesn't get worn again "just around the house." Add the store to tomorrow's after-work loop if that's when you have energy. I would not go tonight if you're already this fried. A tired return conversation is how people accept store credit they didn't want.

I don't know whether this chain cares about dryer settings. Some do, some don't. I'm not going to promise you a refund. The honest ask is: here's the receipt, it doesn't fit after one wash, I'd like to return it. If they offer exchange in the same size, only take it if you still want that brand. A second copy of a shrinking shirt is not a win.

On the QR: scan it later, not while driving. If it opens a return portal, read whether it wants a drop-off or a shipping label. I won't guess the fee. If there's a fee that looks ugly, you can still walk in and ask. Portals lie by omission sometimes.

### User
Tomorrow after work then. Bag tonight. No stretching. If they say it's my fault for the dryer, I just... leave?

### Assistant
If they refuse, you leave with the shirt. You don't argue a novel in the aisle. You can ask once, "Is there a manager who can look?" If that's also no, you're done for the day. Keep the receipt screenshot anyway. That's the ten-minute version stretched across two calendar days, which is how tired people actually get this done.

Go finish your break. Eat. The shirt is not a crisis. It's a bag by the door.
