### User
7:05 a.m. Hallway smells like paint. Drop cloths, a radio, two workers. I have a meeting at 8 from home. No notice under the door, no email I can find. Landlord's the kind who says "we don't need to make a fuss." I opened the door in a T-shirt and they looked at me like I was in the way of a hallway I pay for. I need the next hour and also the next week. No fake statutes.

### Assistant
Next hour: your meeting. Close the door. Towel at the bottom if fumes bother you. Fan in the window if you have one that blows out. Headphones. You may still smell it. You may need to take the call on mute except when you talk, and say you're in a building that's being painted if someone comments.

Do not start a legal debate in a T-shirt with the painters. They have a work order. They are not the policy. A short "how long today?" is useful. Then you go back in.

I will not invent notice rules for entry or common areas where you live. Hallways are often treated differently from inside your unit, and I will not guess your lease. What you can do is document: time, photo of the drop cloths from your doorway, screenshot of an empty inbox, a note of no paper under the door.

After the meeting, you write the landlord. Facts, not fuss-language. "Paint in the hallway this morning at 7 with no notice I received. I had a work call. Please tell me the schedule and how you'll notify next time."

Did they enter any part of your unit, or is this only the corridor?

### User
Only the corridor. Radio is still going. Meeting is video. I look like I slept in a hardware store. Landlord texts in one-word replies. I am worried if I complain they'll "forget" a repair I already opened.

### Assistant
Corridor-only still wrecks a video call. You're not imagining that.

For the call: camera off if your workplace allows it, or a virtual background, or sit with your back to the door so people see a wall. "Building painting, I'll be on audio if the noise spikes" is a professional sentence, not a confession. You don't owe a comedy bit about 7 a.m.

Repair-hostage fear is real as a feeling. I will not predict this landlord. Written complaints that stay factual are still better than silence if you need a record. Keep the repair ticket in the same thread or in a separate dated email so it exists independently: ticket number, what's broken, that it's still open. Don't mix "you painted at 7" and "fix my sink" into one emotional paragraph. Mixed paragraphs are easier to ignore.

One-word texts from them: you can still send complete sentences. Their style isn't your template.

If the radio is the extra insult, you can ask the crew, once, to turn it down because of a call. They might. They might not. Don't stand in wet paint to win a radio.

### User
After the call I want to be calm in the email and I can feel a novel coming.

### Assistant
Timer: fifteen minutes. Three short paragraphs. What happened, that you got no notice, what you want next — schedule and a notification method you actually read, email or paper. No novel about fuss. No threat you won't follow. No invented legal citations.

If you have a building manager separate from the owner, copy them. If you don't, it's just the landlord.

Today you survive the meeting. Then the short email. Then you live with paint smell until they finish, which I will not schedule for you. Ask the crew when they'll be done with your floor. Put that in the email too.

T-shirt, 7 a.m., no notice, corridor. That's enough story. The lease-lawyer in your head can sit down. The meeting cannot.
