### User
I already RSVP'd yes to Farah's wedding on October 12. It's out of town. I booked a bus. My cousin Keisha just set her ceremony for the same Saturday at 2 p.m. in our city and said "family comes first, Farah will understand." Farah will not understand. I was in Farah's apartment when she cried over the seating chart. Keisha is calling me "selfish" in a group text with aunts. I have twenty minutes before I have to answer the group. I don't want to be disowned in a thread. I also don't want to ghost a wedding I promised.

### Assistant
You keep the RSVP you already made unless you actually want to change it. You don't let a group text rewrite a bus ticket.

Answer Keisha first, privately if you can: "I already committed to Farah's wedding on the 12th. I can't do 2 p.m. in town. I'll celebrate with you another time if you'll have me — dinner, a call, the next gathering." If you mean the other time, name a type. If you don't, don't offer a fake brunch.

Don't debate "family comes first" as philosophy in a group of aunts. You'll lose on volume. Private, then a short group line if you must: "I already RSVP'd to a wedding that day. I'll be with Keisha another time. Love you all." Then you mute if it becomes a pile.

I won't invent whose wedding is "more important." I won't invent etiquette law. You promised Farah. Keisha set a date after. That's a timeline, not a personality.

Do you want to go to Farah's, or are you using me to talk yourself into Keisha because the aunts are loud?

### User
I want Farah's. I like Keisha. The aunts are loud. My mom hasn't posted in the thread yet. That's the silence that scares me. If Mom sides with Keisha I'm going to feel like I chose a friend over blood in public.

### Assistant
You already chose a calendar. That's allowed even if blood is in a group chat.

Text your mom outside the thread: "I'm going to Farah's as I RSVP'd. I'll tell Keisha I can't do 2 p.m. I don't want this to be a family trial. Can we not do it in the aunt chat?" If mom sides with Keisha, you still go to Farah's if that's the truth. Mom's siding is a feeling you'll have on the bus. It isn't a court.

If Keisha's 2 p.m. is a ceremony you could theoretically fly between, I won't design a fantasy itinerary. You have a bus. Out of town. You're not in two places. Don't offer to "come for the toast at 9" unless that's real. Fake appearances are how you miss both and still get called selfish.

If they want a villain, they can have the date conflict. You don't have to play the villain in extra paragraphs.

### User
Keisha just texted "if you loved me you'd be there." That's the sentence. I have the typing bubble.

### Assistant
Don't argue love. "I love you. I'll be at Farah's. I RSVP'd first. I want to see you another day." If she repeats, you can repeat once, then stop. You don't have to win "if you loved me." That's a trap with one legal answer, and the answer is her Saturday.

Group text: short, then mute. Don't write a treatise on friendship. Don't trash Keisha's planning. Don't ask Farah to "understand" a skip. Farah has a seating chart. You already said yes.

Next twenty minutes: private no to Keisha, optional note to mom, bus ticket stays. You can be a cousin on another weekend. You don't owe a double-booked heart. Get off the thread. The wedding you promised is still on October 12. That's the whole family-versus-friend problem, solved by the RSVP you already sent, even if the aunts don't clap.
