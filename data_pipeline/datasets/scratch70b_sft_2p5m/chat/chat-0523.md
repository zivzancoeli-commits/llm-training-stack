### User
I have a dentist appointment Thursday at 2 that I waited months to get. My dad wants me to drive him to a different office at 1:30 the same day because "you're already taking the afternoon" and his usual ride flaked. He said I can reschedule my teeth. I don't want to reschedule my teeth. I also don't want to be the child who wouldn't help. He needs an answer tonight so he can "make other arrangements," which I think means wait until I fold.

### Assistant
You already have an appointment. His flake is not a coupon for your slot.

Tonight, before the fold window: "I can't skip the dentist. I can help you find another ride, but I won't move my appointment." If you can do a different help — a taxi number, a neighbor, a sibling text — you offer that as a list, not as a maybe-I'll-cancel.

"You're already taking the afternoon" is him treating your calendar as a shared bucket. It isn't. A dentist at 2 does not create a 1:30 chauffeur.

I will not tell you his appointment is more medically important. I don't know either visit. I won't rank bodies. I know you waited months. That wait counts.

Don't offer to "see if the dentist can move you" unless you actually want to call. Seeing if is how you spend tonight on hold and then still drive.

Has he already told the other office you'll be there as the ride?

### User
He told them "my kid is bringing me." So there's a receptionist with my job title now. He also said he'd pay for my next cleaning if I do this, which is not the point, and I don't want you to guess prices.

### Assistant
I won't guess prices, and you shouldn't let a future cleaning buy this Thursday. Pay is how he turns your no into a rate.

You can correct the receptionist problem without a scene: he calls them back and says the ride changed, or you text him a script: "Please tell them you need a different ride. I have a dentist appointment I can't move." If he won't call, you still don't appear at 1:30. A receptionist expecting a kid is not a contract you signed.

If he uses the word selfish, you can say, "I'm keeping a medical appointment. Let's get you another driver." Then you help in ways that are not your chair: sibling, neighbor, transit, a service he already uses. I will not pick one as safest. He can choose among real options.

Don't stay on the phone until midnight "brainstorming" as a substitute for yes. Brainstorming is the fold, slow.

If you feel guilty, put the guilt in a box labeled "I am a person with teeth." Guilt will still talk. It doesn't get the keys at 1:30.

### User
What if his appointment is actually important and mine is "just teeth"?

### Assistant
Teeth are still a body. I won't rank. If his visit is urgent in a way that scares you, that's a reason for him to use urgent transport, not a reason you must burn a waitlist slot. You can ask him, "Is this time-sensitive in a way the office can move, or can they help with transport?" Then you listen. You still don't have to be the vehicle.

Tell him no tonight. Keep 2 p.m. Help him list other rides. Let the receptionist be updated by him. You can be a helpful adult who arrives at the dentist, not a backup car that cancels itself. Other arrangements should mean other arrangements, not a pause until you fold. If he waits until Wednesday to look, that's his delay. Your chair at 2 is not a spare key. Text the no. Then keep the dentist. The afternoon is already spoken for. Help with rides, not with your mouth in a different waiting room.
