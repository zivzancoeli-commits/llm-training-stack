### User
It's raining, the 22 is twenty-four minutes late according to the app, and I'm standing under a tiny awning that covers maybe my head. I told my roommate I'd be home by nine. It's 8:51. I don't have a spare for a rideshare and I don't know this stop well. I started this job last month and I keep feeling like I'm bad at being a person in a city. What do I actually do in the next half hour that isn't just spiraling?

### Assistant
You're not bad at being a person. You're wet and the bus is late. Those are specific, not a character flaw.

Text your roommate one line: "22 is late, closer to 9:30, I'm okay." Don't write an apology essay. They asked for a time; you're updating the time.

Then look at the stop itself. Is there a live countdown on a pole, or only the app? If the app and the pole disagree, believe the worse number for planning, not the hopeful one. Stay where the awning actually covers you. Stepping into the street to "see sooner" is how you get splashed and still miss the bus.

Do you have a second route from this same corner — another number that goes within a few blocks of home — or is the 22 the only thing that stops here?

### User
There's a 7 across the street that goes near the grocery by my place, but I'd still walk ten minutes in the rain after. The 22 drops me closer. I already texted. Roommate just sent "k". I feel dumb for even asking you. Intern brain. Like I should already know how late is too late.

### Assistant
"K" means they saw it. That's enough. You don't need a warmer reply to be allowed to wait.

Ten extra minutes of rain versus twenty-four more at this stop is a real trade. I can't see how hard the rain is from here, so I won't pick for you. If the 7 is *coming now* and the 22 is still a maybe, the 7 is the drier choice even with the walk, because a known ten-minute walk beats an unknown wait. If the 7 is also "incoming" with no bus in sight, stay. Crossing a wet street to chase a second ghost is how you miss both.

Too late isn't a moral line. It's "will I still get home before the last bus, and am I actually shivering." If you're already shaking, go inside a lobby or a 24-hour place within sight of the stop, if one exists. I don't know this block. Don't wander a dark side street looking for a café you remember from a map.

Keep your bag on the side away from the curb. Phone in a pocket, not an open palm in the rain. When a bus comes, check the number on the *side*, not just the headlights. Night rain makes every pair of lights look like yours.

### User
The 7 just rolled past the other side while I was reading this. Empty. Of course. App still says 22 in twelve minutes. I can see a laundromat light two doors down. Can I wait in there without buying something? I don't want to get yelled at my first month in this neighborhood.

### Assistant
I don't know that laundromat's rule. Some don't care if you stand by the door for ten minutes. Some want you to be a customer. I'm not going to invent their policy.

Kind and specific: walk in, drip on the mat, and ask one sentence. "The 22 is late, can I stand here until it comes if I stay by the door?" If they say no, thank them and go back to the awning. If they say yes, set a timer for ten minutes so you don't miss the bus because you got warm and stopped watching.

You're allowed to ask. Asking is not intern failure. Missing a bus because you were afraid to open a door is the worse night.

When you get on, sit, wring nothing onto another passenger, and send the roommate a second text only if the time slips again. One update is courtesy. Three is noise.

### User
Okay. Asking the laundromat. Timer. Side of the bus, not the headlights. I'll live.

### Assistant
You'll live. Twelve minutes is survivable. If the 22 vanishes from the app after you sit down, message me from inside and we'll pick the next concrete move, not a vibe.
