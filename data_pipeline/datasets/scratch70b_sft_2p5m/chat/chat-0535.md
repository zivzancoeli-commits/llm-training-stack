### User
I'm at a gym I've toured twice. I like the squat racks. The membership person slid a tablet over with a stylus. There's a "hold fee" if I freeze the membership later — travel, injury, whatever. They said "it's standard" and smiled. I have never asked a gym to explain a fee out loud because I didn't want to look cheap or difficult. I also don't want a surprise later. What do I say without turning this into a debate.

### Assistant
You say, "Before I sign, explain the hold fee like I'm new." Because you are new. Standard is not an explanation.

Ask for three facts, slowly: when a hold is allowed, how you request it, and what you pay during the hold versus a cancel. If they talk in "value" or "community," ask again for the number and the steps. I will not invent the fee. They have it. They can show it on the tablet or on paper.

Looking cheap is a story salespeople use. Asking what you will be charged is how adults sign things. Difficult would be yelling. This is a question.

Do not sign while they are still smiling through a fog. Stylus down. Tablet can wait. If they say the deal expires today, you can still leave. Urgency is a tool.

Have they already run a credit check or taken a card, or is this still just the tablet in front of you?

### User
Card is in my wallet. Nothing run yet. They said if I freeze, I pay a smaller amount to keep my rate. They would not say the smaller amount until I "get through the agreement." There is also a cancellation email they mentioned like it's a minor step. I don't travel much. I just don't like mystery fees.

### Assistant
Mystery fees are a reason to keep the card in the wallet.

"I need the hold amount and the cancel steps in writing before I sign." If they can only reveal it after the signature, that is your answer: you don't sign. A contract you can't read is not a membership. It's a vibe.

If they pull up a PDF, read the hold section. Read the cancel section. Ask them to show you the screen, not summarize. Summaries omit the ugly sentences. Look for how many days' notice, whether you must freeze for a minimum stretch, and whether "hold" still bills you. If any of that is oral only, write their words in a note on your phone while they watch. "You said X. I'm noting that." People get more careful when a note exists.

Cancellation as a "minor email" may still have a window, a form, or an in-person requirement. I will not invent which. Ask: "If I want out in 30 days, what exact steps, and what do I still owe?" Sit in the silence after that.

You don't travel. You still get to understand freeze because life happens. Injury, a move, a month you hate the place. Freeze is not only for frequent flyers.

### User
They offered to "just start me today" and waive the start-up thing if I stop asking questions. I felt rude. The racks are still nice.

### Assistant
Waiving a start-up fee to end questions is a tell. The racks will still be there tomorrow. A rushed signature will also still be there.

"I like the gym. I'm not signing until I understand hold and cancel." Rude would be wasting their time after you already understood and then ghosting. This is you doing the tour's actual job.

If they get cool with you, let them. You can walk. You can also sit and wait while they print the page. Either is competent.

Two options: sign after you have hold amount, hold rules, and cancel steps in front of you, or leave and compare with another gym using the same three questions. I would pick leave if they won't show the hold number. Nice racks do not require a mystery bill.

Stylus stays unused until the fee is boring and specific. Cheap is the wrong word for that. Careful is closer.
