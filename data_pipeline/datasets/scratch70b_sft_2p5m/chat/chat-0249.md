### User
It's 7:04 a.m. Saturday. A leaf blower is going in the driveway next door. I'm in a studio with one window on that side. I went to bed at 1 after a late shift. I have to work again at 4 p.m. I can see a guy in ear protection doing a dry patch of sidewalk. There are maybe twelve leaves. I don't want to start a neighbor war. I also don't want to cry into a pillow that is vibrating.

### Assistant
Ear protection on him means he cannot hear you tap the glass like a polite ghost. Don't start with the window. You'll look like a mime and you'll still have the noise.

I won't invent a local noise ordinance or a legal hour. Cities differ. I don't know yours. What you can do at 7:04 is reduce what hits your skull, then decide if you talk.

Close the window if it's open. Run a fan or the bathroom fan for a wall of lesser noise. Put in earplugs if you have them, or headphones with something boring. Lie in the room farthest from that wall even if that's the kitchen floor for twenty minutes. That's not a lifestyle. That's a Saturday after 1 a.m.

Is this a one-off crew, or the same person every weekend?

### User
Same person. He did it last Saturday at 7:20. I thought it was a fluke. There's a HOA email I never opened from March. I don't know if I'm even in an HOA. The twelve leaves are in a little whirl. My shift yesterday was until 11:40. I still smell fryer oil in my hair.

### Assistant
Same person twice is a pattern, not a fluke. You still don't have to open a war. You can open a conversation after the blower stops, not during, unless you like shouting at ear muffs.

For the next ten minutes: window shut, fan on, earplugs or a pillow over the ear that faces the driveway, phone face down so you don't rage-type. Sleep in whatever ugly position works. If you cannot sleep, you still rest with eyes closed. 4 p.m. work needs a nervous system, not a perfect REM cycle.

When it stops, if you have the energy, a doorstep sentence later this morning: "Hey, the blower at 7 is rough because I work late. Could you start after 9?" That's a request with a time. Not a lecture about twelve leaves. Count the leaves in your head, not out loud. People defend their whirl.

If you don't want to talk, a note on the door with the same sentence works. I won't tell you an HOA will save you. Unopened March email is not a strategy. You can look at it after coffee, not at 7:04.

### User
If I ask him he'll say grass doesn't wait. Or he'll be nice and do it at 6:45 next week out of spite. I have to live next to the driveway.

### Assistant
He might say grass doesn't wait. Sidewalk leaves can wait. You don't have to win the botany. You ask for 9. If he says no, you know. Then you keep the fan and the earplugs as the Saturday kit, and you look at whatever building rules actually exist in daylight. I won't pretend those rules will be on your side.

Spite at 6:45 is a story your brain likes at 7:04. Possible. Not certain. Don't skip a calm ask because of a fictional earlier blower. If he does get earlier, that's new information and you can stop asking him and go through a manager or a landlord if you have one. One step at a time.

Wash the fryer out of your hair after you get a scrap of sleep, not now in a noisy shower. Water plus blower is just two noises.

You don't need a war. You need a closed window, a later start if he'll give it, and a 4 p.m. shift that isn't built on zero sleep. Let the twelve leaves finish their tour. Then talk, or note, and protect the rest of the morning like it matters. It does.
