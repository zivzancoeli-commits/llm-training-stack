### User
I came home at 10:40. The door was unlocked. Again. Keys in the dish, door not locked. We live on the first floor off an alley. I've asked twice in a vague way. Theo says "this block is fine" and that locks are "a vibe." I found the door open a crack last Thursday. Nothing missing that I can tell. I don't want a lecture about crime stats. I want the deadbolt on when I go to bed. They're on the couch watching a show with the volume up. Script.

### Assistant
Specific, not vague. You've already done vague.

"I need the deadbolt on whenever nobody is in the doorway. First floor, alley. I'm not debating whether the block is fine. Please lock it when you come in. I'll lock it when I come in." If the crack-open Thursday is true, add that one date. One story, not a memoir.

Don't open with crime stats. I won't invent them. You don't need a chart to want a bolt. "Vibe" isn't a lock.

If they say they forget, that's a systems problem: a sticky note at eye level, a habit of trying the knob after it closes. You can offer the note. You can't be their memory forever.

Are you both on the lease, and is there a second lock you actually use, or only the one they skip?

### User
Both on the lease. There's a chain we never use because it scrapes. Deadbolt is the one. They said if I want it locked I should just lock it, which I do, and then they unlock it for a vape trip to the stoop and don't put it back. They're pausing the show. I think they know this is the talk.

### Assistant
Good. Don't wait for the show to be the clock.

"When you go to the stoop, take keys and lock behind you, or stay in the open door. Don't leave it cracked. I'm going to start checking the bolt before I sleep. If I find it open, I'll wake you." That's a consequence that isn't theatrical. Waking them is annoying. So is an alley door.

If they want to vape, they can hold the door with their body or they can lock. Those are the two adult options. You don't need to police nicotine. You need the door to be a door.

"You should just lock it" is them assigning you the job of following their vape trips. You can refuse the job: "I can't follow you to the stoop. You lock when you come back."

If they get sarcastic about "vibe," you don't match it. Repeat the bolt. Then you go to your room or you sit down. The show can unpause.

### User
They asked if I "don't trust the neighbors." I don't want to insult the building. I also don't trust an unlocked first-floor door. How do I not get dragged into a community speech?

### Assistant
"It's not about the neighbors. It's the alley and the crack last Thursday. I want the bolt on." Then you stop. Community speeches are how this becomes a referendum on whether you're nice. You can like the neighbors and still lock.

Before bed: you check the bolt. That's your ten seconds. If it's open, you lock it and you say so tomorrow, or you wake them if that's the line you set and you mean it. Don't set a line you won't do.

If they go back to the stoop tonight, watch whether the bolt returns. If it doesn't, you lock it and you say so, or you wake them if that was the line. Don't invent a third talk. The first talk is this one.

Next five minutes: the paragraph, then their show. You don't need stats. You need metal in the frame. Get the deadbolt. That's the whole first-floor conversation, without a vibe. A chain that scrapes can wait for a weekend of WD-40. The bolt can happen before the credits. Keys in the dish are fine. An open door is not. Close it like you mean the alley exists. Then you can watch the rest of the episode without counting cars.
