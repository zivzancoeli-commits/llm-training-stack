### User
I rode two buses in slush because my workplace's "we'll announce by 5:30 a.m." message arrived at 7:12 saying closed. I was already at the office door. A security guy shrugged. I am wet, tired, and I used a sick-adjacent mental day I was saving. I want to know what to send my manager that isn't a tantrum and isn't "no worries!!" I also have to get home in the same slush.

### Assistant
You don't have to be cheerful about a late call. You also don't have to write a manifesto on the sidewalk.

Get inside or into a lobby if you can, dry your hands, and send a short message: "I commuted in already. I saw the 7:12 closure. I'm heading home. Please confirm how you want me to code today." That's status plus a question. The question stops you from guessing PTO, sick, or "weather day" — I will not invent your policy. Your handbook or your manager's actual reply is the policy.

Don't stand at the locked door performing loyalty. The building is closed. Go home while the buses still exist. Take the route that is less heroic. Heroic slush is how people fall.

I will not tell you the company owes you a taxi. I don't know your benefits. If they offer a ride benefit, use the real one. If they don't, you use the bus you already know.

Are you salaried with a manager who answers Slack, or is this a punch-clock place with a hotline?

### User
Slack manager, usually decent. The closure came from a facilities account, not from her. People in the channel are joking about "who didn't check." I checked at 5:28. It said normal operations. I feel gaslit by a timestamp.

### Assistant
You are not gaslit by your own screenshot if you have one. If you still have the 5:28 "normal operations," keep it. You don't need to drop it in the joke thread. You can attach it to the manager message if you want the record: "This is what the status said before I left." That's a fact, not a tantrum. Jokers in a channel are doing morale. You don't have to play.

Don't argue with facilities in public. One DM to the manager is enough. If she says take a weather day, you take the label they use. If she says take PTO, you can ask, once, "Is there a closure code? I followed the 5:30 guidance." Then you accept the answer you get today so you can leave the doorway. A policy fight can happen by email when you are dry. A policy fight in wet socks is how you say something you have to undo.

Going home: tell her you're leaving now, not after you "see if anyone else shows up." Closed means closed. You are not the test employee.

If a coworker who lives close is already home dunking on commuters, mute. Their geography is not a personality.

### User
I want to ask them to never do a 7:12 again. Is that a tantrum?

### Assistant
A request for a reliable cutoff is not a tantrum. Put it in a second sentence after you're home, or in the same message if you can stay dry: "A 7:12 close after a 5:30 promise means people have already traveled. Can we treat the early flag as the real one, or send a delay 'pending' so we don't commute?" That's process. All-caps and sarcasm are tantrum. You don't need those.

Right now: message, screenshot if you have it, bus home, dry clothes. Code the day how they tell you. You followed their clock. The clock moved after you did. That's enough story. You don't owe "no worries." You owe a ride home and a calm request that 5:30 mean 5:30. Don't stand in the lobby proving you would have worked. The office is closed. Go be dry.
