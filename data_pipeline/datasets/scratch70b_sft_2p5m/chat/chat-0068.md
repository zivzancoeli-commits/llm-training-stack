### User
We are in the cereal aisle. Not yelling. That almost makes it worse. My partner is holding the expensive granola like it is a moral document and I said we already have oats at home. They said I always do this, kill the little treats, make everything about the budget. I said they always do this, wait until we are in public so I have to whisper. Now we are both staring at a shelf of honey nut whatever, speaking in that tight supermarket voice. A guy with a cart is pretending to read a box eight feet away. I need to not win. I also need to not fake a smile and buy the granola just to make the aisle go quiet.

### Assistant
Do not smile at the granola. Do not give a TED talk about household finance between the Cheerios and the shredded wheat. You are both tired and you picked a fluorescent hallway to have a real fight. That is ordinary. It is also a bad venue.

De-escalate means you lower the stakes of the next thirty seconds, not that you pretend you are fine.

First, stop the "you always" loop. You both already launched one. Those sentences are not about oats. They are about a pile of older evenings. The aisle cannot hold that pile. Say one true, small thing: "This is bigger than cereal and I do not want to do it next to the boxes." That is not cheerful. It is accurate.

Then pick a next action that is not a verdict on the relationship. Two that work: put the granola in the cart without a speech, or put it back without a speech, and move to the next aisle. The content of the choice matters less than ending the performance for the guy eight feet away. If you cannot choose, say "I am too mad to pick this well. Let's skip this row and come back." Walking is not surrender. Standing still is how whispers get sharper.

Do not apologize for having a budget unless you actually jumped down their throat. Do not demand they apologize for wanting a treat while you are still in the store. Both of those are courtroom moves. You are buying food.

If they keep going, repeat the venue problem once: "I hear you. Not here." Then be quiet. Filling the silence with a joke or a soft voice that does not match your face will read as fake, and they will push harder. Flat and brief is kinder than bright and false.

### User
They just muttered "fine, get the cheap stuff, I don't even want it now." That is not actually fine. If I grab the oats I look like the villain in their story. If I grab the granola I am training the public-fight tactic. There is a third thing I am missing because my brain is buzzing.

### Assistant
The third thing is: do not resolve the snack. Resolve the exit.

"I am not picking a winner in this aisle. I am going to get milk. We can talk about treats in the car, or tonight, when nobody is shopping around us." Then you actually go to milk. You can leave the granola where it is. You can even leave the cart for a minute if you need air by the freezer doors. You are not obligated to complete the morality play.

If they follow and keep the mutter going, you still do not need a counter-mutter. "I don't want to do this in the store" is allowed to be the last line. You can finish the list you came for or you can check out with what you have. A half-finished shop is better than a finished argument with an audience.

Later, when you are not under the overhead lights, the real topics are: treats as a category, and using public as a lever. Those are two separate talks. Tonight you only need one of them if you have the energy. If you do not, "I was harsh in the cereal aisle. I still didn't like doing it in public. Can we pick a time." That is not a grin. It is a clean floor.

Skip the performance. Get milk. Let the granola stay on the shelf like it is just food, which is all it ever was.
