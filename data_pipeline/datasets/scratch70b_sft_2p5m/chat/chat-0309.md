### User
I asked Riley twice, once in person, not to tag me in photos. I like being at the thing. I do not like my face going to their 800 followers, some of whom are people from an old job. Today they tagged me again in a story and a post. I untagged myself. They replied with a laughing emoji and "you look cute tho." I am tired of being in a consent loop.

### Assistant
Twice plus a laugh emoji is not a misunderstanding. It is them deciding their post matters more than your ask. Cute is not a rebuttal.

You can untag, you can restrict, you can leave the photo, and you can also make the friendship smaller. You do not have to pick all of them today. You do have to stop treating this as a settings tutorial they have not read. They have read it with their thumbs.

A third ask can be written, short, and final in tone: "I need you not to tag me, including stories. If a photo is posted with me in it, don't name me. If it happens again I'll mute tags / I'll sit out photos. I'm not debating cute." Then you follow through once.

I will not tell you what the app's legal privacy is. Untagging is a tool. So is not standing next to them when the camera comes out.

Do they tag only nightlife, or also ordinary lunches, and is the old-job audience the main risk?

### User
Mostly nights. Old job is the main risk. There is one person I do not want seeing my weekends. Riley knows that in theory. In practice they post in the Uber.

### Assistant
They post in the Uber because the night is still on and your boundary is offscreen. That is the moment you need a pre-agreed physical move: you step out of the frame, or you take the phone and delete the tag before it goes up, or you do not get in the picture. Asking after the story is always late.

If the one person is a safety issue, say that plainly if you have not: "This is not about vanity. There is someone I do not want having my weekends." You do not have to tell Riley the whole history. If it is not safety, only professional discomfort, that is still enough to refuse tags. You do not need a dramatic reason.

Mute their stories if you must, but muting does not stop their followers from seeing you. Untag plus a follow-through on sitting out photos does.

If they call you controlling, they mean they liked using you as content. You can let that word sit without defending your personality for twenty minutes.

### User
If I sit out photos I look like the weird friend in the group shots. I already feel like the difficult one.

### Assistant
Group shots can include a person who stands one foot to the left. Weird is a story they tell. Your old job seeing a Saturday is also a story, with consequences you already named.

For the next ten minutes: send the short final message, untag anything live, turn off tag-suggest if the app has that, and decide one follow-through you will actually do the next time a camera appears — step out, or leave the story. Do not write a thread. Do not explain cute.

You asked twice. The emoji was the answer. Believe it, set the consequence, and let looking slightly weird in a photo be cheaper than another Monday of who saw what. Cute does not get a vote.

### User
There's a thing this weekend. They will have the camera out at dinner. I already said I would go.

### Assistant
Go if you want the people. Decide the photo rule before you sit: you will step one foot left, or you will say "no tag, and crop me if you post." Say it once at the table when the phone comes up, not in a speech on the sidewalk. If they post anyway, untag, then skip the next camera night. That is the follow-through you already wrote. You can keep the dinner and lose the content. Those are different tickets.
