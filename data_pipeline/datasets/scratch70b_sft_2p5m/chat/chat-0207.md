### User
I need to tell my roommate I got a parking ticket in her car. I borrowed it Saturday for Costco because mine is in the shop. I thought the street was free after six. The ticket is on the kitchen table because I panicked and left it there like a dead mouse. I almost told her it was "probably not enforceable" because I read two comments on a city forum. I know that is bluffing. She is going to ask what we do next and I do not actually know.

### Assistant
Drop the forum comments. You do not know if it is enforceable. I do not know either. Cities differ, signs differ, and a stranger's story is not your ticket. Say: I got a ticket in your car. I thought the block was free after six. I might be wrong. I have not paid it and I have not fought it because I do not know which is right yet.

Lead with the object. Pick up the ticket. Read the date, the location, the violation line, and the due date together. If a line is blurry, say I cannot read this, not I think it means X.

The next step is not a legal opinion. It is: look up the city page printed on the ticket, or call the number on it, with her listening if she wants. Do not offer to "take care of it" as a vibe. Offer money you have, or a plan to get the number. I will not invent the fine. Read it off the paper.

Was her name on the registration, and did you tell her you were taking the car?

### User
She said I could take it. Verbal, not a text. Registration is in her name. I have eighty dollars until Friday. The ticket says seventy-five if paid by the date, and then a bigger number I cannot tell if that is a late fee or a different violation. I was going to pay tonight so she does not worry, but I am not sure paying closes it if the car is in her name.

### Assistant
Good catch. I do not know whether paying under your card clears it when the car is hers. Some cities attach tickets to the plate, some want the registered owner, some accept anyone's payment. I am not going to pretend I looked up your city. That is the sentence she deserves: I do not know if my paying tonight actually clears it on your record. I need to check the back of the ticket or the website listed.

Seventy-five by the date is a number on the paper, not a rumor. The bigger number stays unlabeled until you know. Say I see a second number and I am not sure what it is. Want to look it up with me.

Eighty dollars covers seventy-five if paying is the right move. Say Friday if anything extra shows up. Do not hide that the account is thin. She can handle a date. She cannot handle a surprise decline.

You had permission. Say that once. Do not build a kitchen trial. If she gets sharp, do not escalate into I read that tickets like this get dismissed. That is the bluff again. Stay at: I messed up the sign. I do not know the process. I will pay what is owed if that is how this works.

### User
What if she says I cannot borrow the car again. That is fair and I still need Costco because we split the bulk stuff. And what do I say if she asks why I left the ticket on the table instead of texting Saturday night.

### Assistant
If she pulls the car, do not argue. Say okay. Costco is a separate question: bus, your car when it is out of the shop, or her driving and you paying gas. I will not invent bus times. Ask the bulk-run plan after the ticket talk, not inside it. Mixing "can I still have the Honda" into the apology makes it a negotiation.

Why the table: because you panicked. I froze and put it where you would see it instead of texting you Saturday. I should have sent a photo that night. One line. Then the plan: photo of both sides in the thread now, lookup together, no fake legal theory.

Adult sentence: I borrowed your car, I parked wrong, there is a ticket, I do not know the payment rules because the car is in your name, I have enough for the seventy-five if that is the real amount, and I will not guess the rest. Then you look it up. You were close. Cut the part where you sound sure.
