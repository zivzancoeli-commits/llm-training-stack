### User
We're on the back deck. Uncle Craig told a joke about a convenience store clerk. He used an accent. My cousin laughed because that's the family metronome. I didn't. He looked at me and said "relax, it's a joke." This is the first time I'm going to say the thing instead of checking my phone. There are kids at the other end of the deck. I don't want a blowup next to the burgers. I also don't want to nod. He's still looking at me. I have maybe twenty seconds of air.

### Assistant
Use the twenty seconds on one sentence, not a seminar.

"I don't think that joke is funny. Please don't do the accent." That's enough. If you can add "especially with kids here," do. If you can't, the first two clauses stand.

Don't smile to soften it. Don't explain the history of the bit. Don't say "as a [your identity]" unless you want that to be the topic. You can be a person who heard a joke and didn't like it.

If he says "relax," you don't relax on command. "I'm not going to laugh at that." Then you look at the grill, or you go inside for a plate. You don't have to win a definition of racism on a deck. You named the accent and the no. That's the first time. It can be small and still be real.

I won't write a legal definition. I won't tell you he's a lost cause or a secret good person. I don't know him. You need a line and an exit, not a diagnosis of his soul.

Are you able to leave the deck, or are you stuck in the chair until dessert?

### User
I can go inside. He already said "your generation" to the cousin. The kids didn't hear the words, I don't think. My mom is giving me the look that means keep the peace. I can feel last year's phone-check in my hands.

### Assistant
Put the phone down. Go inside for ice or napkins, something true. On the way, if he's still performing, you don't have to do a second verse. You already said it. Repeating five times is how a blowup starts. One clear no, then you remove the audience he wanted — you.

If your mom intercepts you at the fridge with "don't start," you can say, "I said I didn't like the joke. I'm not doing a fight. I'm getting ice." That's keeping the peace and also not nodding. If she wants peace to mean you laugh, she can want that. You don't have to supply the laugh.

If Uncle Craig follows you to explain why it wasn't racist, you don't have to take the lecture. "I heard you. I still don't want the accent. I'm not debating it at a cookout." Then you return to a different cluster of people or you stay in the kitchen with the cousin who didn't laugh. Find the other quiet body. You're not obligated to convert the deck.

The kids: if they didn't hear it, you don't need to hold a press conference. If they did, that's a later, calmer line from a parent, not you giving a TED talk over burgers. You're the person who didn't fake it. That's your job tonight.

### User
He just yelled from the deck, "Come on, it was a compliment to how they talk." I have to walk back out for my drink. What do I do with my face?

### Assistant
Neutral. Don't sneer. Don't smile. Get the drink. If you answer at all: "It didn't sound like a compliment. I'm done talking about it." Then you sit somewhere that isn't his radius, or you stay standing so you can leave again.

If the table goes silent, let it. Silence is not a fire. Filling it with a new joke is how you take care of him again.

You don't have to be the family's diversity officer. You have to not rent your face to a bit you hate. First time doesn't have to be perfect. It has to be true and short. Get the drink, don't debate "compliment," sit with someone else, eat. The cookout can survive a man being told no. If it can't, that isn't on your twenty-second sentence.
