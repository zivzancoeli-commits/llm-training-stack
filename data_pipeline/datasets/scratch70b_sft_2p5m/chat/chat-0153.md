### User
Our group chat exploded. People are leaving, someone made a second chat without two of us, and I'm the one everyone DMs for "what do I do." I think I know, but I also might be about to give advice for the wrong problem. I want you to stop me if I'm guessing.

### Assistant
I need one thing before advice.

What do you actually want out of this — to stay in a group with everyone, to leave quietly, or to fix the exclusion of the two people?

### User
I want the two people not left out. I don't even care about the original chat that much. The new one is where the weekend plans are happening. I wasn't invited either, I found out from a screenshot. I almost told them to just add everyone and be adults, but I don't know if this is a fight about a person or about a joke that landed wrong.

### Assistant
Thanks. That's the clarifying piece: your goal is inclusion for the two, and you also got dropped. I won't pretend I know whether this started as a joke, a crush, a political argument, or someone being tired. If you don't know, don't advise as if you do.

Don't send "be adults." That's a grade. It makes people defend.

Do send, to whoever made the new chat — one person, not a broadcast lecture — something closer to: "I saw there's a new thread for the weekend. Can you add [names], and me if I'm supposed to be there? If this is a smaller group on purpose, tell me that straight." You're asking for the rule. Rules are easier than morals.

If they say it was an accident: great. Adds happen. You don't need a tribunal.

If they say those two are the problem: that's information. Then you choose, still without a speech in the old chat: you can decline the weekend, you can go anyway, you can talk to the two people privately so they're not finding out from another screenshot. I won't tell you which friendship is worth more. I don't live in your group.

What you should not do: paste the screenshot into the old chat as evidence. That lights a second explosion and uses someone's leak as a weapon.

### User
One of the excluded people asked me to "say something." The other one hasn't said a word. I almost have it, I think I should only speak for myself.

### Assistant
Yes. Speak for yourself. "Say something" can mean they want a lawyer. You can say, "I'll ask to be added and I'll mention I want X added too, because I don't want a weekend split like this. I'm not going to speak as your spokesperson in the new chat." That's almost-have-it becoming have-it.

If you mention the names, you're not pretending you weren't asked. You're still not performing a closing argument.

Muted old chat: you can mute. Muting is not a betrayal. It's volume. You can read later.

If people DM you for a recap, one version of the truth: "There's a smaller chat for plans. I asked to include everyone. I don't know why it started." Don't fill the I-don't-know with a theory about who hates who. Theories travel faster than adds.

If the maker of the new chat ignores you: you tried. Then you tell the excluded person what you did, not what you wish you'd thundered. "I asked. They haven't answered." That's complete.

Weekend: if the plans stay exclusive, you don't have to go as a spy. Going as a spy is a messy job you didn't apply for. Staying home is allowed. Going and being kind to whoever is there is also allowed. Pick based on your actual Saturday, not on proving a point.

### User
Ask the maker, include the names, speak for me, no screenshot dump, no "be adults," no fake theory about why.

### Assistant
That's the advice that matches the goal you named. You almost had it; the trap was diagnosing a fight you haven't seen. Ask for the add. Let their answer be the fact.
