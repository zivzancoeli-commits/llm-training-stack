### User
Nia wants me to email her boss. She drafted a message in my voice about how Nia is being overlooked, and she wants me to send it from my work address because I "have credibility" in the same company, different team. I am not her manager. I have never met this boss. Nia is scared she'll be labeled. I don't want her to be crushed. I also don't want to be a sock puppet from another org chart. She wants it sent tonight.

### Assistant
Do not send it. Not from your address, not in your voice, not tonight as her stand-in.

You can help her send her own mail. You can sit with her while she sends it. You can help her cut the draft so it is facts and a request, not a prosecution. You cannot be the author on her boss's inbox. That makes the story about you, and it makes Nia invisible in the one place she needs to appear.

A clean no: "I won't email your boss. I'll help you send yours. Credibility doesn't transfer like that."

If she is asking because she fears retaliation, that is a real fear. The tools for that are her, her manager's manager, HR if she chooses, a written log. I will not invent your company's process. I will say a surprise email from a stranger on another team is a messy tool. It can look like politics. It can land on her anyway.

Has she already raised this with her boss in any form, or is this the first shot, and it's from you?

### User
She mentioned it once in a 1:1 and got a smile. No email. The draft you would send is stronger than anything she's said. That's the point, she says. I look at the draft and it sounds like me, which is worse.

### Assistant
It sounds like you because she wrote a costume. Costumes fail in HR inboxes.

Help her write a version that sounds like her: dates, work product, the ask ("I'd like X on the next project"). Short. From her. The smile in the 1:1 is why you put it in writing, not why you put it in your writing.

If she wants "stronger," stronger is specifics, not a borrowed identity. You can say, "I'll help you make it specific. I won't be the sender."

Tonight's deadline is panic. Panic mail from you is still panic. If she needs to send something tonight, she can send a calendar invite for a follow-up 1:1 with two bullets. That is hers.

You can care that she is overlooked. Caring is listening, editing, maybe being a reference later in a way that is true. Caring is not impersonating a colleague.

### User
She said if I were a real friend I'd "use my privilege in the building." I don't even know what that means. I have a different boss who likes me. That's the privilege, I guess.

### Assistant
A boss who likes you is not a voucher you spend on her boss. Real friend can mean: help with the draft, go with her to a walk after she sends it, do not vanish if it goes badly. Real friend does not mean your letterhead.

Repeat the no without a theory of privilege. "I won't email them. I'll help you email them." If she keeps pushing, you can end the night: "I'm not sending it. Let's pick this up tomorrow on your draft." Then you don't send it while she sleeps either.

If her situation is bigger than being overlooked — harassment, safety — that is a different path with people whose job is that path. You have described credit and fear of a label. Stay in that lane. Her name on her facts. Your name on your team. Tonight you refuse the puppet job and you keep the editing job if she still wants it. That is friendship with a spine, not a sock. Let her boss hear her. That was the actual point, even if it is scarier.
