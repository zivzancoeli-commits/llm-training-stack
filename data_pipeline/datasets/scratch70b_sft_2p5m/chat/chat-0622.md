### User
Uncle wants a "family history packet" by Sunday for a reunion booklet. He means I should write everyone, scan the shoebox, caption the war photo, and make it look designed. I have a job. I am not the historian. I once made a family group chat and now I am Archives. I don't want to dummy a museum. How do I shrink this without a fight about who cares more?

### Assistant
A reunion booklet is not a dissertation. Sunday is not a grant. Shrink it to what you will actually do, then say the number.

Say: "I can do two pages by Sunday: a photo we already have digital and a paragraph. I can't scan the shoebox or interview everyone. If you need more, someone else owns that." Two pages is a product. Museum is a role. Decline the role.

I will not invent what the reunion "must" include. I will not invent a printer deadline at the hotel. If he has a real print cutoff, he can say the time. You still do not have to become a staff.

Do not start DMing cousins "quick question about 1952" tonight. That is how Archives eats your week. If you already have three photos on your phone, those are the packet. Done is a PDF. Perfect is a shoebox.

Has he already told the reunion you are producing it, or is this still an ask?

### User
He put "cousin on design" in an email to twelve people. I was cc'd. There is a shoebox at Mom's. Mom said I can come Saturday. I work Saturday morning. I care about the war photo and I also know I'll get trapped in the garage for six hours.

### Assistant
Reply-all, short: "I can contribute a two-page PDF by Sunday night. I can't take design for the whole booklet or the shoebox this weekend. If someone is near the box, send 3 photos." You are correcting the minutes. If you only email Uncle, the twelve still think you are production.

Saturday morning work stays. Saturday afternoon at Mom's is a maybe with a cap: "I'll come 2 to 4, one box, I leave at 4." A cap is how you do not live in the garage. Set an alarm. When it rings, you leave with whatever you scanned. Partial is a packet. Six hours is a personality.

The war photo: if you know which envelope, go to that envelope first. One caption with the facts you actually know, not a documentary. I will not invent the unit or the year. If you do not know, "unlabeled, family collection" is honest. Do not guess a battle to sound like Archives.

### User
If I do two pages they'll look thin next to Aunt's lasagna poem and I'll look like I phoned it in. That's the trap.

### Assistant
Lasagna poem is a different genre. Thin and true beats a fake archive. You can say in the PDF footer, "More photos live in the shoebox; this is a sample." That sentence manages expectation without a six-hour garage. If they wanted a book, they needed a committee in March. They wanted you in a cc on Thursday.

If Uncle pouts, he can cut lasagna poems or find another cousin with a scanner and no Saturday shift. You already offered two pages and a two-hour cap. That is care. Care is not unlimited labor with a design title you did not vote for.

Do the reply-all tonight. Put 2 to 4 on your calendar or do not go. Phone photos if the box loses. Sunday PDF, two pages, one honest caption. You can care about the war picture and still clock out at 4. Historian is a job. You have one. This is a booklet. Keep it a booklet. Let the shoebox remain a box. You are not a museum. You are a cousin with a deadline who refused to dummy a staff. Send the correction. Then scan what fits in two hours, not what fits in a fantasy.
