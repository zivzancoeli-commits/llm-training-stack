### User
I'm at the station. Wallet is gone. Last sure moment was buying a water at the kiosk on the mezzanine. Train I wanted leaves in twelve minutes. A guy in a vest that may or may not be official asked if I need "help looking" and then asked if I want to "unlock my phone banking so we can freeze things together." My uncle-brain is already saying this is a hustle. I also need a way home. People keep offering to pay my fare if I "just tell them my full name and date of birth." I want a plan that includes a real no, not a fake meeting I have to get to.

### Assistant
No. You do not unlock banking with a stranger in a station. You do not hand over full name and date of birth for a fare.

I will not invent a cute excuse. You are allowed to say no because the ask is wrong.

To the vest guy: "No. I won't open my bank on my phone with you. I'll handle my cards myself." Then step toward light, cameras, a staffed booth, or a uniform you can verify at a window — not deeper onto a quiet platform with a helper you just met. A vest is a piece of clothing. It is not automatically a job.

To the fare-saints collecting your biography: "No thanks. I don't need to give you that." If they persist, repeat no and walk. You do not owe them a story about a dentist appointment. No is the whole sentence.

Twelve minutes: that train may be gone. Home is still the job. Wallet is a separate job. Do not glue them into one panic.

If you have your phone, open your own bank app by yourself, where nobody is coaching you. Freeze or lock cards through the official app or a number you type yourself — not a number a stranger recites. I will not pretend I know your bank's menus. Do not let anyone "help you type."

Ticket home: transit card in the phone, backup card, or official staff at a marked window. Staff procedures vary. I will not fake a policy. Ask them. Do not follow a civilian to an ATM.

### User
The vest guy looked hurt. He said he was only trying to be decent. There's a cop near the ticket machines. My ride home is a connecting bus that I will miss if I miss this train. I keep thinking I should have given him the benefit of the doubt.

### Assistant
Hurt feelings are not a credential. Decent people do not need your banking unlocked in a crowd. You can believe he meant well and still refuse the method. Those stack.

Go to the cop or to a marked staff person, not back to the vest. One sentence: "I lost my wallet near the kiosk. Someone asked me to open my bank app with them. I said no. I need to know where lost and found is and how I can still get home." Let the official person talk. You do not need to prosecute the vest guy unless you want to describe him. Description is optional. Getting home is not.

If the train is leaving, miss it rather than sprint after a stranger's plan. I will not invent the timetable. Look at the board. Call a person whose number is in your phone. "Wallet's gone, I'm at [station name], I may need a ride." Payback is later. Identity theft is now.

Say no without a subplot. "No." "I'm going to staff." You are not being rude. Skeptical is correct when the ask is your phone, your name, and your date of birth.

After you are moving toward staff, list what was in the wallet — cards, ID, cash if you remember.

Benefit of the doubt is for someone who points at a booth. It is not for shared login. Keep the no plain. Get a real ticket or a real ride from people you can name. The wallet might show up tomorrow. Your accounts should still be yours tonight.
