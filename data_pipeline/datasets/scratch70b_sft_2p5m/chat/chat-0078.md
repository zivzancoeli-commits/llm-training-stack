### User
I have to be out of this place in three weeks. New spot is across town, not across the country. I keep opening a notes app, writing "boxes," and then closing it because the whole thing feels like it is already failing. Friends keep saying "it will be fun!" which makes me want to throw a lamp. I do not have a moving company budget. I have a compact car, a few friends who said "maybe," and a lot of stuff I do not even like. I need a plan that does not pretend this is a montage.

### Assistant
Yeah. It is not going to be fun. It is going to be a sequence of ugly, doable hours. You can survive that. You do not have to enjoy it.

Three weeks is tight, not impossible, for a same-city move if you stop treating every object as a decision about your identity. The panic in the notes app is usually "I have to do all of it at once." You do not.

This week, not later: walk the place with a trash bag and a donate pile. If you have not used it in this apartment and you do not have a specific use in the next one, it does not get a box. Kitchen junk drawers are where moves go to die. Be mean to the junk drawer.

I am not going to quote you a price for a van. I do not know what a rental costs this month in your city. Look it up once, tonight, for a cargo van for one day near your move date. If that number is possible, that is your vehicle. If it is not, you are doing multiple car runs. Same-city moves can be three ugly trips instead of one cinematic load.

Friends who said "maybe" are not a crew. Text them a specific window: "Saturday the 12th, 10 to 2, pizza after, I need the van loaded, not decorating advice." "Maybe" evaporates when you give it a clock. Do not pad it with "only if you want." That is how you end up alone with a dresser at 9 p.m.

Pack in rooms, not in vibes. Leave a "survive" kit out: sheets, a pan, chargers, toiletries, one change of clothes, meds if you have them. Label boxes with the destination room in fat marker. Future you is going to be tired.

Heavy stuff wrecks people. Measure the new door. A couch that does not fit is not a later problem. Ask the new building about elevator reservations this week, not on moving morning.

Money: spend on things that prevent injury or a second rental day. A cheap dolly, enough boxes. Do not buy a matching set of totes because a video made it look calm. Cardboard is allowed.

Sleep. A move on three hours of sleep is how people break a wrist on a stair. Set a stop time. The stuff will still be there in the morning, and you will still hate it, and that is fine.

When someone says it will be fun, you do not have to perform gratitude. A flat "it's just a job I have to finish" is enough.

If the "maybe" friends flake, the fallback is: van or car, one helper if you can, and a smaller pile. The pile is the only variable you fully control. Shrink it until the math of the car works. Not inspiration. Arithmetic plus a calendar.

You last year would have tried to keep every mug and then cried in the kitchen at midnight. This year you can throw the extra mugs in the donate bag and leave on time. That is the win. An apartment you actually arrive at.

### User
I keep thinking if I wait until next weekend I'll magically have more energy.

### Assistant
You will not. Next weekend you will have the same body and fewer days. Do one ugly hour tonight: trash bag, junk drawer, donate pile by the door. Then stop. The feeling that the whole move is already failing is just the size of the pile talking. Make the pile smaller. The mood can stay bad. The boxes can still get packed.
