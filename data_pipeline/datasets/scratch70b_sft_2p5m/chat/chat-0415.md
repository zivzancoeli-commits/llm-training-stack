### User
My mother wants Thanksgiving at my place because I "have the city apartment" and everyone is tired of her house. I have a studio. A table that folds. Four plates. She is imagining nine people, a turkey, and a football game on a TV I do not own. I said I'd think about it. She is already telling cousins it's at mine. I need to stop this before I am shopping for folding chairs I cannot store.

### Assistant
A studio is not a banquet hall. You can love your mother and still refuse to host nine.

Call her today, not a group text first. "I can't host Thanksgiving here. It's a studio. We need a different plan." If she has already told cousins, she can untell them. That is her cleanup. You can offer to help find a park picnic, a restaurant, or her house again. You do not have to offer your floor.

Do not "think about it" any longer. Thinking was heard as a soft yes. Soft yes plus cousins is how chairs appear.

I will not invent occupancy limits or fire codes. Nine bodies and a turkey oven in a studio is already a logistics no.

Is she trying to dump the cooking on you, or does she still plan to bring the food?

### User
She would cook the turkey at her house and "just finish" at mine. Finish means gravy, sides, and people for five hours. My bathroom is one door from the bed. There is no place for coats. I feel ungrateful because they helped me move here.

### Assistant
Helping you move did not purchase a holiday venue. Gratitude can be a dish you bring to someone else's table, or a visit on Friday, or a pie. It does not have to be your bed in the background of cousin photos.

When she says finish at yours: "There's no space to finish. We can't do nine here. Let's keep it at your house, or pick a restaurant." If restaurants are a money fight, I will not name a price. You can suggest a potluck at her place, which she already knows how to run.

If cousins message you "what should I bring to yours," reply once: "It's not at my apartment. Check with mom." Do not keep a secret second location.

You can host something that fits: two people for dessert another weekend. That is a studio-sized yes. Thanksgiving nine is a no.

### User
If I hold the line she'll say I ruined the holiday and I'll get the quiet treatment in November. I hate the quiet treatment. I also hate the idea of people sitting on my bed with plates.

### Assistant
Quiet treatment is a bid for you to reopen the studio. You can survive November quiet better than a five-hour crush. The holiday can still happen in a house that has a table.

Send the sentence today so the cousin rumor dies: you are not hosting. Repeat it if she cries. Crying is not a seating chart.

If you want a peace offering, offer a specific thing you can do: pick people up, bring a side to her house, do dishes there. Specific labor is better than an apartment you do not have.

Call her before tonight. Stop the cousin announcements. Keep your bed for sleeping. Turkey can live in a kitchen that already knows how to hold it. Yours does not. That is not ungrateful. That is square footage. Say it.

### User
She just forwarded a cousin's "what can I bring to your place??" into our thread. If I don't answer, the cousin keeps packing a bag.

### Assistant
Answer the cousin once, not through your mother's hope: "It's not at my apartment — check with her. I can't host." Then call your mother. The forward was an attempt to make your studio real by CC. You can still make it not real. Repeat the square footage. Offer the dish at her house if you want a peace offering. Do not start a bring-list for a room that cannot hold nine. The cousin is packing because the rumor is still alive. Kill the rumor in the cousin's thread and in your mother's ear the same hour. Studio stays a studio. Thanksgiving can live where a table already exists. Type the cousin. Then dial.
