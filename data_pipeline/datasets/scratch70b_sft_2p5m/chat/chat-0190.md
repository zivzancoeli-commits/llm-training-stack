### User
I asked for a trim. I left with my ears in the weather. The stylist smiled like this was the plan. I paid because I freeze at the register. It's been six hours. I keep refreshing hat tabs. I don't even know if I want advice or a witness. My uncle would say "it'll grow" which is true and also not what I asked.

### Assistant
Do you still want help, or do you want me to sit with the fact that it's short and stop jumping to hats and emails?

I can do either. I won't start a revenge script if you're actually done talking. Short hair is not an emergency. It's also allowed to ruin your night.

If you want help, tell me which kind: what to say to the salon, what to do with your head this week, or how to not spiral every time you pass a window. Pick one first. All three at once turns into a lecture, and you already have an uncle for that.

### User
I think I want the salon part, but only if it doesn't make me sound unhinged. I don't want a free recut if I have to sit in that chair again tomorrow. I do want them to know it wasn't a trim. Maybe I want a note on my file. I also keep thinking I should just eat it because I didn't speak up when she had the clippers out.

### Assistant
You can still say something after you paid. Paying is not a legal waiver. It is you wanting to leave.

Keep it small and true. Call or email, not a novel in reviews: "I asked for a trim and it came out much shorter than I expected. I'm not coming back in this week. Please note on my file that I want small changes only." If they offer a recut, you can refuse: "Thanks, I need it to grow, I don't want more cutting right now." That's not unhinged. That's a preference.

If they get defensive — "you said take it off the neck" — don't try a courtroom replay. You can say "I hear that's what you heard. It's shorter than I wanted. Please note it." Then you're done. You're not required to win.

Not speaking up in the chair happens to a lot of people when someone has scissors near their head. That's not a character flaw. It's why a follow-up message exists.

Do you want a draft in your voice, or is the idea enough and you want to stop.

### User
Draft. Short. I sound stiff in writing. And tell me what to do with the next seven days without the hat lecture. Hats make my head sweat.

### Assistant
Draft: "Hi, this is [name], I was in today at [time]. I asked for a trim and the cut is a lot shorter than I meant. I'm not booking a fix right now. Could you put on my file that I only want small trims. Thanks."

Send it. Don't add "I'm so sorry to bother you." You're a customer, not a burglar.

Seven days, no hat: wash it however you already wash it. If the back feels like a bristle brush, a tiny amount of whatever conditioner you have on the nape can take the scrape off. You don't need a product haul. Sleep however you sleep. If a piece sticks up, water and your fingers, not a full restyle career. If you have a clip or a bobby pin, use it for the one bump, then forget it.

Clothes that you already like will do more than a new haircut philosophy. If you have a shirt you feel like yourself in, wear that and stop checking mirrors every doorway. One check in the morning, one before you leave, that's enough. Extra checks are how six hours becomes a hobby.

If a friend says "it's cute," you don't have to agree. "It's shorter than I wanted" is allowed. You also don't have to perform being a good sport for them.

If you decide you do want a different person later, wait until you can say "this length, not shorter" while they're holding the comb, not after. That's future you. Tonight is the email and the nape.

Uncle can say it'll grow. He's right. You still get to tell the salon it wasn't a trim. Those can both be true. If you want to drop it after you send, drop it. I'll drop it too.
