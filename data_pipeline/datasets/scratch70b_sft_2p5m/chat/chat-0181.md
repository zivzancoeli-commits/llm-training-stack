### User
I'm at the riverfront festival with my little cousin. She's eight. I turned around at the lemonade stand and she was gone. It's packed. Music is loud. I already walked a loop around the food tents and I didn't see her. I feel like if I keep spinning I'll miss her. What do I actually do, not the "stay calm" poster version.

### Assistant
Stop walking loops. Loops are how you two keep missing each other. Stay at the lemonade stand, the last place she knew you were. Face the crowd so she can see your shirt.

You are not hunting the whole park. You are making yourself findable and handing the search to people with radios.

Look for a vest, a lanyard, or a festival shirt. If you see a volunteer with a walkie, that's your person. Give them, in order: her first name, age, clothes, height compared to you, last place seen. Don't pad it with how scared you are. They already know.

If nobody in a vest is in sight, stay put and call a parent. Same description. Ask them to come to this stand, not to start from the other gate.

What is she wearing, shoes up? Say it out loud once so you lock it.

### User
Yellow hoodie with a ketchup stain on the sleeve. Black shorts. White sneakers with a marker star on the left toe. Two braids. Blue paper wristband. I think she wanted the face-paint booth past the speakers.

### Assistant
That's a usable description. Repeat it that way, stain and star included. Unique marks beat "yellow hoodie" in a sea of them.

Do not go to face-paint yet. If she's using the usual kid rule — go back to where we were — she's trying to get to the lemonade stand. If you leave, she arrives to nobody.

When staff take the description, ask where the lost-child point is and whether they will radio it now. Don't abandon this stand until a named adult is covering it.

If a stranger wants you to "help look" somewhere quieter, no. "I'm waiting for staff" is enough.

Phone in your hand, ringer on. You are the beacon, not a search party of one.

### User
A guy in a gray shirt said he saw a kid in yellow heading toward the river. He wants me to come with him. Staff tent is the other way.

### Assistant
Don't go with him. Thank him, take the information, give it to staff. You walking to the river with someone you don't know is not the protocol.

Kids get mis-seen constantly. Staff can send someone to the bank. You cannot be in two places, and the one that matters is still last-seen.

If the tent is through a wall of people, grab the nearest vest first. Point at the stand: "That's last seen. I'm the cousin. Eight, yellow hoodie, stain on the sleeve, star on the left shoe." Then you can move.

When she's found, don't lecture in the first minute. Hug, water, then one rule for the rest of the day: freeze and shout my name, or go to a vest. Not the river. Not a stranger's shortcut.

### User
What if staff ask a bunch of questions and I blank? I feel like I'm going to cry and then they'll think I'm not in charge.

### Assistant
Crying does not disqualify you. Write the description in your notes app now: yellow hoodie, stain left sleeve, black shorts, white sneakers, star left toe, two braids, blue wristband, last seen lemonade stand. Hand them the screen if your voice goes.

You are in charge of information, not the whole festival. Let them run the radios.

If they ask you to wait to the side, wait. Don't start a second private search "just in case." That's how the kid and the adult both go missing.

Stay put. Describe once. Let the vests move. That's the protocol, and it's enough.

If a parent calls while you're waiting, don't start a new search on the phone. Tell them the stand, the description, and that staff have it. Then get off so your line stays open. You did the job: one spot, one description, one vest. The rest is radios.
