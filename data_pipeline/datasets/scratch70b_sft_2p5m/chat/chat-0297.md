### User
I'm on step 8 of a dresser. The bag of hardware has everything except one cam lock. There is a hole that expects it. The other cams are in. If I skip it the drawer rail looks crooked. The store is a 40-minute drive and closes in an hour. I already threw away part of the cardboard. I am on the floor and a little angry at a tiny metal piece.

### Assistant
Stop forcing the rail. A missing cam is a missing lock, not a suggestion. Tightening around the gap will split a panel or strip a hole, and then you own a worse problem than a drive.

Count the hardware against the list in the manual, not against your memory. Photograph the bag and the step. Check the floor, the trash bag, the other boxes, and any extra "spare" packet. These things hide in folded cardboard.

I will not swear the store will hand you a single cam at 50 minutes to close. I do not know their parts policy. I do know that arriving with the model number, the part name, and a photo beats arriving with a vibe.

Is this a brand with a parts email or chat on the manual's back page, and do you still have the receipt or order number?

### User
There's a QR code and an order number in my email. Receipt is in the email too. I found a spare dowel but not a cam. The store chat says "average reply 2 hours." The dresser is occupying the whole room.

### Assistant
Two-hour chat will not beat a closing time. If you want a chance at a same-night part, you move now or you decide to pause the build.

Pausing is allowed. Lean the panels somewhere they will not warp, bag every screw you already used so they do not join the carpet, and live with a half dresser for a night. A crooked rail is not a place to store clothes.

If you go to the store, take one cam as a physical example, the manual page, and the order number. Ask for the hardware pack for that SKU, not "a little metal mushroom." If they only sell full kits, you get to decide whether a kit is worth it. I will not invent a price.

Do not substitute a random screw from a junk drawer. The cam's job is to grab a bolt head from the side. A wood screw in that hole is a future split.

Do not glue it. Glue is how you make the next true part useless.

If the QR leads to a parts form, submit it tonight anyway, even if you also drive. Two paths. Use the model number exactly.

Your anger at a tiny piece is reasonable. The piece is load-bearing. The company under-packed a bag. That happens. It is still on them to finish the kit, not on you to MacGyver a dresser into a hazard.

### User
If I leave it half-built I will trip on it at 2 a.m. And I feel stupid that I threw cardboard out.

### Assistant
You are not stupid. People throw cardboard when they think they are done with packaging. Fish the cardboard if it is still in your bin; sometimes the cam is in a fold. If the bin is gone, let it go.

Move the half dresser against a wall, not in a walk path. Put a chair in front of the worst corner if you are a night wanderer. Tape a note on it that says "not done" so sleepy you does not lean on it.

For the next ten minutes: search the floor on your hands once more, photograph the missing step, submit the parts form with the order number, and either bag up for the store run or park the panels. If you go, leave with 50 minutes, not 20. If you stay, stop tightening anything on step 8.

One cam lock is annoying and also finite. The dresser can wait. Your floor and a split side panel cannot. Pause like a person who wants furniture, not like a person in a fight with a bag.
