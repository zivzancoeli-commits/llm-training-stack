### User
I pay cash because I budget that way. Clerks here drop coins on the counter and they roll. I have never asked anyone to count it back into my hand. I feel like I am accusing them of stealing. I just cannot see coins against this marble. First time. Line behind me. What do I say that is dry and not a speech about accessibility?

### Assistant
You are asking for a count, not a confession. The sentence is: "Could you count it back into my hand, please?" Hold your hand out, palm up, before they set the coins on the marble. Timing is the trick. Hand first, then they have a place to put it. If your hands are full, put the bag down first. A free palm is the tool.

I will not invent the till policy. I will not invent a price. Counting back is an old ordinary move. It is not an accusation. You do not need to say your eyes or the marble. You can, if you want: "I lose coins on this counter." Optional. Skip the essay either way.

If they already dropped the change, you can still say, "Sorry — into my hand?" and keep the palm there. Do not scramble on marble while you apologize for your personality. Scrambling is how a dime hits the floor and the line sighs.

Are they mid-drop right now, or are you still holding the bill?

### User
Bill is on the counter. They're opening the drawer. I already feel my face doing the sorry smile. There's a plexi window with a gap at the bottom.

### Assistant
Kill the sorry smile if you can. Neutral is fine. Slide your hand into the gap now, not after the coins click on stone. "Into my hand, please." If the plexi makes it awkward, you can say, "Through the gap is fine." Palm still up. They have done this for people with canes, people with kids, people who just like a count. You are not a unique problem.

If they count out loud, listen. If they do not, you can still feel the coins land and then step aside to check. Checking to the side is not a scene. Checking on the marble in the line is a scene.

If they look offended, you do not soothe with "I trust you." Trust is not the point. Marble is the point. Repeat the hand. Then take the receipt.

First time fails when you narrate a lifetime of dropped dimes. Skip the lifetime. Hand. Please. Gap.

### User
What if they put the bills in my hand and the coins on the marble anyway because muscle memory?

### Assistant
Then you leave the coins for one second and say, "Coins too, please." You do not snatch and sprinkle. You wait. If they are already gone in their head to the next customer, a finger tap on your palm is a cue. If a coin is already rolling, you can catch it without a speech. One miss is not a referendum on the method. Next time your hand is in the gap earlier.

If they refuse — rare, but if — you pick the coins off the marble as fast as you can and you do not turn it into a training session for a stranger. You needed a count. You got a drop. You still leave with the money. Later you can use a card or a different line. I will not tell you which store must train cashiers. I will tell you this first time is the sentence plus the palm.

Drawer is open. Hand in the gap. "Count it back into my hand, please." Bills, coins, receipt, step aside. If you need to pocket-sort, do it at the end of the counter, not in the next person's space. No accusation. No marble hunt if you can avoid it. That is the whole first. The line can wait for a palm. A rolling quarter cannot.
