### User
My college roommate is getting married in another country next spring. The site wants an RSVP by Sunday. I cannot go. Money, time off, a passport that would need work, and I don't want to put it on a card. I have been typing "I'm so honored" for twenty minutes and deleting it. If I say no they will think the friendship ended. If I say yes I will spend the winter dreading a trip I cannot actually take. I have never declined a wedding like this.

### Assistant
A no is a complete RSVP. It is not a breakup letter.

You do not need a passport essay on the form. You need the accurate box and a short note. Something like: "I can't make the trip. I'm happy for you. I'll celebrate another way if you want." That is enough for a website. Save the longer feelings for a text or a call if you are actually close.

Do not RSVP yes as a placeholder. Placeholders become plane tickets in other people's heads. Do not write a fake maybe. Maybe is how they assign you a meal and a shuttle.

I will not invent visa rules, ticket prices, or whether your passport can be expedited. You already know you cannot go. Trust that.

Are you declining only the travel, or also any local gathering they might add later?

### User
There's no local gathering on the site. Just the destination week. We still text memes. We are not daily-close anymore. I keep thinking I should offer to throw a dinner when they're back. Then I worry that offer is a new project I also cannot do. Sunday is the form.

### Assistant
Sunday is the form. The dinner is a separate decision you can make later, when they are home and you have a real weekend. Do not staple a new event to an RSVP no just to soften it. Softening is how you acquire a catering job.

Fill the form: no. In the note field, two sentences. Warm, not theatrical. Then send a text the same day so they do not only see a database row: "I RSVP'd no because I can't travel for it. I'm glad you're getting married. Love you." If memes are your language, you can send that after, not instead.

If they reply with guilt, or a plea to "just come for the weekend," you repeat the no without a new spreadsheet. "I can't make the trip." If they ask why, you can name one true constraint: time off, or travel, or the season. You do not have to itemize your bank account. I will not pick a dollar figure for you.

If they go quiet, that may sting. Quiet is not always a verdict. Give it time. Keep sending the ordinary memes if that is still real. Do not flood them with apology voicemails.

### User
What if the plus-one line makes it look like I was going to bring someone and then bailed, and her family talks? Also I already liked the dress photos in the group chat. I feel like I led them on.

### Assistant
Liking photos is not a contract. Families talk. You cannot pre-clear their talk. You can be accurate on the form.

If the site has a plus-one field, leave it empty or mark that you are not attending. Do not add a ghost guest. If you already entered a yes weeks ago, change it now and tell your roommate you corrected it. Late accuracy is better than a silent no-show.

You did not lead them on by being happy for them. You would lead them on by promising a week you cannot attend. Send the no by Sunday. Send the text. Do not offer a dinner in the same message. If you want to send a card or a small gift later, that can be later. I will not name a price. The job this weekend is the truthful box. Fill it, close the tab, go do something unrelated for an hour so you do not reopen the draft.
