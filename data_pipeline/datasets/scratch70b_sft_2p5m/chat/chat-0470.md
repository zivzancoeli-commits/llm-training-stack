### User
It's 11:20 p.m. My flight landed at 9:05. Bag isn't here. The carousel stopped. The airline desk said "maybe the 6 a.m. flight" and gave me a reference number on a thermal printout. I have an interview at 9:30 in a building downtown. My interview clothes are in the bag. I have gym shorts, a wrinkled t-shirt, and this jacket. Hotel is a 40-minute train. I don't want you to invent a reimbursement. I want a sequence so I don't show up looking like I slept in a gym. Desk line is gone. I'm next to a silent belt.

### Assistant
Clothes first, bag second, sleep third. The belt will not become your closet because you stare at it.

Photograph the printout, the empty belt, the time. Then you leave the airport if they're closed. Standing here doesn't sew a shirt. I will not invent what the airline owes you, a dollar amount, or whether they'll deliver by 8. You can call the number on the slip from the hotel. You can ask if they deliver to the hotel before 8. If they say no, you stop bargaining with a closed desk.

Tonight's clothing: hotel iron if it exists, the jacket you have, and a 24-hour option if your city has one — drugstore, big-box, whatever is actually open. I won't name a store or a price. If nothing is open, you plan the most serious version of what you're wearing: jacket zipped, shorts are a problem, so you need pants from somewhere or you email the recruiter at a sane hour — not 11:20 — if you truly cannot look interview-shaped. Emailing at 11:20 looks like panic. If you must, a 6:30 a.m. email is cleaner than a midnight novel. Content: "My bag was delayed; I'll be there at 9:30; I may be more casual than planned. Still coming." Only if it's true.

Don't buy a whole wardrobe. One shirt, one pair of pants if you can. Interview is about answers. Clothes still matter. You're allowed to care.

Do you have the hotel address on your phone, and is there any 24-hour pharmacy on the map between here and there?

### User
Hotel is booked. Map shows a 24-hour Walgreens 12 minutes from the hotel, not near the airport. Train is the last one at 11:50. If I miss it I'm a taxi I don't want to think about. Desk is dark. I have deodorant in my backpack. No pants.

### Assistant
Catch the 11:50. The bag doesn't get a vote.

On the train: charger, reference number in your email to yourself, interview time, recruiter contact. At the hotel, don't unpack a speech. Ask the front desk if they have an iron, a lost-and-found shirt they won't have, a printer, a 6 a.m. cab estimate — you can ask, I won't quote the cab. Then Walgreens: black pants if they exist, a plain shirt if they exist. If they have nothing but snacks, you still have the jacket and you still go in the morning. You use the iron on the t-shirt. You look like a traveler, not a gym. You tell the truth if they comment: bag delayed. Then you do the interview.

Call the airline from the hotel bed, not from the silent belt. Ask delivery time. If they say after 9:30, you stop waiting for a miracle suit. Sleep.

6:30 email only if the Walgreens fails and you need to set expectations. Don't over-explain gym shorts. "Delayed bag, I'll be there."

### User
I'll take the train. If Walgreens has nothing, I still go. Should I go to the airport at 6 for the maybe bag and risk the downtown 9:30?

### Assistant
Don't go to the airport at 6 unless they confirm the bag is on that plane and can be in your hands with time to spare for downtown. A maybe carousel is how you miss 9:30. Interview wins. Bag can meet you after. Ask for hotel delivery while you're in the building if that's on offer. If not, you pick it up after the interview.

Sequence: 11:50 train, hotel, call, Walgreens, sleep, 9:30 downtown, bag later. You don't need a reimbursement story to be a person who shows up. Get off the silent belt. The thermal slip is enough paper for tonight. Move.
