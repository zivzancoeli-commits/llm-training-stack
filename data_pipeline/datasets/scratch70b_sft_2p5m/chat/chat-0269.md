### User
Jordan texted "can I crash a few days, same as April." April was eleven nights, dishes in the sink, and my roommate asking whose socks were on the toaster. I said never again, quietly, to myself. I did not say it to Jordan. They land tomorrow if I say yes. I have a 7 a.m. Thursday. The couch is a loveseat. "Few days" has never meant three. It's 9:30 p.m. I haven't replied.

### Assistant
Not replying is how April becomes twelve nights. You already have the data: eleven, dishes, socks, roommate. A loveseat is not a spare room. 7 a.m. Thursday is not compatible with a guest who treats dawn as optional.

You can care about Jordan and still say no. If you say yes, you need an end date in writing before they land, and you need your roommate in the yes. Roommate lives here. Jordan does not.

I won't invent what you owe a friend in a housing squeeze. I don't know why they need the couch. You can ask one question without opening the door.

Have they said where they'd go if you say no, or is your loveseat the whole plan?

### User
They said their sublet ended messy. I didn't get details. Roommate is at a concert until 11. Last time roommate smiled in front of Jordan and vented after. I don't want to be the person who dumps a friend on the sidewalk. I also don't want socks on the toaster.

### Assistant
Sidewalk is dramatic. No is not a sidewalk. No is "I can't host. Try X." If you don't have an X, "I can't host" still stands. You are not a shelter with a toaster.

For the next ten minutes: text the roommate a heads-up, not a request to rubber-stamp at 11 p.m. after a concert: "Jordan asked for the couch again. I'm leaning no unless you want a hard end date." Then draft Jordan: "I can't do the couch this time. April was too long for this apartment. Hope the sublet gets sorted." If you want one practical kindness, send a link to a hostel or a listing you actually looked at, not a fake "you'll figure it out" with a smiley.

If you are tempted to yes, the only version that isn't April is: "Two nights, out Saturday 10 a.m., dishes nightly, no toaster laundry, roommate has to agree when they're home." Put it in the text. If Jordan argues the clock, that's a no.

Don't wait for 11 to become midnight guilt. 9:30 can send.

### User
They'll say I changed. I did. April changed me. I feel cruel typing a date like a hotel.

### Assistant
You did change. Eleven nights plus a roommate's vent is a reason. Cruel would be a yes you already resent, then a freeze-out on night four. A date is clearer than a slow ice.

"You changed" can be answered once: "I can't host overnight right now." You don't have to litigate your personality. Hotels use dates because open-ended couches turn into April. You can be a friend who buys them a meal tomorrow without giving them a key. Meal is optional. Key is the thing that wrecked the toaster.

If they show up anyway, you don't have to buzz them in. That's hard. It's still your door. I won't script a scene. Most people take a written no.

Talk to the roommate when they get home so you're not the secret innkeeper. Then send Jordan the no tonight. 7 a.m. Thursday needs a quiet loveseat. Few days needs a number, and you don't have to offer one. You already lived the long version. Choose the short no. The socks can stay off the appliance. The sublet mess is theirs to sort with someone who has a spare room, which you do not. Type it. Don't workshop it until the concert ends. You know the answer. April taught it. Use the lesson while it's still 9:30.
