### User
My mentor spent three years getting me into this lab-adjacent job. They wrote me a recommendation. They still send me postings. I want to leave for a completely different kind of work — still employed, just not this ladder. I have an interview next Thursday in the other field. I have not told them. I feel like I am stealing the recommendation retroactively. Coffee is on their calendar for Friday. Do I tell them at coffee, after the interview, or never?

### Assistant
Do not never. Never is how they hear from a LinkedIn update and feel like a fool.

Do not dump it as a confession of theft. You used the help to do the job you had. Wanting a different job later is allowed. Mentors are not owners.

Friday coffee is soon. Thursday is the interview. If you tell them Friday, tell after the interview so you are not asking them to coach you through a field they may not know, unless you want that. If the interview is a first screen, you can wait until you have a real signal. If you already know you are leaving this ladder even if this interview fails, Friday is a values conversation, not an offer announcement.

Script, if you tell Friday: "I'm grateful for the rec and the years. I'm interviewing outside this path. I wanted you to hear it from me. I'm not asking you to undo anything. I wanted to be straight." Then stop. Let them have a feeling.

I will not tell you the other field is wise. I cannot see it. I will tell you that guilt is not a career plan.

Is this person someone who takes pride in lineage, or someone who actually likes when people move?

### User
Lineage, I think. They introduce me as "one of mine." They are kind. They will be hurt. The other field is operations in a smaller org. I have not accepted anything. I might not get it.

### Assistant
Hurt is likely. You can still be straight. "One of mine" is affectionate and a little sticky. You can thank the mine-ness without staying in the mine.

Because nothing is accepted, you can frame it as exploration: "I'm testing a different kind of role. I'll keep you posted. I didn't want the coffee to be fake." Fake coffee is worse than a hurt Friday.

Do not ask them to call the new place. Do not ask for a second rec until they offer. Asking too fast is how gratitude looks like extraction.

If they try to talk you out of it with "you're throwing away progress," you can say, "I know it looks like that from this ladder. I still want to look." You do not need a ten-year plan on a napkin. You need not to lie for an hour about loving the postings they send.

After coffee, when they send the next posting, you can say, "I'm pausing those for a bit." That is the follow-through. Otherwise they will keep filling your inbox with a future you are leaving.

### User
What if they say they regret the recommendation?

### Assistant
Then you let that sentence exist without litigating it. "I'm sorry you feel that. I did the work you recommended me for. I still appreciate it." You do not repay a rec by staying forever. That is not how recs work, even if it is how some people feel.

If the relationship can survive, it will survive on Friday's honesty plus time. If it cannot, that is painful and still not a reason to fake a field.

Go to the interview as yourself. Go to coffee as someone who will not pretend. You are not stealing the past. You are not promising them a twin in the same job at fifty. Tell them on Friday if you can stand the hurt in the room. Wait a week if the interview will change the facts. Do not wait for never. Never is the version that turns a mentor into a stranger with a Google alert. Choose the human version. It will be uncomfortable. It will also be clean.
