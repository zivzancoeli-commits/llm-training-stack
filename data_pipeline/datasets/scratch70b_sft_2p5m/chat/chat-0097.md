### User
I'm at this indoor show and the person in front of me is like a full head taller than everyone else in the row. I paid for a standing floor ticket, not a view of someone's hoodie. I keep leaning left and right like an idiot. Should I tap them? Ask staff to move me? I don't even know if that's a thing. The opener just finished.

### Assistant
I don't know this venue's floor rules, and I shouldn't pretend I do. Some places treat standing as first-come and will shrug. Some will walk you to a side rail if you ask quietly. I have no way to know which one you're in.

What I *can* say without guessing: tapping a stranger mid-set to "be shorter" almost never works, and it can pick a fight you don't want in a packed room. Staff is the less weird path, but only if you can actually reach them without shoving.

Where are you standing — dead center in a dense pack, or closer to an aisle or the soundboard where a worker might already be hanging around?

### User
More like packed center. I can see a guy with a flashlight near the left barrier, maybe twenty people away. I feel stupid complaining. It's a concert. Tall people exist. But I also saved for this and I can barely see the singer's mic.

### Assistant
You're not stupid for wanting to see the thing you paid for. You're also not entitled to a guaranteed sightline on a general-admission floor. Both of those can be true at once. I don't know whether that flashlight person is security, a photographer, or someone checking wristbands. From here I can't tell you they'll help.

If you go, keep it one sentence: "I'm stuck behind someone much taller, is there anywhere I can stand without blocking other people?" Don't narrate the hoodie. Don't ask them to scold the tall person. You're asking for *your* body to move, not for someone else to shrink.

If they say no, that's an answer, not an insult. Then you pick a smaller fix: one step toward the soundboard gap, or a half-step onto a slight rise if the floor has one. Don't climb anything. I don't know your venue's camera pit rules either, so I won't tell you to sneak there.

### User
What if I just wait until a song people actually know and then slide when everyone jumps? That's what my friend said. Also, is it rude to hold my phone up so I can watch the screen instead of the stage?

### Assistant
I don't know if your friend's "slide during the jump" trick works here. In some crowds it just means you end up in someone else's space and they shove back. I won't sell you a move I can't see. If the floor is already locked shoulder to shoulder, waiting for a chorus doesn't create a lane. It creates a wobble.

Phone-as-screen: not illegal in any way I can name, and I'm not going to invent a house rule. Plenty of people do it. It can also make the person behind *you* stare at a rectangle, so it's a trade, not a clever hack. If you try it, dim the brightness. A blinding white rectangle is how you become the new problem.

The honest version: you might not get a clean view of this set. That's a real bummer and it doesn't mean you failed at concerts. You can still hear it, which is most of what you paid for, even if that's a lame consolation at fourteen when you wanted the full thing.

### User
Okay. So flashlight person, one sentence, don't start a fight. If they shrug, I live with it. I still feel like I should have known this would happen.

### Assistant
You couldn't have known the exact height of a random stranger. That's not a skill you missed. Next time, if you care about sightlines, the only thing that reliably helps on a standing floor is getting there earlier or standing near a rail. Even then, I'm not promising a rail will be free. I don't know how this room fills.

Go now, during the gap between songs, not during a quiet verse. Come back if they say something confusing and we'll parse it. I won't pretend I know their script.
