### User
I have a midterm tomorrow. My housemate Jordan is in the living room on a game with the headset off, talking to people who are not in this apartment. The walls are basically cardboard. I already tried headphones. I can still hear the laughs. I'm the new person here — three weeks — and I don't want to be the intern of the house who writes a chore chart about volume. I also cannot fail this exam because I'm being polite. What do I say that is specific and not a manifesto.

### Assistant
You knock, you stand where they can see you, you make one ask with a time on it. Not a personality review. Not "you're always loud." Tonight.

Try: "Hey Jordan — I've got a midterm tomorrow and I can hear the game through my door. Could you use headphones or take the call in your room until 11." If 11 is when you need sleep, say 11. If you need quiet now for two hours of problems, say "until 10:30." A clock is kinder than "be quieter," which nobody can obey.

You're not writing a house constitution. You're booking a window. People can live with a window.

If you only text, keep it the same length. Long texts from the new roommate read like HR. Short reads like a human who has a test.

Which is it — you need quiet for studying still, or you need sleep, or both stacked.

### User
Both. It's 8:40. I need about two hours of actual reading, then I want to be in bed by 11:30. Jordan's friends get louder after 9 when more people hop on. I don't know if Jordan even knows how thin the wall is. They seem fine. I'm the one spiraling.

### Assistant
Then say both times, still without a speech: "I need it quieter from now until 11:30. Headphones or your room would help. I can hear the living room in mine." That's the whole message. You don't need to diagnose whether they "know." After you tell them, they know.

If they say "we'll keep it down," that's vague. You can smile and still specify: "Headphones would actually do it. Down still comes through." Specific, not scolding.

If they offer to pause until you sleep, take the win. Don't add "and also the dishes" or "and also last Tuesday." One night, one ask. Stacking grievances is how you become the manifesto.

You're new, so you might feel like you need a joke. Skip the joke if it turns into "haha I'm so annoying." You're not annoying. You have an exam. That's a normal reason.

If Jordan is in a round, wait until a break if you can hear one coming in the next couple minutes. If it's endless, interrupt anyway. Your reading does not start when the match ends.

### User
What if they get flat and say it's their living room too. That's true. I don't want a debate about whose name is on the lease. I just want the physics of the wall to change for one night.

### Assistant
Agree with the true part, then return to the clock. "Yeah, it's shared. I'm still asking for headphones tonight until 11:30 because of a midterm." Shared space is not a veto on a reasonable ask. It is why you ask instead of slamming a door.

If they won't move, you move for the two hours if you can: library if it's open, a hallway with an outlet, a 24-hour cafe, a friend's floor, even the bathroom with the fan if that's less bad — I'm not going to pretend that's ideal. If leaving isn't safe or possible, you repeat the ask once: "I need this tonight. Please." Then you stop repeating. Three reminders becomes a fight.

Kind is the tone. Specific is the request. Kind without specific is "whenever you get a chance." Specific without kind is a note on the fridge with a lecture. You want the first plus the second: a person, a volume tool, a time.

After the exam, you can have a longer house talk about weeknights. Not at 8:40 with a textbook. Tonight you are not the intern. You are a roommate with a test. Say the times. Then go read.
