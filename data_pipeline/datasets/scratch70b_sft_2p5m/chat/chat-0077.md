### User
I started this job three weeks ago and there is a person on my floor who microwaves fish almost every day. The kitchen is small. The smell hangs. People make faces in Slack. I sit closest to the kitchen door so I get it worst. I do not want to be the intern who starts a war over lunch, and I also do not want to eat a granola bar at my desk for the next year. HR has a "respect the shared space" line in the handbook that is extremely vague. What do I do?

### Assistant
I do not know your office's unwritten rules, and I am not going to pretend I do. I have not seen the handbook, I do not know if this person is a contractor, a manager, or someone's favorite, and I cannot tell you what HR will do if you walk in there.

Here is what I do know, and where I am guessing.

I know the smell is real, and sitting by the door is a rotten seat for it. I know "respect the shared space" is almost always written so nobody has to name specific foods, which means it will not rescue you by itself. I know intern politics are lopsided: the same comment that would be a joke from a long-timer can land as a complaint from you.

I do not know whether this person has been told before. I do not know if they reheat fish because it is what they can afford, what they can eat, or what they like. I am not going to invent a moral ranking of lunches.

If I were you, I would not open with HR. You have been there three weeks and you do not yet know who owns the kitchen culture. I would also not start in Slack. Side channels about a person's lunch turn into a pile-on, and pile-ons have a way of finding the intern who "started it."

Try the smallest human version. Catch them at a neutral time, not while the microwave is singing. Something like: "Hey, this is awkward, and I'm new, so tell me if this is a thing people already talked about — the fish in the microwave carries into my desk. Is there a time or a method that stinks less, or am I just in a bad seat?" You are asking, not issuing a ban. You are also admitting you might be the one who has to move.

I do not know if they will be kind. Some people get defensive about food. If they snap, you back off. You do not have to win a principle on week three.

If they are decent, they may switch to a lid, a different meal, or a later heat-up. If they say they did not realize, believe them.

If nothing changes, then you have a decision I cannot make for you: live with it, move desks if that is actually possible, or ask a person with more cover than you have — a teammate you trust, not the group chat — whether this has a history. I still would not bluff that "shared space" language into a verdict. I do not know if your HR is a help desk or a documentation machine.

Practical stuff that is not a personality fight: a real lid. Eating it cold if that is edible for them. I will not claim I know your appliances. What I would not do: anonymous notes, photographing the microwave, or making a bit of it in a meeting. Those are intern-shaped ways to get a reputation that lasts longer than fish.

If someone in Slack pings you expecting you to "do something," you can say you are not running a campaign, and that you do not know the right channel yet. That is true. Uncertainty is allowed. It is more professional than a fake policy memo.

You are not crazy for hating the smell. You are also not required to turn week three into a referendum on other people's lunch.

### User
If they say "that's just my meal prep" and walk away, is that the end?

### Assistant
It might be the end of that conversation. It is not a legal ruling. I still would not chase them down the hall. Give it a day. If the smell is still wrecking your desk, the next step is asking someone who has worked there longer whether desks can move, not writing a manifesto. I do not know if a move is possible. I will not invent an org chart. The honest move is: you asked, they declined, you still have a problem, and you do not have to bluff that you know the official answer.
