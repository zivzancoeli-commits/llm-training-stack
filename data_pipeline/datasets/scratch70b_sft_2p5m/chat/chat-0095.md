### User
There's a heatwave this week. I don't have AC. My apartment is on the second floor, brick, one fan. People at work keep saying "watch the heat index," "close the envelope," "use night flush," "stay ahead of wet bulb." I nodded like I knew. I don't. I feel like a new intern in my own kitchen. Translate that into things I do with windows and a fan, and tell me what you are not sure about.

### Assistant
I'll put the jargon in kitchen language, then I'll say where I'm guessing.

Heat index is not a different temperature fairy. It is a way weather people combine air temperature and humidity to describe how hard it is for your body to cool by sweating. Sweat cools you when it evaporates. If the air is already crowded with water, sweat sits there and you feel worse than the number on a dry thermometer. When someone says "the heat index is ugly this afternoon," they mean the humidity is making the heat meaner, not that a second sun appeared.

Wet bulb is the nerd cousin of that. People dunk the idea of a thermometer in the idea of a wet sock: if water cannot evaporate, that reading climbs toward the regular temperature. You do not need to calculate one. The practical translation is: when it is hot and sticky, fans help less than they do in dry heat, because a fan is mostly moving air over sweat. Still use the fan. Don't worship it.

"Close the envelope" is building-talk for: stop letting hot outdoor air into the box you live in once the outside is hotter than inside. In intern English: in the brutal afternoon, shut windows, drop blinds on the sunny side, keep doors to hotter rooms closed if that actually traps a cooler room. You are not sealing a spacecraft. You are not feeding the apartment more oven air on purpose.

"Night flush" means the opposite trick when night air is actually cooler: open windows, run the fan to push cooler air through, dump the day's stored heat. Brick holds heat. That's "thermal mass" in one sentence: the walls are a battery for warmth. They keep eating sunlight and then they radiate it at you at 10 p.m. Flushing at night is an attempt to discharge the battery. If the night stays hot and wet, the flush does less. I am not looking at your city's forecast, so I cannot tell you which nights will cooperate.

What I would do with one fan, without pretending I can see your floor plan: aim it at you when you are home and sweating, not at a wall. If you can put it in a window at night blowing out, with another window open on the other side, you get a cheap through-breeze. If you only have one opening, a fan in the room still moves air over skin. Ice in a bowl in front of a fan is a tiny local trick. It is not an air conditioner. Don't expect a miracle.

I am not a clinician, and I will not diagnose you. People take heat seriously when there is confusion, vomiting, someone who stops sweating when they should be soaked, or someone who can't stay awake. That is not a window problem. That is get help.

Water, shade, slower chores in the peak afternoon, a cool shower if you have water. Check on a neighbor if you have one who is older or on a higher floor. I don't know your building. I know heat rises.

I will not pretend I know your humidity tonight, whether night flush is safe given how your windows lock, or whether the power stays on. Don't run a dozen extra appliances just to feel busy. I won't invent your electric bill.

If work chat says "hydrate," they mean drink water before the headache, not chug until you feel sick. "Avoid peak sun" means don't jog at 3. You already knew that part. The rest was vocabulary on top of windows.

### User
Do I close the blinds on the shady side too? Someone said "all solar gain."

### Assistant
Solar gain is sunlight turning into heat when it hits your floors and walls. Shady-side windows gain less. If closing everything makes the apartment a cave you cannot stand, leave the shady side's blinds open for light and close the side that's getting cooked. The phrase "all solar gain" is a max-efficiency instruction, not a moral law. Use your eyes: which pane is dumping light on the floor? That's the one to block first. Brick will still be brick. You are trimming the input, not winning a physics prize.
