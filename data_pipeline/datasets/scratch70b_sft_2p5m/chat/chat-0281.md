### User
Every time we go out, Maya "left her wallet in the car" or "Apple Pay is being weird." I cover it. She Venmos later, except twice she didn't, and once she sent half and said she'd get the rest Friday, which was three Fridays ago. Tonight she already ordered fries before sitting down. I can see her phone on the table. I'm tired of being the ATM who also has to smile. I don't want to blow up the friendship over nachos.

### Assistant
You are not crazy, and this is not about nachos. A pattern of forgotten wallets plus delayed payback is her using your politeness as infrastructure. You can stop funding it without a courtroom speech.

Do not do the theatrical "oh you forgot AGAIN" in front of the table if other people are there. Do not eat the cost to keep the vibe. The middle is a clear, boring process: the bill gets split before anyone leaves, and if she cannot pay, the plan changes before food lands, not after.

I will not guess her bank balance. People forget wallets sometimes. People also train their friends. Three Fridays is data.

Is tonight just the two of you, or a group? And have you ever said a direct sentence about the unpaid ones, or only hints?

### User
Just us. I said "no rush" both times she was short, which I guess was the problem. She laughed and said I'm the organized one. The fries are already coming. I have my card out like a reflex.

### Assistant
Put the card back in your pocket. You do not need to be cruel. You need to stop being automatic.

When the fries arrive, you can still eat with her. The intervention is the check, not the potato. A usable line: "I can't cover tonight. Let's each pay for our own. If that's a problem, we should switch this to something cheaper or take it to go." If she says Apple Pay is weird, wait. Most "weird" resolves when the other person does not reach for plastic.

If she gets tight in the face, let the silence sit. You do not have to fill it with "it's fine." It has not been fine.

About the old balance: tonight is the wrong moment for a full ledger if she is already embarrassed. You can separate the two. Tonight is "I am not paying." Later, in a text, not a pile-on: "You still owe me [amount] from [place] and [place]. Can you send it this week?" Round to what you actually remember. If you are unsure of the exact number, say so: "I think it's around X; tell me if your records differ." Inventing a precise debt you cannot back up will make you the unreliable one.

If she offers to get the next one, do not accept a vague next. Either she pays her share tonight or you leave with boxes. IOUs are how this started.

### User
If I do this she's going to say I'm keeping score. I hate that phrase. Also what if she actually cannot afford this restaurant and I look like a jerk.

### Assistant
Keeping score is what you do when someone else refuses to. Friends who pay their share never need the phrase. If she says it, you can answer, "I'm not scoring personality. I'm not covering another meal." Then stop explaining.

If she cannot afford this place, that is useful information, not a moral failure. You can say, "If money is tight, tell me before we sit down and we'll pick something else." You cannot read her account. What you can refuse is being surprised by her empty wallet after she ordered.

For the next ten minutes: put your card away, eat the fries that are already coming if you want them, and when the server comes, ask for separate checks before the rest of the food. If the server says it is too late, pay only your items if the system allows it, or pay and immediately request her share in the payment app while you are still at the table. Stay seated until the transfer shows or until she says she cannot. If she cannot, decide whether you are buying her dinner on purpose. Accidental charity is the thing you are done with.

After you leave, send the arrears text once. Do not stack three paragraphs of hurt. If she pays and the forgetting stops, you still have a friend. If she punishes you for having a boundary, you learned what the friendship was made of, and it was not nachos.
