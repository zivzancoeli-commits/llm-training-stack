### User
My brother asked to borrow my car for a "quick errand" that is actually an hour each way. He needs to pick up a used couch. His car is in the shop. Mine is the one that is paid off and has my work badge in the glove box. He wants it tomorrow morning. I have a shift at 2. He said he'll "fill it up" which last time meant a quarter tank of the cheap stuff. I don't know how to say no without a family trial.

### Assistant
"Quick errand" plus an hour each way plus a couch is a moving job, not a dash to the pharmacy. You can say no. You can also say yes with limits. A family trial starts when you hint and then explode later.

If the true answer is no, say no to the car, not to his couch problem. "I can't lend the car tomorrow. I need it for work." That is complete. You do not have to add a lecture about last time's gas.

If you are considering yes, the couch is the risk: scratches, a bumper, a strap that fails on the highway. I will not pretend I know your insurance, whether he is listed, or what happens if he gets a ticket. I do not have your policy. If you do not know either, that is a reason to pause, not to hand over keys because he is family.

Does he have another person who can help him lift, and is your car even the right shape for a couch?

### User
It's a hatchback. The couch is "maybe if we fold it." There is a friend who might help. He made it sound like if I say no I'm leaving him couchless. Mom already knows he asked.

### Assistant
A hatchback "maybe" is how bumpers get involved. If it does not fit with the hatch closed, it does not go. You are not his cargo van.

Mom knowing is social pressure, not a court. You can text him before the family chain starts rewriting you: "I need the car for my 2 o'clock shift. I'm not lending it for a couch run. Can your friend take you, or can you rent a van for a few hours?" You are allowed to suggest a van without paying for it.

If you do lend it — I am not recommending it — do it like a boring contract: time back on the driveway (say noon, not "before your shift"), no extra stops, gas returned to the same level you photograph tonight, and the work badge out of the glove box. Ride along if you cannot stand the maybe-fold. Lending the car and not the afternoon is how you sit at work wondering about a couch on the highway.

Last time's quarter tank is data. "Fill it up" as a vibe is not a plan. If you cannot enforce a photo of the gauge, do not lend.

If he goes to Mom with "she's being difficult," you do not have to try the case in the group chat. Repeat the shift time. Repeat that it is a couch, not an emergency.

### User
If I say no he'll borrow someone else's car and still be mad at me. I hate being the responsible one.

### Assistant
He can be mad and still have a couch path that does not involve your bumper. Being the responsible one is often just being the person with a working car. That is not a personality you have to donate.

For the next ten minutes: take the badge out tonight anyway, photograph your mileage and gas gauge so you have a baseline even if you refuse, send the no-plus-shift text, and do not pre-argue with Mom. If she calls, "I need my car for work" is the whole script. If he needs a ride to a rental counter, that is a different, smaller yes you can choose. A hitch to a used sofa is not.

You do not owe him a hatchback origami experiment. Tell him tomorrow is a no. Let the couch be a logistics problem instead of a loyalty test.
