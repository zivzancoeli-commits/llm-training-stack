### User
I'm at the market. I picked out tomatoes and a loaf. The vendor's card reader keeps spinning and dying. I have like four dollars cash. The total is more than that. They're being nice about it — "just Venmo me," "just take it and come next week," "my kid can run your card at the truck." I don't Venmo strangers. I don't want to owe a tomato tab. Last year I would have smiled and invented a reason like my app was "updating." I don't want a fake reason. I just want to put the food back without being a villain.

### Assistant
You put it back. You say no. You don't owe a software story.

"I only have a little cash and I don't Venmo. I'll leave these. Thanks anyway." That's the whole thing. You can buy whatever the four dollars covers if you still want something — one tomato, a small bun, nothing — and you can smile without becoming their accounts payable.

"Come next week" is a debt, even if they're kind. Kindness does not make you a borrower. "Kid at the truck" is a maybe that eats your morning. You can decline without reviewing their business.

Last-year you used "updating" because no felt rude. No is ruder in your head than out loud. Out loud it sounds like a person who pays how they pay.

If they push once, you repeat the same sentence, not a new excuse. New excuses are how you end up in their phone.

### User
They already wrapped the bread. It's in a bag in my hand. Walking it back to the table feels like returning a gift. There's a line. I can feel the line deciding I'm the problem.

### Assistant
The line wants to move. Completing a payment you can't complete does not help them. Handing the bag back does.

Put it on the table. "Sorry — reader isn't working for me and I don't do app payments. I can't take this." If the bread is squished, that's their wrap, not your crime. You're not stealing by returning it.

You can add "I'll come back if I get cash" only if that's true. Don't soothe them with a future you won't do. A clean no is better than a ghosting promise.

If someone behind you sighs, let them. You're not a customer-service mascot. You're a person with four dollars.

Don't start a workshop about why you don't Venmo. "I don't" is complete. Privacy, fees, didn't install it — extra reasons invite debate. Debate is how you stay in the bag.

### User
What if they say keep the tomatoes, really, it's fine, we waste them anyway. That one gets me. Waste is my weak spot. I can hear last-year me taking the bag to "help."

### Assistant
Waste is their inventory problem. It is not a voucher with your name on it. Taking food you didn't pay for still needs a yes from you, and your yes has a string: next week, awkward wave, maybe you avoid the stall.

Say: "That's kind. I'm still going to leave them. I don't want a tab." Then you set them down. If they insist a third time, you can take one tomato you can actually cover with cash, or you walk. Both are nos to the rest.

You're allowed to care about waste and still not be the solution. The market will still close. Unsold food is a real issue and also not yours to fix with a handshake IOU.

If you want to spend the four dollars honestly, ask the price of one thing, pay cash, leave the rest. That's a transaction. That's not a favor.

No without a costume sounds like: "No thanks, I'll put it back." Practice it once in your mouth before you turn around. Then do it. Last-year you had an updating app. This-year you have a sentence. Use the sentence, put the loaf down, and go find an ATM later if you still want tomatoes — or go home. Either is allowed. Owed bread is not.
