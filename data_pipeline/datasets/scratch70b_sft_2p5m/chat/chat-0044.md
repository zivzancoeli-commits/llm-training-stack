### User
Practice is in two hours. I slept badly, my knee has that hot feeling it gets before it gets stupid, and I keep drafting texts to the coach that sound like I'm quitting the team. I don't want a speech. I want to actually send something. I'm sitting in the break room at work eating chips and delaying.

### Assistant
Then we do the next ten minutes like a checklist, not like a TED talk. You're tired. I'm not going to cheerlead you into hero practice. I'm also not a doctor, so I won't name an injury. Hot knee plus bad sleep is enough to justify a rest day as a decision, not as a courtroom exhibit.

Minute one: put the chips down and open a notes app, not the team chat. If you draft in the chat you'll send a half-sentence.

Minutes two to five: write four lines, max. "I need to sit out practice today. My knee is hot and I slept badly. I'll do the mobility stuff at home if you want, but I shouldn't run. I'll check in tomorrow." No apology paragraph. No "I feel like I'm letting everyone down." The coach can feel whatever they feel. Your job is the information.

Minute six: read it once out loud in the break room, quietly. If it sounds like you're asking permission to have a body, cut the question marks. You are informing.

Minutes seven and eight: send it to the coach, not the group thread. Group threads turn rest into a vote.

Minutes nine and ten: put the phone face down. Set a timer so you don't refresh. Eat the rest of the chips if you want. You already did the hard part.

If your coach is the type who replies "walk it off," you still don't owe a second essay in the next ten minutes. You already said no to practice today.

### User
What if they call me? I freeze on the phone. Also I feel like I should offer to still come and watch, which is how I always end up in drills anyway.

### Assistant
If they call in the next ten minutes, let it ring once while you look at the four lines. Then pick up and read the same four lines, slower. You are not required to improvise. "I need a rest day. Knee is hot. I shouldn't run. I'll check in tomorrow." Then stop. Silence on a phone feels rude when you're the intern-brained version of yourself. It isn't. It's the end of the message.

Do not offer to come watch as a peace offering. Watching turns into "just do the warm-up," which is the thing your knee was trying to skip. If they ask you to show up anyway: "I can come if I won't be pulled in. If that's not realistic, I should stay home." Don't put yourself in the room where the extra yes lives. I'm not inventing team rules. Consequences can wait until tomorrow. Today is the rest day.

### User
Okay. I wrote the four lines. I haven't sent them. My ten minutes is turning into twenty because I keep adding "sorry."

### Assistant
Delete the sorries. Count them. If there are three, that's three votes against your own sentence. Send the short version now. Literally now, while we're in this conversation. After it says delivered, do not add a sticker or a second text that undermines it.

Then stand up, fill a water bottle, and name where you'll be at practice time so you're not hovering near the gym. Home, or a walk that isn't a workout. If the knee is more than hot — swollen, locking, you can't bear weight — that's a clinic question, and I still won't diagnose it.

Compressed: notes app, four lines, no question marks, coach only, send, phone down, leave the building in your head.

### User
Sent. No sorry. Phone is face down. I feel like I skipped class.

### Assistant
You skipped a practice you had a reason to skip. That's not a personality collapse. Don't spend the evening writing a make-up novel. Tomorrow you check in with one sentence about how the knee feels, still without a diagnosis. For the next hour, be a person on a break. You already used the ten minutes. Let them be used.
