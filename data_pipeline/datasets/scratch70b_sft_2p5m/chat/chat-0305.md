### User
I'm heating leftover rice. Jordan from another team walked in and started telling me about an HR meeting they just had, names included, voice not low. Two other people can hear. Jordan said "don't repeat this" after already repeating it. I like Jordan fine. I do not want to be a node in this story. The microwave still has two minutes.

### Assistant
You are already a node if you keep nodding. "Don't repeat this" after a kitchen speech is not confidentiality. It is a request that you hold something you did not ask to carry.

You do not have to report them, and you do not have to be their priest. The middle is to stop the details without a speech about policy. "I shouldn't hear this — can we talk about something else?" or "I'm not the right person for HR stuff" plus turning toward your rice. If they keep going, you can leave with the still-hot container. Two minutes of microwave is not a hostage situation.

I will not quote your company's HR rules. I do not have them. I will say kitchens are a bad vault. Names travel.

Are you in any reporting line with Jordan, and did they mention a complaint that involves someone you work with daily?

### User
No reporting line. They named my skip-level once as context, then kept going. I froze and said "that's a lot." They took it as go-ahead. I feel naive.

### Assistant
"That's a lot" is a door they walked through. You can close it now. Naive is just late. Late is still useful.

When the microwave beeps, do not lean on the counter for extra plot. "I need to not hear names. If you need support, that's a friend outside work or whoever you actually trust with this, not the kitchen." Then take the rice. If they look hurt, that is uncomfortable and still allowed.

Do not run to the skip-level with a recap. You would be spreading the same story with a halo on it. Do not Slack a work best friend "you will not believe." That is how you become the second kitchen.

If what you heard sounds like someone is in danger or like a serious legal situation, that is a different fork, and I am not going to diagnose it from a microwave. In ordinary venting, your job is exit, not investigation.

If Jordan follows you to your desk, you can say, "I'm going back to work. I don't want to be in this." Headphones are a visual. They are also honest.

Document nothing unless you are asked by a process you actually belong to. Random notes about a coworker's HR meeting can look like you were collecting.

### User
What if they say I'm being HR-like and cold. I don't want that reputation. I also don't want the skip-level thinking I gossiped.

### Assistant
They may say cold. People who needed an audience sometimes call the audience cruel when it leaves. You can live with "didn't gossip in the kitchen." That reputation is quieter and better.

If the skip-level ever hears a version with you in it, "I asked not to hear it and I left" is the truth. You do not need a preemptive confession.

For the next ten minutes: take the food, say the one line about not hearing names, walk back, do not recap, and eat at your desk. If Jordan texts a recap, do not thumbs-up. A thumbs-up is a signature. Mute if you have to.

You were heating rice. That is still the errand. Let HR stay with the people whose job it is. You can like Jordan and still refuse to be the hallway. Those can both be true while the microwave hits zero. If your stomach is tight, eat somewhere that is not the kitchen so the conversation cannot restart over the sink. A desk and a closed Slack is a complete lunch.
