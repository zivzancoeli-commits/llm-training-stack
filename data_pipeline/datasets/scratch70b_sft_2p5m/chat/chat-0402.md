### User
There's a coworker who does open mics. They asked if they could "run ten minutes" at lunch in the courtyard. Yesterday it was seven minutes of bits about our office, including a joke about my department that used a nickname I hate. I laughed on delay. Today they want to do the rest. I eat lunch to eat lunch. I am not a club. How do I say no without becoming the person who "can't take a joke"?

### Assistant
You can like a person and still not be their room. Lunch is for food.

Say it before they sit: "I can't be a practice audience. I use lunch to eat. Good luck at the mic." If they already sat, say it before the notebook opens. Once the set starts, interrupting feels ruder than a preface. Preface.

You do not need a speech about the nickname today unless you want that as a separate sentence: "Please don't use that nickname, even in bits." Two topics can be two sentences. Mixing them lets them hear only the sensitive one.

I will not invent HR comedy policy. If the bit is about coworkers who did not agree to be material, that is a reason you can name: "I don't want to hear office bits about us."

Do they think you volunteered, or did they just start talking?

### User
They said I have a "good face for testing." I think that means I look like I won't stop them. The courtyard is public. Other people can hear. I don't want to workshop our team in public.

### Assistant
Good face is not consent. Public courtyard is worse, not better.

Use: "Don't test sets on me at lunch, and please don't do office people out here." Then open your sandwich. If they ask why, "I want to eat. I'm not a mic." You can wish them a good Friday night show without booking a matinee.

If they start anyway, you can stand up with your food and move. Relocating is allowed. You do not have to sit through the rest to be a kind colleague. Kind is the no. Sitting is a yes.

If other people laugh, that is their lunch. You still get to leave.

### User
If I move, they'll tell the team I stormed off. I can already hear the bit about me being too sensitive.

### Assistant
They may write a bit. You cannot pre-clear every joke. You can still refuse the rehearsal.

Storming off is slamming a tray. Walking to a different bench with your food is ordinary. If it becomes a story, you can say, "I wanted to eat. I said no to a set." That is a boring correction.

Say the no first so a move is not the first signal. Then eat. You are not a club. You are a person with thirty minutes. Use them. If they need an audience, the open mic is that job. Point them there without your face.

### User
They're walking over with the notebook. Lunch is in the courtyard in two minutes. I packed a sandwich I actually want to eat.

### Assistant
Meet them at the door of the courtyard, not after they sit. Notebook still closed is the moment. "I can't be a practice audience. Please don't run office bits at lunch." If the notebook opens anyway, stand with the sandwich and move to the lobby or your desk. You do not owe seven more minutes because they already walked. Eat where the set is not. If they follow, repeat once and stop adding jokes so they have less material. Your sandwich is the plan. Their open mic is Friday. Those two facts can live on the same calendar without sharing a bench. Say it before the first bit. Then chew. You packed food on purpose. Let the food be the lunch, not their notebook.
