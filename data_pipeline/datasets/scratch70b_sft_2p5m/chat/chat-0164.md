### User
It is Saturday. Every towel I own is in the drum. The machine filled, made a noise like a fork in a blender, and died with the door locked and the tub full of wet clothes and gray water. I have a shift this afternoon. I am tired. Talk to me like we are on a break and tell me what you would actually do in the next ten minutes. Not a repair manual. Ten minutes.

### Assistant
Minute one: unplug it or hit the wall breaker if you can reach it without standing in a puddle. Water plus a live appliance is not a puzzle you solve with optimism. If the cord is behind a wet machine, leave the guts alone.

Minute two: the door. Do not pry it. Many machines unlock after a few minutes unplugged. I will not pretend I know your model. An emergency-release tab, if there is one, is a later minute, not a yank.

Minute three: towels. If the door opens, pull the wet load into a basket. If it stays shut, skip heroics. You have a shift. A locked door is a landlord or a shop problem, not a ten-minute win.

Is this your machine in a house, or a shared laundry you do not control?

### User
Basement of my building. Card-operated. There is a "out of order" marker on the wall that nobody uses. The other washer is full of someone else's jeans. The attendant is not here Saturdays. I already spent the cycle on this one.

### Assistant
Then you are not going to fix a commercial machine in ten minutes, and you should not. You also should not keep feeding the card hoping it reincarnates.

Minutes four and five: stop the water if it is still filling or leaking. If you can see a tap behind the unit and it is a normal shutoff, turn it off. If you cannot tell which valve is which, do not experiment. Take a photo of the error lights, the locked door, the puddle. Photo is your receipt. The machine ate a cycle. I will not invent a refund policy. The photo is what you show whoever restocks the cards.

Minute six: put the out-of-order marker on this machine if it is sitting there. That is for the next person, including you after your shift. If there is a number on the machine or a QR for the vendor, photograph that too. One text to the building contact, not a novel: "Washer 2 dead, door locked, water in drum, 11:40 Saturday. Photos attached." If you have no contact, that is a later problem. Ten minutes cannot create a super.

Minutes seven and eight: the other washer. Someone else's jeans are not your laundry. Do not dump them on a table unless the building has a posted rule that you may. I will not invent that rule. If the cycle is clearly finished and the person vanished, some buildings allow a basket move. If you are guessing, you leave the jeans. You do not start a neighbor war because you are wet and late.

Minutes nine and ten: triage your day, not the appliance. If anything came out only damp, hang it. Gray-water clothes need a real wash later. For this afternoon, wear the least-bad clean thing you still have, or yesterday's shirt. I will not quote a drop-off price. Look up a laundromat on your phone while you walk upstairs.

### User
The door just clicked. Water is still in there. I have eight minutes before I should leave for the bus. Do I drain it or just run.

### Assistant
You run. Eight minutes is not a drain job unless the basement has an obvious front filter you already know how to open without flooding the floor. If you have never opened that filter, today is not the day you learn on gray water in work shoes.

Pop the door, haul the load into something that will not wreck the hallway, shut the door, stick the marker on it, send the photo text, get the bus. Wet clothes in a basket on your bathroom floor are ugly and they will wait. A missed shift because you played plumber is worse.

After work you can come back with dry shoes. Ten minutes was never going to give you clean towels. It was going to keep you from making the basement worse and from being late. You already got the door. Take the win. Leave.
