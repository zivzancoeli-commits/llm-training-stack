### User
It is winter. My roommate showers with the fan off because the fan makes the bathroom cold. Then the mirror sweats, the paint looks sad, and my towel never dries. I have asked once, jokingly. They laughed and said "it's freezing." I need the fan on during and after. I don't want a lecture about mold science. I want a house rule that survives January.

### Assistant
Cold air versus wet walls is the real trade. You can skip the science class and still require the fan.

Say it not as a joke: "I need the fan on for the shower and twenty minutes after. I'll live with the cold. The wet room is worse for both of us." If they hate the blast, they can shut the door, run the fan, and get dressed in their room. That is a sequence, not a personality.

A sticky note on the switch is allowed if they forget: FAN ON, then a timer they already have on a phone. You are not parenting if you also follow the rule.

I will not diagnose the paint or promise mold. Wet plus winter plus no fan is still a bad habit. The rule is the point.

Do they take long showers, or is this a five-minute thing?

### User
Long. Music in there. I wait. I have work. The bathroom is shared. They think I am picky about towels. My towel is mildew-adjacent. I don't want to say mildew because they'll roll their eyes.

### Assistant
You can say "my towel stays wet and smells" without a biology term. Smells are hard to eye-roll if they are standing next to it.

Add a practical: hang your towel in your room until the bathroom habit changes. That is self-defense, not surrender. And still have the talk.

Long showers with music are why after-time matters. "Fan on until the mirror is clear, even if you leave the room" is a clearer rule than twenty minutes if they cannot count. A cheap plug timer is optional. I will not pick a brand or a price. A phone alarm labeled FAN is enough.

If they refuse, you can shower first in the morning so you get a dry room, and you can take the note to a house meeting of two. If they still won't, you are in a wet-apartment problem, not a towel problem. Tonight is the sentence plus the note.

### User
I'll talk tonight. What if they turn it on while I watch and off as soon as I leave the hall?

### Assistant
Then you are in a compliance problem. You can say you noticed and you need it to stay on after they go to their room. If it keeps happening, you document dates for yourself and you decide whether this house works through spring. I will not jump to moving out as a first trick. First is a clear rule, a note, your towel in your room, and one follow-up: "The fan was off again. Please keep it on after."

Talk when the bathroom is dry so it is not a mid-steam fight. Tonight, after dinner. Short. Then you put the note on the switch. Winter is long. The fan can be on anyway. Ask for that. Let the laugh from last time stay last time. This time you are not joking.

### User
They're in the bathroom now with music. Fan off. I can wait until they finish or I can knock during the song.

### Assistant
Wait until they finish the shower so you are not yelling over water. When the door opens, before they vanish: "Fan on, please, and leave it on after. The room is too wet." If they have already left it off, reach in and turn it on yourself this once, then have the evening talk. A sticky note tonight. Towel in your room. You are not picky for wanting dry cloth. You are sharing a box of steam in January. Knock only if you think they forgot and the music will cover a shout — usually the doorway sentence is enough. Catch them in the hall. Then dinner talk. Winter is long. The switch can stay up.
