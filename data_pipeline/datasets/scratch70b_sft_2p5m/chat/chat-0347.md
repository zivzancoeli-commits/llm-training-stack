### User
It's 4:50. The deck is due to the client at 9 a.m. I finished my slides at 3. Ellis comes over, laughing, and says they "haven't really started the appendix" and can I "stay and bang it out" because I "know the numbers." I do know the numbers. I also have a class at 6:30 that I pay for. Ellis had this appendix on the tracker since Monday. Our manager already left. I don't want the client to fail. I also don't want to be the person who always saves Ellis. What do I say in the next ten minutes?

### Assistant
You say you are leaving, and you say what you can hand off in ten minutes.

"I can't stay. Class at 6:30. I can dump the number sources in a doc and show you which slides are already done. The appendix is yours tonight." Then you actually dump the sources. Help is a file, not a second shift.

Do not laugh along. Laughter is how "bang it out" becomes 9 p.m. Do not apologize for the class. The class is a calendar fact, like the 9 a.m. deadline.

If the client failing is a real risk, ping the manager once, facts only: "Appendix still with Ellis. I offered sources. I have to leave at 5:15." Then you leave. You are not required to secretly be the night team.

I will not invent your office's overtime rules. If you are hourly, leaving is also a pay issue. If you are salaried, it is still a pattern issue.

Has Ellis done this on the last two decks, or is this a one-off panic?

### User
Last two decks. I stayed once until 8:40. The other time I rebuilt a chart at 7. They brought bagels the next day. I hate that I want the bagel credit and also hate Ellis a little.

### Assistant
Bagels are not a staffing plan. They are how guilt gets frosted.

Tonight you break the streak on purpose. Ten minutes of sources. Then you walk. If Ellis looks betrayed, that is the withdrawal symptom of a system that used you.

In the tracker, write what you finished and the time. "Slides 1–12 done 3 p.m. Appendix: Ellis." Future-you needs that when someone asks why the appendix was thin. Present-you needs the class.

If Ellis tries "just thirty minutes," you can say, "I don't have thirty." Do not bargain down your class. If you bargain, you will be there at 8:40 again, slightly later, slightly nicer.

Tomorrow, a short note to the manager is allowed: "I want appendix owners assigned earlier. I can't be the late stay on this." You can send it after the client meeting so it does not look like you sank the ship. Or you can send it tonight if you want the timestamp. Your call. The content is process, not "Ellis is lazy." Names in a process note should be about ownership, not character.

### User
Ellis is standing at my monitor. They said "please" in a small voice. My coat is on the chair. If I stand up it will be a whole thing.

### Assistant
Stand up. The whole thing is already happening. It is just happening on their terms while you sit.

Coat on. "I have to go. Doc is in the channel with the number sources. You've got the appendix." If they keep talking, you can repeat "I have to go" while you walk. You do not need a new paragraph.

Do not sit back down "for a second." Seconds are how 8:40 is born.

If the deck is bad at 9 a.m., that is a management problem and an Ellis problem. It is not proof you are cruel. You already did your slides. You already stayed twice. The small voice is effective. It is also a request you can refuse.

Go to class. Put the phone in a bag on mute until the break. You can check whether Ellis sent a desperate ping. You do not have to answer it with your body. Ten minutes of files. Then the hallway. Then the class you pay for. Let the appendix belong to the person who owns it on the tracker.
