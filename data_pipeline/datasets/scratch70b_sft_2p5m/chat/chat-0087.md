### User
Saturday is the first time I'm cooking for my partner's parents. Not a holiday, just dinner at our place. I can cook for us. I freeze. I keep picking a menu and then talking myself out of it because I don't know if they eat pork, or how spicy is too spicy, or whether "homemade" means I have to make the bread too. My partner said "they'll eat anything," which is the least useful sentence I've heard this week. I was going to do a roast chicken, potatoes, a green thing, and a cake mix cake, then I decided that looked lazy, then I decided a three-course thing would look try-hard. I don't actually know what "right" is here.

### Assistant
I don't know your in-laws, and "they'll eat anything" is a guess dressed as reassurance. I would not bet the evening on it.

What I can say without bluffing: first dinners like this usually fail from ambition and mystery allergies, not from a cake mix. A roast chicken, potatoes, a vegetable, and a dessert you can finish is a complete meal. It is not lazy. Lazy is pretending you know their rules when you don't.

I also don't know if anyone is vegetarian, keeps halal or kosher, has a nut allergy, or thinks cilantro tastes like soap. Those are not personality quirks. They are constraints. If you don't have answers, the honest move is to get them, not to design around a rumor.

Ask your partner three questions, written down, not in passing: Do they eat meat, and which meats are out? Any foods that will make someone sick or genuinely upset, not just "Mom isn't a huge fan of olives"? What does a normal Saturday dinner look like at their house, restaurant-fancy or Tuesday-night? If your partner still says "anything," ask them to text their parent one message: "Any allergies or no-gos? I'm planning chicken, potatoes, a vegetable, dessert." That is not rude. That is how adults keep people out of the ER.

Until those answers land, I would not lock a spicy marinade, a pork roast, or a peanut sauce. I would also not invent a bread program. Store bread or a bakery loaf is fine. I am not going to tell you a bakery name or a price. Get whatever you can pick up without turning Friday into a second job.

If they confirm chicken is okay, keep the menu you already almost had. Salt the chicken ahead if you know how. Potatoes you already make. One vegetable you can cook without a new technique. Dessert you can finish even if the chicken runs late. Cake mix is allowed. Fruit and something store-bought is allowed. A from-scratch project you have never timed in this kitchen is how Saturday becomes a hostage situation.

### User
They eat chicken. No nuts in the house is the only hard rule. His dad "doesn't like things too spicy," which still isn't a number. I do a paprika rub on chicken sometimes. Is that too spicy, or am I overthinking the paprika?

### Assistant
I don't know his dad's mouth. Paprika can be sweet and mild or it can be a hot powder, and people use the same word for both. I will not pretend I can taste their dinner from here.

Treat "not too spicy" as: no chili heat you would notice as a dare. If your paprika is the sweet kind and you already use it on chicken for yourselves without anyone reaching for water, it is probably a seasoning, not an attack. If your jar is labeled hot, smoked-hot, or you usually add cayenne "for interest," skip the extra heat for this meal. Salt, garlic, pepper, lemon, herbs you already trust. You can put hot sauce on the table. People who want fire will find it. People who don't will not have to pick around a crust.

Overthinking the paprika is real. Underthinking the nuts is the one that matters. Read labels on the cake mix, the oil, the bread, anything processed. "May contain" is a judgment call I cannot make for a household that said no nuts. If you are unsure about a mix, pick a dessert with fewer mystery ingredients: baked fruit, ice cream with a clean label, a chocolate you can actually read. I am not going to certify a brand.

You already almost had the right menu. The remaining work is timing, not proving you are a chef. Want help blocking the afternoon so the chicken and the potatoes are not fighting for the same oven shelf, or are you good once the spice question is settled?
