### User
The people on the ground-floor patio are grilling. My windows are open because I don't have AC. The smoke is in my living room. My eyes sting. I closed one window and the kitchen still smells like a campfire. I like that they cook outside. I do not like eating their smoke. It's 6:40 on a Tuesday. I have been pacing. I don't know whether to go downstairs in a pointed way or text the building chat. Last time I mentioned smell in the chat, someone called me "city."

### Assistant
Close the remaining windows on the smoke side. Put a fan blowing out a window on the other side if you have a second exposure. A wet towel at the bottom of a door helps a little if the hall is cleaner than the patio air. This is triage, not a personality.

Then decide the human path. Downstairs in person is better than a group chat that already called you "city." Chat is a crowd. A crowd makes people perform. The patio is two or three humans and a grill.

Script, if you go down: "Hey — the smoke is coming straight into my windows. Could you move the grill out, or close the lid more, or give me a heads-up so I can shut everything?" You are asking for geometry and warning, not for them to stop eating.

I will not invent your building's grilling rules. If the lease bans patio grills, that is a later email to management with a date and a time. Tonight is air.

Do you know them well enough to knock, or have you only seen them from above?

### User
I've said hi. They seemed fine. There are four of them and a lot of meat. I would be the person interrupting a party. I have a window fan I can reverse. The chat thing still stings.

### Assistant
Skip the chat. Reverse the fan to push air out if the smoke is coming in that window; experiment for two minutes. Close what you can. If it is still a campfire, go downstairs.

Interrupting a Tuesday grill is allowed. You live above the smoke. Knock, wait, say the script, then leave. Do not accept a beer as payment for your lungs unless you want a beer. A beer is not a filter.

If they say they are almost done, ask "how long" and set a timer. If "almost" is an hour, you can say you'll keep windows shut and please lid it. If they get chilly, you still said the true thing. You do not have to win a debate about charcoal.

If the smoke is so bad you feel unwell in a way that scares you, you leave the apartment for a walk or a store and you do not tough it out as a principle. I am not diagnosing. Stinging eyes plus a closed box is a reason to get other air.

Management in the morning is for a pattern: Tuesdays, smoke in unit, windows. One email. No manifesto about "city." Facts.

### User
I went down and one of them said they'd "try to keep it down." The grill is still open. Smoke is still coming. I feel stupid for asking.

### Assistant
You asked. They offered a vague try. Vague try plus an open grill is not a solution.

Second pass, shorter: "It's still coming in upstairs. Can you put the lid on?" Then go back up. You are not stupid. You are repeating because the air did not change. One repeat is reasonable. A third is a log for management, not a standoff by the coals.

If you cannot get the lid, close up, fan, leave for twenty minutes if you need to. Buy the cheap box fan later if this is their summer hobby. You should not have to, and you might.

Do not return to the group chat to narrate. That is how "city" happens. You already did the neighborly version. Lid, then documentation if Tuesdays keep happening. Eat your own dinner. Let the meat be theirs. Let the smoke stop being yours.
