### User
My manager just pinged me: take this vendor call at my desk in ten minutes. Open office. Keyboards, a blender in the kitchen, two sales guys who laugh like it's a sport. The vendor is going to talk numbers and a thing we haven't announced. I do not want to do this at my desk. I also don't want to invent a doctor's appointment or a fake meeting in the basement. I'm tired of the cute excuses. How do I refuse the desk call without sounding like a toddler, and how do I actually take the call if I have to.

### Assistant
You say no to the desk. You don't owe a medical drama.

To your manager: "I can't take this at my desk. It's not private and it's loud. I need a closed room or I'll call from the stairwell/phone booth/outside. Which room can I use?" That's a no plus a next step. It isn't "I have a thing." It's "this setting is wrong for this call."

If they push "just use a headset, everyone does," you don't escalate into a speech about culture. You repeat the constraint: "Headset doesn't fix the people two feet from me hearing the vendor. I'll take it in a room or I'll reschedule." Then you wait. Repeating once is adult. Inventing a dentist is how you train them that only fake reasons count.

I don't know your office's booking rules. I'm not going to pretend I do. If the rooms are all taken, you look with your eyes, then you say so. "No rooms. I'll take it from my car / the loading dock / the empty conference leftover." Pick the least stupid quiet you actually have. Don't promise a boardroom you didn't get.

What kind of manager is this — the kind who hears "not private" as a real sentence, or the kind who only moves if you make it their problem?

### User
The second kind, probably. He hates "process." He'll say I'm overthinking. The sales guys will definitely comment if I stand up and leave. Also I already accepted the calendar hold, so I feel stuck.

### Assistant
Accepting a calendar hold is not a blood oath to sit in the blast radius. You can still move.

He hates process, so don't send a manifesto. One ping: "Taking this off the floor. Not private here." Then you stand up. The sales guys commenting is weather. You don't explain to them. If someone asks where you're going, "call" is a complete sentence.

If he replies "overthinking," you don't defend your personality. You name the risk in uncle-plain words: "Vendor is going to say stuff I don't want as background noise for twelve other people. I'm not doing that at my desk." If he still wants you at the desk, that's him choosing the leak. You can still walk. I'm not going to invent an HR policy that saves you. Some managers only learn when the call audio is bad and the vendor asks you to repeat yourself four times. That's not a strategy. It's just true.

On the call itself, first sentence to the vendor: "Heads up, I moved so I could hear you. If it drops, I'll call back." Then you do the meeting. You don't apologize for the office. You don't narrate the blender.

If you cannot leave — truly cannot, badge, coverage, whatever — then you still don't invent an excuse to the vendor. You say, "I'm on an open floor. Can we keep this high-level and I'll follow up in writing on the pieces that shouldn't be overheard?" That's a no to the sensitive part, not a fairy tale about why you're whispering.

### User
What if the vendor asks why I won't talk at my desk. They already know we have an open office. I don't want to sound shady.

### Assistant
You're not shady. You're the person who noticed a door. "Open office, I stepped out so we're not broadcasting" is enough. If they joke, let them joke. You don't need a cover story. Cover stories are how this gets weird.

After the call, you don't write a retrospective. If your manager asks why you left, same sentence as before. No extra softness. No "ha ha I'm so extra." Skeptical people relax when you stop decorating.

Next time, decline the desk in the hold: "I'll join from a room; ping me which one." Train the calendar, not your guilt. For the next ten minutes: message, stand, find a door, take the call, come back. That's the no. It doesn't need a costume.
