### User
I moved here from a place where winter meant a hoodie. It snowed last night and I am supposed to get to work. My shoes are canvas. The sidewalk looks like a wet parking lot with extra personality. People keep sending me joke memes about "welcome to real winter." I do not need a personality. I need to not fall over and not freeze on the bus stop. Also I might be overreacting. You can tell me if I should just shut up and walk.

### Assistant
I can help, but I want to check first: do you still want a walk-through, or are you mostly venting and you already decided you are going to tough it out in the canvas shoes? I will not dump a packing list on you if you are done with advice.

### User
I still want help. I have twenty-five minutes before I have to leave. I own one puffy jacket that I thought was a joke when I bought it. No boots. Gloves are those thin touchscreen ones. I do not have a car.

### Assistant
Twenty-five minutes is enough to not be a cartoon, not enough to become a mountaineer. We will use what you have.

Canvas shoes on packed snow are ice skates with extra fabric. If you have any other closed shoe with a harder sole, wear those. If canvas is truly all you have, put on the thickest socks you own, maybe two pairs if they still fit, and shorten your stride. Walk like the sidewalk owes you money and you are collecting it slowly. Hands out of pockets so you can catch a rail. Uncool is cheaper than a wrist.

The puffy jacket is not a joke today. Wear it. Shirt underneath, then the puffy, then if you have any extra layer that blocks wind — even a light rain shell — put that on the outside. Wind at a bus stop is the part that makes warm-city people think the temperature number is lying.

Thin touchscreen gloves are better than bare hands. Wear them. If your ears are uncovered, a hoodie under the puffy, hood up, beats pride.

Phone: put it in an inside pocket. Cold batteries quit. I am not going to invent a percentage. Just keep it warm against you.

Give yourself more time than a dry day. Wipe your feet on the mat so you do not skate in the lobby.

At the stop: stand where you are not in the splash line of the street. If the stop has a wall, use it as a windbreak. If your fingers go numb, that is your body asking for pockets, not a character test.

I am not going to scare you with a frostbite lecture as if I am examining you. I also will not tell you this is "nothing." First snow is disorienting. The memes are people who forgot that.

If you have any plastic bags, a bag over each sock inside the canvas shoe is a desperate trick that can keep meltwater off your skin for one commute. It feels terrible. Take the bags off at work.

If you have a dry pair at the office, carry them in a bag. Commute ugly, work normal.

When you get home tonight, if you still want help, we can talk about actual boots and which sidewalks get cleared. Right now: layers you already own, shorter steps, phone inside the jacket, extra minutes, bags in the shoes if they are going to soak.

I will not add a pep talk. If at any point you decide you would rather just go, say so. I will not keep coaching a person who is already out the door.

### User
The memes are from coworkers. Should I reply?

### Assistant
You do not have to. A thumbs-up is enough if you want to be polite. A joke back is extra labor you do not owe a slush morning. Get to the bus. You can be funny when your socks are dry. If you want more gear talk later, ask. If you are done, you are done.
