### User
The app said ready at 4:00. It's 5:22. I'm at the pharmacy counter. One pharmacist, a line of six, a tech who keeps saying "it's in the queue." This is an antibiotic I was told to start tonight. I don't have extra doses at home. The person in front of me is arguing about vitamins. I have a kid in the car with a tablet on 12 percent. I don't want to be the person who snaps, and I don't want to drive home empty.

### Assistant
You're not empty yet. You're in a line with one pharmacist. That's a bottleneck, not a personal insult. Don't snap at the vitamin argument. It will not make the queue shorter. It will make the pharmacist slower.

I'm not a doctor. I won't tell you what the antibiotic is for or whether delaying a dose is safe. I will say: if the prescriber told you to start tonight, you stay until you get a clear yes, a clear later time, or a transfer. Guessing at home is worse.

Can you see your name on the bag rack, or is it still "in the queue" with no bag?

### User
No bag I can see. The tech looked me up and said the pharmacist has to check it. Kid just knocked on the window. I told them five more minutes which might be a lie. I could come back at 7 but I don't know if they close.

### Assistant
Don't invent closing time. Look at the door, the wall, or ask the tech one factual question: "What time do you stop filling?" Write the number down. If 7 is after that, 7 is not a plan.

For the next ten minutes: stay in the line. When you reach a person, one sentence: "App said ready at 4. It's an antibiotic to start tonight. Is it still being checked?" Then stop. Don't add the kid, the tablet, the vitamin guy. Extra story makes you sound like a scene. They can see the window. They cannot hear a novel.

If they say twenty minutes, you can take the kid on a loop around the store or sit in the car in the lot if you can still see the entrance. Set a timer. Coming back at a made-up 7 without a confirmed fill time is how people find a dark door.

If they say they don't have it, ask whether they can transfer or call the prescriber. I don't know their stock. I won't pretend. A transfer is a real option at some counters and a shrug at others. You need a next step with a clock on it, not a vibe.

Text whoever is at home, if anyone: "Still at pharmacy, kid with me." Then put the phone down so the 12 percent lasts for a game, not for your anxiety refresh of the app.

### User
The vitamin person just got loud. The pharmacist looked at me like I might be next. I hate being grouped with that. Also I might cry which would be worse.

### Assistant
You're not grouped until you match the volume. Stay at indoor voice. Crying is allowed and inconvenient. If it starts, look at the floor, breathe, point at the screen with your name if it's there. You don't have to orate through a tight throat.

When it's your turn, be the shortest person they help all hour. Name, antibiotic, start tonight, time check. If they apologize, "okay" is enough. Don't soothe them and don't punish them for the line.

If you leave without the bottle, leave with a written pickup time or a transfer promise, not with "we'll see." "We'll see" is how tonight disappears.

Kid in the car: five minutes was a guess. Go give them a true update at the window if the line stalls: "It's taking longer. Tablet on low. We're staying until I know." Truth is shorter than a second lie.

You can start the medicine tonight if they hand you the bag. If they don't, you call the clinic number on the label or the after-hours line the prescriber gave you and you say the pharmacy delayed it. That's a clinical question for them, not for me. Your job at 5:22 is the counter, the clock, and a kid whose tablet is dying. Stay in the queue. Keep your voice smaller than the vitamins.
