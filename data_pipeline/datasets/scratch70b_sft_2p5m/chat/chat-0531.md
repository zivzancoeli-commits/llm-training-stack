### User
I buy the dish soap. Every time. Roommate uses it, leaves the bottle in the sink with a millimeter left, never replaces it. We've lived together eight months. I have mentioned it twice in that joke voice. Joke voice failed. I don't want a whiteboard chore chart like we're in a sitcom. I also don't want to keep funding the sink. What do I actually say.

### Assistant
Say it in the kitchen, once, in a normal voice, with the empty bottle in your hand. Not in a group chat essay. Not as a joke.

"I keep buying the soap. You use it. I need you to buy the next bottle, or we split it." Two options. That's the whole meeting.

Joke voice failed because joke voice lets both of you pretend it wasn't a request. Eight months is enough data. You are not being petty about a millimeter. You are naming a pattern.

I will not invent a price for dish soap. Buy whatever you already buy, or ask them to grab any brand that cuts grease. Brand lectures are how this becomes a personality fight.

Do not hide the bottle. Do not write "Sam's soap" on it in marker unless you already tried talking and they still pretend not to see. Start with talking.

Is the rest of the kitchen mostly shared-okay, or is soap the leak in a bigger "I buy, you use" setup?

### User
Bigger setup. Paper towels, trash bags, sometimes my oat milk. Soap is just the thing I can point at because the bottle is empty again tonight. They pay rent on time. They're not a monster. I sound like a monster even thinking this.

### Assistant
Paying rent does not include your soap. Those are different bills. You can say that without a villain speech.

Pick one item for the conversation so it doesn't become a trial. Soap is a good one because it's visible and cheap enough that "I'll get it" is an easy yes. If you dump oat milk, paper towels, and bags into the first sentence, people hear "you are a bad person" and stop listening.

After they agree to the next bottle, you can add: "Trash bags and paper towels too — let's alternate, or we'll do a shared run." Alternate is simpler than a chart. First month they buy bags, you buy soap. Then you switch. If they forget, you remind once with a photo of the empty thing, not a thesis.

Oat milk is yours unless you offered it. "That's mine — grab your own" is allowed. Community food only exists if both people opted in. You didn't opt in by leaving it on a shelf.

You don't sound like a monster. You sound like someone who ran a store out of a shared cabinet.

### User
What if they say they "thought it was communal" and look wounded. I will want to buy the soap just to end the face.

### Assistant
They can think it was communal. You can still change the rule. "I get why you thought that. I'm not doing the buying alone anymore." Wounded face is not a receipt.

If you cave in the moment, you teach the face. Buy the current empty bottle if you need dishes tonight. That's survival. Put the next bottle on their name out loud: "Your turn after this one." Then leave it. If the next one is also you, you ask again, shorter: "Soap's gone. It's on you."

No sitcom chart. No hiding. No joke voice. One kitchen conversation, two options, next bottle assigned. That's the job.

### User
Okay. Kitchen, normal voice, empty bottle in hand, two options, oat milk stays mine. If they joke back I will not joke. Next bottle has a name on it. That's enough for tonight.

### Assistant
Yes. Tonight is dishes and a named next bottle. Don't add the paper towels in the same breath unless they immediately say "what else." If they ask, you can name bags as the next item, still not a trial. If they don't ask, soap is the win.

If the millimeter theater happens again in two weeks, you skip the origin story. "Soap's gone. It's on you" is already written. You can buy a backup bottle and keep it in your room for your own sink nights without turning that into a secret war. Backup is for you. The shared bottle still has a buyer.

You lived here eight months on joke voice. One normal sentence is the upgrade. Put the empty in their hand or on their side of the sink and go. The face can be wounded. The sink still needs soap that someone paid for.
