### User
The building next door started jackhammering at 7:10. My neighbor has a four-month-old. I can hear the baby some nights, not in a nasty way, just through the wall. I keep thinking I should "do something" — offer my apartment during naps, complain to the city, buy them a white noise machine. My uncle voice in my head says this is none of my business and I will make it weird. I still want to be useful if they actually want help. Talk to me like that uncle, but I am asking anyway.

### Assistant
Uncle first: you do not know if they want a stranger in their nap schedule. You also do not know if the job is legal hours or whether a white noise machine would even reach that room. I am not going to invent a noise ordinance for your city. I do not have your address, so I will not quote a number and pretend it is the law.

Help that nobody asked for is how people end up with a neighbor who is now a project.

So the first move is not a gadget and not a complaint. It is a check: do they still want anything from you.

Have you spoken to them this week, or is this all soundtrack and imagination?

### User
I said "rough morning" in the hallway yesterday and they kind of laughed. The other parent had the baby in a carrier and looked wrecked. I did not offer anything. I don't even know their names, just the unit number. I feel like I missed the window.

### Assistant
You did not miss a sacred window. You missed a hallway. There will be another one.

Do not lead with the city. A complaint can be the right tool later. It is a blunt tool. If they are renters, you might be kicking a hornet nest they have to live with after you feel heroic. If they already called, you duplicating it might do nothing. I do not know. Ask before you file.

Offer one concrete, time-limited thing, then let them refuse.

Hallway script, uncle-plain: "I'm next door. The drilling is loud. If you want a quieter room for a stretch this week, you can use my place for an hour. If that's weird, ignore me. If you already have a plan, good." Then you shut up. You do not add the white noise machine in the same breath. That turns a check into a shopping trip.

Notice the out: "if that's weird, ignore me." You are not trapping them into gratitude. If they say no, you say "okay" and you mean it. That is the whole test of whether you wanted to help them or to feel like a helper.

If they say yes, you set a start and stop. Not "anytime." Anytime is how your living room becomes a nursery you did not agree to. "Thursday 1 to 2" is a favor. An open door is a new roommate.

I would not buy them a machine unless they ask for one. People have opinions about sleep gadgets. Also I will not quote a price. If you want to be useful with money, ask: "Do you need anything from the drugstore while I'm out?" That is smaller and easier to decline.

### User
What if they think I'm complaining about the baby, not the jackhammer? And should I still call whoever is in charge of the construction?

### Assistant
Then you name the jackhammer in the first clause so the baby is not the subject. "The construction next door is brutal. The baby is not the problem." One sentence. Adults can hear that if you do not bury it in a speech.

Calling "whoever" without asking them is you deciding their week. Check first: "Have you already reported the hours? If you want a second call from another unit, tell me who you talked to. If you want me to stay out of it, I will." If they do not know who to call, you can look at the permit board on the fence together. I will not invent the agency name. The sign on the site usually has a number. Photograph it. Do not promise results.

If they never answer the hallway offer, you drop it. Uncle was half right: it might be none of your business. The other half is you asked once, clearly, with an easy no. That is neighbor, not savior.

You still want help, or are you mostly discharging guilt? If it is guilt, one honest offer is enough. If they take it, you follow the clock. If they do not, you leave their family alone. That is respect, not coldness.
