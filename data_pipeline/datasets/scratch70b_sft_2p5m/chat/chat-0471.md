### User
Chris is moving "for a month" and asked to put two plastic totes and a bike in my closet. My closet is already a Tetris of winter coats and a vacuum that doesn't sit anywhere else. They said "you won't even notice." I can notice from here. They're coming at 7 with a car. I don't want to be the friend who says no to storage. I also don't want to sleep next to a bike wheel. How do I decline without a speech about square footage?

### Assistant
You decline the closet. You can still help in a smaller way if you want.

"I don't have room. The closet is full. I can't take the totes or the bike. If you need a hand loading a storage unit or a ride, I can do that. I can't be the unit." That's the fork. If you don't want to load, drop the offer. Don't invent a warehouse in a hallway.

"You won't even notice" is their hope. You can notice. "I'll notice. That's why I'm saying no." Last year's you would have said yes and then hated the vacuum. This year's you can have a full closet that stays yours.

I won't invent what a storage locker costs. Don't guess one as a solution unless you actually know. You can say, "A locker might exist; I can't store it here."

Are they already on the way, or can you text before 7?

### User
They're already driving. ETA 20 minutes. They texted a photo of the totes. They're bigger than I pictured. There's a third bag. The bike has a flat. I feel the yes forming because they're mid-drive.

### Assistant
Text now, before the car is at the curb. Mid-drive is not a contract.

"I don't have space. Don't bring them up. I can help you think of the next place when you get here, but they can't come in." If they arrive anyway, you meet them at the car, not the closet. That's geography. A tote on the sidewalk is their problem. A tote in your bedroom is yours.

If they cry or get sharp, you still don't make space that isn't there. You can offer water and a phone charger while they call whoever has a garage. You don't owe a closet to end their discomfort.

The extra bag is how two totes become a household. "Nothing comes in" is easier than negotiating volume on the stairs.

### User
They said "then what was I supposed to do, it was on the way." That's not a question. They're five minutes out. Do I wait downstairs?

### Assistant
Yes. Downstairs. Don't let the first scene be your open closet.

When they pull up: the sentence, no tour of the Tetris. If they try to carry a tote "just to see," you don't open the door for a fitting. "It won't fit. I'm not doing a test." Then you stand there like a person who already decided.

You were supposed to be asked when there was still time, and you were supposed to be allowed to say no. "On the way" is pressure. You can be kind and still not be a depot.

If they ask to leave the bike in the hallway "just overnight," that's still your hallway and still a no unless you actually want a wheel by the coats. "Car or a locker. Not here." Then you can help look up a storage place on your phone on the sidewalk if you have energy. I won't invent a price or a brand. You can also not help look it up. Water is enough help.

Next twenty minutes: downstairs, no, optional water, they keep the totes in the car. Your closet stays coats and a vacuum. That's the whole month of storage, without you living next to a flat bike. Send the text if you haven't. Then go down. The yes can stay unformed. That's allowed, even in a driveway.
