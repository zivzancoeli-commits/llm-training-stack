### User
Dev asked me not to tell our roommate, Lina, that he's been paying fifty less than her for three months because the landlord likes him. I don't even live there. I crash on weekends. Now Lina is venting to me that rent feels uneven. Dev wants me to "just let it go." I feel like a locked box with someone else's money story in it. I don't want to blow up their house. I also don't want to lie to Lina's face.

### Assistant
Do not become the vault for a rent split you do not share. You do not live there. Weekend crash is not a third roommate vote.

I will not invent the real amounts as a rule, and I will not invent a price for anyone's share. You already heard a number from Dev. Treat it as his claim, not as something you will audit. I also will not invent a housing statute. Uneven rent happens for a lot of reasons, some ugly, some boring. Your job is what you will say when Lina vents, not a courtroom.

To Dev, tonight: "Don't put me in the middle. I won't lie to Lina. I'm also not going to volunteer your arrangement. You need to talk to her." That is the split. No volunteer dump. No cover story.

To Lina, when she vents: you can say you don't know their lease math and she should ask Dev and the landlord in writing. You can say it feels off to you as a guest. You should not invent a spreadsheet. You should not pretend you know nothing if she asks you a direct yes/no you already know. Direct questions are the trap. We will get there.

Did Lina ask you a direct question yet, or is she only venting?

### User
Only venting so far. She said "you're around, doesn't it seem like I pay more." I changed the subject to dishes. Dev texted "thanks for having my back." I did not have his back. I just panicked.

### Assistant
Correct Dev's story. Text: "I didn't have your back. I changed the subject. I won't lie if she asks. Talk to her." If you leave "thanks for having my back" on the table, he will treat you as a co-conspirator. Clear it.

For Lina's "doesn't it seem like I pay more": you already dodged once. Next time, do not invent ignorance. You can say, "I don't live here, so I don't see the ledger. If you think it's uneven, ask Dev to show the charges." That points her at the person who actually pays. It does not require you to recite his fifty.

If she asks, "Did Dev tell you something," that is the direct question. You can say yes, he told you they have different amounts, and she should get it from him, not from a weekend guest. You do not have to perform a leak with extra details. You also do not have to say "no" when the answer is yes. Lying is the part you already do not want.

I will not tell you to forward screenshots. I will not tell you to stay silent forever. The middle is: Dev speaks, you refuse the vault job, you refuse to gaslight Lina.

### User
If I tell her he said fifty less, their weekend will explode and I sleep on that couch. If I say nothing, I'm the liar. There is no clean night.

### Assistant
There is a cleaner night than both of those. You are not required to lead with the number. You are required not to deny it if asked, and not to keep Dev cozy.

Push the conversation to them while you still have a couch that is not a bunker: "Dev, I crash here. I need you to tell Lina this week. If she asks me a direct question I will not fake confusion." Give him a clock. This week is a clock. Then you make other sleep plans for one night if you think the talk will be loud. A friend's floor is cheaper than a secret.

If he refuses to tell her, your next Lina conversation is: "You should compare what you each pay. I don't want to be the messenger, and I won't pretend it's even if it isn't." That is not a spreadsheet. That is you stepping out of the box.

Do not keep the secret as a loyalty test. Weekend guests do not owe a rent conspiracy. Text Dev now. Cancel the "had my back" myth. Let their lease be theirs. You can still bring groceries and not bring a cover story.
