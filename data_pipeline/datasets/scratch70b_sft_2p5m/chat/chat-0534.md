### User
A friend is organizing a group order — snacks, skin stuff, a giant bulk thing from a site they love. They put me in the spreadsheet. There's a cell with my name and a blank for how much I'm in for. I cannot afford extra this month. Rent is the month. I don't want to say I'm broke in a group of people who buy things for fun. They already texted "just put something small." Help me get my name off the sheet without a speech.

### Assistant
You can leave the spreadsheet. Blank cells are not a contract.

Message the friend who added you, not the whole group: "I'm going to sit this one out. Please take my name off." Small, final. "Something small" is still money you do not have. You do not have to auction your floor.

You do not owe a poverty essay. "Not this month" is enough. If they press, you can repeat "I'm sitting it out" and stop listing reasons. Reasons get negotiated. The sheet is not a friendship test.

I will not invent what the bulk order costs or what "small" means. You already know it doesn't fit. Trust that.

Are they the kind of friend who will pay your share and say "get me later," which is a second trap?

### User
Yes. They've done "I'll cover you" for concert tickets and then it sat weird for weeks. I paid it back late and felt watched. I don't want a float. I also don't want them to announce in the chat that I'm out because I'm cheap.

### Assistant
Say no to the float before they offer it. "Don't cover me. I'm out, not delayed." That sentence exists because you already lived the concert version.

You cannot fully control whether they announce it. You can ask: "No need to explain it in the chat. Just delete the row." Most organizers would rather delete a row than write a novel. If they still post "X couldn't join," you do not have to reply. Silence is allowed. A reply that says "yeah money's tight" in a group of fun-buyers is optional, not owed.

Cheap is their possible story. Your story is a month that is already spoken for. You don't have to correct the group mythology unless someone asks you directly. If they ask, "Didn't have it in the budget" is one line. Then you talk about anything else.

Stay out of the spreadsheet. Don't put a zero in the cell as a cute joke. Jokes linger. Deletion is cleaner.

### User
They just sent a crying-laugh emoji and "come on, it's not that serious." It feels serious in my chest.

### Assistant
"Not that serious" is about their cart, not your rent. You can answer the emoji in plain text: "I know. I'm still out. Take my name off?" Question mark because you need the action. If they leave the row, you delete it yourself if you have edit access. If you don't, you're done asking after two times. A third plea is you performing.

Chest-serious is information. Group buys feel like belonging. Belonging that costs a bill you can't pay is a bad club. You can still like the friend. You can still skip the sheet.

No float. No small something. No joke zero. Name off. That's the whole job. The emoji can sit there unanswered if you already sent the line.

### User
I sent "I'm sitting this one out, take my name off, don't cover me." They replied "k" and left the row. I can still see my name. Do I delete it or wait so I don't look aggressive.

### Assistant
Delete it if you can edit. Waiting for them to parent the spreadsheet is how your name sits there until someone fills a number for you. A blank under your name is an invitation. An absent row is a closed door.

If you cannot edit, screenshot the "k" and your sit-out message. If a number appears anyway, you reply in the organizer thread once: "That isn't me. Please zero it out. I'm not in this order." Then you don't keep watching the sheet like a stock.

"K" is enough of an acknowledgment. You don't need a warmer emoji from them to make the no real. Group buys move fast and they also forget people who left. Help them forget you on this one. Rent stays the month. The cart can go without you.
