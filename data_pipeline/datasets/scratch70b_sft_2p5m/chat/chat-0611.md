### User
We matched, we texted, they suggested "come over, I'll cook." I do not want to start in their apartment. I want a public place. I have never said that as a first date rule out loud. I don't want a safety lecture that treats me like a child, and I don't want to invent a story about a roommate. First time. How do I say it dry?

### Assistant
You can want a public table without a cover story. The sentence is: "I'd rather meet at a cafe or a bar first. I'm not coming over." If they already offered food, "Let's do food in public" keeps the plan and moves the map.

I will not give you a TED talk about stranger danger. I will not invent crime stats. I will not invent a rule that everyone must do this. This is your first-time line. You do not owe a roommate. You do not owe a trauma paragraph. "Not coming over" is complete.

If they push "I cook better at home," you can say, "I'm sure. Still a public place for a first meet." Then you suggest two actual spots you know, with a time. Empty counter-offers die. Named places live.

If they drop you for that, they dropped you for a door. That is information.

Have they already sent an address, or only the cook idea?

### User
Address and a parking tip. I had started typing "fun" and deleted it. They seem normal from the texts. I feel rude, like I accused them of being a problem.

### Assistant
Wanting a cafe is not an accusation. Normal-from-texts is how most first meets begin. Public is still allowed. Delete "fun" permanently if fun meant yes to the apartment. Send: "Thanks for the address. I'm not coming over for a first meet. Free at 7 at [cafe you know] or [bar you know]. If that's not your thing, no worries." No worries is a door they can take. It is not you apologizing for having a body.

If they argue you wasted their groceries, that is their shop. You did not order the groceries. You can say, "Sorry the plan changed on your end. I'm still up for public." Then stop paying for their fridge in your head.

Parking tip can wait until you agree on a public lot. Do not keep the apartment pin in the chat as a maybe. Maybe is how you end up on a stoop "just for a minute."

Rude is a feeling. It is not a verdict. You can be polite and still not enter.

### User
They replied "we can just hang on the stoop then go in if it feels right." That feels like a funnel. Also my friend says I should share my location. I don't want a production.

### Assistant
Stoop-then-in is the apartment with an extra stair. Decline the funnel: "Stoop still isn't a first meet for me. Cafe or nothing tonight." If they cannot do the cafe, you are done for tonight. You can reschedule public or you can not. You do not need to negotiate the threshold.

Location sharing: it is not a production if it is a tap to a friend you already trust. "If I go to [cafe], I'll share for two hours" is ordinary. I will not tell you that you must. I will tell you it is a small tool, not a film crew. If you skip it, tell someone the cafe name anyway. A name in a text is enough for many people.

Do not go to the stoop to be nice. Nice is how fun got typed. First time means you send the cafe, you leave the address unused, you wear shoes you can walk away in, you meet where a bartender or a cashier exists. Cook later, maybe, if you want, after you know them in the world. Tonight the world. Not their hallway. Not a funnel. A table. Send it. Sit only if they show there. If they do not, eat anyway. You still did the first time right: public, plain, no fake roommate.
