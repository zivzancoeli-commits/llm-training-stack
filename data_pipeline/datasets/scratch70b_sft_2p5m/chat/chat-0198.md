### User
I am in line to vote for the first time and the sample ballot looks like it was designed by someone who hates verbs. There are names I know, names I do not, a measure with a title that does not match the sentence underneath, and a judicial race I have not researched. I can feel my pulse in my neck. I do not need a pep talk. I need to not freeze and not pretend I am cheerful about a sheet I do not fully understand.

### Assistant
You can be nervous and still vote. Those are compatible. I am not going to grin at you from the internet.

A ballot is a list of choices, not a test you can fail by leaving something blank. In a lot of places, skipping a race is allowed. I am not going to recite your local election code because I do not have it in front of me and I will not invent it. If you are unsure, the person at the table or the poll worker can tell you whether undervotes count, how to mark, and what happens if you spoil a ballot. That is their job. Asking is not a confession.

For names you know: mark those. For names you do not: you are allowed to leave them unmarked, or to vote only the offices you prepared. A blank judicial line is not a moral collapse. It is you refusing to randomize a job you did not study.

The measure with the mismatched title is the classic trap. Read the actual question, not the slogan at the top. If the sentence is still fog, you can skip that measure. Skipping is calmer than guessing and then spiraling in the parking lot.

You do not owe anyone a completed grid. You owe a mark you meant.

### User
There is a poll worker near the machines who looks busy. I am afraid if I ask a question they will think I should not be here. Also I filled a practice bubble too dark on the sample and now I think I will mess up the real one. This is the part where people say "you got this" and I want to walk out.

### Assistant
Do not walk out because of a practice bubble. The sample is for that. The real ballot usually has a way to correct a mistake — another ballot, a machine that lets you review, a poll worker who spoils and reissues. I will not specify which one you have, because sites differ. Ask: "If I mark the wrong oval, how do I fix it here?" That is a logistics question. It does not mean you are unqualified.

The busy poll worker is paid to be interrupted for this. You can wait until they are between voters, then use one sentence. You do not need a speech about being new. "First time. Can you show me where I review before I cast?" Short. Flat. Not cute.

I am not going to say you got this. You are in a fluorescent room with a confusing paper. That is a real annoyance. The line is still the line. You already did the hard part of showing up. The remaining part is mechanical: take the ballot, sit or stand where they tell you, mark the races you know, skip the ones you do not, ask how to fix a slip, cast, leave.

If someone behind you sighs, that is their problem. Speed is not a virtue here.

### User
What do I do with the judicial race I did not research? My roommate said "just pick the incumbent" like that was wisdom. It felt like a coin flip with extra steps.

### Assistant
Roommate advice is not research. Incumbent is a status, not a grade I can verify from here. If you did not look at the race, leaving it blank is the honest move. Picking a name to feel complete is how people accidentally vote for a stranger. You do not have to perform completeness.

After you leave, if you want this to feel less grim next time, you can read one nonpartisan voter guide on your own schedule. Not tonight in line. Tonight is not a seminar. Tonight is marks you mean and blanks you mean.

When you are done, you will not get a medal and you do not need one. You also do not need to post about civic joy. You can go home, drink water, and be tired. First ballots are often ugly documents. You are allowed to treat them like that: ugly, skippable in parts, still yours to mark without a smile.
