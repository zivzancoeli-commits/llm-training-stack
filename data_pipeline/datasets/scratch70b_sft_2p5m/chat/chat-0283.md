### User
The people next door got a parrot. It starts screaming at about 5:40 a.m. like someone is dying in a jungle. I work early some days and I used to like those hours. Now I wake up furious and then I can't get back to sleep. I haven't said anything because they seem nice and I don't want to be the neighbor who hates an animal. I also don't want to keep living like this.

### Assistant
You can dislike a sound without hating a bird. Dawn screaming is a real sleep problem, not a personality defect on your side.

Do not bang the wall. Do not slip an anonymous note that sounds like a threat. Do not start with "your bird is ruining my life." Start with time, not character: you are waking at 5:40, it is daily, and you want a conversation about what they can actually change.

I will not invent local noise ordinances or claim a parrot is or is not allowed. Those rules vary, and guessing them makes you sound sure when you are not. What you can do is talk, document, and adjust your own room while you wait to see if they care.

Have you ever spoken to them in daylight about anything, or is this going to be the first conversation? And is the bird on a balcony, in a window, or somewhere you cannot see?

### User
We've waved. I borrowed a screwdriver once. The cage is in the kitchen, I think, because that's the wall my bedroom shares. They leave a window cracked. I already tried a fan. It helps until the bird hits a certain pitch.

### Assistant
A screwdriver history is useful. You are not a stranger with a complaint; you are a person they already know as reasonable. Use that. Catch them on a Saturday afternoon, not at 5:45 when everyone is their worst self.

Keep it to one minute. "Hey, I wanted to flag something before I got weird about it. The parrot starts around 5:40 and my bedroom is on that wall. I can't sleep through it. Is there anything you can do with the window or moving the cage in the morning?" Then let them talk. They may not know it carries. People who live with a noise go deaf to it.

Possible things they can try, none of which you can force: close the window at night, move the cage off the shared wall, cover the cage at a consistent hour, or keep morning chaos away from that room. I am not a bird trainer. I do not know this species or whether covering is kind or stressful for that animal. If they say they already tried everything, believe that they believe it, and still ask about the window. A cracked window is the one variable you can both see.

Meanwhile, treat your room like a problem you can nibble. Heavy curtains, a door sweep, earplugs plus the fan, phone on the other side of the bed so you are not grabbing it at 5:41. None of that is a moral concession. It is you buying time.

Do not record them secretly to "catch" the bird. If you later need a log, a dated note of start times is enough for a conversation. A hidden recording turns neighbors into opponents.

### User
What if they laugh and say "that's just how parrots are"? I will lose it. Also I feel guilty because I like animals.

### Assistant
If they laugh, do not match it. Repeat the time: "I get that it's a parrot. 5:40 is still waking me. Can we try the window for a week and then talk?" Laughing it off is a stall. A week with one change is a test.

Liking animals and needing sleep can sit in the same person. You are not asking them to dump the bird at a shelter. You are asking for a quieter shared wall.

For the next ten minutes: write the one-minute script on your phone so you do not improvise anger, pick a daylight time you are likely to see them, close your own window and add a folded towel at the bottom of your bedroom door tonight, and start a simple log: time it started, whether their window was open if you can tell. If you see them tomorrow, use the script. If you do not, knock once at a civil hour, not at dawn.

If nothing changes after a good-faith ask and a week, that is when you look up whatever your building or city actually publishes about quiet hours, instead of quoting folklore. First move is still the screwdriver neighbor conversation, said once, clearly, without a speech about jungles.
