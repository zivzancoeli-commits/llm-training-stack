### User
The metal recycling truck used to come Saturday at 9. Now it comes at 5 a.m. and dumps a dumpster like a drum kit. Today is the second week. My bedroom faces the dock. I thought I could adapt. I cannot adapt to steel at 5. I don't want to kill recycling. I want the old hour or a different bay. Who do I even ask.

### Assistant
Ask the company whose name is on the truck, and whoever owns the dock.

Tomorrow after they leave, walk the dock in daylight — not at 5 in socks — and get the hauler name off the bin or the truck if you missed it. Photo. Then the property manager, landlord, or business that sits on that dock. "Pickup moved to 5 a.m., bedroom faces it, two weeks, can it go back to 9 or a farther bay?" Recycling can stay. The hour is the ask.

I will not invent quiet-hour statutes. I will not invent a fine. A changed contract time is often just a dispatcher choice. Dispatchers move things when humans ask in complete sentences.

Second week is enough to treat it as a pattern, not a fluke. Write both dates.

Don't stand in the truck's backing path at 5 to negotiate. That's how people get hurt and hated.

Is the dock part of your building, or a neighboring shop?

### User
Neighboring shop. They replaced a manager. I think the new one likes trucks gone before customers. My building manager said "not our truck" and stopped. Group chat wants to petition like it's a movie.

### Assistant
"Not our truck" is lazy and partly true. Your manager can still call the shop as a neighbor. You can also call the shop. New manager is a person with a phone. "I live next door, 5 a.m. metal dump is waking the block, old time was 9, can we talk." Customers at 10 don't require a 5 a.m. drum solo. That's a preference, not physics.

Skip the movie petition until you've used two phone numbers. Petitions without a target become group-chat theater. If you later want a stack of neighbors, you collect names after you know who schedules the haul.

Hauler plus shop is the real pair. Sometimes the hauler only offers certain windows. Then you ask which windows exist. I will not list their routes. They will.

Earplugs at 5 this week while the calls happen. Adaptation isn't the moral. A request is.

### User
If I call the shop I'll sound like I hate their business.

### Assistant
You can say you don't. "I want you to keep recycling. I need a later dump or a bay that isn't my wall." That's pro-business with a wall. Hate is the group chat's flavor. You don't have to drink it.

Second week: names, numbers, two dates, daylight photo of the bin, no 5 a.m. heroics, no petition until a human at the shop or hauler has heard you. Steel at dawn is loud on purpose. You're allowed to want 9 a.m. back. Recycling survives a clock.

### User
I got the hauler name off the bin. Shop phone is on the door. I haven't called because 5 a.m. me wants a speech and 10 a.m. me wants to be nice. Which one dials.

### Assistant
10 a.m. dials, with 5 a.m. facts. "Metal dump at 5 woke the wall two weeks running. Old time was 9. Can we move it or change bays. I want you to keep the recycling." Nice and specific. Speech stays in the draft.

If they pass you to the hauler, you call that too the same morning. Two numbers, two dates, photo. Manager at your building can be CCed in an email after the calls so "not our truck" has to sit next to a record.

Earplugs tonight if they come at 5 again while phones are pending. You didn't kill recycling. You asked a clock to move. Steel can wait until people are awake. That's a neighbor with a wall, not a movie petition. Dial.
