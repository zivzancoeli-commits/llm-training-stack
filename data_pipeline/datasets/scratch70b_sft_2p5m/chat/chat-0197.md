### User
I am at a standing show. The person in front of me is very tall. I paid for a general admission floor and I can see a sliver of the drummer and a lot of a jacket. I am the new person on this friend group outing. They all know the etiquette and I do not. I do not want to start a fight and I do not want to pretend I know a rule that might not exist. What do I actually do?

### Assistant
I do not know a universal concert rule for height. Anyone who talks like there is a posted law is bluffing. Venues differ. Some rooms treat the floor as first-come sightlines. Some treat tapping a shoulder as normal. I have not been in your room tonight.

You bought a floor ticket, not a guaranteed view over every head. Height is not a crime. A flag, a tablet held high, or a friend on shoulders is a different choice, and houses handle even that differently.

First, check whether you can move two steps left or right without shoving. If the floor is packed solid, that option is gone. If there is a gap, take it before you talk to anyone.

If you cannot move, try one polite sentence, not a lecture. "Hey, I can't see at all — any chance we could shift a little?" Then accept the answer. They might say no, not hear you, or be kind. I will not predict which.

### User
There is no gap. I already looked. My friends are two bodies to the left and they can see because they are taller too. I feel like the short intern of the group. If I say something and they laugh, I have to stand here for ninety more minutes in the joke. Is there a staff person for this? I do not want to be the one who fetches security over height.

### Assistant
I would not fetch security over someone being tall. I do not know your venue's policy, and I will not invent one where staff rearrange genetics. Staff are for groping, thrown drinks, a crush that feels unsafe, a medical issue. Height is uncomfortable. It is usually not that list. If the floor pressure feels unsafe, leave the pocket and find an usher. That is a different question.

You can still skip the talk. Try a side rail, a back rise, or the merch aisle for one song. Missing two songs while you relocate can beat missing the set in a sliver. I do not know this hall. A flat floor with no rail might only get you a different tall person. I am saying that so I do not sell you a magic balcony.

If you speak, keep it to the person, not the group. A side comment to friends ("I'm going to try the right") beats a group performance of justice. Intern energy is wanting a rulebook so you cannot be wrong. There is not a clean one here.

### User
What if they have a kid on their shoulders later? That feels more like a choice. And what do I tell my friends so I do not sound bitter on the ride home?

### Assistant
Shoulders is a choice. Whether the house allows it, I still do not know. Some floor shows stop it. Some do not. If a kid goes up, ask staff what the house does rather than police a parent. "Are shoulder rides allowed in this section?" is a question. Calling a stranger a bad person is a fight. I will not pretend I know which way the usher will go.

Ride home: "I couldn't see much from where we stood. Next time I might hang back or grab a seat if they sell one." Preference, not a prosecution. Bitter sounds like a campaign. Specific sounds like you learned the room.

If you stay put, watch the screens if they have them, listen, and decide this format is not for you. That is allowed. GA floor is a gamble on bodies. You lost tonight on sightlines. You did not fail etiquette school. Move if you can, ask once if you must, do not call security on height, and do not bluff a rule you cannot point to. That is as far as I can go without lying.
