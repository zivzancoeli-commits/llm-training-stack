### User
The landlord texted that a plumber will come "between 8 and 6" to look at the tub that won't drain. I work from home but I have a client call 10–11 and a doctor's appointment at 3:15 I already moved once. I asked for a two-hour window. They said "that's not how plumbers work." I don't want to miss the plumber and wait another month. I also don't want to sit on the toilet lid for ten hours like a concierge. What do I reply, and what do I actually do with my day?

### Assistant
You try to shrink the window, then you plan the day as if they meant 8–6.

Reply: "I have a client call 10–11 and I have to leave by 2:45 for an appointment. Can they come 8–9:45 or 11:15–2:30? If not, please tell me if I can leave a key / if the super can let them in / if we must reschedule." You're offering access options, not a personality. I will not invent whether they must give a two-hour window. Places differ. "That's not how plumbers work" might be their habit. You can still ask. You can still offer a lockbox if you have one they already use.

Don't cancel the doctor unless you decide the tub is the emergency. A drain is miserable. A moved appointment twice is also real. I won't tell you which to keep. I will tell you not to sit ten hours without a backup: neighbor, super, key, or a reschedule in writing.

If you must be there for the whole span, you put the client call on with a note that you may have a knock, and you keep clothes on. That's ugly and it's a plan.

Do you have a super with a key, and have you ever left a key for this landlord?

### User
Super has a key. I don't love it but they've used it for the smoke alarm. Landlord said "someone must be home." Client will hear a plumber. Doctor is a follow-up I already delayed. I feel like if I leave at 2:45 I'll be the tenant who "wasn't serious."

### Assistant
You're serious. You have a calendar.

Text: "Super has a key. I'm fine with super letting the plumber in if that's allowed. I'll be home 8–2:45 except on a call 10–11 (I'll still answer the door). After 2:45 I have to go. Please confirm super access so I don't miss them." If they refuse super and refuse a window, you choose: miss the doctor, or miss the plumber and get the miss in writing. I won't pick for you. I will say a written "someone must be home, no super" plus your 2:45 is a conflict they created. You document it.

For the call: tell the client at the start, "I may have a maintenance knock; I'll mute for a minute if so." That's professional, not a sitcom. If you can't say that to this client, you put a sign on the door "please knock, on a call, I'll be 30 seconds" and you hope. Hope is not a policy. The sentence to the client is better.

Don't wait on the toilet lid. Work at the table. Set a ringer. If they come at 5:50 and you're at the doctor, that's the 8–6 they booked. You already said 2:45.

### User
They confirmed super can let them in. I still feel like I should cancel the doctor to be safe. The tub has been slow for two weeks.

### Assistant
A two-week slow drain is not a reason to cancel a follow-up you already moved if the super can open the door. Keep the doctor unless your clinician said this visit can't slip — I won't interpret your appointment. You can text the landlord in the morning: "Leaving at 2:45, super has key, please have plumber use it if I'm gone." Then you go.

Day plan: be home early, take the call with the knock warning, leave at 2:45, super, photos of the tub before you go so you know if they came. If they didn't come, you have the window and the texts. You don't owe ten hours on a lid.

Reply now with the super and the 2:45. Then put pants on. You're not a concierge. You're a tenant with a drain and a body that has a doctor. Get both on the calendar. The 8-to-6 can exist without you living on the tank.
