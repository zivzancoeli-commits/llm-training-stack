### User
There's a guy on my team, Marcus, who parks behind my chair and reads Slack over my shoulder. Today I had a message from my manager about a raise conversation. I felt him go still. He asked "big news?" I locked the laptop. He laughed and said I was jumpy. I have a 1:1 in forty minutes and a spreadsheet open I don't want him narrating. I sit in an open row. There is no door.

### Assistant
Locking the laptop was correct. Jumpy is his word for a boundary he walked into.

You don't need a door to have a screen. You need an angle and one sentence you can say without a speech about privacy theory.

I'm not going to invent your company's policy. What you can control in the next forty minutes is the geometry and the 1:1.

Turn the laptop a few degrees toward the wall or the aisle you don't share with him. Open the raise thread only when you need it. If the spreadsheet is sensitive, switch to a dummy tab when you feel someone land behind the chair.

Was he standing there waiting to ask you something work-related, or did he arrive to hover?

### User
He arrived to hover. He does this when he's bored between standups. Sometimes he asks about a cell in a sheet. Today he had a coffee mug and just watched. After I locked it he said he'd "catch the 1:1 vibe later," which I hated. I still need to finish two rows before I walk into that meeting.

### Assistant
Coffee-mug watching is not collaboration. Treat it as a visit with no ticket.

For the next ten minutes: finish the two rows with the laptop angled, notifications off, raise thread closed. If he comes back, don't lock in a panic. Lock as a normal move, then say, without smiling it off, "I need the screen to myself. Ping me if you need a cell." Then keep typing. You are not required to narrate the 1:1 vibe. "Catch the vibe later" is him trying to stay in the story. You can say "it's a private meeting" and stop. Private is a complete word. You don't have to specify raise, manager, or money.

If he laughs again, let the laugh die in the air. Filling the laugh with a joke is how this stays a bit. The bit is him reading Slack.

After the two rows, close the spreadsheet, take the laptop if it's a portable, and walk to the 1:1 early. Sitting in the row with a locked lid and a person hunting for vibe is how you arrive rattled. Better to be five minutes in a stairwell or a quiet corner than to give him a second still moment.

I don't know whether your manager would care. If you want this on a record later, a short note to yourself with date and what he said is enough for now. Don't send a manifesto today unless this is already a pattern you've documented. Today is the meeting.

### User
If I tell him to stop, he'll tell the row I'm sensitive. We already do the "open culture" thing. Also the raise thread is still in unread.

### Assistant
Open culture is not an obligation to broadcast compensation. People who say open culture while standing behind chairs usually mean open for them.

You can tell him to stop without a town hall. The sentence is small: "Please don't read my screen. Walk around if you need me." If he reports that to the row as sensitive, that's his story. Your story can stay boring: you asked for space to work. Boring stories travel worse, which is useful.

Don't mark the raise thread unread-read-unread while he's nearby. Read it in the 1:1 room or after, with the lid at an angle and your body between the aisle and the glass. If you must glance now, glance, don't scroll the whole history.

If this keeps happening after a direct ask, involve your manager with facts: dates, "stood behind my chair," "commented on a private Slack." I can't predict how they'll handle it. A raise thread leaking into a row is a real problem.

Finish the two rows. Angle the screen. Walk to the 1:1 with the laptop. Leave Marcus the empty chair. The unread thread can wait ten minutes.
