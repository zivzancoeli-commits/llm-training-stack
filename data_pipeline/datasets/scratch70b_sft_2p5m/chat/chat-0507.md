### User
A friend asked me to watch their four-year-old Saturday night. I said I could do three hours. They said "perfect" and then mentioned they might be "a little later" and that I'm "so natural with kids" so it should be fine. There is no second adult, no neighbor on call, no bedtime routine written down. I have sat for them once for ninety minutes while they were at the store. This is bigger. I don't want to be the person who ruins their night, and I don't want an open-ended kid.

### Assistant
Three hours is the offer. "A little later" is a different job. You can keep the first job.

Reply before Saturday, in writing: "I can do 6 to 9. At 9 I need you back. I can't do open-ended. If you might be later, you'll need another adult as backup." That's not ruining a night. That's describing the night you actually offered.

If they have no backup, that's their planning hole. You don't have to fill it with your sleep. Natural with kids is a compliment they are using as a staffing strategy. Smile at the compliment if you want. Don't staff from it.

I will not tell you the legal rules of babysitting in your place. I don't know them. I will tell you not to accept a kid with no emergency contact, no bedtime notes, and no way to reach a parent who might go silent. That's not a vibe. That's a list.

Did they tell you where they'll be and whether their phone will be on?

### User
Dinner and "maybe a drink after." Phone on, they said. They also said the kid "goes down easy" which was not my experience in those ninety minutes. There is an inhaler in a kitchen drawer they waved at. I don't want a medical surprise and I don't want to be dramatic.

### Assistant
An inhaler in a drawer is not a briefing. If you still take the three hours, you get a five-minute handoff that is boring and complete: parent numbers, a second adult's number, when the kid last ate, bedtime steps, where the inhaler is, and what they actually want you to do if the kid needs it. I will not tell you how to use an inhaler. I am not a clinician. If you are not comfortable being the person with that drawer, you say so: "I can't be the medical plan. You need someone who is." That is not drama. That is a limit.

"Goes down easy" you already tested. Believe your ninety minutes. Put bedtime in the written window: "I'll start bedtime at 8 so you can be back at 9." If they roll their eyes, they can hire a later sitter.

Maybe a drink after is how 9 becomes 11. You already named 9. If they text at 8:50 that they're on dessert, you can say "see you at 9" not "lol no rush." Rush is the job.

Don't invite a friend over to help unless the parent agrees. That's a new adult in their house.

If you decide the whole thing is too thin, you can cancel the sit with time to spare: "I can't do Saturday without a backup adult and a real handoff. I need you to find someone else." Earlier is kinder than 5 p.m. Saturday.

### User
I still want to help. I just don't want to be the backup plan and the plan.

### Assistant
Then help inside a fence. Three hours, two phone numbers, a written bedtime, a parent who treats 9 as real. You can like a child and still not be an open night. Send the fence now, while "perfect" can still be rewritten. Their night can exist. Yours can end at 9. That's a complete friendship, not a lesser one. If they cannot find a backup, they shrink the night to your window or they find another sitter. You are not required to expand until you snap. Natural with kids is not a blank check. Write the hours. Keep them.
