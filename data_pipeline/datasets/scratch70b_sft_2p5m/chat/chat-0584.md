### User
My cousin just had a baby. The uncles are on the porch with a box of cigars. Someone already put one in my hand. I don't smoke. I also don't want to do a whole speech about lungs at a party where people are crying happy tears. First time I've been offered one like it's a handshake. How do I give it back without becoming the health department?

### Assistant
Put it back in the box or hand it to the person who gave it to you. The sentence is "I'm good, thanks" or "I don't smoke." That is the whole first time. You do not owe a lecture about tar. You do not owe a puff to prove you love the baby.

I will not diagnose anyone on that porch. I will not invent a medical claim about one cigar. I will not invent a price for the box. This is manners plus your lungs, which are yours.

If they put it in your hand, your hand can return it. Standing there holding it is how you get photographed with a cigar you did not want. Set it down on the rail if the box is far. Then pick up a soda or your phone so the hand is busy.

If someone says "it's tradition," you can say, "I'm glad you're celebrating. I'm skipping the cigar." Tradition can keep the baby. It does not get to keep your mouth.

Are you already on the porch with it in your fingers, or are you still inside trying to plan the walk out?

### User
It's in my fingers. Uncle Ray is holding the cutter out. People are watching because they think this is a cute passing-the-torch thing. I smiled already, which I regret.

### Assistant
The smile was not a contract. You can still decline with the same face. You do not have to switch to a scowl to be believed.

Turn your body slightly toward the box. Hold the cigar out horizontally. "I'm going to pass. Congrats though." If Ray still holds the cutter, you do not put the cigar in it. You put the cigar toward his other hand or the box. Two objects cannot both be your problem.

If the watching relatives go "aww come on," you repeat once: "I'm good." Then you ask a baby question. "What's the nap situation" is a dull, perfect pivot. Dull is how you leave the ritual without a TED talk about smoking.

Do not fake-puff and cough for comedy. Comedy still puts smoke in you and still takes a photo. Do not take it home "for later" unless you actually want a cigar in your coat. Later is how it lives in your car for a year.

If they light theirs anyway, you can step back from the smoke without a speech. "I'm going to check on the food" is a location, not a sermon. Porch smoke is a cloud. You are allowed to stand upwind or go inside. That is not a boycott of the baby.

### User
Ray just said I used to be more fun. There's going to be a group photo in two minutes. I don't want a cigar in the picture and I don't want to look like I stormed off.

### Assistant
"Used to be more fun" is a hook. Do not take it. You can say, "Still fun. Still no cigar." Then you stay for the photo if you want the photo. Fun is not a burning leaf.

For the picture: empty hands or a glass. If they try to put the cigar back in your fingers for the shot, you can laugh once and keep your hands on the glass. "Not in the picture" is a complete sentence. If Uncle Ray sticks it in your breast pocket as a joke, take it out after the shutter. You can even take it out before if you feel it. A pocket cigar is still a prop.

You do not have to storm off. You can stand in the back row, smile the same smile you already used, and not light anything. After the photo, go inside on purpose. Check on the parent who is actually tired. That is a better cousin job than a torch ritual you do not want.

Give the cigar back now, before the shutter. Hands empty, one line, photo if you want it, then a room with less smoke. That is declining without becoming the health department. The baby does not need you to inhale to count as family.
