### User
I was on the night bus and a stranger sat next to me and started trauma-dumping. That is the word my friend used. I did not know how to stop it without being cruel. I kind of nodded for twenty minutes and now I feel greasy, like I agreed to be their free counselor. Translate this into normal words and tell me what to do next time. Talk like I am fourteen and sharp, not like a psychology poster.

### Assistant
Trauma-dumping is a trendy label. In plain speech it means: someone told you heavy private stuff very fast, without checking if you wanted that job, and they kept going. It does not automatically mean they are dangerous or that you are now responsible for their life. It means the conversation skipped the usual steps, like names and small talk, and jumped to the worst chapters.

You did not agree to be a counselor. Nodding is what people do when they are stuck in a seat. A bus is a trap with manners. Your body chose the cheap option: look polite, wait for your stop.

Greasy is a good word. Your face said go on when your brain said please stop. That is uncomfortable. It is not a contract.

Next time, you interrupt earlier, while it is still a story and not a confession pile. You do not need a clinical reason. You need a seat reason. Try: I am not the right person for this, I hope you have someone to talk to, I am going to put my headphones on. Then you actually put them on. Headphones are a wall that does not require a debate.

If they keep talking through the headphones, you can say I need to stop this conversation. Then you look at the window or you stand up if there is a pole space. You do not owe a better excuse. I have a lot of homework is fine. I do not know you well enough for this is also fine and more honest.

What you do not do: diagnose them, ask follow-up questions that invite more chapters, or share your own worst story to make it even. Matching pain is not kindness on a bus. It is gasoline.

You also do not have to hunt them down later to apologize for leaving. You left at your stop. That is the deal of public transit.

Want a one-sentence version? Twenty minutes of nodding means you waited for a perfect speech that never comes.

### User
Yeah, one sentence, and also what to do with the greasy feeling tonight. I keep replaying that I should have been nicer because they looked like they might cry. My friend said I was being codependent which I only half understand.

### Assistant
Codependent, in poster language, is a big relationship word. In plain speech here it just means: you treated their feelings as a job you had to finish. You do not have a relationship with a stranger on a bus. So that word is mostly extra. The simple version is: you over-helped because crying faces are hard to ignore.

One sentence: I am sorry you are going through that, I cannot talk about this with you. Sorry is courtesy. I cannot is the door. Sorry alone sounds like continue. I cannot alone can sound like insult. Together it is adult. You are not the night-shift therapist.

Tonight, the greasy feeling: name it as leftover adrenaline plus manners. Drink water. Tell one person you trust a short version, not a replay of every detail they told you. You do not need to carry their facts. Those facts are theirs. Repeating them in your head is how the bus ride stays longer than the route.

If you cannot sleep because you are arguing with a ghost passenger, write the sentence you wish you said, once, on paper, then stop rewriting. You are not going to time-travel. Next bus, headphones go on before the story gets heavy, not after.

You were not cruel for wanting the conversation to end. Cruel would be mocking them. Wanting quiet is allowed. Nodding for twenty minutes was too polite, not bad. Polite has a cost. Next time you pay less.

If they had asked are you okay hearing this, you could have said no. They did not ask. That is why it felt like a dump. You can still use the missing step: I cannot hear this right now. Headphones. Stop. That is the whole skill. It is not a diagnosis of them. It is you running your own mouth like a person with a destination.
