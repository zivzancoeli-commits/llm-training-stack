### User
There is a construction crew starting at seven all week on our block, and the couple next door has a baby who was finally sleeping past six. I can hear the baby crying through the wall now. I want to help but I also do not want to be the neighbor who knocks with advice. Talk to me like last-year me, the one who would have written a paragraph note and then regretted the tone. I am not even sure they want anything from me.

### Assistant
Do you still want help with this, or do you want permission to leave them alone. Last-year you would have helped to manage your own guilt about the noise, which is not the same as them wanting a visitor.

If you want to offer, offer one small thing that is easy to refuse. If you want to stay out, stay out. Construction weeks end. Babies cry. You are not the city.

I do not know your quiet hours or whether seven is legal. I will not invent a regulation. If the noise seems wrong for the permit on the fence, look at the notice yourself. A city call is later, not a speech at their door.

Offer that does not sound like a paragraph note: a text if you have a number, or five lines on a sticky. We hear the crew too. If you want a quiet room for the baby for an hour, you can use our back room this week. No pressure. Cut hang in there if it sounds like a poster.

What you do not do: explain sleep science, recommend a sound machine brand, or say I used to be good with babies. Last-year you would have stacked credentials. Credentials at a door feel like a job interview they did not post.

If they do not answer, that is an answer. You do not follow up daily. Daily is a campaign.

Still want to offer, or did you mostly need to talk because the crying is in your body too?

### User
I still want to offer once. I do not have their number. Sticky note feels like a parking ticket. I could catch them when they take the stroller out. I am scared I will start talking too long because I am also not sleeping.

### Assistant
Catch them at the stroller. Do not block the sidewalk. One breath: the construction is brutal, if you ever want our place for a nap hour this week the couch is empty, no worries if not. Then shut up. Count to two. If they say they are okay, say okay and walk. If they look interested, give a time you will actually be home. Do not offer a key you do not mean. Last-year you would have offered a hotel. Fifteen minutes in the hallway while they shower is a real gift if you truly want it. Come anytime is a trap for you.

Tired mouths leak. Put your hands on a grocery bag so your body has an exit. You are not hosting a meeting. If the baby is crying, do not comment on the cry. They can hear their child.

This will not fix the week. Crews still start at seven. Offer for them, or skip it. If you are offering for a good-neighbor story, buy earplugs instead. I will not pretend they cancel a jackhammer. They can take the edge off a wall.

One offer. Stroller moment. Short. Easy to refuse. Then you live your week. Last-year you would have written the note, rewritten the note, and still been mad. You already know the shorter version. Use it.

### User
If they say yes to the couch hour, do I clean like company is coming. That is how I overdo it.

### Assistant
No. You pick up the floor trip hazards and you put out water. You do not deep clean. A parent with a baby is not coming to rate your sink. If you start cleaning, you will resent the hour you offered. Resentment leaks. Last-year you cleaned as a way to deserve the good-neighbor medal. Skip the medal. Couch, water, door, goodbye. You wanted to help. This is help. You still want it, so keep it small enough that you do not take it back with a mood.
