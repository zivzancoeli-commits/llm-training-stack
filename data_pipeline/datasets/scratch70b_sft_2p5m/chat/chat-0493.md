### User
My neighbor watches games with the TV on the wall we share. Tonight's match went late. I could hear the announcer and every crowd roar. I have to be up at 6. I already did the polite ceiling tap last month and they turned it down for ten minutes. I don't want a note that sounds like a HOA. I also don't want to lie awake cataloging scores. Game is still on. What do I do tonight versus what I say tomorrow?

### Assistant
Tonight is sleep logistics. Tomorrow is the conversation. Don't write a manifesto at 11:40 p.m. Notes slid under doors at night read like ambushes.

Tonight: earplugs if you have them, a fan or a rain sound if that helps you, pillow over the ear that faces the wall. If you truly cannot sleep and the roar is still a room in your room, a knock now is allowed if you can do it without a speech. Knock, step back, one sentence: "Hey, the TV is coming through the wall and I have an early morning. Could you turn it down or use headphones?" Then you go home. You don't stay to rate their volume like a referee.

If you don't feel safe knocking at night, you don't. You survive the night and you talk in daylight. Safety first, pride never.

I will not invent quiet-hours law for your building. If you have a posted rule, that's theirs. If you don't, you're still allowed to ask a human to turn a TV down.

Is this a one-off final, or is it twice a week?

### User
Twice a week at least, plus leftovers on replay. They are nice in the hallway. That's part of why I haven't done more than the tap. I don't want to become the neighbor who hates joy.

### Assistant
Asking for a quieter wall is not a ban on sports. Headphones, a TV on a different wall, or a lower bass setting are still joy. You are asking for your 6 a.m., not for a forfeit.

Tomorrow, catch them when they are not in the fourth quarter. Hallway, mailboxes, a text if you already have a building group that's actually used for this. "The TV on the shared wall is loud on game nights. I need to sleep by 10:30 on work nights. Can we keep it lower, or move the set if that's easy?" Offer one concrete time. Vague "be more considerate" is how people nod and change nothing.

Don't lead with last month's tap as a prosecution. You can mention it once if they claim ignorance: "I tapped once and it helped briefly." Then you return to the ask.

If they say they didn't realize, believe them long enough to let them fix it. If they say you should move your bed, you can say you'll try a different orientation and still want the volume down. You shouldn't have to rearrange your life around a stadium in a box.

I won't tell you to call the police on a game. That's a last resort for a different kind of night, and I don't know your block. Building management is a maybe if you have a management that actually answers. Try the human first.

### User
What if they agree in the hallway and then the replay starts at 11 again?

### Assistant
Then you treat the second night as data, not as a betrayal of the species. Same sentence, shorter: "It's coming through again." If you have a text thread, use it so you aren't only brave in person once. If it keeps happening, then you consider management with dates and times, not adjectives. "Tuesday and Thursday after 11, announcer audible in my bedroom" is a report. "They hate joy" is not.

Tonight: earplugs or a knock with one line. Tomorrow: the hallway ask with a time. You can like a neighbor and still need a wall that isn't a stadium. That's ordinary building life, not a HOA.
