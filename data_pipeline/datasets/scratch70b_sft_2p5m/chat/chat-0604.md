### User
Dad wants me to "just pick a place" for Grandma this afternoon because a hospital social worker mentioned discharge. I have a list of five names I googled in the parking lot. I have never toured any of them. He says I am the organized one. I am the one with a laptop. I don't want to pretend I can choose a nursing home in four hours. I don't want you to diagnose Grandma or rank facilities. How do I slow this down without looking like I don't care?

### Assistant
You cannot honestly pick a home in one afternoon from five search results. Caring looks like refusing a fake choice, not like clicking the first map pin.

Say to Dad: "I can help gather facts. I cannot pick a facility by dinner. We need more than a parking-lot list." Then name what you will do in the next four hours that is real: call the social worker back, ask what discharge date they actually said, ask whether home with help is on the table, ask which places take her insurance without you guessing the coverage. I will not invent that date. I will not invent a price. I will not diagnose Grandma. Discharge pressure is real. It is still not a reason to lottery her room.

If he says organized people decide fast, you can say, "Fast is how we get a bad fit. I will not pretend."

Has the hospital given a written discharge day, or only a hallway mention?

### User
Hallway mention. Grandma is asleep. Dad is in the cafeteria refreshing a ranking site. My aunt texted "whatever you two pick." So I am the pick by default. I have to be back at work tomorrow.

### Assistant
Default is not a qualification. Reply to the aunt: "We are not picking today. We are getting the discharge facts." Put Dad on the same sentence in person. Take the ranking site away as the oracle. Rankings are ads with stars. You can still use a list as a call sheet: phone, ask about a tour, ask about a bed, write the answers in a note you both can see. Calls are this afternoon. Choosing is not.

Work tomorrow is a boundary you can say: "I can do two hours of calls tonight. I cannot run a placement from my job tomorrow." If Dad has more time in the cafeteria, he can own two of the calls. Split the list. Organized does not mean solo.

If the social worker is findable, find them before you fall in love with a pin. "What is the estimated discharge, what paperwork, what happens if we do not have a bed?" Write it. If they will not give a day, you do not invent one to make Dad calm.

I will not tell you which place is kind. Kind is a visit, a smell, a staff person who answers a bell, Grandma's actual needs which I do not know. Today you collect. You do not crown.

### User
Dad will say if I don't pick, he'll pick the closest one to his house so he can visit, which might be fine and might not. I don't want a fight in the cafeteria.

### Assistant
Closest-to-Dad is a criterion, not a crime. You can say, "Closest can be on the list. It cannot be the only filter we use in a cafeteria." If he still picks closest tonight, you can refuse to sign anything you have not read. I will not invent what signatures exist. If a form appears, you read it. If you are not the legal decision-maker, you say so. I will not guess who is. Ask the social worker who the hospital thinks can sign.

Cafeteria fight: lower your voice, repeat the two-hour plan, stand up if he loops. Standing is not abandonment. It is the end of a loop. Go back to Grandma's room and be a grandchild for ten minutes, which is a job a ranking site cannot do.

Afternoon: social worker facts, split calls, no crown, no diagnosis from me, no fake expertise from a laptop. Care is the slow no. Let Dad feel the panic. You do not have to convert it into a pin on a map by dinner. Tomorrow you work. Tonight you gather. Placement is a series. Today is episode one, not the finale.
