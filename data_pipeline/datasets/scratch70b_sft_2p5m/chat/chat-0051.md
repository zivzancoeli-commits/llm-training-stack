### User
I stayed two hours past the end of my food pantry shift because the closer did not show and there were still crates in the hallway. I am not mad at the people who needed food. I am mad that I keep being the person who cannot leave a crate in a hallway. Coordinator texted thanks so much you are a star which feels like they just booked me as the default leftover. I almost have the words. I want to be kind and specific, not a speech. Help me say this like a person.

### Assistant
Kind and specific is shorter than you think. You already have the facts: shift ended, closer no-show, hallway crates, two extra hours, a thank-you that treated coverage failure like a personality trait. Lead with the hours and the missing closer. Skip star language. Skip I am happy to help whenever. You are not.

Draft: I stayed until seven because the closer did not arrive and the hallway still had crates. I can do my scheduled shift. I cannot be the automatic extra two hours. Next time a closer is missing, I need you to call someone else or leave a close procedure I can finish in fifteen minutes, not two hours. Thanks for the text. I need a plan, not a compliment.

That last sentence is the kindness. You are refusing to be paid in adjectives.

Softer swap if you need it: I like this place. I will burn out if leftover closing keeps landing on me. Can we put a backup closer on the calendar.

You almost had it. Name the two hours as a scheduling problem, not a character test. Crates are a task. You are not the task.

Text, or face-to-face at the next shift so they cannot heart-react and ignore it?

### User
Text is safer because I get shaky in person. I do not want to sound like I am quitting. I also do not want to offer a solution I cannot keep, like I will write the whole closing manual. I can laminate a one-page checklist if that actually helps.

### Assistant
Text it. Do not add I am not quitting. That sounds like you are about to. Stay on the incident.

Offer the checklist only if you mean it this week, and cap it. I can laminate a one-page close checklist this week. I cannot stay late as the backup closer. Gift plus boundary. Gift alone and they still text you at 5:10.

Keep: two hours, seven o'clock, closer did not show, hallway crates, scheduled shift only. Cut: I do not mind, I know everyone is busy, I should have asked earlier. Those turn a reasonable complaint into a confession.

If they reply we are short-staffed: I know. Short-staffed is why we need a named backup, not why I stay. One reply. Then stop.

If they do not reply, you still leave on time next shift. The boundary is the leaving, not their acknowledgment. An unread text is not permission to become the hallway person again.

Send it before you rewrite it nicer and erase the two hours.

### User
Last worry. A regular always chats with me at lockup. If I leave on time I will look like I am blowing them off. How do I be specific with them too without making it a speech.

### Assistant
Do not give them the coordinator speech. One line at the door: I have to leave at five now. I like talking to you. Catch me during the shift, not at lockup. If they look hurt, add I am not upset with you. Regulars can be disappointed. You can go home.

Do not explain the closer, the crates, or the star text to a client. That becomes parking-lot gossip. Kind is the same warmth, earlier goodbye. Specific is five, not around five, not after these crates.

If they start a story at 4:55: I want to hear the rest next time, I have to close. Stand up and pick up your bag while you say it. Body first. People finish stories if you keep standing still.

You already know how to be decent. You were treating overtime like manners. Manners is greeting people. Overtime is labor you did not agree to. Send the two-hour text. Tell the regular five o'clock. Leave. That is the kind version because it is true and small enough to repeat.
