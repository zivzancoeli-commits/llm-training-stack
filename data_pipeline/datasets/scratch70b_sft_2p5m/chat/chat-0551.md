### User
Lab at 8:15. Paper said fasting. I ate toast at 6:40 because I woke up starving and I thought a little wouldn't matter. Front desk asked if I'd had anything. I said toast. They said the draw is delayed, maybe rescheduled, they have to check with the order. I'm in a chair in a gown-adjacent building feeling like I failed a test. I need the next hour, not a diagnosis, not a lecture about willpower.

### Assistant
You told the truth. That's the part you didn't fail. Eating toast when the paper said fasting is common. Labs would rather delay than run a useless tube.

Sit. Drink water if they allow water on your order — some fasting still allows water, some don't. I will not invent your order. Ask them: "Is water okay while I wait?" Then follow that.

Don't sneak another snack in the bathroom to "keep going." Don't leave without a next time. The hour is: wait for their check, get a new slot or a same-morning draw if the clinician says this test can proceed, and ask whether this particular test is actually fasting-required. Sometimes a panel has mixed items. They have the order. You don't guess.

I will not interpret your blood. I will not tell you the toast ruined a diagnosis. I will not tell you it's fine. That's the lab and the person who ordered it.

Shame is optional and useless in a vinyl chair. Hunger is real. Next time you put the fasting note on the fridge the night before. That's later. Now is this building.

Did they send you back to the waiting room, or are you still at the desk?

### User
Waiting room. They took my name again. A tech said "sometimes we can still do it" and walked off. I don't know if I should flag someone or sit like a statue. I have work at 10. Parking is a meter I already fed.

### Assistant
Don't sit like a statue until 10 and miss work on a maybe.

At a pause, go back to the desk with a clock question: "I have to leave by 9:20. If we can't draw this morning, can we book the next fasting slot before I go?" That's logistics. You're not rushing their medical judgment. You're not disappearing.

If the tech returns with a yes-we-can, ask which tests they're running anyway and whether you need to come back fasting for the rest. Mixed answers are normal. Get it in the after-visit summary or on a sticky if they're old-school.

Meter: if you must extend, do it before you sit another forty minutes. A ticket doesn't make the blood better.

Flagging every five minutes will not help. Once, with the time constraint, is enough. Then you sit where you can see the door they took you through.

### User
If they reschedule, do I have to tell my boss I ate toast like an idiot.

### Assistant
No. You tell work you had a lab delay and you're heading in, or you need the later slot and a new morning. Toast is not the boss's business. "Appointment ran long / moved" is enough.

When you rebook, pick a time you can actually fast for. Set an alarm labeled fasting. Put a note on the coffee maker. Hunger at 6:40 is information: earlier appointment or a scheduled last snack the night before, whatever your clinician allowed. I will not prescribe the snack.

This hour: water if allowed, one desk question with your leave-by time, no extra food, no self-trial. You didn't fail a personality test. You ate, you disclosed, you're waiting on an order. That's a lab morning, not a character reveal.

If they draw you this morning anyway, ask what to do about eating before the next one so you don't guess with toast again. Write it on the after-visit paper. If they send you home, put the new slot on a calendar with the word fasting where you will see it at 9 p.m., not at 6:40 when you're already starving.

You disclosed. You're waiting. Work gets a delay line, not a toast confession. Vinyl chair, name called again, leave-by time on the desk's record. That's the hour. Willpower speeches can wait in the parking lot. You don't need one from me and you don't need one from yourself.
