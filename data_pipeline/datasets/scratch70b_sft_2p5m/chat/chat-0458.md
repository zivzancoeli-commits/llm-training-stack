### User
We're in the kitchen at a barbecue. Leah is holding her four-month-old out toward me like a transfer. She said "you're so good with kids" because I have nieces. I don't want to hold the baby. I have a thing about being the sudden responsible adult with a neck I don't know. I also don't want to look like I hate babies. This is the first time I'm going to decline instead of taking the kid and freezing. Leah's arms are already tired. The baby is mid-air in that way. Twenty seconds.

### Assistant
Keep the baby in her arms. Your hands can stay at your sides or take the plate instead.

"I'm going to skip holding — I'm not comfortable. I can grab you a chair / a water / a burp cloth." Offer a different help if you want to be useful. Don't invent a cough you don't have. Don't invent an illness. Last year's you took the baby and stared at a wall. This year's you can have a no that isn't a medical lie.

If she jokes "come on," repeat once: "I don't hold babies I don't know well. I'm good down here." Then you turn slightly toward the chips so the geometry changes. Mid-air is why you speak now, not after the weight is on you.

I will not tell you you're dangerous or safe. I won't diagnose your "thing." You don't want the hold. That's enough. You don't hate babies by declining a transfer.

Is there a chair within reach, and is anyone else in the kitchen who actually wants the baby?

### User
There's a folding chair. Her brother is at the grill. He likes the baby. I said "I can get the chair" but she still has the kid extended. She said "just ten seconds, my back." The baby made a noise. I feel like a monster.

### Assistant
Ten seconds is still a hold. You can help the back without the transfer.

Pull the chair behind her. "Sit. I'm not taking the baby. I'll get your brother." Then you walk to the grill: "Leah's back, can you take the baby?" That's competence, not a freeze. You didn't become a monster. You refused a job and found the person who wants it.

If she looks hurt, she can sit hurt with a baby in her own arms, which is the normal configuration. You can stay and talk to her while she sits. Conversation is not the same as a hold. You can still be good with your nieces and not be a public baby rack.

If she puts the baby against your chest anyway, your hands can stabilize for a second so nobody drops, then you return the baby to her or the brother as soon as it's not mid-air. Safety of a second is not a personality change. Then you still say, "I need you to take them back." Don't laugh it off as if you agreed.

Monster is the story you're telling because the room likes a willing extra adult. You can be the extra adult with a chair.

### User
Brother has the baby. Leah said "I didn't know you were like that." I have to stand next to her with a soda. Face.

### Assistant
Neutral. Don't over-explain. "Yeah. I'm more of a chair-and-water person with babies I don't know. Your kid is cute. I still don't want to hold." If that's too many words, "I'm like that about holding. Not about you." Then you ask her something about sleep or the grill so she has a track that isn't your character.

You don't have to perform delight at the baby to prove you're not cold. You already got her a chair and a brother. That's the first-time no, with logistics.

Next hour: soda, talk, no second transfer. If someone else tries to hand you a different baby, same sentence, shorter. You don't owe the barbecue a hold. You owe the baby a person who wants them. You weren't that person. You found one. That's a successful decline, not a hate. Stand there. Drink the soda. Let "like that" be true.
