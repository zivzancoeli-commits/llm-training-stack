### User
Okay so I am currently in the bathroom of this pizza place because I needed thirty seconds to not be a person. The date is going... well? I think? We both showed up eight minutes early and pretended we hadn't, then we both ordered the same drink and laughed like it was a coincidence instead of the menu having three options. She knocked over the Parmesan shaker. I tried to catch it and knocked over the red pepper. The table looks like a crime scene for Italian seasoning. And then she said, "This is going great," completely deadpan, and I said, "Yeah, we're nailing the vibe," and now we cannot stop quoting that at each other. I like her. I keep almost saying something real and then making a joke instead. What do I even do when I walk back out there.

### Assistant
Walk back out. Wash your hands, check your collar for pepper flakes, and sit down like you meant to take a bathroom break, because you did.

The Parmesan disaster is already a shared story. Do not apologize for it again. Wipe your side of the table if it is still a mess and leave hers alone unless she asks. Hovering over her plate reads as fussy.

When you sit, skip recapping how weird this is. You both already named it. The joke is doing work; if you feed it every thirty seconds it becomes a shield. Try one specific, kind sentence that is not a punchline: "I keep wanting to ask about the ceramics class you mentioned, and then I get distracted by the cheese." That is not smooth. It is better than smooth. It tells her you were listening.

If she laughs and dodges, let her dodge once. If she answers, follow the answer. Ask what she made last, or whether she hates the wheel. Stay on her actual life for two exchanges before you bounce back to seasoning.

You do not have to declare that you like her. She can tell from the fact that you are both still here. Tonight's job is: be a person who is glad to be at the table.

### User
The ceramics thing is real, she brought it up in the chat last week and I actually remembered. I am scared that if I go sincere I will sound like I rehearsed it in the bathroom, which, to be fair, I kind of am. Also we already split an appetizer and I cannot tell if she wants dessert or to leave. How do I check without making it a referendum on whether the date succeeded.

### Assistant
Remembering her class is not a script. It is paying attention. If the sentence feels too polished, shorten it. "Wait, the ceramics. What are you making right now?" Then shut up. That sounds like a thought finally got to the front of the line.

Dessert is not a verdict. Ask about the next twenty minutes, not the relationship. "Dessert menu, or a walk around the block?" Two pleasant options, neither meaning you failed. If she is full, you move. Do not negotiate her into tiramisu to prove the night was good.

You have known her in person for under two hours. Asking is the whole skill.

When you get back, do not lead with the bathroom. No "sorry, I needed a minute." Sit. If the pause feels loud, the ceramics question fills it.

### User
Okay. Walking back now. Last thing. If it keeps being this mix of clumsy and actually fun, how do I end the night without either vanishing or doing the whole "so this was nice" speech that sounds like a performance review.

### Assistant
End it the same way you have been talking: one kind, specific sentence and a next step that is small enough to be real.

When you are outside, try: "I had a good time. The pepper flakes were a highlight. Walk or coffee later this week?" That names what happened and gives an easy yes or no.

If she says yes, pick a day range: "Thursday or Saturday afternoon," not "sometime." If she is busy, say "totally fine" and mean it. Do not undercut the ask with a joke.

Then leave. Text when you get home: "Made it back. Thanks for the pizza and the Parmesan incident." Specific, kind, done. Go sit down. Ask about the ceramics.
