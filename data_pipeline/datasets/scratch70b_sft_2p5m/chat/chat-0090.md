### User
I asked for a trim. I showed a photo of my hair at shoulder length. I said keep the length, just clean the ends. I walked out with a cut that sits above my collarbone. I paid because I didn't want a scene in the chair. I've been home an hour. I keep looking in the bathroom mirror like the length might grow back if I stare. I don't know if I should go back tomorrow and say something, or live with it, or cry in a useful way. I also don't know if I even want advice. I might just be talking. Last year I would have spiraled until 1 a.m. I'm trying not to do that, which is why I'm here instead of texting five people the same paragraph.

### Assistant
Do you still want help with what to do next, or do you want me to listen and then stop? I can do either. I don't want to hand you a plan if you only needed to say it out loud.

If you want the plan, say so. If you want the listening version, I can stay on that: it is reasonable to feel ambushed when you asked for a trim and got a new haircut. Paying to avoid a scene is common. It does not mean you agreed the length was right. You don't have to decide tonight whether you "overreacted." The mirror is telling you something true about inches.

I'm asking because last-year-you would have wanted a nine-step recovery protocol, and this-year-you might want a sandwich and a closed bathroom door. Which is it right now?

### User
I think I want help, but small help. Not a speech about how hair grows. I know hair grows. I want to know if going back is a normal thing or if that's being a problem customer. And I don't want you to tell me I look fine. You can't see me.

### Assistant
Going back is a normal thing if the shop still has your time on the books as a paid cut and the result is not what you asked for. It is not a personality type. It is a complaint about a service. Whether they will fix it is a different question. Some places will even it, reshape, or not charge a correction. Some will say they did what the photo implied. I will not pretend I know this salon's policy, and I will not invent a refund rule.

If you go, go with the ask, not the autobiography. Bring the photo again. Say: "I asked to keep the length and only take ends. This is shorter than I wanted. Is there a correction you can do, or not?" Then you hear the answer. "Not" is a possible answer. If they offer to go shorter to even a mistake, you can say no. Shorter is not a gift right now.

If you don't go, that is also a complete choice. You are not required to spend another hour in that chair to prove you have standards. Living with it until it grows is boring and valid. The useful version of "live with it" is practical, not spiritual: a clip or a headband you already own, a part you already know how to do, not a new identity.

What I would not do is write a novel in a review tonight while you are still hot. If you want to leave feedback, wait until you can say one sentence about the length request and the result. You can still do that tomorrow.

I also would not text the five people yet unless one of them is actually calming. Five mirrors is how 1 a.m. happens.

You said small help. That's the small version: one conversation at the shop if you want a shot at a fix, or no conversation and a boring hair week. I am not going to sell you a product, a vitamin, or a "you actually look so much more grown."

Still want me on this, or is that enough for the bathroom hour?

### User
Enough. I might go in the morning before I can talk myself out of it. If they say they can't add length, I'm going to feel stupid for asking.

### Assistant
They cannot add length. That is not you failing a quiz. That is hair. You would be asking whether they can make the shape less of a shock, or whether they will own the miss. If they can't help, you leave. Feeling stupid is optional, and it is not information. You asked for what you asked for. The cut is short. Those two facts can sit next to each other without a verdict on your character.
