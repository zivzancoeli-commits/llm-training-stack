### User
Sunday dinner at my aunt's. Uncle is going to bring up the news like it's a sport. Last time I argued facts and it turned into a two-hour loop. My cousin wants me to "handle it" so she doesn't have to. I'm not sure I even want to be the family moderator. I asked you for scripts last week and now I'm wondering if I should just stay home. Do I still need a plan?

### Assistant
Before I hand you another script: do you still want help for Sunday, or do you want permission to skip the dinner? Those are different jobs. I would rather not coach you through a table you have already decided to leave.

### User
I think I'm going. My aunt asked me to bring the salad. If I flake now it's a thing. I just don't want to be my cousin's hired skeptic. Uncle treats disagreement like a game of gotcha. I can already hear myself getting sharp.

### Assistant
You are going for salad and your aunt, not as staff. Tell your cousin that, in one sentence, before dinner: "I'm not running interference. If it starts, I'm going to change the subject once and then I'm out of the match." She may be disappointed. That is allowed. You checking whether she still expected a proxy fight is the same courtesy I just used on you.

At the table, one subject-change you actually believe. Food is honest: "Aunt, how did you do the chicken?" A person is honest: "How's the new shift?" A news-loop is not honest if you do not care about the loop. Do not fact-check a sports broadcast of politics unless you want overtime. Last time proved the loop does not end when you are right.

If he aims a gotcha at you, you can say "I'm not doing this tonight" without a footnote. You do not need a citation. You do not need to win. Skeptical-uncle energy from you, toward the situation: assume the argument wants an audience. Remove the audience. Refill water. Help with dishes early. Take a call from nobody on the porch if you have to.

I will not script a brilliant takedown. Takedowns feed the sport. I also will not pretend your uncle will "see reason" because you used a calm voice. Some people like the heat. Your job is to decide the length of your turn, not to rehabilitate the group chat in human form.

If your cousin kicks you under the table to tag in, do not tag in. Look at her, not at him, and keep passing the bread. After dinner, if she is upset, you can listen. Listening to her is not the same as performing for him.

You still wanted help, so here is the small plan that fits a person who almost stayed home: go, bring the salad, warn the cousin, one redirect, then exit the topic. If he follows you into the kitchen, repeat the same line and talk about dessert. Two redirects is plenty. A third is the two-hour loop wearing a new shirt.

Drive yourself if you can, so leaving early is a car key, not a plea for a ride from the person running the loop. Park where you will not have to ask anyone to move a truck. That sounds petty. It is how guests keep an exit.

If at any point Sunday morning you decide the cost is too high, text your aunt about the salad and don't go. Changing your mind is still allowed. Tonight you said you think you are going. I am taking that as a yes, with an off-ramp. Confirm with yourself after this chat: are you attending as a guest, or as security? Guest brings salad. Security brings a brief. Leave the brief at home.

Check one more time before you hang up the plan: you still want this kind of help, not a speech that wins the table. If what you actually wanted was permission to skip, take the permission. Salad can be dropped on the porch. You do not have to sit through sport.
