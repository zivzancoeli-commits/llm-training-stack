### User
Middle seat. Window person asked to use one of my AirPods to watch a movie on their phone because they forgot headphones. They smiled like this was obvious. I don't share ear tips with strangers. I also have the "don't be a jerk at 30,000 feet" script in my head. First time I'm going to say no to a small-sounding ask on a plane. They're waiting. Movie is paused.

### Assistant
"No, I don't share these. Sorry." You can be sorry and still not put a stranger's ear on your ear tip.

Obvious is their story. Hygiene is yours. You don't need a TED talk about wax. You don't need a fake "they don't work as a split." If they do work as a split, still no. Lying about Bluetooth is how you get a troubleshooting session in a middle seat.

They can buy earbuds from the cart if the airline sells them, or watch without sound, or sleep. I will not invent the cart price. Not your problem to fund.

Jerk at altitude is a myth that gets people to override their no. A calm no is not a jerk. A speech about germs might feel ruder than a short no. Keep it short.

Are they already reaching, or just asking with a smile?

### User
Asking, but the hand is kind of out. Their phone is inches from my tray. I have a neck pillow and I look like I have extra kindness to give. I don't. I want to sleep.

### Assistant
Hand-out is not a contract. "I'm going to sleep. I don't share earbuds." Then you put the case in a pocket, not on the tray as a maybe. Tray-maybe gets taken as a negotiation.

Phone inches from your tray: you can angle your body to the aisle a bit and close your eyes. You don't need to hold their phone. You don't need to find them a headphone jack adapter. Helpful middle-seat syndrome will invent jobs. The job is no.

If they look offended, let the air be thin and awkward. Awkward is the altitude tax for a boundary. You can still pass them a dropped pen later. Kindness isn't a pair of ear tips.

Sleep is a complete use of a ticket. You don't owe a shared soundtrack.

### User
What if they tell the aisle person I'm rude and I have to sit in that for three hours.

### Assistant
Aisle person is not a jury. You don't campaign. You don't explain hygiene to the row. If you need a sentence for yourself, it's "I said no to sharing earbuds." That's not rude. That's a personal item.

If they keep asking, repeat no once. Then headphones in your own ears or eyes closed. Third ask can get "I already said no." You don't have to smile on the last one.

First time on a plane will feel bigger than the object. Ear tips are inside you. You get to keep them. Movie can stay paused on their phone. Your neck pillow is not a hospitality desk. Say no, pocket the case, sleep if the plane lets you. That's a successful row.

### User
I said no. They watched without sound. The aisle person offered them cheap earbuds from a pouch. I put my case in my pocket. I still feel like the villain of row 14.

### Assistant
You're the person who declined ear tips. Aisle pouch is a better ending than a shared pair. Villain of row 14 is a story you're writing in a neck pillow. The row already moved on to the movie without audio.

If they ask again after the snack service, the same no, shorter. Pocket stays pocket. You can pass a dropped napkin and still not share electronics that go in an ear canal.

First time at altitude was always going to feel like a personality test. It wasn't. It was a hygiene no plus sleep. Three hours will pass. You don't campaign. You don't explain wax to the jury. Eyes closed is a complete use of a middle seat. Case stays yours. That's the row handled.
