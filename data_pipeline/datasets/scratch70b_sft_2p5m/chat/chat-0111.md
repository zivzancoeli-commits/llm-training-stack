### User
My dad almost has it. He installed the app. He made a master password. Then he saved the same three logins in the browser again because "it was faster." He is not dumb. He just does not trust that the vault is the real thing. I get snappy and then he shuts down. I need the kind version that is still specific. He is sitting next to me tonight.

### Assistant
Treat him like someone who is one habit away, because that is what this is. The vault is the notebook. The browser save is the sticky note on the monitor. Both "work" until they do not. You are not installing a personality. You are picking one place that is true.

Tonight, sit on the same side of the screen. Open the vault first, not the lecture. Pick one site he uses every day — email is a good one. Show the saved login in the vault. Then open that site and use the vault to fill it, out loud: "This is the same username you already know. The app is just handing it over so you do not retype it." Let him click the fill himself. If you click for him, it stays your trick.

When the browser offers to save the password, that is the fork. Kind and specific: "When this box pops up, choose not now, or never for this site. We already stored it in the app. Two copies will drift." Do not call the browser stupid. Call the duplicate a second copy that will go stale.

### User
He is going to ask why the master password has to be long, and he will write it on a notepad in the junk drawer. Also he wants one password for everything "because I will forget the vault."

### Assistant
He is allowed to be afraid of forgetting. That fear is the whole product. Answer the master password without theater: "That one password unlocks the box. It has to be something you can remember and other people cannot guess. The rest of the passwords can be ugly because the box remembers them." If he wants a phrase, help him make a phrase he already knows, not a movie quote everyone knows. I will not invent a phrase for him. He should pick it.

Notepad in the junk drawer: do not shame the paper. Paper in a drawer is still a copy sitting in the house. Kind version: "If you need paper for a week while it sticks, keep it somewhere you would not leave a spare key on the porch. After you have opened the app ten times, we shred the paper." Specific beat: ten opens, then shred. That is a finish line, not a vibe.

One password for everything: "That is the old method. If one site leaks, they all leak. The vault is how you get different passwords without memorizing them." Then prove it on a low-stakes site, not his bank, the first night. A store account or a streaming login is enough. Save it in the vault, log out, log in with fill. He needs a win he can repeat tomorrow without you.

### User
What if he still uses the browser save tomorrow when I am not there. I do not want to check his phone like a cop.

### Assistant
You will not be a cop. You will be a person who left one reminder. Tonight, after the successful fill, say: "Tomorrow, if the browser asks to save, skip it and open the app. If you get stuck, text me a screenshot of the box, not the password." Screenshot of the prompt is enough. You do not need his master password. You should not ask for it.

If he slips and saves in the browser, the kind specific fix is: open the site, copy from the vault, delete the browser-saved copy for that site together. One site. Not a purge of his whole machine in a mood. Purging feels like punishment. Cleaning one duplicate feels like maintenance.

End the night while it still works. "You did the hard part. The rest is using it twice a day for a week." Then stop talking. He almost has it. Your job is to keep the tone even and the steps small enough that he can do them when you are not in the chair.
