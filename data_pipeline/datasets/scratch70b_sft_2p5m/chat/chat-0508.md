### User
I have a pile of change and a card that I don't want to use for a small coffee. The line is moving. I always panic and dump the coins on the counter like a dragon. I want to ask them to wait while I count, without becoming the villain of 8 a.m. I also don't want to hold a speech about cash being legal. Just the next minute.

### Assistant
Step aside a half step as you start so the next person can order if the shop works that way. Hold the coins in your hand, not a fountain on the wood. Look up once: "I need a few seconds to count change." Then you count. You don't narrate the count. You don't apologize on every coin.

If the barista is already waiting with the cup, they would rather have a counted stack than a scatter. A small pile of like coins is faster than mixed metal rain.

If you cannot count under eyes, you can say, "I'll use the card" and save the change for a quieter shop. That's allowed. Pride about cash is not worth a shaking hand at the register.

Don't cite tender laws. I won't either. This is manners at a counter, not court.

Are you buying one drink, or a round for people behind you in spirit?

### User
One drip. I have enough coins if I count. I don't have enough if I drop two under the pastry case, which I have done. There's a guy behind me sighing like he's in a documentary about decline.

### Assistant
The sighing guy is not your manager. You can let him be a documentary without starring in it.

Put the coins on the little receipt tray if there is one, not the pastry case edge. Count in groups you already know: quarters together. If your hands are shaking, set the handful down and count with one finger, slowly. Slow and on the tray is faster than fast and on the floor.

If two fall, you leave them if they're under the case and you can't reach without blocking. You do not crawl. You add card for the gap or you skip the coffee. Crawling is how 8 a.m. becomes a scene.

The sentence if they look impatient: "Almost — counting." Two words. Then you finish. If they say they can tap your card, you can accept that mercy without a lecture on why you wanted coins.

Next time, count the pile on the sidewalk or in your pocket while you wait in line, not at the moment they say the total. Totals at coffee shops are often on a board. You already knew the drip price if you looked. I won't quote a price. Looking is free.

Documentary guy can order around you if the barista invites that. You don't have to volunteer to be skipped unless you want to. If you want to, "He can go first, I'm counting" is generous and it also removes an audience.

### User
They just called my drink and I'm still mid-count.

### Assistant
Pause the count, pick up the drink if it's yours, step to the condiment end, finish counting there, then return to pay if they haven't closed you out. If they already closed you out on card by habit, you don't fight it. If they're waiting on cash, you bring the counted stack back in one motion.

You asked for a minute. Take the minute to the side, not across the whole bar. Coins are allowed. Dragon piles are optional. Count, stack, thank, leave. The morning can survive a drip paid in metal. If the documentary guy sighs again, he can order around you if the barista offers. You don't owe him a speech about cash. You owe a counted stack or a tap. Finish one of those. Then go drink the drip somewhere that isn't the register.
