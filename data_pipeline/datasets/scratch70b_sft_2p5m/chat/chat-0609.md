### User
Devin asked me to "run my deck through a quick AI rewrite" and send him the file so he can present it as his. It is my analysis. He missed the last two working sessions. He says it is just wording. I know it is not just wording. I don't want to dummy his byline. I also don't want a Slack manifesto. The meeting is tomorrow.

### Assistant
Do not send him your deck to wear. A rewrite, human or machine, does not change whose work it is. His byline on your charts is still your charts. Tomorrow's steering meeting will not know the difference unless you keep the name honest.

Say: "I won't send a version for you to present as yours. I can present it, or we can both be on the slide, or you present your own." Three doors. None of them is a ghostwrite.

I will not invent your company's AI policy. I will not invent plagiarism rules. I will tell you the practical split: help with a comma versus handing him a product. Comma is a comment in the doc. Product is the file he asked for. Decline the file.

If he already has an old copy, you still do not send a cleaner one with his name. Old and messy is his problem if he intends to claim it.

Did he ask in writing, or only at your desk?

### User
Desk, then a DM: "just paste into the tool and drop it here, I'll tweak." He put his name on the title slide in a screenshot. My charts are still in the background. Tomorrow is a steering meeting. My manager will be there.

### Assistant
The screenshot is useful. You do not need a manifesto. Reply on the DM: "Don't put your name on my charts. I won't paste this into a tool for you. I'll present the analysis, or we list both names if you actually contribute tonight." Then you tell your manager a shorter version if the steering meeting would otherwise surprise them: "The deck is mine. Devin asked to present it as his. I'm presenting it." That is not a war. That is a map of who talks.

If he "tweaks" by changing fonts, that is not contribution. If he adds a slide of his own from his actual work, that slide can have his name. Charts stay yours.

Do not paste into the tool for him. If you use a tool on your own words for your own clarity, that is your process. Doing it so he can wear it is the dummy. Skip it.

Tomorrow, if he opens with your chart and his name, you can say in the room, "I'll speak to this analysis — I built it." Flat. No TED talk. Then you speak.

### User
He said if I don't help he'll tell people I don't collaborate. I do collaborate. I just don't launder.

### Assistant
Collaborate is a word he is using as a lever. You can collaborate on a shared doc with comments. You cannot collaborate on a costume. If he tells people you do not collaborate, your DM is the record. Your manager map is the record. You do not need a counter-rumor campaign. You need to present the work you did.

If he wants a path back, he can take a section he actually owns, tonight, and you can link it. If he does not, he can sit in the meeting and listen. Listening is also a team sport.

Do not dummy the rewrite. Do not drop the file. Title slide stays honest. Tomorrow you talk to your charts. He can tweak his own sentences on his own time. Your analysis is not a skin. Keep it on your bones. Send the DM now, ping the manager if the byline would land wrong in steering, then go back to the actual deck. The tool can wait. The name cannot be his.
