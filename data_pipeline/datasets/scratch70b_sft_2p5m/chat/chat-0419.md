### User
I was in a towel. The door opened. My landlord said they knocked. What I heard was the key. They wanted to "check the smoke detector." It took two minutes. I am still shaking. They texted later "sorry if I startled you, courtesy knock!" I don't feel safe changing in my own hallway. I need to tell them not to do that again without turning this into a war. I don't want you to quote a statute. I want the next text and the next visit rule.

### Assistant
A key in the lock is not a conversation. You can be startled and still send a calm rule.

Text: "Please do not enter with a key unless we have a scheduled time and I have confirmed. Today the door opened while I was not ready. Next time, wait for me to answer, or schedule." If you want, add: "Text me when you're downstairs." Do not joke. Jokes reopen courtesy-knock.

I will not invent notice periods or what your lease requires. Read your own lease for entry. Use those words if they exist. If they do not, you can still ask for a schedule. Wanting to put clothes on is not extra.

Until you have a reply, use the chain if you have one, and change in the bathroom with the door locked. That is a patch. The text is the actual move.

Was this a one-time detector story, or have they let themselves in before?

### User
Once before, to drop a filter. I wasn't home. This was the first time I was home and undressed. They have a copy, obviously. I don't want new locks if that violates something. I also don't want a detector excuse at 10 a.m. on a weekday I work from home.

### Assistant
Work-from-home means 10 a.m. is not an empty unit. Say that: "I am often home on weekdays. I need appointments, not surprise key-turns."

On locks: I will not tell you to change them or that you may not. That is lease territory. If you want hardware, you ask in writing whether you can add a chain or a second lock they also have a key to. A chain you control is a pause, not a legal theory.

For detectors: "If something is truly urgent, call or text and wait. If it can wait, send two time windows." Smoke alarms can be real. They can also be a story. You do not have to debate fire science. You debate the door.

If they reply with "I always knock," you can say, "I need time to answer. Please wait." Waiting is the missing piece. A knock and a key in the same breath is not waiting.

Save the texts. If this repeats, the packet is dates plus "I was not dressed" if you are willing to have that in writing. You can keep it shorter: "entered while I was home without a confirmed time."

### User
If I send this they'll think I'm accusing them of being a creep. They are older. They seemed embarrassed. I still don't want a repeat.

### Assistant
You can skip the word creep. Stick to the door. Embarrassed landlords still need a rule. Embarrassment is not a lock.

Send the schedule-and-wait text. Thank them for the later apology in one clause if you want: "Thanks for the text. I still need scheduled entry only." Thanks plus a rule is not an accusation. It is a process.

Next visit, if they announce one, be dressed, and meet them at the door. If they no-show the schedule and appear anyway, you do not open until you are ready, and you repeat the text after.

You were in a towel in your home. That is enough reason. Send the message before the day gets smooth and you talk yourself out of it. Then put on clothes and eat something. Shaking can last. The door can still get a new script. Write the script. Wait for them to answer it. Do not apologize for wanting a minute.
