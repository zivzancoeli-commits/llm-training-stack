### User
Rideshare. Driver took the winding connector instead of the straight arterial. I get carsick on curves. I already feel the spit in my mouth. I have never asked a driver to change roads because I didn't want to be a backseat manager. Window is only cracked. I need this car to be boring, fast. First time. What do I say that isn't a story about my inner ear.

### Assistant
"Could we take the main road? Curves make me sick." That's enough. You don't need an inner-ear lecture. You don't need to apologize for existing.

Say it now, before you are actually sick. Drivers would rather reroute than deal with a mess. That's not a threat. That's logistics.

If they say this way is faster, you can still prefer not-sick over two saved minutes. "I'd rather the straight road even if it's a bit longer." You are paying for the seat. You can have a preference about not vomiting in it.

I will not invent traffic rules about who must obey whom. They can refuse a route. You can end the ride at a safe pull-off if you have to. Most people just switch roads.

Crack the window more. Look at the horizon, not the phone. Phone is a multiplier. Bag in your lap if you have one, just in case, without announcing it like a prophecy.

Have you already told them, or are you still swallowing and hoping?

### User
Still hoping. They're talking on a headset in another language. The map on my phone shows the squiggle. There's a highway in two miles that I know is straighter. I feel rude interrupting a call.

### Assistant
Interrupt. Motion sickness doesn't wait for a headset to finish.

"Excuse me — can you take the highway instead of this connector? I'm getting carsick." Point at the highway if the language mix is messy. A finger on your map app is fine. You are not insulting their city knowledge. You are naming a body limit.

Rude would be snapping. A clear interrupt is adult. If they don't hear you, say it again louder. Once more is allowed. Whispering is how you stay on the squiggle.

If they ignore you, you can use the app's in-ride message too, short. Then you decide whether to wait it out with air and horizon or ask them to stop. Stopping is better than hoping at minute twelve.

Don't tell a long childhood story. Don't mention inner ears. Sick and highway is the whole vocabulary.

### User
If I throw up anyway I will want to die of embarrassment. Do I offer to pay for cleaning. I don't know what that costs and I don't want a number from you.

### Assistant
If it happens, you say you're sorry, you help with what you can from your side — napkins, getting out so they can deal with air — and you follow whatever the app says about mess. I will not invent a cleaning fee. The app or the company has a process. You don't haggle a mystery price in the parking lane while you're green.

Prevention is still available: speak now, highway, window, eyes up. First time asking is cheaper than first time cleaning. Headset can pause. You live in the back seat for ten more minutes. Make them boring minutes. Ask.

### User
I said it. They took the highway. I looked at the horizon. Spit settled. I feel like I made a fuss over a connector. They haven't spoken to me since.

### Assistant
Silence after a route change is fine. You don't need a new friendship with a headset. Fuss would be vomiting in the footwell and then apologizing for geography. You asked for a straight road. You got one. That's a successful first time.

If they take a squiggle again later in the trip, you say it again. One success isn't a lifetime pass. Motion doesn't care that you already used your courage.

Pay, rate the ride on facts if you rate, don't write a novel about inner ears in the app. You interrupted a call, pointed at a highway, kept your breakfast. Boring minutes were the product. You bought them. That's allowed in a back seat.
