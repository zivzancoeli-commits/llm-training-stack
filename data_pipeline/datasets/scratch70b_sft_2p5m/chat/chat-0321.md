### User
I hit a pothole that I swear was not there last week. The rim is bent. The tire is holding air, I think. I'm in a grocery parking lot two blocks later because I did not want to stop in the lane. I have photos of the hole from a weird angle because I went back on foot like a detective. I don't know whether to call a shop, the city, my insurance, or all of them. I also don't want to file something and then find out I did it wrong.

### Assistant
Safety first, paperwork second. If the tire is holding and the car does not pull like a shopping cart, you can move it to a shop rather than sit in a lot all night. If it pulls, wobbles, or the wheel looks like a taco, you do not drive it as a hero. I cannot see the wheel. If you are unsure, a tow is the boring expensive option that does not turn a rim into a crash. I will not quote prices.

Do not call all of them in a panic stack before you have photos and a location. You already did the detective walk, which is useful. Add a screenshot of the map pin, the street name, and the time. Photograph the rim next to the car, not just the hole.

I will not tell you that a city "must" pay, or that insurance "must" cover a rim. Those depend on policies, deductibles, and local processes I do not have. I can tell you the usual order people use: make the car safe, then report the hole through whatever official 311-style portal your town actually publishes, then talk to a shop about the rim, then decide whether your insurance is worth calling after you know a number.

Did the car shake after, and is the tire actually firm when you press it with your hand?

### User
It shook once and then felt mostly normal. The tire feels firm. There's a shop I use about a mile away, still open. I have a deductible I cannot remember. I feel like if I wait the city will say I should have reported tonight.

### Assistant
Go to the shop while it is open. A firm tire and one shake can still hide a bent rim that fails later. Let them look. You do not have to decide insurance in the lot.

Report the hole tonight if you have a portal that takes photos after hours. Many do. If you cannot find a real city page, do not use a random ad form. Use the website on a parking sign or the town's own domain. If nothing is open, morning is not a moral failure. Your photos have timestamps.

Write down what you remember: direction of travel, weather, whether the hole was marked, whether you were avoiding something. Memory fades. Do not invent a cone that was not there.

Call insurance only when you have a repair estimate or a clear reason. A deductible you cannot remember is a reason to log into your account, not to guess on the phone. If you are in someone else's car, stop and call that person before you promise anyone a claim.

I am uncertain whether a report tonight changes money. It does create a record that the hole existed. That is enough reason to click a form after the shop, not instead of the shop.

### User
If the shop says I can drive on it I will want to ignore everything else. That's my habit.

### Assistant
If they say it is safe, you can drive. You can still submit the hole photo. Two minutes in a portal is not a personality change. Ignoring the hole is how the next person finds it at night.

For the next ten minutes: press the tire once more, drive the mile if it still feels normal, ask the shop to look at the rim, and while they look, upload the hole photos to the official report if you found it. Text yourself the location string. Do not argue with a cone theory.

A pothole wrecked a rim. That is a Tuesday in a lot of places. Get the wheel checked, plant a record of the hole, and wait to talk money until someone who can see the car gives you a number. You did not fail a form by not knowing the whole flowchart in a grocery lot.
