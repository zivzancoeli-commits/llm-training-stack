### User
A friend asked if their new haircut looked bad. I said it looked harsher than the last one, not that they looked bad as a person. They went quiet, then posted a story about "friends who think they're honest." They also asked last month if they should text their ex, I said I wouldn't, and they texted anyway and were mad I "jinxed it." I am tired of being invited to be honest and then billed for it. They want to get coffee to "clear the air."

### Assistant
Clear the air often means you apologize for answering the question they asked. You don't have to.

You can go to coffee if you want the friendship. You don't have to go to be sentenced. A useful line: "I'll come if we're talking about how to ask me things. I won't come to apologize for answering." If that feels too sharp, "I can talk. I'm not going to pretend the haircut answer was an attack."

On the pattern: people who want honesty only when it matches them are asking for a mirror that agrees. You can stop volunteering for that job. Next time they ask, you can say, "Do you want my real take, or do you want cheerleading?" Make them pick. If they pick real, you give a short real. If they punish it, you have data. Data means you cheerlead or you don't play. You don't have to keep paying.

I will not tell you their story post is abuse or that you must cut them off. I don't live your friendship. I will tell you you can mute a story without a press release.

Do you still want this friend in the ordinary week, or only in the guilty coffee?

### User
I want the ordinary week. The coffee feels like a trap. They texted "don't be distant" after the story, which is rich. I wasn't distant. I was at work.

### Assistant
"Don't be distant" after a vague story is a hook. You can answer the hook with a calendar, not a grovel: "I was at work. I can do coffee Thursday if you want to talk, not if you want me to take back the haircut sentence." If Thursday is a trap you can already see, you skip coffee and you keep the ordinary texts about shows and errands. Ordinary is the friendship. Tribunal is optional.

At coffee, if it happens, don't bring a binder of last month's ex texts. One pattern sentence is enough: "When you ask me and then post about honest friends, I don't want to answer next time." Then you see if they can stand that. If they cry-perform, you can be kind without retracting. Kind is "I care about you." Retracting is "your haircut was always perfect." Don't.

If they want rules going forward, propose one: questions get labeled. Advice or comfort. You will not guess.

Don't write a matching story. Don't recruit mutuals. This is small and it can stay small.

### User
What if they ask about the haircut again at coffee to "test" me?

### Assistant
You name the test. "If you're testing me, I'm going to pick comfort and say it's growing on me, or we can not do hair. I don't want a quiz." Then you drink the coffee or you don't. Quizzes are not intimacy.

You can be an honest friend without being an unpaid consultant who gets billed in stories. Ask them to choose the product. Stop delivering free takes into a punishment machine. Ordinary week, fewer tribunals, shorter answers. That's a viable friendship if they want one. If they only want a mirror, the mirror can be someone else. You can still text about a show tonight. Ordinary is the repair, if there is one. Skip the hair quiz. Keep the week. Honesty was never the crime. The billing after is. Stop paying.
