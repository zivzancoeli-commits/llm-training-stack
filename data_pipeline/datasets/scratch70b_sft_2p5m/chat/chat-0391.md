### User
I'm in the back of a taxi on the way to the airport. The driver is taking turns like we are late for a movie. I am not late. I am nauseous. I have never asked a driver to slow down. I keep thinking they'll take it as an insult to their skill. There is no divider. I can see the speedometer. I have ten minutes before the highway entrance. Help me say it once, clearly, without a joke.

### Assistant
You can ask for a slower car. That is a passenger sentence, not a review of their identity.

Say it to the back of the seat, loud enough: "Could you slow down, please? I'm getting carsick." Then stop. The reason is useful. It is not an insult. Skill is not the topic. Your stomach is.

If you do not want to mention nausea, "Please slow down" is still complete. Add "I'm not in a hurry" if that helps them drop the airport-hero pace.

I will not invent a speed limit or a ticket risk. You can see the needle. You do not need a number in the request unless you want one. "A bit slower" is enough.

Are you able to speak without raising your voice, or is the radio up?

### User
Radio is medium. They asked if I was late to the airport and I said no, but they still drive like I lied. I have a paper bag. I don't want to throw up in a stranger's car. If I sound polite I think they won't hear me.

### Assistant
Polite and audible are compatible. Turn your face toward them. Skip the paper-bag theater until you need it. "Hey — please slow down. I'm not late, and I'm getting sick." If the radio eats it, "Excuse me" first, then the line.

If they laugh and speed up, you repeat once: "I need you to slow down." Flat. If they still do not, you can ask them to pull over when it is safe. Getting out of a car that is making you ill is allowed. You can finish the trip another way if you have time. I will not pick a fare or a rideshare brand.

Do not apologize for having an inner ear. Do not compliment their driving as a sandwich around the request. Sandwiches bury the ask.

Hands on the door are fine. Leaning forward is fine. You are not being rude. You are preventing a mess they also do not want.

### User
What if they get offended and dump me on the shoulder? I have a flight. I also don't want to be the passenger who films people.

### Assistant
I will not predict whether a driver will dump you. Most people slow down when a carsick passenger asks once, clearly. If they stop in an unsafe place, you do not hop out into traffic. You ask for a safer spot. If they stop safely and tell you to get out, you get out with your bags and you call the next ride. That is ugly and still better than vomiting at highway speed.

Do not film as a first tool. Your voice is the first tool. Filming escalates a nausea request into a content fight.

You have minutes before the ramp. Use them. Say the sentence now. If the speed drops, say thanks once and look out the window. If it does not, repeat, then ask to pull over. Your flight wants you upright. The driver wants a clean back seat. Those goals match. Speak.

### User
I said it. They slowed a little, then sped up on the ramp. I still have the bag. Airport is twenty minutes.

### Assistant
Say it again, now: "Please keep it slower on the highway. I'm still sick." If the needle climbs, ask them to use the right lane. If they refuse and you are still nauseous, "I need you to pull over when you can." You already used your voice once. Repeating is not rude. A paper bag in a fast cab is a worse story than a two-minute stop. Keep the bag. Keep the sentence. You are not reviewing their skill. You are asking for a speed you can swallow. Ask again.
