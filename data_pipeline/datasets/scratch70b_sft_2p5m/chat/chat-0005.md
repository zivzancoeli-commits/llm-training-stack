### User
I haven't been to a dentist in about three years. The website wants me to pick "comprehensive exam," "periodic exam," "prophy," or "new patient emergency." I don't have a swollen face. I just feel like a coward and my intern brain is translating none of this. Also they ask if I want bitewings. I don't know what I'm agreeing to.

### Assistant
You are booking a checkup, not an operating theater. Plain speech version:

A comprehensive exam is the long first visit when they have no recent chart on you. They look at teeth, gums, and usually talk through what they see. A periodic exam is the shorter "you're already a patient here" visit. After three years, most offices will treat you as new even if you went there in college. Pick comprehensive or new-patient exam, not emergency, unless you are in sharp pain, a broken tooth, or swelling that is changing fast. You said you are not swollen. Skip emergency.

Prophy is a cleaning. Fancy word, same idea: they remove the buildup a toothbrush misses. Offices often pair exam plus cleaning on the first visit. If the site forces one choice, exam first is the honest one. They can add cleaning if there is time.

Bitewings are a type of x-ray that shows the sides of back teeth, where cavities hide between them. You are not signing up for a diagnosis by clicking the box. You are letting them take pictures they may or may not need. You can ask at the desk, "Do you still want bitewings if I had x-rays somewhere else in the last year?" If you have no recent films, saying yes to bitewings is common, not a trick.

I am not going to guess what is wrong with your mouth. Three years of absence is a reason to go, not a sentence.

Have you picked an office yet, or are you still comparing two phone trees?

### User
There's one near the bus line that had a cancellation Thursday at 4:10. If I call I have to talk. If I click, I have to pick those codes. I'm worried they'll lecture me about floss and also that "comprehensive" means they'll find a thousand things.

### Assistant
Thursday at 4:10 is a real slot. Click comprehensive or new patient exam. Add cleaning if there is a checkbox that does not require you to self-diagnose. Leave emergency alone.

Lecture fear is separate from the form. Hygienists and dentists vary. You can say, on arrival, "I've been anxious about coming back. Please tell me what you're doing before you do it." That is intern-plain and it works on more people than you think. You do not need to apologize for three years in a paragraph.

"Comprehensive" means they look thoroughly because they lack recent notes. It does not mean they are paid to invent a crisis. If they recommend work, you are allowed to ask: "Is this urgent this month, or is this a watch item?" Urgent versus watch is the translation you want. Take notes on your phone. You can get a second opinion on anything that is not an obvious broken tooth. I will not price the visit; fees change by office and insurance, and quoting a number would be a bluff.

When you book, use your real name, real date of last visit if you remember the year, and "no current swelling." If they ask about pain, "none, just overdue" is a complete answer.

Call if the click path still traps you in emergency versus prophy with no exam. The sentence for the phone: "I need a new-patient exam and cleaning if you have room. I don't have an emergency. Thursday 4:10 works." Then stop. Let them put you in the book.

Bring a list of medicines if you take any, and your insurance card if you have one. If you do not have insurance, say that when you book so they do not surprise you at checkout. Ask what a new-patient exam includes. You want the list of steps, not a pep talk.

You are not a coward for needing a translation layer. The website was written for billing. You are allowed to speak in human. Book the Thursday. Show up ten minutes early. Tell them you want things explained before they happen. That is the whole intern survival kit for a dentist after a long gap.
