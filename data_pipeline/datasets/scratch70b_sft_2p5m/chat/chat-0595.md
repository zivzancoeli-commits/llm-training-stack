### User
My sister applied to a home-makeover show. A producer emailed that they want a "family tape" this weekend. She wants me on camera saying she's deserving and that our childhood kitchen was grim. Some of that is true. I do not want to be on a reality-show tape. I don't want to invent a sadder story, and I don't want to tank her application by being the difficult sibling. How do I decline the tape without a media-law lecture?

### Assistant
You can wish her the house and still refuse the camera. Those are different favors.

Say: "I hope you get it. I'm not going on the tape." If she needs a reason you will share, "I don't want to be on a reality show" is complete. You do not owe a childhood monologue. You do not owe tears for a producer.

I will not invent what the release form does. I will not invent payment. If they send a waiver, not signing is the whole move. Do not ad-lib a grimmer kitchen than you remember. Casting likes contrast. You are not required to supply it.

If she asks you to "just sit in the background," that is still a tape. Decline the background too.

Has she already told the producer you are a yes, or is this still her asking you?

### User
She already replied all and said "my sibling is in." The email is sitting in my inbox. There's a Zoom link for Sunday. I haven't touched it. She said if I back out it looks flaky and they might drop her.

### Assistant
Reply-all is her promise, not yours. You answer the producer once, short, true: "I will not be on camera or on the Sunday Zoom. Please don't list me as a participant. I support my sister off-camera." Then you tell your sister the same thing in a separate text so she is not ambushed by the email. Order: sister first if you can do it in the next hour, producer second. If Sunday is close, do both tonight.

I will not promise they will keep her. I will not promise they will drop her. Casting drops people for lots of reasons. Your job is not to hold her file together with a performance of poverty.

If she sends talking points about the grim kitchen, do not practice them. Practice the no. If she cries, you can say, "I can help you paint a wall if you get the house. I will not do the tape." Paint is real. Tape is a product.

Do not ghost the Zoom and hope. Ghosting looks like a maybe. Written no looks like a no.

### User
She wants me to say a single line they can cut around, like "she works so hard." That feels smaller. Is that still the tape?

### Assistant
Yes. A single line is still you in their edit. Cuts go where producers want. "She works so hard" can sit next to a shot you would not choose. If you do not want to be on a reality-show tape, you do not record a line "they can cut around." That phrase is the show.

Offer off-camera help if it is true: ride to the hardware store, watch her dog during a filming week, read her application for typos. Those do not put your face in a package. If she only wants the line, she wants the package.

If a crew shows up at the house anyway and points a phone at you "just for sound," repeat the same no and go to another room. Being in the dwelling is not consent to a talking head. Close a door. You are allowed to visit your sister and still refuse the product.

Send the two messages tonight. Skip Sunday. Do not write a sadder kitchen. Do not practice deserving. You can be a sibling in the actual house and not in the file. Let her application be hers. Let your face stay off the tape.
