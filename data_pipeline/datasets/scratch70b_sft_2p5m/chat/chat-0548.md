### User
Family dinner. Aunt makes a point of second helpings. I said I was full. She put more on my plate anyway, then looked wounded when I didn't start. "You used to love this." I did love it. I am full. I have never let the food sit. I also don't want a fight about love expressed as casserole. I'm still at the table. What do I do with the extra and with her face.

### Assistant
You can leave the extra on the plate. You do not have to eat to manage her face.

"It tastes good. I'm full. I'm going to stop." You can smile without chewing. Chewing is the old contract.

If she reaches for the serving spoon again, a hand over your plate is allowed. Physical and calm. "I'm good." Two words beat a lecture about bodies.

I will not diagnose why she does this. Love-as-food is common and still not a veto over your stomach. You don't owe a medical story. "Full" is enough. Don't invent a stomach bug. Don't start a diet speech. Both become the rest of the night.

Used-to-love is nostalgia, not a serving size. You can say "I still like it. I don't have room." Like and room are different.

Is this a table of witnesses, or mostly the two of you plus a couple of people pretending to talk about weather?

### User
Witnesses. My mom is giving me the look that means don't make it a thing. Cousin is watching to see if I fold. Folding is how I became the clean-plate person. There's a lot on the plate now. I feel rude if it goes to the trash in front of her.

### Assistant
Mom's look is about her sister, not about your stomach. You can not make it a thing and still not eat it. Not making it a thing means no speech. It does not mean you finish the pile.

If trash in front of her is the landmine, you can leave it, talk, and when plates get stacked, it goes with the other leftovers. You don't have to narrate the trash. If she says you'll waste it, "I didn't ask for the second serving" is factual and sharp. Use it only if she pushes. Softer: "Save it for tomorrow if it keeps." Leftovers are a door. Some aunts take it. Some don't.

Cousin watching: folding is a show for them too. You can be the person who leaves food. That's allowed at  a table. You don't need to announce a personal growth arc.

Talk to someone else on your other side. Subject change is not cowardice. It's how dinners survive.

### User
She just said maybe I'm sick and she should make me tea. I am not sick. I am full. If I accept tea I'm in her kitchen for twenty minutes of concern.

### Assistant
"I'm not sick. I ate. Tea would be nice later if you want to sit, but I don't need it as medicine." Or skip tea entirely: "No tea, thanks. I'm fine." Concern is a second serving in a mug.

Don't laugh it off in a way that sounds like a confession. Don't list calories. Don't attack the casserole. The food can be good. Your stop can be real.

First time will feel like a scene because she treats plates as loyalty. Witnesses will survive. Mom's look will survive. You can help clear dishes in a minute so you're useful without being a garbage disposal.

Plate extra stays. Face stays hers. You stay full. That's the whole decline. Tea is optional and not a diagnosis. You already ate. You're allowed to be done.

Clear the plates when you can. Usefulness is not the same as finishing the extra. If she packs it for you to take home, you can accept a container and still not eat it at the table. Home is a different room. You can leave it in a fridge and decide later without an audience.

You used to love this. You can still like it. Full is a present-tense fact. First time letting food sit will feel like a story. Witnesses will find a new topic. Mom's look will find the car. You don't have to narrate any of that at the table. Stop. That's the decline.
