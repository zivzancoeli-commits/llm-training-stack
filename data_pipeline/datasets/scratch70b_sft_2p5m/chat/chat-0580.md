### User
I always freeze when they start stacking plates. I still want the rest of the pasta. I have never actually said "can you box this" before they walk off with it. Last time I watched my leftovers leave and then felt stupid in the parking lot. I don't want a speech. I just want the sentence and the timing, because I talk too late.

### Assistant
The timing is before the plate leaves the table, not in the parking lot. When they reach for it, that is the cue. You do not need a story about waste or a long apology. You need one sentence while your hand is still near the dish.

The sentence is: "Could I get this boxed, please?" If they already have it in the air, "Wait — can that be packed?" is still allowed. You are not interrupting a sacred ritual. You are keeping food you paid for. I will not invent the price of the pasta or a law about leftover rights. The practical move is you speak while the plate is in the room.

If you freeze on the word boxed, "to go" works. "Can I take the rest home?" works. Pick one and stop decorating it. "Sorry to be a pain" is how the plate keeps moving while you explain your personality.

Do this with your body too. Finger on the rim or a slight hold on the plate is a signal they already know. Eyes plus a sentence is enough. You do not have to stand up.

Are they already hovering with the stack, or are you asking this so you have it before dessert menus?

### User
They're going to come by in a minute. There's still a third of the pasta and half the bread. I'm with a coworker. I don't want to look cheap. Also I never know if I should stack it myself or let them do it.

### Assistant
Looking cheap is a story you brought. Taking leftover pasta is ordinary. Your coworker has seen boxes. If they have not, that is their evening, not a grade on yours.

Do not pre-stack unless the server asks you to. Some places want the food in their containers; some will hand you a box. You can say, "Box is fine, I don't need to pack it." Then let them take it. If they set a box down and leave, pack it without a documentary about your technique. Bread can go in the same box if there is room; if there is not, "bread too, please" is a second short line, not a new personality.

When they arrive: pause your coworker talk for two seconds. "Could I get the pasta boxed?" Point at the plate. Then you can go back to the conversation. The two-second pause is the whole social cost.

If they say the kitchen is slammed, you can still ask. If they say they already dumped it, you are done. Do not argue with a trash can. Tonight the food is still on the table. Use that.

I will not guess a takeout fee. If a surcharge appears later, that is a different question on the bill. Right now the job is the sentence before the stack.

### User
What if they clear my coworker's plate first and I'm still chewing? I don't want to talk with food in my mouth and I don't want to miss the moment.

### Assistant
Swallow or hold up a finger. The finger is the first-time tool. It means wait. Then you swallow and say the line. You do not owe a clean theatrical swallow. You owe a sound.

If they already took your coworker's plate and are reaching for yours while you chew, a muffled "box please" with a point is ugly and effective. Waiters have heard worse. Perfection is how last time's pasta left.

If you miss it anyway, you can still flag them on the next pass: "I wanted the pasta packed. Is it too late?" Sometimes it is not. Sometimes it is. Ask once. Do not send your coworker to the kitchen like a diplomat.

For the next minute: when they hit the table, finger, swallow, "Could I get this boxed, please?" Bread if you still want it. Then you sit there like a person who meant to eat later. That is the whole first time. The parking lot does not get a vote.
