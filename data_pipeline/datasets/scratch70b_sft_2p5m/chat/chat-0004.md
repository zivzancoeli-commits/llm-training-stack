### User
I washed a button-down once, cold water, hang dry, and it still came back looking like it belongs to a smaller cousin. Tags still on the collar. Receipt is somewhere in my email. Store is a fifteen-minute walk. I can go now. My uncle already said "they won't take it" like that's physics. Walk me through the next ten minutes like I'm not going to get lectured at the counter.

### Assistant
Ten minutes, starting now, no speeches.

Minute one: find the shirt, the hanger if you still have it, and the care tag. Photograph the tag, the fit on your body if you want evidence, and any wash-code icons. You are not building a court case. You are making it easy for a tired clerk.

Minute two: search your email for the store name plus the word receipt or order. Download the PDF or screenshot the line item. If you paid in the app, open the app while you still have wifi. Do not rely on remembering the date when you are standing in fluorescent light.

Minute three: put the shirt in a bag, not a crumpled wad. Bring the original packaging if it is still in the closet. Wear a different shirt so you are not returning the one on your back.

On the walk, decide your opening line. Not a novel. "I washed this once on cold and hang dried. It shrank. I'd like a refund or exchange under your return policy." Then you stop talking. Extra autobiography is how clerks start hunting for a reason to say no.

What did the tag actually say for washing? Machine wash, dry clean only, or something cute like "gentle story"?

### User
Machine wash cold, hang dry, 100 percent cotton. I did that. Uncle is convinced cotton always shrinks and stores know it. I paid with a card. Order email is from three weeks ago. There's a thirty-day window in the footer if I'm reading it right. I'm not 100 percent sure the footer is the real policy.

### Assistant
Good. You followed the tag. Cotton can tighten a bit; "always shrinks into a doll shirt" is uncle folklore, not a store rule you have to accept on the sidewalk.

Minutes four through six are the walk. Do not rehearse a fight. Rehearse the pause after your sentence. If they say "all cotton shrinks," you answer with the tag photo and "I followed this." If they say "final sale," you ask them to check the SKU because your email shows a thirty-day window. You are asking them to look, not accusing them of a scam.

I will not quote you a dollar amount or a law. Policies differ by chain and by whether the item was marked down. The footer is a clue, not a contract I can swear to. The clerk's screen is the thing that matters.

Minutes seven and eight: you arrive, you skip the clothes racks, you go to returns. You wait. You do not start folding other people's shirts to look helpful. When it is your turn, shirt out, receipt out, sentence out.

If they offer store credit, you can take it or you can say "I'd prefer the original payment method." Prefer is enough. You do not need a speech about principles. If they refuse and point to a posted sign you missed, you can ask for a supervisor once. Once. Then you either accept the ruling or you leave with the shirt and write their customer service later, at a table, not in the aisle.

Minutes nine and ten: you get a yes, a credit, or a no. You say thanks either way. You do not linger to win the argument for your uncle. He is not in the queue.

If they take it, walk out. If they do not, you still used the ten minutes like an adult instead of turning the shirt into a family myth. That is the part he will not expect.
