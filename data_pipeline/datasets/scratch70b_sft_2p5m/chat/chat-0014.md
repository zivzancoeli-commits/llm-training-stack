### User
We're two miles into a six-mile loop. My coworker Mara just sat on a log and said she's done. Not dramatic, just done. She's pale, her left sock is bloody at the heel, and she keeps looking at the trail like it personally insulted her. I packed extra water. She packed a granola bar and optimism. There's no cell service. The map says the shorter way back is retracing, the loop continues over a ridge. I'm the one who picked the trail. She asked me to "just decide." I have ten minutes before I want us moving. What would you actually do in those ten minutes?

### Assistant
I would stop treating this like a debate club and treat it like a body problem.

Minute one: sit down with her, not standing over the log. I would ask three things out loud: dizzy or not, nauseous or not, pain only in the heel or anywhere else. I would not diagnose. If she said chest pain, confusion, or she could not keep her eyes focused, I would not "tough it out" toward a ridge.

Minute two: water and food. I would put my bottle in her hand and make her drink. I would split whatever I have and eat something myself so it does not feel like I am nursing her. Bloody heel means the shoe is already winning. I would take the sock off, look, and not pretend a blister is a mindset issue.

Minutes three and four: the foot. Tape or a bandage on dry skin. If I had nothing, a spare sock or a scrap of fabric between skin and shoe. Loosen the laces over the heel. I would not pop a blister with a pocketknife.

Minute five: time, clouds, temperature. A ridge in wind with a limping person is a different hike than the parking-lot sign.

Minutes six and seven: the map on the ground, both of us looking. Retrace is two miles of known trail. The loop is four more plus a ridge. I would say, "Back the way we came is the shortest path to the car." Then I would shut up for ten seconds so she can hear herself think.

Minute eight: one sentence, not a speech. "I misjudged the distance for a first hike together. We turn around if that's what your foot needs."

Minutes nine and ten: we retrace, slow if she cannot put full weight on it, my pack taking her water so she is lighter. Check-in after fifteen minutes. Her in front so I can see her wobble. I would not let her apologize the whole way down.

### User
She says she isn't dizzy, just embarrassed. The heel is a popped blister, wet, maybe a quarter-size. She wants to "not ruin the day." There's a fork in twenty yards we haven't taken yet. I still feel responsible.

### Assistant
Embarrassment is not a compass. A wet quarter-size blister on a downhill is going to shout louder than her pride.

I would still retrace. The fork in twenty yards is a temptation, not a plan. Unknown forks with no service are how "we'll just peek" becomes dusk. I would say, "The day is already a hike. Finishing the loop is optional. Getting to the car on your own feet is the win."

I would tape that blister now. Wet skin plus more miles is a wider wound. The heel gets the tape, not a wrap up the calf.

Responsible looks like this: I carry the heavier pack, I keep the pace boring, I do not narrate how pretty the ridge would have been. At the car I would not joke about her quitting. Then I would drive, because a person who just limped two miles does not need to merge onto the highway while hungry.

If she insisted on the loop, I would still refuse the ridge if daylight was thin or she was pale. I would not invent a park rule. I would say, "I'm not taking you over that with a bleeding heel and no signal."

Ten minutes from now we are already walking back, her in front, talking about something stupid from work so the heel is not the whole afternoon.
