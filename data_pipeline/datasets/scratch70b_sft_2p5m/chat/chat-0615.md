### User
Leah is going away for two weeks. She dropped off a ficus that is already dropping leaves like a dog sheds. She said "you have good light" and "don't let it die." It arrived crispy. I do not want a plant eulogy. I also do not want to dummy a miracle. How do I handle this without becoming a bad friend or a fake botanist?

### Assistant
You can water a guest. You cannot reverse a plant that arrived mid-drop. Say the true thing tonight: "I'll water it on a schedule. I can't promise it rallies. It already looks rough." That is not cruelty. That is a receipt. Two weeks is a long time to pretend you are a greenhouse.

I will not diagnose the ficus. I will not invent a fertilizer or a grow light you must buy. If you know how you water your own plants, do that less rather than more. Crispy plus extra love is how people drown a thirsty look. Stick a finger in the soil. If it is wet, wait. If it is bone, water and stop. Then walk away from the pot so you do not hover it to death.

Take a photo tonight. Send it: "This is how it arrived." Timestamp. If it is a skeleton in two weeks, you have a before. Before is how you skip the eulogy fight.

Do you actually have the good light she thinks, or a cave with one window?

### User
North window. Her apartment is a south oven. I told her that. She said plants like a vacation. I have one watering can and a cat who chews leaves. The pot is on the table because the cat already noticed.

### Assistant
North is not a south oven. Vacation is a story. Move the pot to the highest shelf the cat cannot use, or behind a closed door that still gets the window. Cat plus crispy ficus is a double funeral. I will not tell you the plant is toxic. If you do not know, keep the cat off it anyway. Chewing is the issue.

Watering can: once, then calendar a reminder twice a week, not every time you feel guilty. Guilt watering is a flood.

Text Leah: "North window, cat in the house, I'll do what I can. Don't expect south-oven ficus when you back." If she wants a greenhouse, she can ask a different friend. You are a north window and a cat.

If it dies, you tell her with the arrival photo, not with a novel about your character. "It didn't make it. Here's day one." Then you stop. You are not a nursery.

### User
She'll say I didn't try. She said that about a succulent two years ago that was already mush. I still feel the mush.

### Assistant
Mush was hers then. Crispy is hers now. Trying is a finger in soil, a shelf, a photo, two weeks of not drowning it. That is a try. It may still fail. Failure of a pre-dying ficus is not a friendship grade. You already learned that on the succulent. You do not have to relearn it as a personality defect.

If she says you did not try, you can say, "I did. It arrived dropping. I sent you the picture." Then you do not enter a trial about mush. Old succulent is a different corpse. Leave it buried.

For two weeks: high shelf, boring water, no pep talks to a tree, no secret fertilizer you saw in a video. When she collects it, it is her plant again, alive or not. You can be a good friend who told the truth on day one. You cannot be a sun. Put the pot up. Send the photo. Let leaves fall without writing an obituary in the group chat. She went away. The ficus was already leaving. You are just the hallway.
