### User
My birthday is Saturday. My cousin's is Sunday. Aunt Val wants me to cancel my small dinner so I can drive two hours and "make Sunday the family day" because cousin is turning 40 and I am turning 31, which she called a nothing year. I already booked a table for four. I don't want a war. I don't want to skip my own birthday. How do I say that without a speech about whose life counts?

### Assistant
Thirty-one is a year you get to keep. Forty is a year they get to keep. They are adjacent, not a merger. You do not have to donate Saturday because Sunday has a rounder number.

Say: "I'm keeping Saturday. I can do a call on Sunday. I'm not canceling my table or doing the two-hour drive." If you want to offer a slice, offer a slice you mean: a Sunday morning text, not a vanished Saturday.

I will not invent what the restaurant will charge to cancel. I will not invent gas money. I will tell you a booked table is already a plan. Nothing-year is a ranking she invented to free your calendar. You do not have to accept the ranking.

Do not offer "maybe I'll see how Saturday feels." Maybe is how you drive tired on Sunday and resent the cake.

Has she already told cousin you are coming, or is this still her asking?

### User
She posted in the family chat "everyone is in for Sunday." I had not said yes. My four are a friend, my roommate, and two people from work. I want that dinner. I feel selfish writing this.

### Assistant
You are not selfish for keeping a table you booked. She posted a fiction. Reply in the family chat, short: "I'm not in for Sunday. Happy early birthday to [cousin]. I'm doing my own plans Saturday." Then text cousin separately if you like them: "Val put me on a list. I can't make Sunday. Hope it's good." That split keeps cousin from hearing you only through Val's loudspeaker.

Work people and a roommate are a real party. Two hours and a nothing-year speech are a different party. You can love cousin and still not merge the weekends.

If Val says family is blood, you can say, "I'll celebrate cousin another time. Saturday is mine." Another time can be a drink next month if you will actually do it. Do not promise a makeup if you will not drive it.

Selfish is a word for people who needed your car. Let it sit. Eat your dinner.

### User
She'll call my mom. Mom will do the disappointed pause. Also the restaurant only holds until 7:15 and my friend is already a late person.

### Assistant
Mom's pause is a sound, not a command. You can tell Mom first if you want to cut the ambush: "I'm keeping Saturday. I'm not driving Sunday. Val listed me without asking." Then Mom can pause at Val. If Mom pauses at you anyway, you still go to the table. You can love Mom and not let her face recast your birthday as a leftover.

Friend late: tell them 7:00 means 7:00 because of the hold. "If you're not there I may have to let the table go" is a true clock. I will not invent the restaurant's mood. You already know they hold until 7:15. Use it. Text the late friend tonight, not Saturday at 7:10.

Keep Saturday. Skip the two-hour apology. Cousin gets Sunday with the people who are actually in. You get pasta with the four you chose. Nothing-year can stay Val's phrase in a chat she does not get to run. Booked is booked. Be there before 7:15. Let Sunday be a call if you have the energy, or silence if you do not. Your turn as the birthday is allowed to be local and small. Small is still yours.
