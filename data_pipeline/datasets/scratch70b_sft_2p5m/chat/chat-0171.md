### User
I missed my friend's birthday. Not "I forgot the card." I forgot the day. Completely. She posted a picture at midnight and my stomach dropped. We've been friends since junior year. I always remember. This year I had a work crunch and I just... didn't. She liked my last message from Tuesday like everything was fine. I don't want a speech. I want to fix this without making it about my guilt. I think I know I should apologize, I just keep writing paragraphs that sound like I'm the sad main character.

### Assistant
You're close. The fix is small and specific, not a memoir.

Don't open with how bad you feel. She already knows you feel bad if you write five sentences about your week. Open with the fact, the apology, and one thing you will do that is about her, not about you being forgiven on a timeline.

Something like: "I missed your birthday. I'm sorry. That's on me, not on work. I would like to take you for [the thing she actually likes] this week if you want, and I'm not going to make you manage my guilt about it." Then stop.

Name the thing. If she likes the terrible dumpling place, say the dumpling place and a day. "We should hang soon" is how missed birthdays stay missed. If you don't know her week, offer two times, not an open tab.

You don't need a gift theory. If you already know the book she mentioned or the candle she always burns, that's enough. If you don't, don't guess a personality. Show up with the dumpling plan and your whole attention. I won't tell you a store or a price. A specific evening beats an expensive object you picked in a panic.

### User
She does like that dumpling place. I was going to say "I'm the worst" because that's our joke, but I think this isn't a joke night. Should I acknowledge that I always remember, or does that sound like I'm bragging about the years I didn't mess up?

### Assistant
Skip "I'm the worst." That's a joke that asks her to comfort you. Kind and specific means you don't hand her a second job.

Skip the streak talk too. "I always remember" makes this year sound like a fall from a pedestal. She doesn't need the pedestal. She needs you to have noticed the day. One sentence of sorry, then her.

If you want a second sentence that isn't guilt, make it about her year, not your memory: "I hope the day itself was good. I want to hear about it Friday if you're free, 7, dumplings, my treat if that's okay." If money is tight, say "my treat on the dumplings, not a whole production." Don't fake a budget you don't have. Don't perform a huge party to cancel a missed date. That's still about your embarrassment.

Text, don't bury it in a voice note she'll have to replay. If you usually call her, a call is fine, but write the three parts down first so you don't ramble into your crunch week.

If she answers short, that's information. "It's fine" might mean it's fine, or it might mean she doesn't want to host your feelings tonight. Reply once: "Okay. The invite stands. Friday 7, or tell me a better night." Then leave it. Following up every hour is how an apology becomes a drip.

### User
What if she doesn't want dumplings because the night is already ruined in her head.

### Assistant
Then you don't argue the dumplings. You ask what she wants instead, and you mean it. "If a make-up dinner sounds annoying, tell me what would feel better — a walk, a later month, or just the apology and space." That's still specific. You're giving her a menu, not a quiz.

You almost had this. The only part to cut is the part where you star as the person who failed. She is the person who had a birthday and you weren't there. Repair is showing up on a real calendar square with a real place, and letting her be a little cool about it without you narrating the coolness.

Do it today, not after you "find the right words." The right words are already boring, and that's why they work: you missed it, you're sorry, Friday 7, dumplings, her stories, you listen. Send that. Then put the phone in another room so you don't edit it into a novel.
