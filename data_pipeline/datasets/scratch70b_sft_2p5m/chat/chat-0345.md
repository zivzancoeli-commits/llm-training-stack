### User
We're going to a cabin in three weeks. Sam sent a packing list in the group: hiking boots, rain shell, headlamp, "real backpack not a tote." I own sneakers, a city raincoat, and a backpack I use for groceries. I cannot tell if this is a serious trail weekend or Sam's personality. I don't want to buy a gear kit for one trip. I also don't want to be the person in canvas shoes on a ridge. Nobody has said what the actual walks are. How do I ask without looking cheap or unprepared?

### Assistant
Ask about the walks, not about your budget. The walks decide the shoes.

In the group, one message: "Can we list the actual hikes — miles, elevation, and whether it's rock/mud? I don't own boots. I need to know if sneakers are fine or if I should rent/borrow." That is preparation. It is not a confession.

If Sam answers with vibe — "it'll be gorgeous," "come on, it's not Everest" — ask again for a park name or a trail link. A link is kinder than arguing about toughness.

I will not invent what you must buy. Boots are required for some trails and costume for a gravel loop to a lake. The list may be Sam packing as if everyone is Sam.

Do not silently order a cheap pair that will wreck your heels. Blisters are not cheaper than asking.

Has anyone been to this cabin before, or is Sam the only one who has hiked with this group?

### User
Sam hiked a lot in college. Two of us haven't. The cabin photos are a porch and woods, no cliff. There's a state park twenty minutes away. Sam said "we'll definitely do a big one." No name. I have $0 extra this month unless I skip other stuff.

### Assistant
"A big one" is not a plan. You need a name.

Reply: "I'm in for the cabin. For trails, I need a specific one. I can't buy boots this month. If the big hike needs them, I'll sit that one out or do a shorter loop in sneakers." You are still on the trip. You are declining an unnamed ridge.

If they want you on the big hike, they can name it and you can see whether sneakers or a borrowed pair work. Borrowing from a friend is allowed. Buying under pressure is how packing lists drain people.

City raincoat plus grocery backpack is fine for a porch weekend and a muddy road. A headlamp is a few dollars if you want one, or a phone flashlight if you do not. Do not let "real backpack" shame you into a shopping spree. A grocery pack carries lunch.

If the group chat goes quiet because people are embarrassed, wait a day, then ping Sam directly. Groups freeze. Direct is how you get a trail name.

### User
Sam reacted with a hiking-boot emoji and "we'll figure it out." That usually means they will figure it out by handing me a plan on Friday night.

### Assistant
Do not wait for Friday night in a parking lot.

Text Sam alone: "I need the trail name this week, not at the cabin. If it requires boots I don't have, I need that in writing so I can sit it out without drama." Then let the rest of the group see a summary later: "I'm cabin-yes, unnamed-big-hike-maybe." Clarity now prevents you being the problem on a rock.

If Sam never names it, your default is sneakers, shorter walks, porch. That is a valid weekend. You are not cheap for having a month without gear money. You are a person packing for the trip that actually exists. Make Sam attach a map. Pack the raincoat and the grocery backpack this week so you are not shopping in a panic on Thursday. Until then, do not buy a personality. A porch and a named trail are two different trips. You only paid for one of them so far. Get the name.
