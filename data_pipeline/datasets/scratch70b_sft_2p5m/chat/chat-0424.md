### User
I have a spare room I use as an office. My uncle asked if he could "stash a few boxes" while he paints his place. Then he sent a photo of a storage unit's worth of stuff: a bike, bins, a mattress on its side. He said it would be a month. His last painting project was a summer. I work in that room. I already said I'd think. He is driving over on Saturday "just to show me." I need a no that doesn't become a maybe when I see the mattress.

### Assistant
A mattress is not a few boxes. Saturday cannot be a viewing for a yes you do not have.

Text before he loads a van: "I can't store your things here. The spare room is my office. Sorry about the painting. You'll need another plan." If he is already coming, add: "You can still stop by, but I'm not taking the stuff." Do not let Saturday be an unloading.

I will not invent what a month of storage is worth. You do not have to rent him the office. Family can still use a real storage place or a friend with a garage.

Thinking was heard as a ramp. Close the ramp today.

Does he have another option you know of, or would a no leave him in a true bind this weekend?

### User
He has a garage at a friend's that he said is "full of junk." I think he wants my clean room instead of sorting his friend's junk. I feel like the mean niece with the extra door. The office is how I earn rent.

### Assistant
The extra door is how you earn rent. That sentence can be in the text if you need it: "I work in there. I can't give it up for storage." Mean is a story uncles tell when a clean room is convenient. You can survive the story.

Do not offer a corner of the office "just the bike." Corners become mattresses. If you ever wanted to help in a bounded way, one sealed bin for two weeks with a calendar end is the maximum, and only if you actually want it. From your description, you do not. So no bins.

If he shows up with a truck Saturday anyway, you do not have to open the office. Meet him at the curb or in the kitchen. "I texted. I can't take it." Repeat. Do not start rearranging your desk to prove there is no space. There is space. You are not offering it.

Help him think of the friend's garage as the real plan: "Can you make a hole in that garage this weekend?" That is a useful question. Your office is not a hole.

### User
If I hold the line he'll tell my parent I wouldn't help family. Parent will call. I already know the script. How do I not reopen it on that call?

### Assistant
On the parent call: "I said no to storing a household in my office. He has a garage option. I'm not changing it." Then you can ask how they are. You do not have to prosecute uncle. You do not have to take boxes to prove love.

If parent offers to "just keep a few things at yours," same no. Few is how the photo happened.

Send uncle the text tonight. Do not wait for Saturday's face. Faces make maybes. Your rent room stays a rent room. Painting can happen without your filing cabinet under a mattress. Say no while the van is still at his place. That is the kindest timing you have left. Use it.

### User
He just double-texted a photo of the mattress in his hallway. Saturday is still on his calendar as a drop-off.

### Assistant
Reply to the mattress: "I can't take that. Please don't bring it Saturday. The office isn't storage." If he is already driving, you still do not open the spare room. Kitchen or curb. Repeat the text he already has. A photo is not a booking. Your parent call, if it comes, gets the same no. He can make a hole in the friend's garage. You can be a niece without a bike in your filing cabinet. Send it before you sleep so Saturday is not a surprise truck. The extra door earns rent. Keep earning. Let the mattress stay in his hallway until he finds a wall that is not yours.
