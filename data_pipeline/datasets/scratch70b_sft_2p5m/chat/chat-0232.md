### User
I told the group chat two weeks ago I can't do the mountain cabin. Money and a Saturday shift. Tonight they're sending bunk photos and a poll: "who's bringing the skillet." My name is still in the poll. Someone tagged me "we know you'll fold." I already said no. I don't want to re-litigate it in front of eight people, and I don't want to wake up Saturday with them waiting in a driveway.

### Assistant
You're not folding. They are treating a closed answer like a soft maybe because the poll still has your name and a joke is cheaper than updating the list.

Don't argue the mountain in a thread of bunk photos. Argue the list. One message: "I'm not on this trip. Take me off the poll. Have a good time." That's the whole filing. You already gave money and a shift as reasons. Repeating them now invites bargaining: they'll offer to cover a night or move your shift in their imagination.

I won't invent what the cabin costs. You already decided you can't do it. That's enough.

Are you still in a shared spreadsheet or a split-the-house app, or is this only the chat?

### User
There's a notes app link with initials next to rooms. Mine is still next to the twin by the window. I never put it there. Dana did "so we remember you." I muted the chat for a day and unmuted because I was afraid they'd book a grocery run in my name. I'm tired of being the responsible adult in a joke.

### Assistant
Unmute was reasonable. Staying as the ghost roommate is not.

For the next ten minutes: open the notes link, delete your initials from the twin by the window, and write in the chat, not in a side text to Dana: "Removed myself from the room list. I'm not coming. Don't put groceries on me." Then mute again. Side-texting Dana keeps you as the secret manager of a trip you declined.

If the notes app won't let you edit, comment in the doc or the chat: "Dana, please take my name off the twin. I'm not in." You don't need a paragraph about respect. Names on beds are a logistics error. Treat it like one.

"We know you'll fold" is a dare. Don't answer the dare. Don't send a joke back. Jokes keep you in the bit. The bit is how people end up in a driveway at 7 a.m. holding a skillet they don't own.

If someone DMs "just Friday night," that's a new invitation. Decline that too if the original no still stands. A slice of a trip you can't afford is still a trip.

You are allowed to be the person who said no once. You are not required to be on-call for their packing list.

### User
Dana just sent a voice note that I'm "killing the vibe" and the cabin needs eight for the rate. I haven't opened it fully. I can see the waveform. I work Saturday 6 to 2.

### Assistant
Don't finish the voice note if the caption already told you the content. "Killing the vibe" is mood talk. "Needs eight for the rate" is their math problem. Neither one can move your Saturday shift. I don't know the cabin's rate structure and I won't guess a number. If the house needs eight, they find an eighth person or they pick a different house. That search is not your job.

Reply in text, not in a matching voice note. Voice is how this turns into a feeling contest. Text: "I can't come. I work Saturday. Please take me off the lists. Hope you get an eighth." Then stop. If they keep pinging, you don't have to keep answering. Mute is a tool, not a tantrum.

You already said no two weeks ago. The responsible-adult trap is answering every new format — poll, notes app, voice note — as if each one is a fresh hearing. It isn't. One written no per channel is plenty. After that, the vibe they want is your body in a twin bed. You need that body at work at 6.

Let the waveform sit unplayed if you want. You don't owe a performance review to a cabin. Take yourself off the bed, say it in the group once more if you haven't since the poll, and go to bed at a time that makes a 6 a.m. shift possible. That's the trip you actually have.
