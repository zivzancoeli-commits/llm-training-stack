### User
We're at a place with a cocktail list the size of a novella. They asked what I'm drinking. I used to say "I'm on antibiotics" or "I'm driving" even when I wasn't. I'm not driving. I'm not on anything. I just don't drink. This is the first time I want to say that without a prop. They already said "the rye flight is fun." I can feel the old sentence in my mouth. The server is coming back in thirty seconds. Help me do this once, clean.

### Assistant
"I don't drink. I'll take a ginger ale" — or soda, or water, or a food thing. Order the replacement in the same breath so the table doesn't have a hole.

You don't owe a reason. "Period" can stay in your head. Out loud, "I don't drink" is complete. If they ask why, you can say "It's just not my thing" or "I don't, but I'm good here." You don't have to tell a medical story. I will not diagnose you and I will not invent a health claim you should use as cover. Last year's antibiotics were a cover. Tonight you can have a preference.

If they push the rye flight, "I'm set with this" once. If they keep pushing, that's data about the date, not a failure of your phrasing.

Don't apologize. Don't say "I'm boring." Don't offer to "just hold a glass so you don't feel weird." Holding a prop is the old habit with ice.

Is this a first date, and do they already have a drink in front of them?

### User
Second date. They have a beer. They just said "you can have one, I won't tell." They think they're being cool. I like them so far. The server is at my shoulder. I need the sentence in order.

### Assistant
To the server: "No alcohol for me. Ginger ale, please." Then to your date, after the server leaves if you need a second: "I don't drink. I'm not being polite. That's the whole thing." If "I won't tell" comes back, you can smile without agreeing: "There's nothing to tell. I'm good."

Don't take a sip of theirs to be a team. That's a sip. You said period.

If they get insecure — "Is it because I drink?" — you can be kind and still separate: "You can drink. I'm not. I'm still here." If they can't enjoy a beer next to a soda, that's useful now rather than at a wedding later. You don't have to manage their beer feelings.

If the server botches it and brings a mocktail with a wink "so you don't feel left out," you can drink it or you can say "I asked for ginger ale." You don't have to perform a fancy non-drink if you didn't want one.

### User
They asked if I'm "in recovery" in a whisper like it's a password. I don't want to lie. I'm not. I also don't want to open my whole life. The rye flight is still on the table like a dare.

### Assistant
You don't have to accept the whisper categories. "No. I just don't drink." That's true. If they look disappointed they didn't get a dramatic plot, they can live. Recovery is real for some people and not a costume you put on to make a no fancier. You also don't have to prove you're "casual" by splitting the flight.

If the dare energy continues — toasting at your glass, "come on" — you can end the date later with the same data. For the next hour, you can still see if they're decent when the bit dies. One sentence, then you ask them a question about their day so the table has a second topic.

First time telling the truth is allowed to be awkward. The server already has the ginger ale order if you got it out. If you froze and they ordered you a rye, you send it back: "That's not for me. Ginger ale, please." You don't have to drink a mistake to keep a second date smooth.

Next thirty seconds: drink order, "I don't drink," no password, no prop. The novella of cocktails can stay their reading. You get a soda and a person who can handle that, or you find out they can't. Either one is a successful first time.
