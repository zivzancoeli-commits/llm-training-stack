### User
The thermostat says 64. It's June. I am wearing a hoodie at my own kitchen table. Mara sets it every morning before she leaves for the gym. If I bump it to 70 she texts "please don't, I pay more of the electric." I don't think that's how our split works, but I also don't want to audit a person over a degree. My fingers are cold. I have to work from the living room. How do I talk about air without becoming the villain of the bill?

### Assistant
You talk about the room you have to sit in, not about her gym identity.

Don't open with "meat locker." That's a joke that makes her defend a personality. "64 is too cold for me to work in the living room. I need it closer to 68–70 while I'm home during the day. We can turn it down at night or when the apartment is empty." That's a schedule, not a moral.

On the bill: I will not invent how your electric split works or what a degree "costs." You don't know either, and she's using a number she hasn't shown you. You can say, "I'm okay looking at a bill together. I'm not okay working in 64. If the split is the issue, let's look at the actual statement." If she won't look, the "I pay more" line is a feeling, not a spreadsheet.

If you have a bedroom door, you can heat your room separately with a small heater if that's safe and allowed. I won't quote a rule. If you don't, the shared thermostat is a shared object. It needs a shared number.

Are you both on the lease, and is 64 the all-day setting or only mornings?

### User
Both on the lease. 64 from 7 a.m. until she gets home at 6, then she sometimes lets it rise. I work 9–5 here. She said her body "runs hot." I believe her. I also believe I can't type. She gets home in an hour. I keep hovering my hand over the plastic like it's a crime.

### Assistant
Move it now to a number you can work in, then tell her before she walks into a surprise. Text: "I set it to 68 so I can work. Let's pick a daytime number when you're back. 64 isn't workable for me on WFH days." That's notice, not a sneak.

When she gets home, don't start at the door. Let her put the bag down. Then the same content out loud. If she goes straight to the thermostat to slam it back, you can say, "We need a number that isn't only yours. Daytime 68, nights your call" — or whatever split you can actually live with. If 68 is still too hot for her, she can wear less at home; you should not have to wear a hoodie in June as the default.

If she brings electric again, ask for the last bill on the table. If she doesn't have it, the conversation waits for paper. You still don't sit at 64 until paper arrives.

Don't run a secret war of moving it five times a day. That's how roommates get weird. One setting, named, for the work block.

### User
She just texted "I can see you changed it" from the hallway app. She's not even home. There's an app. I didn't know. I feel like I got caught.

### Assistant
You weren't stealing. It's a shared thermostat. The app is a fact, not a verdict. Reply: "Yes. I need 68 to work. Talk when you're home. Please don't drop it from the app while I'm at the table." If she drops it anyway, you send one more line and you have the in-person talk anyway. Don't get into a remote tug-of-war. That's a video game.

When she's home, add the app to the agreement: who changes it, and when. If she wants 64 when she's alone, that's a different block than your 9–5.

Next hour: stay at 68, don't apologize for having a body, bill on the table if she wants to talk money. You're not the villain of the bill. You're the person who has to type. Get a number you can feel your fingers in. That's the whole meat-locker conversation, without the joke.
