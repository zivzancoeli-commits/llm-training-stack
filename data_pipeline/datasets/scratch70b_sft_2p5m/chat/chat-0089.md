### User
I took my cousin to the city art museum on a free-hours evening. We were in a room with a temporary show. I lifted my phone to take a picture of a painting she liked so she could show her mom, who couldn't come. A guard walked over and said no photos in that gallery. I put the phone down. I wasn't trying to start a fight. My cousin looked embarrassed, and I felt like I had done something shady in front of a person whose job is to watch people. We still had an hour. I wanted her to have something to take home that wasn't a twenty-dollar postcard. I'm an intern. I do not have souvenir money. How do I handle the rest of the visit, and what do I do next time without turning it into a scene?

### Assistant
You already did the important part: you stopped. The guard is enforcing a room rule, not grading your character. Temporary shows often ban photos because of loan agreements. I don't know that museum's policy, and I won't invent a statute. If a sign said no photos, that is the rule in that room. Other rooms might allow them. That split confuses people. It is still the split.

Don't hover near the same painting with the phone "just in case." That reads as waiting the guard out. Move. Look. Talk about the work if she wants that. Memory is free. If she still wants something to take home, work the budget you actually have, which sounds like near zero after transit.

Two cheap paths. First: pencil. A lot of museums let you sketch if you are not using ink or a huge pad. I am not promising yours does. Ask at the desk: "Are pencil sketches allowed in the temporary galleries?" If yes, she draws the big shapes and a color note. If you didn't bring paper, ask whether the desk has a free gallery guide she can write on. Second: words. Outside that room, a voice note or a notes app: colors, what the figure is doing, why she liked it. Her mom gets a two-minute story. That is not a jpeg. It is still a visit she can share.

Skip the gift shop unless there is a free rack. Don't ask the guard to make an exception "for my aunt." Don't photograph the painting from a weird angle as a "detail of the frame." Same photo, extra steps.

### User
The desk person said sketches are fine in pencil, no charcoal, no stools. We didn't have a pencil. I wasn't going to buy a five-dollar museum pencil. We left. Next free evening is in two weeks. I want a plan that assumes I have a bus fare and whatever is already in my bag.

### Assistant
Then the plan is: bag check the night before, not heroics in the lobby.

Put in the bag: a pencil you already own, a few sheets of printer paper folded, a cheap eraser if you have one. Fill a water bottle at home if they allow bottles. Eat before you go. That is the whole budget. You are not building a plein-air kit.

At the door, read the photo signs before you lift a phone. If the temporary show is no-photo, you treat that floor as a looking floor. If the permanent collection allows photos without flash, that is where the phone earns its keep, and only if the sign agrees. I will not guess their signage. You look.

Pick one work she cares about, not twelve. She sketches for ten minutes. You keep time so you don't camp in front of a canvas while a class is trying to see it. If a guard says the pad is too big, you fold the paper smaller and you don't argue.

If she hates drawing, skip it. Do the voice note on a staircase or outside after, plus three sentences in a note: title if the wall text gives one, what she saw, what she would tell her mom. That costs nothing.

You don't need to apologize to the first guard on a return visit. You weren't running a scam. You need a bag that makes the second visit cheaper than shame. If your cousin would rather not go back, say that out loud before you spend the bus fare. A free evening is still an evening.
