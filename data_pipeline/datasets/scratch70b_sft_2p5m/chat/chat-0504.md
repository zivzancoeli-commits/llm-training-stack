### User
I came home and the living room wall is a color I would call mustard. My roommate painted it while I was at work. They left windows open, a tray of brushes, and a note: "Needed cheer!! We can always paint over." I pay half the rent. I did not vote for mustard. I don't want to scream about a wall and I also don't want this to become how decisions get made. The paint is still tacky in spots.

### Assistant
A wall in a shared room is a shared decision. Cheer is not a substitute for a text. You can say that without a trial about taste.

Don't touch the tacky parts. You'll make it worse and you'll get mustard on your life. Open windows if the fumes are strong; that's ventilation, not approval.

Talk tonight, not via a retaliatory note. "I saw the wall. Please don't paint shared rooms without asking me. I don't want this color as a surprise." If you actually hate it, say you want it changed, not in a month, on a timeline you both can do. If you can live with it, you can still insist the process never repeats. Those are different asks. Pick one.

I will not invent whether the lease allows paint or what a deposit will do. Read your lease if you care about that. I won't guess a landlord's mood. If the lease says no paint, that's a third party you both now share as a problem. Don't hide it from each other.

Did they paint only the living room, or did they start a hallway too?

### User
Just the one wall. They said they bought leftover gallons cheap from a guy in a parking lot, which does not make me calmer. They think I'm going to "relax when it dries." I am not relaxing.

### Assistant
Parking-lot paint is a reason to be less calm, not more. You don't need a chemistry lecture. You can say, "I'm not going to relax about a surprise wall. Next time you ask first. For this one, I want us to decide whether it stays." Then you decide.

If it stays, you still get a process: no more surprise projects, shared rooms need a yes from both, and leftover fumes get managed until they stop. If it goes, you pick a weekend and you both paint, because they created the labor. You can help and still not pretend you invited mustard.

Don't start a second color tonight as revenge. Two bad walls is not justice.

If they say "it's my half of the rent too," you can say, "Half the rent is not half a mandate. It's a reason we both get a vote." Then stop. Proverbs about ownership are how this becomes an hour.

Photograph the wall if you think a landlord might care later. That's documentation, not a threat you have to send tonight.

I won't tell you the parking-lot paint is toxic or safe. If you feel lightheaded, you leave the room, you get air, you don't play chemist. If you're worried, you can look up the can if there is a label. If there is no label, that's part of why leftover gallons are a bad surprise.

### User
Do I have to help paint it back if I never wanted this?

### Assistant
You don't have to pretend it was your idea. You may still want it gone faster than they will move. That's a practical trade: you help for a few hours to get your living room back, or you wait on them and look at mustard. Neither is morally pure. Pick the one you can stand.

Tonight: no tacky touching, one conversation, one decision — stay or go — and a rule about asking. Cheer can be a throw pillow. Walls wait for a vote. If they already love the mustard, they can still hear that surprise paint is done. A color can stay and a process can still change. That's the grown version of "we can always paint over." Always is a weekend you both pick, not a note on a tray of brushes.
