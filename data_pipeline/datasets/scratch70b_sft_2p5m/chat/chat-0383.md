### User
Third time this month. My name is on the lid. Container was in the fridge this morning. At 12:40 it is in the break room trash, empty, and Priya is telling the table, "Whoever left that pasta should season it next time." People laughed. I did not. I bought that food. I don't want to be the lunch cop. I also don't want to keep skipping lunch because someone thinks a label is a joke. I have to sit near them in the afternoon standup.

### Assistant
A labeled lunch is not a communal snack. A joke at the table does not convert it into one.

You can handle this in two layers: the food, and the audience. The food is a direct sentence to Priya. The audience is optional. You do not need a speech for the whole table unless you want one.

Today, before standup if you can catch them without a crowd: "That was my lunch. My name was on it. Don't eat my food." If you only have the table, you can still say it in one line, then stop. You do not have to joke back. Joking back is how this stays a bit.

I will not invent a company policy or what HR will do. If your workplace has a fridge note or a kitchen rule, you can use it later. You do not need a policy to name the theft.

Did they admit it was yours, or are they still pretending it was anonymous pasta?

### User
They know. Last time I said "hey that was mine" and they said "you should've put it on a higher shelf" and offered me half a granola bar. I took the granola bar like an idiot. Today I didn't take anything. I just stood there. Standup is in twenty minutes.

### Assistant
Do not take the granola bar again. That is a receipt that the swap was fine.

Twenty minutes is enough for one sentence and a lunch plan. If you will see Priya before standup, use the sentence. If you will only see them in the meeting, wait until after. Do not open standup with pasta. The meeting has a different job.

After standup, walk with them if that is the only private-enough path, or message if you work in chat: "Please don't eat my labeled food. Today was the third time. I need you to stop." No emoji. No "haha." If they escalate to "it was a joke," you can say, "I didn't find it funny. Don't do it again." Then you go get food.

For the fridge: put today's replacement in a less theatrical spot if you want, but do not accept "higher shelf" as the moral. Labels are the system. If the office has a shared doc or a paper on the fridge, you can add "labeled food is not shared" without naming Priya. Naming is for the direct talk. The sign is for the room.

If this continues after a clear stop, that is a pattern, not a mix-up. Then you can loop in whoever actually owns facilities or your manager, with dates and the label. That is not being a cop. That is not wanting to skip lunch. I will not promise a disciplinary result.

Eat something before the afternoon if you can. A skipped meal plus a grievance makes your voice sharp in the wrong room.

### User
If I tell my manager I look petty. If I don't, I keep buying lunch for the table. Priya is well liked. I'm the quiet one. What do I actually do at 3 p.m. if they bring it up as a joke in standup?

### Assistant
If they bring it up in standup, do not perform. Standup is for work. You can say, "Let's take that offline," and then you actually take it offline after. If they need a one-liner in the room, "That was my lunch" is enough. Then the next ticket. You are not required to laugh so the group can relax.

Petty is eating someone else's labeled food. Reporting a third time with dates is ordinary. You can still start with Priya at 3 p.m., not the manager. Manager is the backup if the stop does not hold. Quiet people are allowed to have containers.

3 p.m. script: find them, one sentence, stop, buy or pack food for tomorrow with the same label. If they want to hug it out, decline the hug. If they want to Venmo you, that is their offer; I will not name an amount. You can accept reimbursement without reopening the joke. You cannot accept a granola bar as the ending. Get through standup, say the line, eat. That is the rest of the day.
