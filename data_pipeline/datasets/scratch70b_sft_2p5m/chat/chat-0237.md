### User
The RSVP card has a line for a plus-one. I'm not dating anyone. The couple is cousins-of-cousins; I like them. I don't want to drag a coworker into a Saturday in a barn. I also don't want to sit at a table of pairs like I forgot my homework. The card is due Sunday. It's Thursday night. The card is on my kitchen table next to a pen I keep not picking up.

### Assistant
You can RSVP as one person. A plus-one line is an offer, not a requirement to audition a date by Sunday.

Don't recruit a coworker for a barn. That's a long day of small talk plus a ride home. A friend you already like is different, and only if they actually want cake and chairs, not if they want to do you a favor.

I won't invent the couple's seating rules. Some tables are mixed. Some are all couples. You cannot control the table from your kitchen. You can control the card.

Are you close enough to text the couple a plain question, or is the RSVP card the only channel you have?

### User
I have the bride's number from a shower in March. We don't text much. I don't want to sound like I'm asking for a date budget. I also don't want her to scramble a seating chart because I was awkward. I eat dinner alone most nights. The table of pairs is more about me than about them.

### Assistant
Then the card can stay simple: your name, one meal choice, plus-one blank or crossed out. If the card forces a number, write 1.

If you want to use the number you already have, one text is enough: "Coming solo — that okay?" That's logistics, not a date budget. I don't know how they assigned plus-ones and I won't guess. If she says yes, you're done. If she says they already counted two, you can still come as one and let them fix the chart. Charts get fixed. Awkward silence on a Thursday does not help a chart.

For the next ten minutes: pick up the pen, fill your name, pick the chicken or the pasta without a committee, leave the guest line empty, put the card in an envelope if you have one. Put a stamp on it tonight even if you mail it tomorrow. The kitchen table is where this card goes to die.

The table of pairs is a feeling. It is real and it is not a reason to hire a coworker. Sit next to whoever is also solo, or next to the cousin who talks about dogs. You can get up for air at the edge of the barn. You are not a missing assignment.

### User
There's a friend from college who might come if I asked. We haven't talked since December. Asking now feels like using them as a prop. Sitting alone feels like a statement I didn't mean to make.

### Assistant
A December silence plus a Thursday ask is a prop. Don't. If you wanted that friend there, you would have asked when the save-the-date showed up, not when the card grew teeth.

Sitting alone is only a statement if you perform it as one. You can talk to the people on either side, eat, dance or not, leave at 9:30 if the DJ gets loud. That's a wedding, not a verdict on your year.

You don't owe the couple a pair. You owe them a count. One is a count. Mail the card. Text the bride only if you need the seating question off your chest. Then put the pen away. Sunday is the deadline, not a personality test. Thursday night you can finish this in less time than it takes to spiral about barn tables. Write 1. Seal it. Put it by the keys. Then go do the dishes that are actually in the sink.
