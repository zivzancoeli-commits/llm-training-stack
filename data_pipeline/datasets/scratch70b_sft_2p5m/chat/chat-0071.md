### User
I missed my roommate's birthday. Not by an hour. I missed the entire day. We had a calendar on the fridge. I walked past the calendar. I even liked a photo of a cake on someone else's story and did not connect it. She did not say anything last night. This morning she was extra polite about the coffee. I feel like a cartoon of a bad friend. I want to fix it without making her host my guilt. I have never had to recover from this and I am one step from writing a paragraph that starts with "I know this doesn't make it okay but." Please talk like I just started this job and I still want to do it right.

### Assistant
Do not send the paragraph. The clause "I know this doesn't make it okay but" is a runway for excuses. She does not need your runway. She needs two specific things: you noticed, and you are going to do a real make-up, not a mood.

Say it in person if you will see her today. Short:

"I missed your birthday. That was careless. I am sorry. I would like to take you for dumplings tomorrow night if you are free, and I already put next year on my phone with two alerts. If tomorrow is a bad night, tell me a better one."

Kind and specific. You named the miss. You did not explain the calendar, the story, the week you had, or your personality. You offered a concrete repair with a time, not "we should celebrate sometime." You gave her an easy out on the day so the apology is not also a trap.

If dumplings are not her food, swap in the thing she actually likes. The point is a plan she can picture, not a generic treat. If you do not know what she likes, ask one question: "Dinner out, or a night in with that movie you mentioned." Two options, still specific.

What you do not do: make her comfort you. No "I feel like a monster, please tell me we are okay" while she is still extra polite about coffee. She may not be ready to sign off on okay. Your job is the apology and the repair, not a receipt.

If she says it is fine, believe the part where she does not want a scene, and still keep the dumpling plan unless she clearly declines the plan too. "It's fine" often means "do not make me manage your feelings in the kitchen." It does not always mean "please do nothing." If she says she does not want to go out, ask whether a small thing at home would feel better, and then stop asking. Repeated invitations become another job for her.

Write next year's date into your phone before you talk to her, so you are not promising a system you have not set. One alert the week before, one the morning of. The fridge calendar already failed you. Do not lecture yourself about being a cartoon. Change the reminder.

If you live together, a handwritten note on her door is optional and only if it is three lines, not a confession. Same content as the spoken apology. Then go to work on the dumpling reservation or the grocery list. Recovery is a calendar event, not a personality essay.

You are new at this particular miss. That is allowed. The competent version is small, kind, dated, and done. Coffee politeness will thaw on its own time. You do not get to speed that up with a longer message.

### User
I keep wanting to explain that I have been slammed at work and that I am bad at dates in general, not just hers. It feels honest. It also feels like I am asking her to grade my character. And I do not actually know if she likes dumplings. I guessed because we ordered them once in January.

### Assistant
Work-slammed and "bad at dates" are about you. A good apology stays on her day. "I missed your birthday" is the fact. "I have a lot going on" asks her to understand you. Kind and specific means she does not carry your explanation.

If dumplings were a January guess, ask: "I want to take you out this week. Dumplings if you still like those, the noodle place, or something quieter at home. Which sounds better." Still a plan. It just checks.

Skip the character essay. Set the two alerts. Say the short apology when you see her. Then do the thing she picks.
