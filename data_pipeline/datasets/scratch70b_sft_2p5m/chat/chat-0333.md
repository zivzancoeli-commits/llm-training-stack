### User
I parked on a side street near my friend's place. There were no signs I noticed. This morning the car is gone and a neighbor said a tow truck was there at 6 a.m. I called the non-emergency number and they gave me a lot name and an address across the river. I've never done this. I don't know what to bring, whether I should go now, or if I need cash. My insurance app is open and I haven't pressed anything. I feel like if I say the wrong sentence they'll keep the car.

### Assistant
Slow down. This is a retrieval errand, not a trial.

Before you drive to the lot, confirm the car is actually there. Call the lot, read the plate, get a yes. Write down hours. Lots close. Showing up at a gate with no one inside is a wasted afternoon.

Bring what they usually ask for: photo ID, proof you can take the car — registration or title if you have it in a drawer, insurance card if you have a card, and the keys. If the car is financed or a parent is on the title, that person may need to come or send whatever the lot accepts. I will not invent your city's rules. Ask the lot, out loud: "What documents do you need from me to release this car today?" Write the list. Bring those things, not a speech about the missing sign.

Payment: call and ask what they take. Do not assume cash in an envelope is required, and do not assume a card always works. Ask. I will not guess the fee.

Do not tell a story about the neighbor, the 6 a.m. truck, or how you are a good person. They have a clipboard. You have an ID.

Have they already confirmed the plate is on that lot, or do you only have a name from the non-emergency line?

### User
They confirmed the plate. Open until 6. They said ID, registration, and the person on the registration has to be there. The car is in my name. They said they take cards. I still don't know if I should argue about the signs.

### Assistant
Go while they are open. Argue later if you want to argue. The lot's job today is to match you to the car, take payment, and let you drive out. Signage fights happen with whoever ticketed or towed, often in writing, after you have the car. Standing at a chain-link counter explaining street parking theory rarely unlocks a gate faster.

Print or screenshot the registration if the paper is in the glove box of the missing car. That happens to people. If the only copy is inside the car, tell them that on the phone now: "The registration is in the vehicle. I have ID. What do you accept?" Get an answer before you cross the river.

When you are at the window, be boring. Name, plate, ID on the counter. Pay what they quote if you are there to leave with the car. If a number looks wrong, you can ask them to itemize. Asking for a line-item is not a fight. Inventing a discount is. I will not tell you a "fair" price. I do not have their board.

Inspect the car in the lot before you drive off: photos of bumper, wheels, glass, interior. Tow lots are dusty and rushed. Photos are for you, not for a scene.

If they want you to sign a form, read the part about damage. You can write "inspected, photos taken" if that is true. You do not need to sign a novel about fault.

### User
There's a second name on the insurance, my sister, but not on the registration. She offered to come because she's better at talking to offices. Should I bring her?

### Assistant
Bring her if she makes you calmer. Do not bring her as a substitute owner. They asked for the person on the registration. That is you. She can stand next to you and not take over the window. If she starts narrating, you can put your ID on the counter and keep talking. Offices respond to the matching name.

Leave soon enough that you are not doing this at 5:50. Traffic across the river is part of the errand. Charge your phone. Screenshot the lot's hours and the plate confirmation.

You do not need a perfect sentence. You need ID, the registration situation clarified, a card that works, photos in the lot, and the car pointed at the exit before they lock the gate. The missing sign can be a later email. Today is get the car.
