### User
I asked for a latte. They started steaming. I remembered I wanted oat because dairy wrecks my afternoon. I didn't say oat at the register. Line behind me. Pitcher is already screaming. First time I'd rather ask them to redo than drink it and feel awful. I also feel like I made their job worse on purpose. What do I say that isn't a speech about my stomach.

### Assistant
You say it now, before they pour.

"Sorry — can that be oat? I should have said it at the register." That's it. No stomach tour. No identity lecture. Timing is on you. Redo is still allowed.

They may charge a difference. I will not invent the upcharge. You pay what they say or you take dairy if you decide the afternoon isn't worth it. That's your trade. Don't argue a menu.

Line behind you will survive. Baristas redo drinks. It's annoying. It is not a crime. The crime would be waiting until the cup is in your hand and then looking tragic.

If they already poured dairy in the cup, same sentence. Cups are washable or recyclable depending on the shop. Not your speech.

Are you actually going to feel wrecked, or is this a preference you could swallow today?

### User
I will feel wrecked. I know my afternoon. I froze at the register because the person in front ordered a novel and I panicked and said "latte" like a hostage. They're pouring now. I have three seconds.

### Assistant
Three seconds: "Wait — oat milk please." Hand up if you have to. Volume over poetry. Hostage-latte is a real register brain. You still interrupt the pour.

If they finish the pour over your wait, you still ask. A finished dairy latte is not a binding ritual. "I need oat, I missed it at the till." They'll remake or they'll tell you they can't. If they can't, you can decline the drink. You don't have to consume a problem you flagged.

Don't mime a stomachache. Don't blame the novel-order person. Your freeze is yours. Fix it in a clause.

Pay the extra if they ring it. Don't disappear down the line without the drink settled. That's how tickets get messy.

### User
If they sigh I will want to take the dairy and leave a big tip as penance. I shouldn't, right.

### Assistant
Right. Don't drink dairy as penance. A sigh is a work noise. You can still want oat.

Tip what you were going to tip, or a bit more if you want, without buying an afternoon of feeling wrecked as the rest of the apology. Penance lattes are a bad product.

First time asking mid-steam will feel huge in your head. At the machine it's a milk swap. Say oat, pay if needed, take the remake, get out of the bar path. Stomach stays off the script. Register freeze happens. Redo happens. You still get to have the drink you can actually drink.

### User
I said wait, oat. They dumped the pitcher, remade it, charged a difference I paid without asking the amount like a whole story. They did not sigh. I invented the sigh. Line moved. I have the cup.

### Assistant
Good. Invented sighs are how register freeze writes fiction. You interrupted a pour, paid the difference they rang, took oat. That's a first time with no stomach speech.

Next time you can say oat at the till even if the person in front ordered a novel. Hostage-latte is a habit you can interrupt one word earlier. If you freeze again, you still have the mid-steam sentence. It worked.

Don't leave a bigger tip as a novel about your body. Tip like you were going to. Walk out of the bar path. Afternoon can stay usable. You didn't make their job worse on purpose. You corrected an order while the milk was still a pitcher. That's the point of a counter. Drink the oat.
