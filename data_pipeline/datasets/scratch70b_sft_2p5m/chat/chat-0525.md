### User
A friend asked me to tell their partner that they were at my place last night. They were not. They said it's "not a big deal" and that I "just have to confirm if anyone asks." They offered to do the same for me someday like that's a gift card. I don't want to be in their relationship as a prop, and I don't want a fight in a group chat. They want me to be ready in case they get a call tonight.

### Assistant
I won't help you build a fake alibi. I won't script what to say if the partner calls. If they weren't at your place, you don't say they were.

Your line to the friend: "I won't lie to your partner. Don't give my name. If anyone asks me, I'll say I didn't see you." That's the whole readiness: none.

"Not a big deal" is their scale. A call to you makes it your deal. You don't have to know what they were actually doing. You don't have to investigate. You have to keep your mouth off their story.

The reciprocal gift card — I'll lie for you later — is not a perk. Decline that future too.

If you want to stay friends, you can still say you care about them. Caring is not a location.

Have they already told the partner they were at your place, or is this pre-positioning?

### User
They already sent a text that they were "at [my] watching a movie." So my name is in a thread I never joined. I found out because they screenshotted it to me like I should be proud. I feel trapped. If the partner texts me, silence might look like confirmation.

### Assistant
You're not proud. You're named without consent. That's the part to close.

Text the friend, not the partner, first: "Take my name out of it. I wasn't with you. I will not confirm that movie. If they contact me, I won't play along." You can add, "Don't use my apartment as a cover again."

I will not tell you to message the partner with a reveal. That's their relationship and it can get messy in ways I won't manage. I also won't tell you to protect the lie with silence. If the partner contacts you directly, you don't have to write an essay. You can say you don't want to be in the middle, or you can say you weren't together if you are asked a direct question and you choose to answer. You should not confirm a movie that didn't happen. If you don't want to be the messenger of truth either, you can refuse the conversation: "Please talk to them, not me." What you don't do is nod along.

If you fear for someone's safety, that's a different situation than a cover story, and you use real-world help, not a fake movie. I won't diagnose their household from here.

Don't join a group chat debate. Don't post. This stays a DM to the friend who used you.

### User
They'll say I'm choosing the partner over them.

### Assistant
You are choosing not to be a false location. That's not a ranking of humans. It's a ranking of sentences.

Send the no before tonight's possible call. Don't leave your phone as an unmanned alibi booth. You can like your friend tomorrow in daylight, as a person who was not at your place. You cannot like them as a character in a movie you didn't watch. Keep your apartment yours. Let their night belong to them, including the part where they tell the truth or don't — without your name as furniture. If the partner texts anyway, you don't confirm the movie. You don't invent a second story. You stay out of the lie. That's the whole alibi you can give: none. Your place was empty of them. Keep it that way on the record.
