### User
It's 7:50. The regular bus didn't come. The district site still shows the normal route, no snow delay. Radio said some schools are on a two-hour delay. Mine didn't list us. My kid is in grade three, standing in boots that are already wet. I have a stand-up at 8:30. I don't want you to invent the district policy. I want the next twenty minutes: wait, drive, call, what.

### Assistant
Do not treat a blank website as a guarantee the bus is coming. Blank is often a lag. Radio delay plus an empty corner at 7:50 is enough to stop betting on the yellow bus.

Twenty minutes: get the kid out of the wet slush into the car or the lobby of the building you are standing in, if there is one. Wet boots plus hope is how you get a crying eight-year-old and a missed stand-up. Call the transportation number on the handbook or the magnet on the fridge, not the main office first if you have the bus line. Sentence: "Stop [name], regular time, no bus, website shows no delay. Is there a snow route or a cancellation?" Then listen. Write the answer on your notes app.

I will not invent whether you must drive. I will not invent a late bell. If they say the bus is twenty minutes out, you can wait in the car with heat. If they say they do not know, you decide with the clock you actually have. 8:30 stand-up is real. Grade three in wet socks is real. Pick with both, not with the website's optimism.

Is there a neighbor at the stop who usually knows, or are you two alone?

### User
Mrs. Chen is here with her fifth grader. She says last year they just never updated the site. She's waiting. My kid wants to wait with them because of a friendship on the bus. I can do the stand-up from my phone in the car if I have to. The roads look sloppy but not impassable from here.

### Assistant
Mrs. Chen is a data point, not a dispatcher. Last year's silence can repeat. It can also not. Use her wait as a maybe, not as your plan.

Call anyway, now, in front of the kids if you must. If transportation confirms a late snow bus with a time, you can wait with Chen in a warm car, not on the curb. If they voicemail, leave the stop name, time, and your number, then look at 8:10 as a decision point. Friendship on the bus is not worth a frozen foot. You can say to your kid, "We'll sit in the car. If it doesn't come by 8:10, I'm driving." A named time stops the endless maybe.

Stand-up from the phone: mute, camera off if you can, kid with a snack. Tell your meeting you may be driving a snow delay. One sentence in chat. Do not pretend you are at your desk if you are in a hat.

I will not tell you the road is safe. Sloppy from one corner is not a full route. If you drive, go the way you already know, slower, child buckled. If you do not feel the road, keep waiting in heat and miss the first five minutes of stand-up. Work can survive a late join more easily than a spinout. That is not a slogan. That is a ranking for this hour.

### User
Transportation just picked up and said "check the site" and hung up. Site still normal. Chen's kid is eating snow off a glove.

### Assistant
Then the site is a broken oracle and the dispatcher was useless. You are the adult at the corner. 8:10 still works. Tell Chen your cutoff so she is not surprised when you pull away. You do not have to take her kid unless you already have permission and seats and you want that job. Do not add a second child on a sloppy road to be nice.

If you drive, email the teacher from the parking lot later: no bus, website showed regular, arrived by car. That is for attendance, not a fight. If you wait past 8:10, you are choosing the stand-up on a phone and wet boots as a maybe. Name it.

Next twenty: car heat, 8:10 cutoff, no more faith in the site, kid's socks if you have a spare in the trunk. You asked for a snow route. They gave you a hang-up. You still have a clock. Use the clock. The bus can be late. Your kid does not have to stand in the slush while a website lies calmly.
