### User
I got a text at 3:40: "Showing your place at 5:30, please have it tidy." It is 4:05. I work from the kitchen table. There are dishes, a drying rack, and my laundry on the chair. I have a call at 5. I don't know if I have to say yes. I don't know if I can say "not today." I also don't want a bad note in a file I have never seen. The lease is in a PDF I haven't opened this year. Two hours feels like a raid.

### Assistant
Two hours feels like a raid because it is a scramble, not because you are a bad tenant.

I will not invent your local entry-notice rules or what your PDF says. Those matter, and they vary. Open the lease now, search for "show," "enter," "notice," "hours." If it says 24 hours, you have language. If it says "reasonable notice," two hours may still be something you can push back on. Read the actual clause. Do not argue from a vibe.

While you read, text something that buys a clock without starting a war: "I have a work call at 5. 5:30 today isn't possible. What other times this week work?" That is a reschedule, not a veto of all showings.

If they reply "we already confirmed with the visitor," you can still say you will not be off a call, and that you need more notice next time. You can also offer 6:30 if your call ends and you can live with that. Offering a later slot is optional. It is not required for you to be "cooperative." It is a tool.

Is this a sale, a new tenant for when you leave, or a mystery showing?

### User
I'm month-to-month. I haven't given notice. I think they might be testing the market or showing to a friend. They've done this once before with four hours. I said okay last time and hid in the bedroom. The visitor opened the bedroom closet.

### Assistant
Month-to-month does not mean your afternoon is public property. It means the relationship can end with whatever notice your lease and local rules require. I will not quote those. I will say that a closet tour while you hide is a reason to be more explicit this time, not less.

Reply with the call and the ask for other times. Add one boundary: "Please don't open closets with my things if I am not there. I need at least a day's notice for showings when possible." You can put that in writing even if they ignore it. Writing is for the file you are afraid of. Silence is also a file; it just isn't yours.

Do not spend the next ninety minutes deep-cleaning like a punishment. Do the dishes if you can stand it. Move the laundry. You do not owe a staged apartment on a two-hour clock. "Tidy" in a panic is how people throw away mail.

If they show up at 5:30 anyway, you can keep the call with headphones and stand in the kitchen, or you can say at the door, "I said this time doesn't work." If you freeze, go to the bedroom and sit with the door closed. You already know hiding does not stop closets. A closed door plus "that's private" is louder than hiding.

### User
They just texted "see you at 5:30, it's quick." My call invite is with a client. I feel like I'm choosing between looking difficult and looking unprofessional.

### Assistant
Keep the client call. That is your job. The showing is their errand.

Text: "I can't do 5:30 — I'm on a client call. After 6:15, or another day with more notice." Then start the call on time. You do not need to wait for their blessing to work.

If they enter during the call, mute, one sentence: "I'm on a work call; please keep it to the living room." Unmute. You are not a tour guide today.

Difficult, in this context, is a story landlords tell when a tenant has a calendar. Unprofessional is missing a client. Choose the client. Read the lease tonight so the next text is not a raid. Two hours can be their habit. It does not have to stay yours.
