### User
I need to ask for more time on something and I don't want to sound like I dropped it behind the couch. I keep drafting "sorry, crazy week" which is both true and useless. Help me ask like a person who almost had this under control.

### Assistant
Before I write you a message, I need one thing I shouldn't guess. What is the something — a work deliverable with a named person waiting, school work with a posted date, or a favor for a friend — and how many days late are you already, if you're late at all?

That changes whether you're asking to move a future date or apologizing for a date that already passed. I don't want to coach the wrong one.

### User
Work. A writeup for my manager. Due tomorrow at noon. I'm not late yet. I have a draft that's 70 percent. The last part needs a number from another team that they promised today and haven't sent. I could turn in a hole, or I could ask for Friday.

### Assistant
That's not flaky. That's a dependency. Flaky is hiding until noon tomorrow. Asking today, with a specific new time and a reason that is about the missing number, reads like someone who almost finished.

Don't lead with "crazy week." That sounds like your life is the problem. Lead with the state of the work: what's done, what's blocked, what you're asking for.

A clean version:

"The writeup is mostly drafted. I'm blocked on [the number] from [team]. They said it would come today; I don't have it yet. I can send a version tomorrow with a placeholder, or I can send a complete version Friday if we can move the noon deadline. Which do you want?"

That last question matters. You're not only asking for Friday. You're offering the hole-tomorrow option so your manager can choose. People trust you more when you don't pretend there's only one path.

If you already know they hate placeholders, skip that fork and ask for Friday, still with the blocker named. Don't list three other reasons. One true blocker. If you also had a crazy week, keep it off the page unless they ask.

### User
I'm scared she'll think I should have nagged the other team sooner. I kind of should have.

### Assistant
Then include one line that shows you already acted, not a confession spiral. "I pinged them again this morning. If I don't have it by end of day I'll [send the placeholder / follow up with X]." That's ownership. "I messed up, I'm the worst" is a vibe, not a plan, and it sounds flakier than the miss.

You still ask before tomorrow noon. Asking at 11:50 a.m. tomorrow is what makes a reasonable delay look like a scramble. Today, during her afternoon, is the adult time.

Keep the message short enough to answer on a phone. No paragraph about your workload. No emoji. No "so sorry to even ask." You're asking because the artifact isn't complete, not because you're delicate.

If she says no, you send the best incomplete version on time and label the hole in the doc: "Number pending from [team]; I'll drop it in when it arrives." That's still not flaky. That's transparent. Flaky is silence, then a surprise.

If she says Friday, you put the new time on the calendar and you don't spend Thursday adding extra scope to look devoted. Extra scope is how extensions become a second personality problem.

### User
Okay. I'm sending it after this. One more fear: I always say "no worries if not" which undercuts the ask.

### Assistant
Cut that phrase. You do need a decision. "No worries if not" tells her the deadline was fake. End on the choice: placeholder tomorrow or complete Friday. Then stop typing.

You're not pretending you had it finished. You're showing you almost did, named the missing piece, and asked while there was still time. That's the whole difference between flaky and late-but-straight. Send it.
