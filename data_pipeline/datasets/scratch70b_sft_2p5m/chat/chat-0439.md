### User
The window unit in my bedroom rattles like a cart with a broken wheel. I reported it. Building email said "window ACs are tenant responsibility" and "we don't service them." It's 1:10 a.m. I have to teach at 8. I can turn it off and sweat, or keep it on and not sleep. They installed the bracket when I moved in and now it's a philosophy problem. I don't want a legal fight. I want the noise to stop or a different way to sleep. Talk me through tonight and the boring next step.

### Assistant
Tonight is sleep math, not a lease seminar.

I won't invent whether the building is right that they don't have to service a window unit. Leases and local rules differ. I also won't invent a price for a new unit or a repair. You need a quieter night and a written trail, in that order.

Tonight, pick the less bad: off plus fan if you have one, or on plus earplugs, or sleep in the other room if that room is cooler and quieter. If the rattle is the chassis vibrating in the window, a towel roll or a shim where the unit meets the frame can sometimes kill the buzz. It can also not. Don't force metal. Don't start unscrewing a window at 1 a.m. if you're not sure what's load-bearing. If you're not sure, leave the hardware alone after midnight.

Email is already a start. Save it. Photograph the unit in the window. That's tomorrow-you's file, not tonight's project.

Is the rattle new this week, or has it always been a little bad and now it's worse?

### User
Worse this week after they pressure-washed the courtyard. I think they knocked the housing. There's a gap on the left where you can see screen. Super said "tighten the side screws" and then left. I have a screwdriver. I'm afraid of the unit falling into the alley. It's a third floor.

### Assistant
Don't become a window engineer at 1:10 if falling is the picture in your head. Third floor plus a heavy box is a good reason to stop. Tighten only if you can see a loose screw that is obviously a screw, not if you're guessing which bolt holds the unit to the building. If the super told you to tighten and you're not confident, text them: "I'm not comfortable tightening on the third floor at night. The unit is louder since the courtyard wash and there's a gap. I need someone who can do this safely, or written confirmation that I shouldn't touch it." You already have "we don't service." Make them say whether safety is still "tenant."

Tonight: unit off if the rattle is the main injury, fan, other room, earplugs. Heat is miserable. Falling is worse. If you must run it, stay out from under the window while you test. I'm not joking.

Tomorrow, during daylight: ask a friend who's actually handy, or a company that mounts window units, if you decide this is now your object. I won't name a brand or a fee. Get the gap photographed in light. If the bracket looks bent, that's a photo, not a vibe.

You can still write one calm paragraph to management: date of the wash, new gap, new noise, you were told tenant responsibility, you're asking them to check the mount because they hit the building. They may say no. You still asked about the mount, not about "vibes."

### User
I'll sleep on the couch. In the morning I want one email that doesn't sound like a lawsuit and doesn't beg. Also: is it stupid to run it on low for an hour to cool the room then shut it?

### Assistant
Not stupid. Cool the room, then shut it, then couch. That's a reasonable experiment. If the rattle is violent, skip even the hour. You already chose the couch. Keep choosing it.

Email: "Window AC in 3B has rattled loudly since the courtyard pressure wash this week. There's a visible gap on the left. Super suggested tightening screws; I'm not comfortable doing that on a third-floor mount. Please advise whether maintenance will check the bracket, or confirm in writing that I should hire someone. I need to sleep and I need the unit not to fall." Dates. No threats. No "habitability" lecture unless a lawyer you actually have told you to use that word. I won't put legal terms in your mouth as facts.

Send it in the morning. Sleep now. You asked about a bracket. Get the hour of cool if it's safe, then the couch, then daylight photos. You have to teach at 8 without a window on the sidewalk.
