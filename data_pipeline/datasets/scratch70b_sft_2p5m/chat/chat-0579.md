### User
Maya wants me to be her "gym accountability partner." She bought a January membership in September last year and went four times. Now she's texting me screenshots of a class schedule and asking me to message her at 6 a.m. if she hasn't checked in. I already know she will not go. I don't want to be the nag, and I don't want to lie that I'll do it. How do I say no without sounding like I think she's lazy?

### Assistant
You are not her alarm clock. Accountability only works if both people actually show up to the same kind of plan. She is asking you to police a habit she has already dropped. That is a different job than spotting someone who is already in the parking lot.

Say the true thing in one line: "I don't want to be the 6 a.m. checker." Then one true reason you will share. True can be "I am bad at that kind of reminder and I will resent it" or "I don't want our texts to become a report card." It does not have to be a speech about her character. Do not diagnose her as lazy. Four visits is data about a membership, not a personality verdict.

Do not offer a fake softer version. "I'll try to remember" is how you become the person who forgot, and then she is mad at you instead of at the unused badge. If you want to stay in her corner, pick a smaller real offer: a lunch walk on a day you already leave the house. That is not a 6 a.m. surveillance contract.

I will not invent what her gym costs or what their freeze policy is. If money is part of her panic, she can call the front desk.

Has she already put your name on a trainer form, or is this still a text thread?

### User
Still texts. She sent a screenshot of a 6:15 spin class and wrote "don't let me flake." I go to a different gym at lunch. I am not going to spin at dawn. Last year I said yes to a similar thing for a 5K and I ended up writing pep talks while she slept.

### Assistant
Then the screenshot is a request, not a signed job. Answer tonight, not after three days of guilt-editing.

In the thread: "I'm not going to text you at 6 if you haven't checked in. I will flake on being that person, and I don't want that between us. If you want company, I can do a lunch walk on Thursday. Dawn spin is a no." That is the translation. "Don't let me flake" means she wants you to carry the willpower. You can decline the carrying.

If she replies that a real friend would do it, you can say, "A real friend will not pretend." Then stop stacking reasons. Two sentences is a boundary. Five sentences is a trial.

The 5K pep talks are useful memory. Last year's you wrote essays at 9 p.m. to a person who was already in bed. This year's you can refuse the essay job before it starts.

Do not volunteer to "just this week" unless you mean every morning this week. Temporary yes is how September memberships get a second narrator.

### User
She is going to say I don't believe in her. I kind of don't believe the 6:15 part. I do believe she wants to want it.

### Assistant
Wanting to want a class is real. It is also not a 6 a.m. text from you. Use the short split: "I think you want it. I'm still not doing the 6 a.m. texts."

You do not have to pretend the 6:15 is likely. You also do not have to say "you will not go." Predicting her failure out loud is a fight. Declining the job is a calendar.

If she keeps sending class screenshots, answer once more: "Same answer. Not the reminder person. Thursday lunch walk still stands." Then mute the class photos if you need to. Muting is not a betrayal.

Send the no tonight while it is still a text, not a trainer clipboard. Offer only what you will actually do. You can be a friend at lunch. You cannot be a badge swipe from another gym.
