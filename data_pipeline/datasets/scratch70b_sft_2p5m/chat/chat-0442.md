### User
My brother wants my credit card number to "book a flight tonight before it jumps." He says he'll Venmo me after payday. He already asked once in March and I said I needed to think. He heard think as yes-in-a-week. Now he's in my passenger seat outside the pharmacy. He says it's an emergency because the cousin's wedding is Saturday and the cheap seat is disappearing. I don't want to be the sibling who ruins a wedding. I also don't want my card on a website I didn't open. How do I say no in a parked car without a legend being born?

### Assistant
You don't lend the number. You can still care about the wedding.

"I'm not giving you my card. If you need a flight, we can look at your card, a debit card, or you call the airline on your phone. I'm not a backup card." That's the whole policy. "Just this once" is how March becomes a method.

Don't narrate his money life. Don't audit payday. Don't invent what a seat "usually" costs. I won't invent a fare. If the cheap seat dies, that's his booking problem, not your APR.

If you want to help in a way that isn't a card, you can offer a ride to the library wifi, or sit while he calls. You can even offer a small amount of cash you actually have, if you want — I won't tell you a number to give. If you don't want to give money, don't. Help and a card number are not the same kindness.

He's in the passenger seat. You can end the scene by opening your door for the pharmacy or by saying you need to go. The car is not a bank.

Does he already have a card that might work, or is the story that he's frozen out of everything?

### User
He has a debit card. He says it "might not clear." He doesn't want to call our parents. He asked me to type the number while he looks away so I "stay in control." That sounded thoughtful and also like a trick. Wedding is real. Saturday is real. I can feel last year's me reaching for the wallet to make the silence stop.

### Assistant
Don't type the number. Looking away is not a security model. It's still your account.

"Use the debit. If it declines, that's information. I'm still not putting my card on it." If he's scared of a decline, he can call the bank on speaker. You can sit there for that. You don't have to be the parent. You don't have to be the card.

If the debit fails and he panics, you still don't become Visa. You can say, "Then you talk to the hosts about arriving Sunday, or you look at a bus, or you call whoever you don't want to call." I won't design his whole trip. I don't know the city. You can refuse to solve the whole wedding in a pharmacy parking lot.

If he says you'll ruin the wedding, you can say, "I'm not booking it. I'm not ruining it either. Those are different." Then you go into the pharmacy or you start the car. Repeating the no is allowed. Designing a better no is how you stall until you fold.

Last year's wallet was a silence-killer. This year's you can let the silence be loud. He can be mad in the passenger seat. That's a feeling. It isn't a PIN.

### User
He just said he'll remember this at Thanksgiving. I have my wallet in my hand because I was going to pay for his ibuprofen. That's separate, right? Or did I already start the yes?

### Assistant
Ibuprofen is separate if you keep it separate. Pay for the box if you want. Don't put the flight on the same plastic. "I'll get the medicine. I'm still not doing the flight." If he tries to bundle them, you unbundle out loud.

Thanksgiving can be awkward later. That's not a reason to open a credit line in a parking lot. If he actually punishes you at a meal, you'll handle that meal. You don't pre-pay for a hypothetical scene with a card number.

Put the wallet on the medicine, not on the airline. Next two minutes: no to the number, yes or no to the painkiller as its own tiny decision, then you drive. He can book on debit or he can not go Saturday. You can love him and still not be the card. That's the once you're actually allowed — once, forever, no.
