### User
We said $25 each for a group gift. There are six of us. That's $150. Priya, who volunteered to "just order it," sent a screenshot of a $280 thing and a payment request for $46.67. The chat went quiet. Two people hearted the screenshot. I did not. I cannot do $46 without moving other bills. The party is Saturday. How do I say the number is wrong without being the person who ruined the gift?

### Assistant
The number is wrong because the group agreed $25. You are not ruining a gift. You are keeping the agreement.

In the chat, today: "I can do the $25 we said. $46 is over my budget. Can we stick to the cap or split a smaller version?" That is a full message. No apology essay. No "I'm broke" autobiography unless you want to give that. Budget is enough.

Hearts are not a vote. Quiet is people hoping someone else speaks. You can be that someone. It is uncomfortable and useful.

If Priya already ordered, that is her timing problem as well as a money problem. Ask: "Has it already been charged?" If yes, the group negotiates who covers the extra. It should not automatically be you. If no, cancel and buy the $150 thing.

I will not invent a fair-split law. I will say you do not owe $21 of surprise.

Did anyone actually say "go bigger" in words, or only hearts?

### User
Only hearts. Priya said "they'll love it" and sent the request. I haven't opened the payment app. Saturday is their birthday, not Priya's. I like the person. I don't like being bounced.

### Assistant
Do not open the request until the chat agrees. Opening and lingering is how you pay at 11 p.m. out of shame.

If Priya argues that hearts were consent, you can say, "I need a written yes on a new number. I didn't agree to $46." Written is petty in a good way. Group gifts go off the rails on vibes.

Offer a specific cheaper object or a card-plus-smaller-thing. Specific beats "let's think." If you cannot think of an object, "we're staying at $25 each, you pick something in that total" puts the shopping back inside the fence.

If two people want to go in on the $280 without you, they can. You can still sign the card and put $25 toward something else, or toward the same gift if they cover the rest without squeezing you. You do not have to match the luxury to prove love.

Saturday will still have a gift. It may not be Priya's screenshot. That is allowed.

### User
Priya DMed me "please don't make this awkward in the group." So now it's me versus the DM.

### Assistant
Answer the DM with the same number, then put the number in the group anyway. Awkward already exists. It is hiding in a request for $46.67.

"I'm not trying to perform. I can do $25. I'll say that in the chat so the others can too." Then you send the group line. Priya wanted the conflict private because private is where people cave. You are not required to cave.

If they already bought it, you still pay what you agreed unless you choose otherwise. You can say no to the extra in one sentence and still go to the party with a card. Being bounced into a higher tier is not a friendship test you have to pass. Pass the $25. Skip the $46. Let Priya's screenshot remain a screenshot. That is how caps work. They only work if someone names them after the hearts.

### User
Two people in the group just sent me "we can cover you" which feels like charity I didn't ask for.

### Assistant
You can say, "Thanks — I'm in at $25. I'm not looking to be covered into a $280 gift." Coverage still buys the thing you didn't agree to, with your name on the card. If they want the expensive object, they can put their names on that object. You can still sign a cheaper card or chip in at the cap for a second, smaller thing.

Charity that restores the original cap is different from charity that completes Priya's screenshot. Take the first if they actually mean "we'll lower the ask." Decline the second. Then post the $25 line. Saturday still happens. You still show up. You just don't get bounced. That was the whole point of a number in the first place. Use the number.
