### User
Jordan dropped a 92,000-word file in my inbox at 4:20 and said "honest notes by morning? you're the only one I trust." I have work tomorrow. I have not read a novel in a sitting since college. I like Jordan. I do not like being the overnight workshop. They've mentioned agents in a way that makes this feel high-stakes. How do I say no to tonight without saying their book is doomed?

### Assistant
No to tonight is not a verdict on the book. It is a calendar.

Reply before you open the file. Opening 92,000 words is how you accidentally start. "I can't do overnight notes. I can read a chapter this weekend and tell you if I can do more. If you need morning-after feedback, you need someone who's free tonight." That offers a smaller yes and a clear no.

Do not say "I'm sure it's great." You have not read it. Empty praise is how they hear a yes.

Do not say "I'm not qualified." They will reassure you and send a second file. Qualification is not the point. Time is the point.

I will not workshop the novel. I cannot see it. Honest notes take hours. Morning is not hours.

Have they asked you to read before, or is this the first dump?

### User
First dump. They said a friend of a friend is "maybe looking next week." I think that's why the panic. I already opened the first page. It's... fine. I closed it. I feel guilty that I closed it.

### Assistant
Close is allowed. Fine-on-page-one does not create an overnight debt.

Send the message. If the maybe-agent is real, rushing a tired friend is not a strategy. If it is not real, even more reason not to wreck your workday.

This weekend, if you still want to be kind, read chapter one and give three concrete notes: what you understood, where you got lost, one sentence that worked. That is a review. It is not a line edit of 92,000 words. Put that limit in the same message: "I won't be able to line-edit the whole manuscript."

If they guilt you — "you're the only one" — you can say, "I can still be a friend and not be overnight editorial." Only is a compliment with a trapdoor.

Do not introduce them to some other victim unless that person asked to read. Passing the hot potato is how friendships get burned in a chain.

### User
They replied "even a vibe check tonight??" I could skim twenty pages. I also know twenty pages becomes fifty.

### Assistant
Do not skim tonight. Vibe checks at midnight become you, angry, at 1 a.m., typing "loved the voice!!" which is a lie or a seed of a lie.

"Can't do tonight. Weekend chapter. Good luck with the maybe-next-week thing." Then mute until morning if you have to.

You already closed the file. Keep it closed. Work tomorrow is the novel you actually owe. Jordan's book can survive a Thursday without you. Your sleep cannot become the first round of agent panic. Send the sentence. Leave the 92,000 words for a weekend you choose. That is honest, which is what they asked for, just not on their clock.

### User
They sent a second file: "short version, 12 pages, please?" I still have work in the morning.

### Assistant
Twelve pages is still tonight if you open it. Same no. "Can't tonight. Weekend, the first chapter of the original, if I have it in me." Do not reward the second attachment with a skim. Second files are how overnight becomes a series.

If they are truly on a maybe-agent clock, they need a reader who is free, or they need to wait. You are not the emergency valve. Put both files in a folder named later and do not open later until Saturday, if then. Work morning is still the obligation that pays for your lights. Jordan's panic can sit in the inbox. You already answered. Repeating the answer is allowed. Opening the sequel is not required. Go to bed. The 92,000 words will be there. So will the twelve. Neither one is due from you at dawn.
