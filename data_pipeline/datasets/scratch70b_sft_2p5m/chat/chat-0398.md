### User
I took the old bed apart on Tuesday because the delivery window was Wednesday 9–1. Wednesday they sent a text: delayed. Thursday: delayed. Today is Friday. The new bed is "out for delivery" with a window that already closed. I have slats in a pile, a mattress on the floor, and a roommate who has stopped stepping over it politely. I called twice and sat on hold. I need a tonight plan and a what-to-say-to-the-company plan. I don't need a lecture about not dismantling first. I already know.

### Assistant
You already know. We can skip the lecture.

Tonight is sleep and a path through the room. Put the mattress somewhere flat and clean enough: floor is allowed. If you still have the old frame pieces, a temporary rebuild of the old bed is worth an hour if the hardware is in one bag. If the hardware is gone, do not invent a frame from chairs. Floor plus sheets is safer than a wobbly stack.

Clear a walking lane for the roommate. Piles against one wall. That is rent peace.

For the company: one call or chat with the order number, the missed windows, and a question: is it coming tonight, tomorrow, or do you need to reschedule. Write the agent name and time. I will not invent a refund policy or a compensation amount. Ask what they offer. If they offer nothing, you still need a date.

Is the mattress usable on the floor, and is anyone coming over besides the roommate?

### User
Mattress is fine. No guests. Roommate has a date tomorrow and asked if the woodpile can vanish. Tracking still says out for delivery. Support said "the driver may still come" at 6:40. It is 7:10. I feel like if I rebuild the old bed the truck will arrive as a joke.

### Assistant
If the truck arrives, you can take a bed. If it does not, you still need a floor you can live on. Rebuild only if it is fast and the old hardware is complete. Otherwise, stack the woodpile neatly, cover it with a sheet, and treat that as vanished enough for a date. A date can survive a covered stack. A maze of slats cannot.

At 7:10, "may still come" is a maybe. Set a cutoff: if there is no driver contact by 8:00, you message the roommate "it's the floor tonight" and you stop refreshing. Refreshing is not a delivery.

Tomorrow morning, contact them again with the three missed windows listed by date. Ask for a new guaranteed window, whatever that word means in their system. If they only have another 9–1, decide whether you can be home. Do not take the old bed apart again for a maybe. You already paid that lesson.

I will not tell you to charge a fee or threaten a chargeback. Those are tactics with consequences I will not script. Stay with dates, order number, and a request for a real window.

### User
What do I tell the roommate about the date? They already think I am the chaos tenant. And if the bed comes at 10 p.m. while their person is here?

### Assistant
Tell the roommate the truth in one text: "Delivery still unmarked. Wood will be stacked and covered tonight. If a truck shows late I will handle the door and keep it quick." Ask whether 10 p.m. would be a problem. If yes, you can tell the company you cannot accept late-night, if they even offer a say. If the driver is already on the way, you may not have a say. Then you meet the driver, you do not give a tour, you stash boxes in your room.

Chaos tenant is a story. A delayed bed is a common story. Stack, cover, cutoff, sleep on the mattress, write the dates in the morning. You cannot will a truck into the alley. You can make the floor kind. Do that now.
