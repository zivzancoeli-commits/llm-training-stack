### User
The delivery photo shows my box at 4B. I live in 4A. I knocked. My neighbor already opened it. It's a jacket I ordered, still in the poly bag, tape sliced. They said they thought it was theirs because the name looked close. They handed it back with this little laugh. I don't know if I should be mad, polite, or calling the seller. The jacket looks unworn. I'm standing in the hallway with the box.

### Assistant
Take the jacket into your apartment first. Don't negotiate in a hallway. Once the door is shut, check the packing slip, the size tag, and whether the poly bag is the only thing cut.

I won't invent what the carrier or the seller owes you. Policies vary, and I don't have your order page. What you have is: wrong door, opened by someone else, item returned to you.

The laugh is cover. You don't have to match it. "This was my package" is enough. You don't need a lecture on names that look close.

Is the packing slip still in the box, and does it show your name or theirs?

### User
My name. Size is right. One piece of tape is cut on the poly bag. They said they only looked because the label was facing away. I didn't take a photo of them holding it. I feel stupid that I didn't. They still have my tracking screenshot because I texted it when I knocked, which now feels like I handed them evidence that I was the messy one.

### Assistant
You are not the messy one. You texted a tracking photo to get your box. That's ordinary. They opened a parcel that named you. That's the actual event.

You don't need a photo of them holding it for the next ten minutes. You need a record of the state of the item and a decision about whether you keep it.

Do this in order. Photograph the shipping label, the packing slip with your name, the sliced tape, and the jacket laid on your table with the tag visible. Open the seller's app and find the order. If the jacket is the one you wanted and it looks unused, you can keep it and stop there. Opening by a neighbor is ugly. It is not automatically a ruined garment.

If anything feels off — perfume, a missing sticker, a tried-on crease — don't wear it yet. Message the seller with the photos: "Delivered to 4B, opened by neighbor, returned to me. Please advise." I can't tell you what they will offer. Don't promise yourself a free extra jacket.

A sliced poly bag and a laugh is a building problem, not a detective show.

To the neighbor, later, one sentence: "Please don't open boxes that aren't in your name. Leave them at 4A or with the office." No "haha all good." If it is not all good, don't type that.

### User
They asked if I wanted to come in for tea like that would reset it. Also should I tell the front desk? I don't want a war on the fourth floor. We share a laundry room.

### Assistant
Decline the tea. Tea is a soft reset they want, not a fix you need. "I'm going to deal with the order" is a complete answer. You can say it from your doorway. You do not have to stand in their kitchen and make the jacket into a funny story.

Front desk: if your building has a package room or a log, tell them once, in one sentence, that a carrier left a 4A box at 4B and it was opened. Ask them to note your unit on deliveries. That's a logistics note. It is not a wanted poster. I don't know your building's rules and I won't invent them. If there is no desk, skip the speech to whoever sits in the lobby.

Laundry-room peace is real. You can keep it without pretending the opening was fine. You got the item back, you documented it, you asked them not to open other people's mail. Then you use the washer like a person who still lives there.

If they keep bringing it up, say "I heard you" and change the subject to detergent. You don't have to say it's okay. You also don't have to write a manifesto on the bulletin board.

Wear the jacket if it checks out. If the seller wants it back, follow their return label, not the tea. Photos, a short seller note if you need it, and a boundary that fits on one line.
