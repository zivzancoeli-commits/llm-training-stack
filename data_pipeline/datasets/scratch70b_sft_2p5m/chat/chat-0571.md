### User
I'm in a shop where the fitting rooms are locked. A sign says "see associate." I have two shirts and a need to know if they fit. I don't want to buy first and return if I'm wrong — the return line is a second trip I won't take. I've always just bought the maybe. First time I'll ask them to unlock it. I feel like I'm requesting a vault. There's one associate folding pants.

### Assistant
You're requesting a fitting room, not a vault. That's what the rooms are for.

Walk to the folding associate with the shirts visible. "Could you unlock a fitting room?" If they have a limit on items, they'll tell you. Two shirts is not a raid.

I will not invent their theft policy. Some stores want a count. Some want you to leave a bag. Follow the boring rule they state. Don't argue philosophy. If they say rooms are closed, you ask when they open, or you decide whether to buy blind. Closed is an answer. You still asked.

Buy-first-as-personality is how you own shirts that pull at the shoulder. A five-minute unlock is cheaper than a closet of maybes. I will not invent the price of the shirts.

Are you carrying a big bag they might want to hold, or just the two shirts?

### User
Backpack. It looks stuffed because of a laptop. I already feel like I look like a problem. They're still folding. I am hovering.

### Assistant
Stop hovering at a weird distance. Close the last two meters and speak. Hovering is how you become suspicious in your own head. Talking is ordinary.

If they want the backpack at the counter, you can say yes or you can leave. I won't tell you which stores must. Laptop in a backpack is a reason you might not want to dump it. You can ask, "Can I take it in, or do you want it at the desk?" That's a normal negotiation. If they require the desk, you decide if you trust the desk. If you don't, you thank them and walk. That's allowed. Trying on shirts is not worth a missing laptop.

Don't over-explain the laptop. Don't joke about stealing. Jokes in fitting rooms land badly.

Two shirts on the hanger, question, wait. Folding will pause. You are not interrupting a sacred fold.

### User
If they say I have to buy one to try, I will want to fold and leave forever.

### Assistant
You can leave. You can also say "I need to try them, I won't buy without a room." If the policy is really buy-first, that's your cue to walk. Forever is a big sentence for one shop. There are other rooms in other places.

First-time ask: associate, unlock, item count, backpack rule, try, then buy or don't. No vault energy. No automatic purchase as manners. Hover ends when you use a sentence. That's a store, not a test of whether you deserve a mirror.

### User
They unlocked it. They counted the two shirts. They wanted the backpack at the desk. I kept the laptop on me and put the bag in the room. Tried both. One fit. I bought that one. Vault feeling gone. Hovering was the worst part.

### Assistant
Hovering is usually the worst part. Two meters and a sentence was the actual request. They counted because that's their job. You kept the laptop because that's yours. One shirt home is better than two maybes.

If the next store wants the whole backpack, you decide then. Today you found a middle: bag in the room, machine with you, door unlocked. First time asking didn't require a purchase as manners. You still bought the one that fit, which is a different yes.

Return line stays unused. Shoulder that doesn't pull is the point of the mirror. Folding associate survived the interruption. You can do this again without a vault story. See associate, unlock, try, then buy. That's a shop.
