### User
Book club is Thursday at Nadia's. I finished the novel. I did not like it. The prose felt proud of itself and the ending punished the only character I cared about. Everyone in the group chat is posting lines they loved. I have been reacting with hearts I do not mean. I usually just say "it was interesting" and let the fans run. This time Nadia asked me directly in a side text: "You're quiet — you hated it, right?" I don't want to perform a takedown. I also don't want to lie for two hours with a glass of wine. How do I tell the truth without becoming the person who ruins book club?

### Assistant
You can dislike a book without filing a lawsuit.

Nadia already opened the door. Answer her privately with something true and small: "I didn't like it. I'll say so without a speech if it comes up." That lets her stop fishing, and it lets you stop hearting quotes.

In the room, wait until someone asks you, or until it is your turn if you go around. Then: "It didn't work for me. The voice felt pleased with itself, and I didn't want that ending." Two sentences. Sit back. Fans will explain why you are wrong. Let them. You are not obligated to convert.

Do not match intensity. If they loved it at a 9, you can be a 3 without becoming a 0 with slides. "I finished it" is useful. It means you did the homework. Skipping is a different conversation.

Avoid "anyone who likes this is fooled." That ruins book club. "I bounced off the style" does not.

Is this a group that votes on the next book, or does Nadia just pick?

### User
Nadia picks. There are seven of us. Two people treat the pick like a personality. Last year someone said a book was "trash" and it got weird for a month. I don't want to be that. I also don't want Nadia to think I'm ungrateful because she hosted.

### Assistant
Host and pick are not the same as "please lie." You can thank Nadia for dinner and still not like the novel. Lead with the host if you need a buffer: "Thanks for having us. The book wasn't for me." Then the two sentences about voice and ending. Then a question for someone else: "What did you make of the last chapter?" Questions move the spotlight. Takedowns hold it.

Skip "trash." Skip sarcasm. Skip the group-chat hearts from now on; they are training you to fake it. A simple like on a logistics message is enough.

If the two personality people press, repeat once: "I know it worked for you. It didn't for me." Repetition is a wall. Debate is a tunnel. Do not enter the tunnel.

You do not have to offer a better book in the same breath. If they ask what you would have picked, you can name one title you actually liked, without making it a campaign against Nadia's taste. One title. Then wine. Then the dip.

### User
What if they ask me to rate it out of ten and I know my number will look hostile next to their nines?

### Assistant
You can refuse the number. "I'm not a scores person — it wasn't for me" is allowed. If they insist, a 4 next to nines is not a crime. Hostile is a tone, not an integer. Deliver it without a smirk.

If you freeze, you may still say "interesting" once. Then add the truth as a repair: "That's a dodge. I didn't like it." Late honesty works in a living room.

Thursday: tell Nadia now, briefly; in the room, two sentences plus a question; no trash, no fake hearts. You are allowed to be the person who didn't like this one. That is a book club, not a choir. Leave when it is late enough to be polite. You do not have to stay until you like the ending.
