### User
I start a new job Monday. They do a daily standup at 9:15. I've been on teams that used standup as a status dump, and teams that used it as a tiny planning meeting, and one team that used it as a guilt circle. I don't know which one this is. I don't want to overshare on day one, and I don't want to look like I have nothing to say. I almost have a script: yesterday / today / blockers. Then I remember I will not have a yesterday. Help me not sound like I memorized a blog post.

### Assistant
Before I hand you a script: is this standup in person, on a video call, or in a chat channel where people paste bullets? Those run differently, and I don't want to coach the wrong room.

### User
Video call. Camera on, they said. Fifteen minutes for a team of eight. I'm the ninth. I don't even have a laptop login yet. They told me I'll get that Monday morning before standup.

### Assistant
Then the honest first standup is short, and the login situation is a real blocker, not a joke.

You won't have a yesterday of work at this company. Don't invent one. Don't recap your old job. Don't say "yesterday I ramped." That's a blog word.

A clean first pass, out loud, when it's your turn: "I'm [name], first day. This morning I'm getting my laptop login and sitting with [whoever is onboarding you] on the setup. Blocker is I don't have access yet, so I can't pick up a ticket until that's done." Then you stop. That is a status. It is not a speech. It is not a confession.

If they go around with yesterday / today / blockers, you can map onto it without pretending: yesterday is none here; today is access and onboarding; blocker is access. If they don't use that template, don't force three headings. Match the length of the people before you. If everyone is twenty seconds, you don't do two minutes of origin story.

I don't know your team's culture from here. Some standups want only work items. Some want a human sentence. Listen for one round if you aren't first. Copy their altitude, not their jokes yet.

Questions are allowed if they are actually blocking you. "Who should I ping when the login email doesn't arrive?" is a good question once, not a seminar. Personal questions about people's weekends are optional. If the room does that, a single true sentence is enough. If the room doesn't, don't be the person who introduces icebreakers on day one.

Camera on: sit where your face is lit enough that you aren't a silhouette, mute when you aren't talking, don't eat a loud breakfast on screen. That's not morality. That's not making your first impression a crunching sound.

If someone asks you to take a ticket before you can open the repo, say so plainly: "I can take it once I can log in. I can't yet." That sounds like an adult who almost has it, because you do. You understand the ritual. You are missing credentials, not a personality.

### User
There's a guy on the team who talks for two minutes every standup, according to my future teammate. I'm afraid the 15 minutes will die and I'll be the last person and they'll skip me or I'll rush.

### Assistant
If they skip you, a calm "I can go in ten seconds" is allowed. If they don't skip you, still do ten seconds. You are not responsible for the two-minute person on Monday. Don't police them. Don't match them to look serious. Long updates from a first-day hire read as nervous, not committed.

If the call runs over, your onboarding person should still get you the login. Standup is not the only door. After the call, message that person: "I flagged access in standup. I'm at the desk. What do you need from me to get the account?" That is the real first-day move. The meeting is a window. The work is the account.

You almost had the script. Drop "yesterday." Keep today and the blocker. Sit on mute. When they look at you, say the three sentences and let the next person go. That's the whole thing.
